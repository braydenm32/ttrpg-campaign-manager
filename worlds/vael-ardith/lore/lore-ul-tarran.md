### What it was
When the Nine became stone, their children did not. The Lithic of the first generations were tall, grey, long-lived and barely touched by the curse, and they did what children of demi-gods do: they built. By the second century AW, Ul-Tarran stood as nine ward-towns joined by stone roads, with a capital, Tarran-Ulm, at the Seventh Ward in what is now called the Standing Fields. For nine and a half centuries after the Warding it was the only kingdom on the continent worth the name. The dwarves carved [[Hollowhall]] in 412 as its northern gate. The first humans along the Ardis paid it tribute and learned its count of years.

It was not a conquering kingdom. It was a watch. Every Lithic was raised knowing what lay under the land and what their family had done, and the whole of Ul-Tarran's law was a rota: who stood at which ward, and when, and who came after.

### How it ended
Slowly, which is how the Lithic do everything. The stone climbed faster each generation. Children were fewer. The rota got shorter. By 700 AW the ward-towns were half statues and the roads between them were kept by the dwarves, who had begun to think of the Lithic as holy because the Lithic had stopped answering. In 940 AW the last king, Anuvar the Still, took his throne in Tarran-Ulm for the last time, gave his last orders (the rota, again), and did not get up. He has not. [[The Silence of Ul-Tarran]] is dated from that sitting. The kingdom did not fall. It stood, and is standing.

### What is left
- **The Standing Fields** at the Seventh Ward: Tarran-Ulm's ruins and several hundred statues facing the stone. At the heart of them is the Hall of Standing Kings, a roofless hall that no outsider has reached since the delegation of 941, where the king sits on a chair of grey basalt. The Lithic say the statues speak to one of their own, at about a sentence an hour.
- **The ward-towns**: a barrow at the Third, a drowned quay under the Fifth, sealed galleries beneath the Eighth, and at the Ninth, a town the glass took. The village under [[Cinderford]]'s ford was built on it, and [[The Glass Barrow]] is its upper storeys, fused. A waystation's road-marker survives at [[Ambercote]] ([[A Lithic Road-Marker]]), and the Ninth Road's culvert runs under [[The Burned Mill]].
- **The script**: a tall, spare writing read top to bottom, kept by the Lithic and taught to no one. Most of it is the rota, and the glass of the Ashenreach is full of it. Nobody but a Lithic is known to read it.
- **The count**: AW, which everyone uses and nobody remembers was theirs.

### GM hints
- The statues in the Standing Fields are all awake. They speak to a Lithic, or to anyone holding [[The Ward Shard]], at about a sentence an hour. The script can be read by a Lithic, by someone who has dreamed in the Ward Shard's company for a month, or by nobody.
- [[Vexa Glassjaw]]'s diggers at [[Fort Mercy]] have broken into a lower ward-town chamber and found Lithic script on every wall, which she cannot read and is trying to sell. The script there is the Ninth ward-town's rota: the names of who stood at the Ninth and when, and the last entry is Uthrem's line, and the entry after it is blank. A Lithic reading it will go very quiet.
- Anuvar's last orders, if anyone reaches the Standing Fields and waits long enough to hear them, are the method for giving a ward a new heart. It takes a day to hear. It was meant for a Lithic. It does not say it has to be.
- The Remembrancers of Sael want Ul-Tarran's records because Ul-Tarran kept records of its neighbours, and in 1102 one of its wardens at the Fourth was still awake enough to see what the elves did.
:::
