### Appearance
Broad as a door and about as quick, a Shellback is a tortoise stood upright: a domed shell the colour of old bronze, a leathery neck that extends with great deliberation, a beaked mouth, and small black eyes that have seen your grandfather default. They stand a little shorter than a human and weigh three times as much. They dress the shell rather than the body: velvet caparisons, ledgers strapped to the carapace, a house-seal hung at the throat. The old ones' shells are carved with their account-books, line by line, the oldest entries worn smooth.

### Culture
Shellbacks count. They left [[The Ness of Thurm]], the mountainous wedge off Sael's west coast whose great peak their songs still name, in 1350 AW ([[The Shellbacks Leave the Ness]]), came east along the southern bays, and founded counting-houses in all seven before the humans had agreed what a bank was. [[The First Concord]] was signed in a Shellback house. Every loan the empire ever took ran through their ledgers, and the empire is gone, and the ledgers are not.

They do nothing quickly. A Shellback conversation is a thing you clear the afternoon for; a Shellback decision is a thing your heirs will hear. They speak slowly because they are choosing words that will still be correct in a hundred years. They are honest, in the way a tide is honest, and they never forgive a debt, because forgiveness would be a lie about the past. Their houses are families and their families are firms: Tollamund & Daughters, Veramund & Heirs. A Shellback with no house is a thing that does not occur.

They have a god and will not say who. Priests of [[Corvex]], whose books they keep more faithfully than anyone, find this discourteous.

### Customs and superstitions
A given name is known to two people and used once a decade; to be told a Shellback's given name is to be made an heir, or a hostage. "Have you ever been to the sea?" means you do not understand, and the only answer that does not make it worse is no ([[Sayings, Oaths and Curses of the March]]). A dumpling is eaten over an hour, in silence, and a human who finishes first has lost something they will not be told about. A debt is never forgiven, because that would be a lie about the past; a debt may be bought, which is not the same, and the Shellbacks have built a civilisation on the difference. They do not name their god and they do not go back to the Ness, and these are the same custom.

### Names
Long given names, used once a decade, and a house-name ending in the old word for a tally. Ammoset Tollamund, Orrunder Veramund, Bassiline Keelmund, Dromadune Harrowmund, Elevaris Pennamund, Caulwen Oldmund, Isserine Ashmund, Pellamor Giltmund, Thessaumund Vayle (an old form), Morravund the Younger. In daily use a Shellback is Grandmother, Grandfather, Aunt or Cousin and the house-name. [[Grandmother Tollamund]] has a given name; two people alive know it.

### Where they live
The counting-houses of [[Sevenhaven]] (where Grandfather Veramund keeps [[The Ledger Island Assay]], the oldest weigh-house in the Concord), [[Marrowport]], [[Cowl]], [[Gilt]], [[Harrowquay]], [[Keel]] and [[Vessary]], and the branch-houses that follow debt inland. [[Tollamund & Daughters]] in Cinderford is a branch-house, which should tell anyone paying attention how much debt is in Cinderford.

### How others see them
Patient, inevitable, faintly comic until you owe them. Humans joke about their speed and pay on time. Halflings respect them as the only people slower to forgive than a Mother of the Water. The orcs' Mothers deal with them as equals and both sides find it restful.

### Playing a Shellback
You are slow and you have decided that is everyone else's problem. Decide your house and what it is owed. You remember everything, you hurry for nothing, you are unfailingly courteous, and you will outlive this argument. You are curious, under the shell, about the one thing your people do not discuss, and you may be the first Shellback in sixteen centuries to go and look. Let the slowness do the threatening and the honesty hold the wall.

:::gm
The Shellbacks left the Ness for a reason they will not discuss, and the reason is their god: the Slow One, who lives under the great peak at the root of the wedge, whom they do not name, and who told them to go. Why, they have never said, and most of them no longer know. The Concord Houses' purchase of the Ashenreach's debts is Shellback policy from the Concord Houses down: own the march, own the lantern. [[Grandmother Tollamund]] believes the lantern is a key, and that the door it opens is the one the Slow One shut behind them, and that she will be the one to walk home through it. The charts fail at the Ness. Something is waiting on the far side of the failure, and it has been counting too.
:::

== GM notes ==

Tollamund is the long game at the table: she will never threaten, never hurry and never lose a sum. Speak her lines with a pause after every clause. A Shellback PC is a wonderful comic foil and a slow-burning mystery; the Ness is the campaign after this one.
