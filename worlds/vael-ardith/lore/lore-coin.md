Everyone counts in tens: ten copper to the silver, ten silver to the gold. Everyone agrees on that and on almost nothing else, because three mints and a stack of paper are all passing through the same three thousand pairs of hands in Cinderford, and a coin's face tells you whose pocket it came out of. In this town, this autumn, that is worth knowing.

### The Regency's coin
The empire's money, struck at the Mint in [[Caer Ardith]] by the same Seat of the Council that always struck it, with one change.
- **The crown**, gold, with Aurelan's sunburst on the face. An old imperial crown and a Regency crown are the same weight and nobody in the Ashenreach has seen either in a year. Old documents count in crowns, and so, from habit, do the clerks of Tollamund & Daughters.
- **The mark**, silver, the coin of wages and rents. Since 2981 the reverse has shown the Empty Throne, a chair with nobody in it, which is why a Regency silver is "a chair" in the Smokes. The year is struck under the chair, small; a mark of 3011 is a coin that has come a long way very recently.
- **The penny**, copper. The old ones show [[Aurel IX]] in profile, the long nose and the slight frown, as though listening to something, and soldiers keep them for luck ([[An Emperor's Penny]]). The new ones show the sun again and nobody keeps those.

Soldiers who learned to count under the empire call a silver a **shilling** whoever struck it, and the word has stuck to the whole denomination; a mark is a shilling, a shell is a shilling, and a Candle tab is reckoned in shillings and pence, as the slate says.

### The Concord's coin
The seven ports strike silver and little else, because gold between Shellback houses moves as ink and never as metal. Sevenhaven's silver carries the harbour Chain on the face and the striking house's mark on the reverse: Tollamund's is a tortoise-shell with a tally-stroke across it. A Concord silver is **a shell**, or **a toll**, because that is what most of them are spent on. A shell is good silver and rings like it; the Shellbacks have never debased a coin in fourteen centuries and would consider it a lie about the past. There is no Concord copper. Pennies are beneath them.

### Elsewhere
- **Iskaveth** strikes bright copper **lamp-pennies** with the Saint's flame on them, worth a penny to a baker and rather more to a pilgrim; they turn up in the offerings at the Treaty Stone and in the niche on Temple Row, and the Smokes' children swap them as charms.
- **The Grimmark** has no coin and has never wanted one. The Holds reckon in salt-barrels, oaths and favours owed, and a surface dwarf who has taken Regency pay is still, in his own head, owed a barrel by somebody. The Salt Feud was fought over the count of them ([[The Salt Feud]]).
- **The orc companies** are paid in coin and in nothing else, by [[The Mothers' Compact]], which says a licensed company is paid in the Emperor's coin. For seven centuries and more that meant the Mint's. The Mothers have never said what they think a shell is.
- **Sael** barters, remembers, and settles once a century. Nobody in the March has tried.
- **Ardmere Receipts** are the Regency's paper of 2995, promises to pay "upon the settlement of the realm", bought up by the Concord at forty in the hundred ([[The Ardmere Receipts]], [[An Ardmere Receipt]]). A soldier who still has one keeps it folded very small. "I'll take it in Ardmere Receipts" means no.

### Telling good from bad
Mistress Hebbe at the gatehouse drops every coin she is handed onto the stone step and listens. Good silver rings; lead sings flat; a coin that has been sweated rings a note she says is "sorry about itself". She has not been wrong in eight years, and once a month a coin rings wrong enough that it goes to the Reeve's board as a job. Fennick bites. Spoons has a little spoon-balance. Tollamund's clerks do not test coin at all; they count it, because they know which mint it came from before it leaves your hand.

### What a copper buys
A jack of small beer at the Candle, a loaf of ash-bread at [[The Baker's Wife]], a stone pear at Gutter Market, four tallow dips at Hesk's, a foot across the toll bridge, a crossing on Nan Crupp's ferry (or something true instead), a prayer written down by Brother Audit. A silver buys a bed in the Candle's loft, or a thimble of the last pear brandy, or a week of dips and a pint of rape-oil to burn them in. A gold buys a hank of glass-spider silk, after dark, from a man who will not hold it. A ranker of the Ninth earns five copper a day and found, when paid, which is a jack of beer, a loaf, a pear and a crossing, with a penny over for the Emperor. For the whole list, see [[What Things Cost in the Ashenreach]].

### Slang
- **A candle**: a copper, in the Smokes, where light is bought a penny at a time.
- **A chair**: a Regency silver. **A shell**, **a toll**: a Concord one. **A listener**: an old Aurel penny.
- **A crown**: a gold, whoever struck it, and also, with a straight face, the Regency.
- **Nine pennies**: a night's sleep. "I've nine pennies" means I can sleep indoors tonight. From [[The Ninepenny]].
- **A Fennick**: a debt you will die owing, entered honestly. Spoons's word.
- **In goats**: real money. "What's he worth in goats?" The March's only hard currency.
- **Arrears**: nothing. "What did you get?" "Arrears."
- **A never**: a promise with no date on it. Spoons's again, from a bet in his book that nobody has ever come to collect.
- **Paper**: anything the Regency sends instead of coin. "They've sent paper." Said the way other towns say "they've sent the plague".
- **Pay-day**: never.

:::gm
**The two silvers are the campaign's first clue, and they point two ways.** New **Regency marks** dated 3011 are Quiet Office money: Mote's pay to Sallow, Sallow's to Dannic. Sallow's rounds at the Candle, the twelve in Dannic's purse in the Cold Room, the one in his belt-pouch on the Flooded Street, the coin he paid Hennet's tab with on 5 Ashfall (in a cup under her bar, the first new Regency coin anyone in the Ashenreach had seen in two years) and the silver Pruitt carried to Ottun Fray for the wanted bill on the 13th are all the same mint and the same year. Investigation DC 12 on any of them finds the date. **Concord shells** with Tollamund's house-mark are the wagon: the 5 cp the sealed House cart paid Hebbe at dawn on 7 Ashfall, and the 600 gold in new Sevenhaven coin in the strongbox at Fort Mercy. The escort sergeant's shells at the Three Cups and the escort's purse that Jaspin Sweet bought from Glasser runners are the escort's own pay, not the box. The "crate of Concord coin with a Regency die" at Gutter Market is a Quiet Office decoy, meant to make the two trails look like one. Keep them separate at the table: Regency silver is Mote; Concord coin is Tollamund.

**The Compact lever.** If a PC thinks to ask the Mothers whether a Concord shell is "the Emperor's coin", the answer in session 7 is Grashka's to give, and it is no. An orc soldier of the Ninth cannot be made to take Tollamund's wage under licence. Vosk knows this and has been waiting for somebody else to say it.
:::

== GM notes ==

Make coin tactile: Hebbe drops it, Hennet keeps it in a cup, Spoons weighs it, Tollamund never touches it. When a PC pays for anything, ask what the coin looks like once, early, so that the first time they see a chair with 3011 under it they already know to look.
