Cinderford talks in noise. A town on a ford at the edge of the ash, with a garrison on the hill and a Rent on the horizon, learns to listen first and ask afterwards, and everyone over the age of six knows what most of it means. Newcomers learn the hard way, usually by asking what the bell was for while everyone else is already running.

There are three alarm languages in town, and they belong to three different people: the bell to the Ford, the drum to the company, the lamps to the plain. They have never once been made to agree.

### The Keep's watch-bells
The hours. The Keep rings them from the tower, counted from dawn: the **first bell** is dawn muster, and the day bells run on from there; after dusk the count starts again with the **night-bells**. The town keeps its hours by them. [[The Assessor's Office]] is open from the second bell to the fifth; [[The Last Candle]] stays open to the third night-bell, and a soldier caught in the Smokes after the fourth had better have a reason or a friend.

### The muster handbell
A dented handbell on [[Lantern Hill]], rung by the duty corporal, which carries further than it has any right to. A long, even ringing is **muster** (dawn and sunset). Fast and broken is **stand to**: every soldier to the yard, armed, now. Townsfolk who hear it shut their doors.

### The Treaty Bell
[[The Treaty Bell]] at the Ford speaks two ways, and only one of them counts as ringing.

**The rope** is knotted up out of reach and pulled only on the Keep's order, for alarm or for treaty business. It has been pulled nine times in nine years: the signing; two greywinds that reached the walls; three red moons when the dead walked in from the plain; the day the Regency pay stopped; a drunk, who was flogged; and once, on the night of 2 Ashfall this year, by nobody.

**The mallet** is the bell-ward's, struck from the bell-house stair, and is the town's everyday voice:

| Strokes | Means |
|---|---|
| One | The ford closes (at dusk) or opens (at dawn) |
| Two | A death |
| Three | Fire |
| Three, a pause, three | Someone in the water |
| Four | The end of the world |

Four has never been struck. [[Dunny Pike]], who has kept the bell since his father died, says he practises it in his head. He learned the strokes from old [[Halloran Pike]] by the Candle fire.

### The drum
[[Thistle Weirford]] beats the company's calls on a drum nearly as big as she is, and is the only person in the company who can keep time, which is why the drum is hers. The calls are on a card nailed inside the guardroom door, in a hand older than the Ninth:
- **The Muster**: three beats and a roll.
- **Stand to**: one long roll, as long as it takes.
- **The tally call**: taps for the tally's number, one to fourteen, then a flam. "Tally nine to the gate" is nine taps and a flam, and nobody in tally nine can count past five when it is raining.
- **The Wicks**: a fast skipping beat that calls the recruits to the yard. The company calls it the Wicks' Jig and dances to it badly, at funerals.
- **Rations**: two beats and a scrape. It brings the company faster than Stand to, and the officers have given up minding.
- **The Captain**: a double beat that is meant to be nothing in particular and sounds exactly like *Mar-row*. Thistle swears she does not do it on purpose.
- **The Dead March**: one slow beat for every step to [[Corvel Rise]].
- **Stand down**: the last line on the card, which says only *by the Captain's order. Not taught.* Thistle has never beaten it and does not know how it goes.

### The lamps
On the signal tower at the south end of Lantern Hill, looking out over the Smokes to the plain, [[Noume Flarewick]] works the shuttered lamps at night and talks to the company's far patrols on the ash road in lamp-code, a whole sentence in the time it takes to say one. Most nights the conversation is short:
- *All well.*
- *Road buried at the stakes. Coming in slow.*
- *Greywind from the east. Shutters.*
- *Glassers on the road. Six. Friendly. Loud.*
- *Send soup.* (The patrol always says send soup.)

For the whole town there are the flares: a **red** flare is riders on the road, a **white** flare is the dead walking in from the plain, which has been fired three times in nine years and every one of them a red moon.

### The rest of the town's noise
- **The Smokes' door-bells**, rung twice: it is a friend, open up. A tradition since [[The Night of Open Eyes]]. A single ring in the Smokes after dark is answered with a bolt being shot.
- **The ferry**: when [[Nan Crupp]] lays her pole across the landing, she is not crossing tonight, and no fare will change her mind.
- **The Hollowborn** have a signal nobody taught them: a child of the Smokes who stops dead in the lane and stands aside is letting somebody pass. Walk round the space, not through it.

### Rumours
- Thistle reads every message she carries and sells the good ones.
- The drum card in the guardroom was copied off the Seventh Company's, and the Seventh's drummer is buried with the original.
- The tenth pull of the Treaty Bell's rope will be the last one.
- On red-moon nights the signal tower gets answers from posts the company does not have.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True, and she sells them to [[Kettle Brindlewake]] for toffee. 2. True about the copy; the Seventh's drummer has no grave, like most of the Seventh. 3. Near enough: the tenth pull is the night the lantern leaves the Hill (session 8). 4. True (below).
:::

:::gm
**The three flashes.** On the red-moon night (session 3) Noume signals the far patrols and gets back a message from nobody: three short flashes from inside the Rent, from far past where any lamp should carry. The Ninth's code has no meaning for three flashes, and neither had the eight dead companies'. It is older than any company: the lamp-code of the Last Wardens, and the sender is [[Warden Uthrem]] on the Warden's Shelf, answering the flame the only way left to him. In session 6 Noume (or whoever stands where Noume would) sees the same three lamps cut into the rock of the Shelf beside the ring of figure-stones, with a Lithic line under them that Uthrem will read aloud: *Holding. Send relief.* He has been holding for forty years, and the relief he means is the Reseal: a willing living heart at the socket, his own first, if anyone will carry him to it.

**Sallow's call.** Sallow's men use the Rations call with one beat dropped (a beat and a scrape) to mean *the Hock's back room, tonight*. Thistle noticed in Reaping, because she notices anyone getting her drum wrong, and has sold it to Kettle, who has not decided who to sell it to. A PC who hears the wrong Rations call on a Tideday or Wardday can follow it.

**The night the signals disagree (session 3).** At midnight the lantern gutters to a blue bead in sight of the whole town. Run the three languages against each other:
- On the hill, [[Mirrel Vosk]] roars for Stand to; Thistle beats the long roll; the handbell goes, fast and broken. The Sleepless sitting round the hill hear it as a beginning and start to sing.
- In the square, Dunny sees the light go blue and then go out for a breath. There is no stroke for the lantern. He strikes two, a death, because that is what it looks like from the square. Doors open all down Ford Street; [[Odile Fairweather]] sends a runner up the hill to ask who has died; [[Spoons Aldane]] opens a book on the answer.
- On the tower, Noume signals *Stand to* to the patrols. The patrol at the Treaty Stone answers *All well. Send soup.* The second patrol, out past the stakes, does not answer at all (its lamp-man has fallen asleep, and is flogged for it in the morning). From the dark beyond the glass come three short flashes.
Brother Tallow is, at that moment, collapsing at the cage, and for an hour half the town believes the two strokes were for him. Whoever goes down to the square and tells Dunny to stop has a twelve-year-old in tears and a friend for life.

**Session 8.** As the lantern goes down the hill on its handcart, Dunny strikes four, for the first time, then hauls down the knotted rope against every order he has ever had and pulls the tenth. Thistle beats the Muster all the way down to the bridge and the Wicks' Jig across it, because nobody told her not to. If the ward is resealed and Marrow reads the contract aloud at the Stone, someone has to beat Stand down: Thistle makes it up, there and then, and it is three slow beats and a roll, and every drummer in the March plays it that way afterwards.
:::

== GM notes ==

Use the signals as a clock the whole table can hear. A bell stroke or a drum call is the cheapest scene-change there is: describe the sound, let the players ask what it means, and let an NPC answer while running. Keep the meanings fixed (players will learn them, and should be rewarded for it) and let the surprises come from who is striking and why. The four strokes, the tenth pull and the Stand down call are each used once in the whole campaign; save them.
