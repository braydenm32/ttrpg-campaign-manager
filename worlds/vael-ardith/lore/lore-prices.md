Every price here is the one on a board, a slate or a shop wall somewhere in the March, at the going rate, in the coin that rate is quoted in. Haggling shifts them by a copper or two and a Tollamund clerk shifts them the other way. Bring a Regency coin and a Concord one and see which the keeper reaches for; for what the coins are and why that matters, see [[Coins and Money Slang]].

### A roof
- A pallet at [[The Ninepenny]], 9 cp. A blanket, 1 cp. The room with the door, 3 sp. The cheapest roof in Cinderford, and the only one that asks about your dreams.
- A bunk in the loft at [[The Last Candle]], 1 sp, two to a pallet and three on a payday.
- A bed in the long room at [[The Sunburst]], Whitepost, 8 cp. A room with a door, 2 sp.
- A bunk in the bilge at [[The Thirteenth Barrel]], Sedgemere, 3 cp, "dry above the third rib; do not sleep below it".
- A place on the planks at [[The Ripe One]], Tally's Cut, 1 cp, bring your own blanket. The glass underneath is warm.
- A loft bed at [[The Seventh Chair]], Sevenhaven, 1 sp; 2 for the one with a door.

### What people earn
- A **ranker of the Ninth Lantern:** 5 cp a day and found, when paid. A **corporal:** 8 cp. A **sergeant:** 1 sp 5 cp. A **lieutenant:** 3 sp. The **captain:** 6 sp. On the books; the books are the Regency's.
- A **Glasser cutter** on a paying dig, 5 sp a day (7 for the cold ledge), 3 for a breaker. A dig pays about one day in ten.
- A **kiln-hand,** 3 cp a day, and the dust-cough thrown in.
- A **Reeve's clerk,** 1 sp a day, and a hat.
- A **carter** to Fallowden, by the job: the 4 gp.

### What a copper buys
A jack of small beer. A loaf of ash-bread. A stone pear. Four tallow dips. A crossing on the bridge or the ferry. A prayer written down. A day's rent of a handcart. A strand of rope. A ranker's day's wage buys any five of them, and he is not paid.

:::gm
**What the figures say.**

- **The company's quarter is about six hundred gold.** A ranker earns 5 cp a day: 45 sp a quarter. A hundred and thirty-eight ranks, with the corporals, the sergeants and four officers on their own rates, come to about 6,000 sp, which is 600 gp, give or take the odd forty. That is what Tollamund's advance in Ashfall was, in new Sevenhaven coin. Nobody has been paid since 1 Hollowfrost 3009, so eleven quarter-days have gone by unpaid, including this one: about 6,600 gold on paper, the debt the Concord bought at forty in the hundred in 3009. [[Brother Audit]] will tell a PC the exact figure, if asked, down to the copper, and the number is the horror.
- **The Regency's figures are not these.** The tax notice demands 3,882 silver, of which 3,240 is the lamp tax at a silver a night for nine years: more than a ranker earns in two. Its Hearth Levy (418 sp, assessed on "wages intended") matches nothing in this list. Pell cannot explain it either.
- **The Hock's rate is the quiet outrage.** A copper in ten a week is a tenth every six days; Tollamund's is a tenth every ninety. A man who borrows 1 sp at the Hock owes 1 sp 1 cp the next week and 2 sp 3 cp by the quarter; Tollamund would ask him for 1 sp 1 cp at the quarter. Bushel does not say this aloud. Tollamund, who licensed him, does not need to.
- **Ford Street's slates are the best census in the March** and the best price survey: a Tollamund clerk copies the whole street's boards every Coinday. If a PC asks a shopkeeper what a thing "was last month", they will find out which of the prices has moved, and why.
- **The Candle's loft is 1 sp, not 5 cp.** If a player cites the old figure from a stale handout, Hennet's slate wins.
- **Coin is the clue.** Regency silver dated 3011 is Mote's money; Concord shells with the Tollamund tortoise are the wagon's. A shop that quotes a price in silver and takes the coin offers a free look at which kind it reaches for: Hebbe rings it; Hennet puts it in a cup; Bushel counts the change in coppers, which he says clerks do not do.
:::

== GM notes ==

Use this page as the answer when a player asks "what does it cost?" and as the sanity check when a price comes up that the book does not hold. Roll up from a copper: if the party can buy a thing for a ranker's day, it is cheap; if they need a week, it is dear; if it is a month, it is a quest. The quartermaster's slate, the Hock's ledger and the Candle's chalk will all give the same story. In session 1 give a PC a handful of coppers and let them feel how far five of them go.
