### Appearance
Grey. A Lithic child is born the colour of river pebbles and warm to the touch; by thirty the skin at the knuckles and heels has the grain of sandstone; by seventy the forearms and shins are stone to the elbow and the knee, and the Lithic walks with a slow deliberation that is not age but weight. The face goes last. The oldest Lithic are statues with eyes: a figure in the pose it chose, moss in the folds, and in the face two living eyes that follow you, and a mouth that can still, very slowly, speak. They are tall, long-limbed and still-featured, and their hair is the last thing to grey.

### Culture
The Lithic are what is left of [[Ul-Tarran]], the kingdom that rose around the Great Wards in the first age and petrified around them over nine and a half centuries. Their culture is a ritual of departure. A Lithic family prepares its elders for the Standing the way other peoples prepare for a wedding: the place is chosen, the pose, the last words, which are spoken while the mouth still moves and recorded by whoever is left. Then the elder walks, as far as they can, to a ward, and stands, and does not stop being alive.

They know the Sleeper's true name. They do not say it to outsiders. They do not say much to outsiders; the Lithic are courteous, formal, and so slow in speech that a conversation is a commitment. They keep the old script, which the glass of the Ashenreach is full of, and they teach it to no one.

### Customs and superstitions
Wardday is theirs: nothing is built or dug, and the surface dwarves come and sit with them. Ketterin's luck is the only luck they recognise, the luck of staying ([[The First Standing]]). Bread is left at a Stander's feet by whoever passes, and a Stander who is never passed goes unfed, which does not kill them and is noticed. The last words are spoken while the mouth still moves and written down by whoever is left; a Lithic who stands with nobody to write them is the loneliest thing on the continent, and Uthrem has been that for forty years. They do not say the Sleeper's name to outsiders, and they do not sing the tenth verse of the tomb-song where a human can hear it ([[Songs of the March]]).

### Names
Old, heavy, two syllables, the stress on the first. Omneth, Sarrik, Telune, Hadrun, Ishkeh, Morrun, Quellith, Bastrem, Anuvar, Keth. No family names; a Lithic is named for the ward their line stands at: Sarrik of the Seventh. [[Warden Uthrem]] is Uthrem of the Ninth, and the last of that line.

### Where they live
Nowhere, any more. A handful of Wardens at the ward sites, keeping stones that the nations around them have forgotten are anything but landmarks; a few families in the Grimmark, where the dwarves treat them as holy and tiresome; a scholar or two in [[Caer Ardith]]. [[The Last Wardens]] are most of what remains organised.

### How others see them
The surface dwarves revere them as ancestors made stone. The Regency's scholars collect them. Most people have never seen one walking and think of them as a kind of monument that occasionally blinks. The Vesh can see what they are standing on and are polite about it.

### Playing a Lithic
You are the last of something and you know exactly what. Decide how far the stone has reached and how long you think you have before you stand; decide whether you mean to stand at all, because a Lithic who refuses the Standing is the loneliest person on the continent and the most alive. You are patient to a fault, literal, and carry the weight of a secret you did not choose. Play the slowness as gravity, not weakness.

:::gm
The stone is a curse, laid by [[Tharos]] on the line of the Nine for the Godslaying, and it is also the wards: every Lithic who stands at a Great Ward adds their divinity, thinned by a hundred generations, to the stone, and every living statue is still awake and holding. Fewer Lithic are born each generation because the wards are drinking the line dry. [[Warden Uthrem]], who can barely move, has stood at [[The Ninth Stone]] forty years and waited nine for someone who can hear the flame, and knows the Ninth needs a new heart, and knows it does not have to be Lithic; it has to be willing. He is waiting for [[Ysse Lampwright]], because the flame already has her.
:::

== GM notes ==

A Lithic PC is a slow fuse the campaign can light: the Standing is Ending A's price with a face. If nobody plays one, Uthrem carries it. The Lithic script in the glass at Fort Mercy and the Glass Barrow is readable only by a Lithic or by someone who has handled the Ward Shard long enough to dream in it.
