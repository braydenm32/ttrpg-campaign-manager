### What the world knows
Almost nothing, and most of that wrong. Common folk know that something lies under the continent and that the Great Wards keep it there; children in the Regency are told it is a dragon, in the Grimmark a drowned giant, in Sael nothing at all. The Sleepless of [[The Smokes]] say it is a god who dreams, and that it dreams of an end to pain, and that it only needs waking. Scholars who have read the Lithic fragments say the Nine killed a god and buried what would not stay dead, and are not asked to dinner twice.

### What the Lithic know
Its name is Ysmereth. It was the god of dreams and endings: the one who, some myths say, dreamed the first mortals at [[The Godsdawn]], and who would have dreamed the last. The Nine killed it at [[The Godslaying]] to prove that mortal-born could unmake a god, and learned the one thing the gods already knew: a god cannot fully die. What was left they held under the land with nine wards made of their own divinity, and became stone to do it. See [[The Nine]] and [[Ul-Tarran]].

### What it is now
Something between death, coma and undeath, and nobody, not the Lithic, not the pantheon, knows which. It dreams. The dreams leak where the wards are thin: the [[The Hollowborn]] are born of them, the [[The Weeping Orchard]] bleeds with them, and at every full red moon half of [[Cinderford]] dreams the same dream. Each full Ember the dream is clearer than the last. [[Warden Uthrem]] says that is not the Sleeper waking. He says it is the Sleeper learning to speak.

### What it wants
To be used. This is the secret under the secret. The Sleeper does not want to wake, or does not only want that; it wants a hand on the leash, because a hand on the leash is a mouth it can borrow. Whoever holds [[The Lantern of the Ninth]], lit from a shard of the Ninth Stone, begins to hear it; first in dreams, then in daylight, then in their own voice. [[Brother Tallow]] has held the flame nine years and has started to say things he does not remember saying. [[Ysse Lampwright]] hears it already and thinks it is the lantern. It hides this want well. It lets the Sleepless believe it wants waking, the Saint believe it wants drinking, the Regency believe it wants ruling, and lets each of them pull at the crack.

### The pantheon
The living gods could never finish it: it lies under mortal wards no god can pass, and [[Hathe]] kept the door besides. Hathe is dead and the Ninth Ward is cracked, and every god has a different plan for what comes through. See each god's page. The Sleeper knows this too. It is not afraid of them. It has been dying for three thousand years and has had time to think.

### The clock
**The Sleeper stirs** has twelve segments and starts at 3, one for each failing ward: the Fourth weak, the Eighth breached, the Ninth cracked. It ticks +1 at each full Ember (sessions 3 and 8), and that is all it does unless you choose to speed it up (below). Spells cast in the Rent, or within a mile of the Ninth Stone, always frost the nearest wall, so the players can see the cost. The Glass Barrow and the Cold Seam are minor ward-nodes: spells there frost the walls and feed the Cold Captain, but never tick the clock.

**Speeding it up.** Left alone, the clock is slow on purpose: it reaches 5 by session 8, the dead of Cinders stay in the ash, and the horror stays a promise. If your table wants the world to come apart faster, add any of these, one at a time, between sessions:
- +1 if the Lantern goes fully out for more than a breath (Ysse failing the session 3 tending, say).
- Spells cast in the Rent, or within a mile of the Ninth Stone: roll a d6, and on a 1 the clock ticks. For a fast clock, tick on a 1 or 2.
- +1 for battle sorcery (any spell of 5th level or higher cast in anger) near the Ninth Stone, with no roll.
- +1 when a PC answers the Choir in the Rent, or drinks the Weeping Orchard's sap at full Ember.
- +1 when the Sleepless get a full rite done (a drowned body walked, a lamp of lake-ash lit on Lantern Hill).
- +1 each time whoever holds the flame lets it speak through them.
With one or two of these, a typical party reaches 6 by session 8 and the dead of Cinders stand on the lip for the finale. With all of them, expect 7 or 8, and the aftermath is already arriving as the campaign ends.

At six, the dream reaches everyone in the March of Cinders, sleeping or not, and the Cinders dead stand on the lip. At nine, the dead of the Ashenreach stand up on their own at red moon without a ward-crack to help them. At twelve, the Sleeper speaks through whoever holds the flame, in their voice, and does not stop. Nine and twelve belong to the aftermaths of Let It Crack and Use It. Reseal takes three off. The growth steps of the Cinders-born at each full Ember are a separate thing and are not clock segments.

== GM notes ==

The Sleeper is never a monster on the table. It is a voice, a dream, a feeling of being wanted. Its dreams are on the Sleeper Dreams table; its voice, when it comes through a flame-holder, should be reasonable, tired, and sympathetic to whatever that PC most wants. The Ending C road (use it) is the Sleeper's road: let it feel like winning.

Keep "Ysmereth" out of the players' hearing until Warden Uthrem says it in session 6. The name is a key: a Lithic who hears a mortal say it knows that mortal has been to the Rent.
