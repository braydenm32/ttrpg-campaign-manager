The pear country fed half the Regency once. Now the March grows goats, ash-grass and a grey barley that comes up thin in the lee of the walls, and everything else comes up the river on a barge or down the road on a cart that somebody has paid double for. Cinderford eats what Kettle brings, what the Smokes can stretch, and what the dead left in the glass, and it eats it with more ceremony than you would expect, because food is the one thing a town on the edge of the ash can still do properly.

### Bread and the pot
- **Ash-bread.** Grey, heavy, good. The barley is ground with the chaff in, the water tastes of ash, and the loaf comes out the colour of the plain and keeps for a week. A penny at [[The Baker's Wife]] on Ford Street, which bakes for the town and, nine days late, for the company.
- **The Hearthday loaf.** Baked on Hearthday for [[Maelis]], marked with a cross, two pence, and given free to anyone who comes to the hearth-house hungry. Mother Brisk's are better than the Baker's Wife's and both women know it.
- **Stone-pear dumplings.** Suet and barley, boiled, the size and heft of the fruit they are named for and, the joke goes, about as digestible. Two pence. Kettle Brindlewake says her barge was named first.
- **Keel oil-fish stew.** The staple. Keel ships its fish south in the same sealed tins as its whale-oil, preserved in the oil, and the tins are cheaper than the fish in them; a tin, an onion and a kettle of river water make the stew that feeds the Candle, the Keep and every barge on the Cinder. Three pence a bowl with bread. Hennet's is better than Brannoc's and Brannoc carries his down every Restday to be told so.
- **Hennet's soup**, which is a different thing, made from whatever is left on the plates, and which lit the ninth lantern, she says, and she says it sober.
- **Eleven-day stew.** The company's name for what Brannoc makes when the flour will stretch eleven days at full ration and he has been told to make it last thirty: oil-fish stew with the oil left in and the fish mostly out. By the third week it is grey barley in hot water with a memory of fish, and the company eats it standing, so as not to see the bottom of the bowl.
- **Burnt-hand stew.** Brannoc's for the 14th of Ashfall: whatever is in the pot, scorched on the bottom on purpose, because that is how it was the first time. Eaten at the cooks' camp in silence. See [[Feasts, Fasts and Holy Days]].

### Fish, goat and worse
- **Stillwater blind-fish.** Pale, eyeless, caught in a lake that does not ripple and dried on racks above it. Tastes of nothing; keeps people alive. Kettle trades salt and iron for it. The Stillwater folk will not eat the heads and will not say why, and a Cinderford cook who serves a blind-fish with its head on is a cook who has never been to Stillwater.
- **Grimmark salt-cod**, by the barrel of twelve, never thirteen. Hask's boats catch it past the ice and the Holds sell it south through Skarn's pass. Gerda Hollin's tally eats it on Wardday and nobody else can get near the gatehouse.
- **Goat.** When there is goat. Eight pence at the Candle for a plate, and the plate is small, and the goat was probably somebody's. The March swears by it ("by the goat") because it is the one thing worth swearing on.
- **Ardmere boots.** A dish of nothing. The Hungry Winter of the siege ate the town's horses, dogs and boots, and "we're on Ardmere boots" is what the Ninth says on half-rations. Marrow, who was inside the walls, does not laugh at it, which is how the company learned to say it quietly.

### The fruit of the pear country
Pears were the valley, before. A few trees still stand alive in the lee of walls: at the Burned Mill, in the Reeve's yard, a sucker on Temple Row. The rest is [[The Weeping Orchard]], four hundred and eleven trees in stone, bearing.
- **Stone pears**, a penny at Gutter Market, warm at red moon, sweet if you can bite them. Nobody has. See [[Stone Pears]].
- **Dried pears** from before the war still turn up in the glass, in a cellar jar, black and hard as the stone ones and infinitely more precious; a Glasser who finds a jar eats one, sells the rest and does not say where.
- **Pear brandy, called Mercy.** The valley's spirit, aged in casks the fire did not reach. The soldiers of the fort at the road's end bought it there in the first years and called it after the place, and the name outlived both the fort and the trade. One true cask is left in the Ashenreach, under the bar at [[The Last Candle]], the colour of the Ember because it stood in the Orchard that night; a silver a thimble, thirty-one measures left, and Hennet counts aloud. Everything else sold as Mercy is stone-pear spirit and hot water: a penny a cup at the Baker's Wife in winter, five at the Glassers' camps, and worth it on the road.
- **Stone-pear spirit.** The Glassers' drink, made at Fort Mercy and Tally's Cut by a process nobody will describe from the stone fruit nobody can bite. Two pence a thimble, clear, and it tastes of pears the way a memory does. The Glassers say it is the Orchard's sap that makes it; the Orchard's sap is a different thing again, and a Hollowborn who drinks it dreams true.

### Drink
- **Small beer**, a penny a jack, everywhere. The water tastes of ash; the beer tastes of ash and hops, and is safer.
- **Fallowden red**, four pence a cup, two silver a jug, "and don't ask what year". The last comfortable town on the road sends its worst casks east and its carters charge double for the privilege.
- **The Returned.** Grimmark whey-spirit, six pence a thimble, milky, fierce, called the Returned "because you come back without your shadow". The Returned dwarves of the Smokes drink it in silence and nobody has worked out whether they find the name funny.
- **Thirteenth-barrel ale**, from [[The Thirteenth Barrel]] at Sedgemere downriver, which is named for the Salt Feud's barrel that was not there and brews as though to make up for it.
- **Barge tea.** Kettle's: black, stewed in the same pot since the Toll War, with a drop of something from a brown bottle "for the damp". Served to anyone who sits on her deck and talks, which is the price.
- **Bitter tea.** The Sleepless drink it to stay awake: Orchard bark and salt and ash-grass boiled black. Nobody else does twice.
- **Halcyran wine** at the House of Unburnt Light, at supper, with a lutenist. Free. That is the point.

### Feast days at the table
Pallas pies on the 15th of every month, three pence, mutton when there is mutton and goat when there is not, eaten the day rents fall due as a consolation. The Hearthday loaf. Mother Brisk's Last Sheaf cake on 30 Reaping, barley and dried pear and goat's butter, better than it sounds. Mercy at a funeral, if the dead man was liked. Bread left at the foot of a standing Lithic by the dwarves on Ketterin's name-day, and at the Ninth Stone by nobody for forty years, which Warden Uthrem has noticed. On Doorsnight a loaf on the step of the Doorkeeper's Chapel for the road. On Hearthnight a plate set for whoever comes.

### Further off
Sevenhaven oysters off the Chain, eaten standing at the quay with Concord silver. Marrowport's black-resin punch, hot wine with a drop of the harbour chandlers' sweet resin in it, which makes the dreams come easily and the bay's bone-carvers rich. Cowl's drunk-clerk punch, a counting-house drink nobody admits to in the fog. Halcyra's orange-blossom water, handed to pilgrims at the top of the Thousand Steps by people who have just healed them. A Shellback dumpling, eaten over an hour. The Grimmark's whey and cod and nothing else, in the same six stories. Soldiers of the Ninth have eaten all of it, somewhere, and will tell you so over a bowl of eleven-day stew.

:::gm
**Food is the Mutiny clock with a smell.** Brannoc's pot is the barometer: the thinner it is, the closer the company is to breaking, and a party that brings the Keep real food rolls the clock back one. The stores on 14 Ashfall are flour for eleven days at full ration (Fennick says nine), three weeks on Brannoc's half-rations, which runs out about 5 Leafrot, session 7's morning; grain for three weeks more if the horses are eaten; salt for one week. Fennick's four casks of good oil went to Kettle for the company's bread, which is why the Baker's Wife is nine days owed and not thirty. The "friend of the company" credit that turns up in Fennick's books every Coinday is Sorrel Vey's, and it buys flour.

**Things that are not food.** The Orchard's sap: a true dream for a Hollowborn, a DC 12 Wisdom save against a short horrible one for anyone else, and dropped in the lantern it burns white (session 5). Stillwater blind-fish heads: the folk say the fish see the dead; a PC who eats one dreams the lake that night and sees who is standing at the bottom of it, which is a free Sleeper Dreams roll and not a kindness. Marrowport resin in the punch is the same resin Isaurana's houses burn, and the Velvet Ledger has a bottle. Bitter tea does what it says and costs a level of exhaustion a night after the second.
:::

== GM notes ==

Put a bowl in every scene. What is in it tells the players how the company is doing faster than a clock does, and Hennet counting the Mercy aloud ("thirty. Twenty-nine.") is the campaign's smallest timer and one of its best.
