This page is the front of the adventure path: read it once before session 1, and come back to it between sessions. Each session's notes then run scene by scene, with a **Development** block at the end of every scene saying what happens next and where to go. [[Start Here]] is the five-minute tour; [[The Lantern Gutters]] is the spine; [[The Clocks]] is the crib for the clocks. The page your players can read is the [[Player's Guide]].

### Adventure background
Three thousand years ago the Nine killed a god and could not finish it. They buried it under the land and became the wards that hold it down. Nine years ago, on 14 Ashfall 3002, the last battle of the civil war burned the plain around [[Cinderford]] to glass, and the Ninth Ward cracked. A splinter of [[The Ninth Stone]] flew sixty miles west into a cook-fire, and [[Mother Hennet]] lit a lantern from it. That lantern has burned in its cage on the Keep's east wall ever since, and [[The Ninth Lantern]] holds the town on a contract that ends the night it goes out. Nobody in the company knows that the flame is part of what holds the crack shut. [[Brother Tallow]] suspects.

Now everyone wants the flame. [[Marius Vell]]'s Quiet Office, through [[Ansel Mote]] and [[Garrick Sallow]], is poisoning it with ash so the company stands down and the lamp can be taken. [[Grandmother Tollamund]] has bought the company's debt and written the lantern into the collateral. [[Seraphine Ashe]] wants it carried to Halcyra for the Unburnt Saint. [[The Dreaming Widow]] wants it out so the dreamer underneath can wake. [[Mother Corvane]] will come to believe that putting it out lets the dead go home. The full answer to who is cutting the oil, and how, is on [[The Lantern Gutters]].

The campaign opens on the quarter-day the pay does not come, which is also the ninth anniversary of Cinders.

### The campaign in three parts

**Part One: The Pay and the Poison** (sessions 1 to 3, levels 1 to 2, 14 to 16 Ashfall). Three days and nights. The pay notice reads nothing, a drowned deserter walks out of the ford, and his trail leads into [[The Glass Barrow]] and a tin of poisoned oil. The lantern gutters. The party chases the missing pay wagon to [[Fort Mercy]] and finds the money was never the Regency's, then gets home for the worst night of the year: the full red moon, a town that dreams one dream, and a second guttering that puts Brother Tallow on his back and the first cage key in Ysse's hand. *By the end of Part One the players know someone is poisoning the flame, roughly who, and that the company owes money it never saw.*

**Part Two: What the Flame Is** (sessions 4 and 5, level 3, 17 to 28 Ashfall). The pace slows and the party chooses where to go. The Saint's church courts them at [[The House of Unburnt Light]]; Sorrel Vey's [[The Velvet Ledger]] trades in secrets; Sallow is exposed or goes to ground. Old Pike sits up at his own funeral on [[Corvel Rise]] and tells the truth about the night the lantern was lit; the Rook-Court's ledger spells out five words; and the party finds what is burning in the lamp's bowl. *By the end of Part Two the players know the flame is a piece of the ward, and that someone has to go to the Stone.*

**Part Three: The Lip of the Rent** (sessions 6 to 8, levels 4 to 5, 29 Ashfall to 9 Leafrot). The party rides east to [[The Rent]], climbs down to [[Warden Uthrem]], and hears the truth of the Godslaying. They come home to find every faction moving at once: the Concord at the gate, Mote with his knives, the Saint's wardens, and Sallow's mutiny. Then the company carries the lantern east under the next full red moon, and the party decides what becomes of it. *The campaign ends at the Ninth Stone with one of three endings.*

### Levels
The campaign uses milestones. Level up at the **end** of sessions 1, 3, 5 and 7, so the party plays at levels 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4 and 5. If you use experience points instead, the fights alone fall well short: give each PC a story award at the end of each session about equal to the session's biggest fight, and check the table above to stay on pace. Every fight in the sessions says which party it was built for. They were not built for a party that is behind.

### Where the PCs start
Session 1 has the default opening (five new recruits, about to be sworn in at the quarter-day muster), a short prologue for the night before, and four other ways in: already-sworn soldiers, strangers off the post-road, townsfolk with business on the hill, and your players' own characters. See "Before you start" in session 1. Players making their own characters should read the [[Player's Guide]] and pick a tie to the Ninth.

A day in town is a scene or two. A day's ride out and back is a short session of its own. If the players want a whole evening of side quests, run one between sessions 4 and 5: nothing on the main line is due until Pike dies on the 24th.

### Keeping it moving

**Every big answer has three ways in.** If the party misses one, another is waiting. Each session's opener has a "catch-up" box for its own clues; these are the four that matter most:
- *Sallow is behind the swaps:* Spoons cracks (session 3), Bray folds after the oil store (session 3), or the unsigned letter in Sallow's room (session 3). Brother Audit's ledger can also do it in session 7.
- *The pay was Concord coin:* the strongbox (session 2), Hebbe's toll-log (session 2), or Nance Oddy's Toll at the Burned Mill (session 2).
- *The lantern was lit from a stone:* Pike's confession (session 5), Aurelio Bask the tailor (session 5), or Tallow confronted at the Keep (session 5).
- *The way down to the Stone:* Vexa's chart (session 2), Pike's copy from Hennet (session 5), or the Cold Seam (sessions 2 and 6).

**If the party splits up.** Cut between the groups at each scene's Development line, not in the middle of a fight. The natural splits: in session 3 (one group on the cage, one at the oil store, one in the Smokes), session 4 (the House by day, the Ledger by night) and session 7 (the Concord at the gate while someone watches the Assessor's Office). Never split the party in the Glass Barrow or the Rent.

**If they skip ahead.** A party that rides for the Rent straight after session 2, with Vexa's chart in hand, can do it. Run session 6 as written, but Uthrem knows only what he can see from the Stone: the flame is the Ninth's, it must come home, and it needs a heart. He does not know who lit it, or how it got up the hill. Old Pike still dies on the 24th, so session 5 is waiting when they get back, and its answers land harder.

**If they go off the map.** Fallowden is four days by cart and Caer Ardith eight. Let them go if they want to, and keep the dates table running. The players will feel the cost when they come home to a different town. Push a clock or two while they're gone, and leave a rider on the road with a letter from Marrow.

**If a key NPC dies early.** Someone else picks up the thread. If Spoons dies, Bray carries his confession. If Pell dies, Widow Pruitt keeps the office and his papers, and Kettle can read the cipher for a price. If Sallow dies, Bray leads the mutiny, more honestly and less well. If Tallow dies (he shouldn't before session 5), Ysse is keeper a session early and the shard waits under the cage for her to find. If Mote dies before session 7, the Quiet Office sends his replacement on the next post-rider, and his knives come anyway.

**If a PC dies.** The company swears in new wicks any dawn, not just on quarter-days. A new player can come in as a wick from the Keep's lines, a Glasser from Fort Mercy, a Sleepless deserter, or one of the company: the Dunmore twins, [[Gerda Hollin]] or [[Noume Flarewick]] all make good PCs. Give the newcomer a tie from the [[Player's Guide]] and one rumour the party hasn't heard.

**Smaller or larger tables.** The fights are built for five PCs, and each one says so. With three or four players, give them an NPC from the company (the Dunmore twins, Gerda or Skarra) or drop one foe. With six, add one foe, or take away the NPC ally.

### Without the five ready-made PCs
The campaign is written around [[Varra Nine-Knuckles]], [[Ysse Lampwright]], [[Borrin Halt]], [[Tibbet Marlowe]] and [[Quorl Many-Words]]. If your players bring their own characters, these are the beats each pregen carries, and who takes them instead:
- **Ysse (the keeper).** Somebody has to tend the flame in session 3 and carry it in session 8. Offer the first key to the PC with the most Arcana or Religion, or to whoever is closest to Tallow. The dreams in the warm voice go to whoever holds that key. Uthrem waits for them, and Seraphine wants them. This is the campaign's central role, so give it to a player, not an NPC.
- **Quorl (the last words).** The seven whispered sentences can come from [[Skrell Already-Gone]], the Rook-Court's runner, who has been hearing them at the corpse-cart for years and tells a PC who helped with Dannic. Any PC can do the rite with the rhyme. In session 5 Corvane reads the ledger to whoever was kind to Skrell.
- **Tick (the Cinders-born).** Marla is seen by any Hollowborn PC, or not at all. The Widow's scene in session 3 becomes a plea to the whole party ("Let the dreamer wake"), and the Standing Village letter comes addressed to "the soldiers at the Keep".
- **Borrin (the shadow).** The shadow is a thread for a second campaign. Drop it, or move it onto [[Mardle Ock]] at Fort Mercy, who then comes east with the column in session 8.
- **Varra (the Kingslayer's granddaughter).** Grashka's letter still arrives in session 7, because Skarra wrote home whether or not Varra did. The knot-script line simply has no reader, and Vosk's secret stays his.

:::gm
**What the PCs don't know at the start.** The five pregens' secrets (on their pages, marked for each player) are each a key to one door in the campaign: Ysse's dreams to the voices, Quorl's seven sentences to the shard, Tick's mother to the Widow, Borrin's shadow to the Eighth Ward, Varra's grandmother to the Kingslaying. A player who keeps their secret to themselves until it matters is playing it right.
:::

### Treasure
The Ninth Lantern is broke, and so is this campaign, on purpose. Money comes in small amounts (Aelith pays silver for things from the Barrow, Kettle's Cargo pays 20 silver, Ferrow Gant 20 more) and most of the rewards are favours, debts forgiven, bread, and a few strange things that should worry the party more than they help. The one big sum, 600 gold in the strongbox, isn't theirs, and what they do with it is a session 3 decision. The odd items along the way: [[An Emperor's Penny]] (session 1), [[The Melted Crown]] (session 2), [[A Dreamless Charm]] and [[Orchard Amber]] (any time in town), [[Hathe's Door-Key]] (lent in session 5), [[The Saint's Candle-Stub]] (offered in session 4), [[Thunderjars]] (Skarra's last two, session 7). Each session lists its own treasure at the end of the scene where it turns up.
