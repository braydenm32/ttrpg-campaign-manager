This page is for the players. It has no spoilers: everything here is what your character could know before session 1.

### The campaign
**The Lantern Gutters** is a campaign of eight sessions, from level 1 to level 5, set in [[Cinderford]], a grey stone town at the ford of the river Cinder, on the edge of a plain that burned to glass nine years ago. You play new hands in [[The Ninth Lantern]], a ragged, unpaid mercenary company that holds the town under the Treaty of Ashes. The company's contract is carved over the hall door of the Keep: *"The company shall hold the Keep and the ford for the signatories, and shall stand down if the lantern ever goes out."* The soldiers say it shorter: "We go when it goes."

The lantern is real. It burns in an iron cage on the Keep's east wall and has not flickered once in nine years.

Expect investigation, arguments, funerals, a dungeon under a river, long rides across the ash, and a few hard fights. Not every fight is meant to be won with a sword. Running away is allowed, and so is talking to the dead. The factions in town all want something, and they keep moving whether you're watching or not.

### The tone
Grim and funny in about equal parts. Soldiers swear, people die plainly, and desire is part of the story (behind a closed door). There is real darkness here and real kindness, and the gallows humour that keeps a company together when the pay doesn't come. Before session 1 the table agrees its lines and veils (**Play → Safety tools**), and anyone can use the X-card at any time without explaining why.

### What everyone in Cinderford knows
- **The war.** The Emperor was murdered thirty years ago and the war for his empty throne ran for twenty-one years. It ended at [[The Battle of Cinders]], on 14 Ashfall 3002, when battle sorcery set the plain alight. The Treaty of Ashes was signed afterwards, and eight companies were set to hold the peace. The Ninth was added a year later: the newest, the poorest and the strangest.
- **The company.** About 140 sworn soldiers and 60 followers, under [[Ilse Marrow|Captain Marrow]], with [[Mirrel Vosk|Sergeant Vosk]] as her Second Blade and [[Brother Tallow]], an old Vesh, as lamp-keeper. The pay comes from the Regency in Caer Ardith, four times a year on the 14th of the month. It has been late for a long time.
- **Wicks.** A recruit is a *wick*. On the morning a wick signs on, the lamp-keeper lights a small hooded tin lamp, the size of a fist, from the lantern's own flame. Every soldier wears one at the belt and must never let it go out. A soldier whose lamp dies climbs to the cage at dawn and asks for a light, and the whole yard laughs.
- **The town.** Ford Street runs from the West Gate to the Treaty Bell. [[The Last Candle]] is the company's tavern, and [[Mother Hennet]] keeps every tab on a slate the size of a door. [[The Smokes]] is the tanners' quarter, where the Hollowborn children were born. The Ravelers bury the dead on [[Corvel Rise]]. Out east, past the glass, is [[The Rent]]: a crack in the world that breathes.
- **The moons.** Pallas, the white moon, is full on the 15th of every month and sets the rent. [[The Ember]], the red moon, rose on the night of Cinders. When it is full the dead dream, and sometimes they get up.
- **Magic** is common, and it is drawn from the old wards that hold the land together. Every spell spends a little of them. See [[Magic and Its Cost]].
- **Money.** Ten copper make a silver and ten silver a gold. A loft bed at the Last Candle is 1 silver, a small beer 1 copper. Everything else is on [[What Things Cost in the Ashenreach]] and [[Coins and Money Slang]].

### Your character
There are five ready-made characters, each with a secret only their player reads: [[Varra Nine-Knuckles]], [[Ysse Lampwright]], [[Borrin Halt]], [[Tibbet Marlowe]] and [[Quorl Many-Words]]. They already know each other and the town, and the campaign is written around them.

If you'd rather bring your own, any of the peoples of the March can join the Ninth: [[Humans]], [[Dwarves]], [[Orcs]], [[Elves]], [[Halflings]], [[The Vesh]], [[Ravelers]], [[The Hollowborn]], [[Shellbacks]] and [[The Lithic]]. Then pick one tie from the list below, so your character has a reason to be on Lantern Hill on the morning the pay doesn't come. Each tie names someone you know and gives a small edge. Your GM may suggest numbers for your game.

### Ties to the Ninth
1. **Wick on promise.** You turned up at the Keep a few weeks ago, and the company has fed you on the promise of swearing you in at the quarter-day muster. Fennick the quartermaster wrote your name in his book in pencil. *Edge:* you know the Keep's people by name and their routines by heart: the dawn oil measure, the change of the gate watch, who drinks where.
2. **Born in the Smokes.** You grew up among the tanneries, the culverts and the cellars of the Smokes, and joined the company because it was the only work going. You know [[Auntie Brace]] at [[The Ninepenny]] and old [[Ottilie Marlowe|Mam Ottilie]], the midwife. *Edge:* you can find your way through the Smokes, over it or under it, in the dark.
3. **Owed by the Ninth.** You sold the company something on credit (boots, rope, arrows, a mule) and never saw a copper. Joining was the only way to eat the debt. Fennick avoids your eye. *Edge:* you can read a ledger and spot a fiddled sum, and the company owes you a favour it can't refuse.
4. **Off Kettle's barge.** You came downriver on the *Grey Dumpling*, and [[Kettle Brindlewake]] charged you nothing, which worries you. You owe her one. *Edge:* you know the Low Quay and its floating market, and Kettle will always hear you out.
5. **A name on the slate.** You have a tab at the Last Candle, and Mother Hennet calls you by its total. You haven't paid it. She hasn't asked. *Edge:* a bed and a bowl on credit at the Candle, and Hennet tells you what the room is saying.
6. **Glass-dug.** You spent a season digging the glasslands with the Glassers around [[Fort Mercy]] before you came to the company. You left on good terms, or you didn't. *Edge:* you know the glass: how to cross it, what the diggers fear, and the Glassers' price for things.
7. **Raised on the Rise.** You were one of the war's orphans, fed and taught by the Ravelers on Corvel Rise. You know the rhyme they say over the dead, and the runner [[Skrell Already-Gone]] still calls you "chick". *Edge:* the Ravelers trust you, and you can lay out a body properly.
8. **The Saint's mercy.** You lay a month in the infirmary at [[The House of Unburnt Light]], a mile out on the Ash Road, and walked out healed. The sisters fed you and asked nothing. *Edge:* you're welcome at the House at any hour, and you know its ways.
9. **A red-moon dreamer.** Since the first night you slept within sight of the glass, you have dreamed on every full red moon: a warm dark, a door with no handle. You have never told anyone. *Edge:* you always wake a moment before trouble on a red-moon night.
10. **A ghost of another company.** You were at Cinders, nine years ago, in a company that's gone now. You don't talk about it, and the veterans who were there don't ask. *Edge:* the old soldiers treat you as one of them, and you can climb the ladder and touch the cage on Cinders morning.

:::gm
**How each tie pays off.** (1) Fennick trusts the wick with the oil store's routine: they notice the dawn measure is wrong before anyone else (session 1). (2) The Smokes tie opens the Widow's yard and the Salt Lake's warm culvert in session 3. (3) The debtor is the first to see Fennick's four missing casks (session 1, Investigation with advantage). (4) Kettle offers the Cargo job to this PC first and tells them about the clerk on the bridge without being asked. (5) Hennet shows this PC the cup of new silver in session 1 and asks them about the slate-book in session 4. (6) Vexa will talk to this PC in session 2 without a check; if they left on bad terms, she wants an apology first. (7) The seven sentences in Quorl's notebook can come to this PC through Skrell, if Quorl isn't in the party. Dannic Rook was a Corvel Rise foundling too: this PC knew him a little, and knows the poster's description is wrong. (8) Seraphine remembers this PC by name in session 4, and their private moment is the strongest she offers. (9) The shared dream in session 3 is one this PC has had before; on [[Sleeper Dreams]] they roll twice and pick. (10) Halloran Pike tells this PC the lantern story right, not wrong, and asks them to keep it quiet until he's dead.
:::

### Before session 1
Bring answers to these, or make them up at the table:
- What did you spend your last silver on, and do you regret it?
- Who in Cinderford would miss you, and who would be glad?
- What would make you walk out of the company tonight?
