**THE CHARTER OF THE OPEN RIVER**

*Given at the river-camp below the Fallow fords, on the ninth day of Greenreach, in the year 1255 of the Warding, to Captain Posy Reedmarsh and the boats of the water.*

*Whereas* the four kingdoms of the Ardis have for thirty-one years burned each other's boats on the question whether a barge made fast at both banks is a bridge; and *whereas* the question has been answered by each party in the manner it found convenient; and *whereas* the river, which is the subject, has not been consulted, and is in no position to answer:

**First.** The river belongs to nobody. A boat upon it belongs to her captain.

**Second.** No toll shall be taken of a barge, her crew, her cargo or her cat, upon any river, in the water or upon the bank within a long rope's length of her, for ever.

**Third.** No army, levy, officer or toll-keeper shall stop, board or hold a barge without a writ signed by the sovereign in whose water she lies and countersigned by a Mother of the Water. A writ not so signed is not a writ.

**Fourth.** A barge is not a bridge. This is declared once, and shall not be argued.

**Fifth.** Any lord who stops a barge unlawfully shall pay the barge's value, the cargo's value, and the captain's inconvenience, which shall not be reckoned low.

**Sixth.** Disputes under this Charter shall be heard at the Quay-Moot of the nearest port, whose finding binds.

*Under the seals of: Hadrel, the Reed-King; Ostric, King Upriver; Marrane, Queen of the Bays; and the Lord of Ardmere, for the season.*

*Writ number one of four. The first is held by the Reedmarsh Water, and shall not be lent.*

:::gm
**How to use it.** Wave it at a checkpoint and the checkpoint becomes a negotiation. It is the legal basis for "It's not a bridge, it's a barge", and the reason [[The Low Quay]] takes no toll and asks no questions. [[Kettle Brindlewake]] has a (good) copy in the Grey Dumpling's parlour; [[Barge-Mother Posy Reedmarsh]] carries the first of the four on her person.

**Who honours it.** The Concord, always, because its own ships rely on the same principle. The Regency, grudgingly. The Treaty garrison: [[Mirrel Vosk]] laughs and waves them through. The Reeve's toll-keeper, [[Mistress Hebbe]], reads the words "a long rope's length" and puts a precise number to them.

**The long rope.** The Second clause is the whole quarrel. The bridge toll is a penny a head, and a barge's crew who walk from the Quay to the town are, on the Reeve's reading, no longer *within a long rope's length of her* once they pass sixty paces. The Quay is seventy paces from the bridge steps. [[Barge-Mother Posy Reedmarsh]] holds that "a long rope" is the longest rope the barge carries, and the *Long Patience*, flagship of the Reedmarsh Water, carries a hundred-pace hawser, which is why she is called the *Long Patience*. By that reading the whole Quay is within her rope and the toll is void. She has never tried it, because it would be undignified. **The rope trick:** any PC with a hundred paces of rope, a barge that will lend its mooring-end, and the nerve to tie the other end to the bridge steps has made the bridge a barge, and Hebbe will have to write down "exempt" for every head within its length. Hebbe will be appalled and delighted; the Reeve will appeal to the Quay-Moot; the Quay-Moot will find for the rope.

**The private clause.** In the Reedmarsh copy only, in the margin of the Sixth, in a different hand: *"A barge may carry any letter, from any hand, to any hand, without being searched."* No court has ever tested it. It is the foundation of the Clans' post-and-spying network (see [[The Toll War]]).

**The Lord of Ardmere** signed "for the season" because he did not expect to hold the seat for the length of the ink.
:::
