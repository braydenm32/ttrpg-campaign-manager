*(Grey hide-paper, the Mothers' iron-red seal: a clenched fist holding a broken chain. The hand is a sapper's, square and careful. It is addressed to the Captain of the Ninth Lantern, at the Lantern Keep, and under that, smaller: "and to the exile with nine knuckles, if she is standing".)*

**If Varra wrote to the Mothers:**

> Hold the lamp. I am sending what I can.

**If she did not:**

> Hold the lamp. I am coming to see what you have kept.

*(Then, in either case:)*

> The Iron licensed your company at the Treaty Stone and the Iron has not withdrawn the writ. Whoever holds your debt does not hold your word. Tell the Shellback so, slowly, so she hears it.
>
> The Rust Choir is between contracts. Its Mother has leave to hire it to whoever holds the lamp, for a price the exile will know.
>
> The Fist who carries this will stay. Feed him.
>
> *Hesha, for the council. The seal is Grashka's.*

:::for Varra Nine-Knuckles
At the foot of the sheet, below the seal, in a second ink, brown and shaking, in a hand you know the way you know your own:

> I did it. Not for the throne. For this. Ask Vosk.

Nobody but you will know that line is there unless you show them. The hand is your grandmother's.
:::

:::gm
**When to show it.** Session 7, scene 6, on the 6th, while the Saint's wardens are at the gate or Sallow's people are breaking out in the yard. A Fist of the Iron brings it: an orc officer with a short message and no expression, who stays.

**What it reveals.** The Mothers have known where Varra is since spring, through [[Skarra]]'s letters to [[Mother Hesha]]. The first line depends on whether Varra wrote home: "sending what I can" means the Rust Choir, Varra's mother's company, can be hired for the Rent (forty orc pikes in session 8, if Varra pays the price, which is her oath to the Iron again or something harder); "coming to see" means Grashka herself may arrive at the Rent with a company at her back, which is the big finale if the GM wants it. The paragraph about the writ is a weapon against Tollamund: the Mothers' licence makes the company's word a Treaty matter, not the Concord's property.

The second ink is for Varra alone, and the passage above shows it only to her player. It is [[Grashka]]'s confession of [[The Kingslaying]]: she struck the blow in 2981 to stop Aurel IX opening a ward, and [[Mirrel Vosk]] held the door. "Ask Vosk" hands Varra's player the scene with the Second Blade, who has known whose granddaughter she is all along and said nothing.
:::
