### How it plays out on the night
Under the full red moon (9 Leafrot) the party carries [[The Lantern of the Ninth]] down the north wall of [[The Rent]] to [[The Ninth Stone]]: three hundred feet of old rope steps, the Glass Stair, the Halfway Ledge, and a short traverse onto the Warden's Shelf. Nobody makes it easy. Run the night as a gauntlet of at least four challenges, each one costing something:

1. **The descent under pursuit.** Whichever factions the party has crossed come after the lantern: Quiet Office knives in the rope steps, Sallow's mutineers if the mutiny wasn't broken, Glassers who think the flame is worth more than their lives.
2. **The Choir.** The Sleeper's leak speaks in the voices of the party's dead. Every PC hears someone they've lost. Wisdom saves, and good roleplay, to keep walking.
3. **The priests at the lip.** [[Seraphine Ashe]] and priests of three or four gods are waiting, and each of them has a reason the party should stop (see [[Ending: Let It Crack]]). Some of them are very persuasive. One of them is a friend: [[Mother Corvane]], who has come to the lip to ask the party not to do this.
4. **The stone itself.** The flame must be set back into the crack where the shard came from, and the ward needs a heart: a living, willing one. [[Warden Uthrem]] offers first, but he's nearly spent and it may not hold. A PC can choose. [[Ysse Lampwright]] is the one the flame has been calling. The rubbing's warning matters: a heart that is *taken* rots, so nobody can be pushed in.

When the heart is given, the crack closes with a sound like a held breath let go. The shard goes back into the heart-socket; the crack closes over a night; the flame in Ysse's hands goes out. [[Ilse Marrow|Captain Marrow]] reads the contract aloud at the Stone, and the company stands down. The cage on Lantern Hill is dark when they get home, and the ninth pole at Corvel Rise gets its lantern. The Ember dims to copper over a season and goes dark by Longnight if the seal holds the year.

### Who pushes for it
Almost nobody, and that's the point. [[Warden Uthrem]] and [[The Last Wardens]] want it. [[Grashka]] and [[The Mothers of the Iron]] want it, and may send help if Varra asks (it is the same choice Grashka made in 2981, and she'll say so). [[Brother Tallow]] wants it and is ashamed of how much he wants it not to be Ysse. [[Mother Corvane]] supports it once the ledger is read: "The lamp is the lock. You do not sell a lock."

### Consequences
- **The Ninth Lantern.** By contract, when the lantern goes out, the company stands down. It's over. Captain Marrow reads the clause aloud (*"The company shall hold the Keep and the ford for the signatories, and shall stand down if the lantern ever goes out"*) and nobody laughs. What they do next (re-form under a new name, go home, stay as townsfolk) is a scene for the epilogue.
- **Cinderford.** Without a garrison, the town is open. If the Reeve's charter sale went through, the Concord owns it by spring. If it didn't, the town has to decide who it is.
- **The Church of the Unburnt Saint.** Denied the leak, Ilyra's ascent stalls. Her church does not forgive. Seraphine is recalled, or stays and becomes something sadder.
- **The Quiet Office and the Regency.** Furious, and blind: the ward's power is gone where they can't reach it. [[Marius Vell]] needs a new plan for his puppet emperor.
- **The Concord.** Tollamund's collateral is a cold lamp. She takes it anyway. She is patient.
- **The Sleeper.** Its world clock drops by three segments. It is not dead. It is never dead. But it sleeps deeper.
- **The PCs.** Whoever became the heart is gone, or rather, is now standing in the stone, awake, for a very long time. Their player should write the words that get carved on the stone.

### Epilogue: one line per thread
- **Mutiny and Sallow.** Whatever the Mutiny clock stood at, the company stands down together: Sallow is exposed, in irons, or at the head of the diehards, and either way the argument is over.
- **The Sleepless.** The Widow's people stand on the lip till dawn and watch the crack close; some weep, some pray, and the Widow asks Tick to visit.
- **Tick and Marla.** The ghost beside Tick is quiet for the first time in nine years, and Tick is not sure he likes it.
- **Borrin's shadow.** It lingers at the Stone a beat after the crack closes, then walks home behind him. It is still a beat late.
- **The Reeve's charter.** Odile seals it or tears it up; Cinderford learns which by spring.
- **Pell.** Pell Quillon files the last form of his career, Form 44-A, and for once it is stamped.
- **Kettle.** The *Grey Dumpling* is first on the river with the news and sells it three times.
- **Hennet's Candle.** The Last Candle opens at dawn. The slate is still up. She rubs out one name.
- **Corvane.** She puts the key back in the reliquary and asks Quorl for the eighth entry in his own hand.
- **Spoons's pool and Keld's stake.** The pool pays out on the night it went out, and Spoons has to find the winner. Keld's stake, grown for nine years, stays in the strongbox.

### Hooks for a second arc
The Ninth is sealed, for now. The Eighth Ward under the Grimmark is breached, and whatever wears Borrin's shadow came from there. The Fourth Ward in Sael is weak, and [[Aelith Sorn]] now knows how a ward is resealed. And the gods, who wanted the crack, have lost: some of them will take that personally.

== GM notes ==

This ending should feel earned and expensive. Don't let it be the obvious right answer handed over by an NPC: let the party find the rubbing, read it, argue about it. If no PC volunteers and Uthrem goes in alone, roll a d6 at the end of the epilogue: on a 1-2, the seal holds only a year.
