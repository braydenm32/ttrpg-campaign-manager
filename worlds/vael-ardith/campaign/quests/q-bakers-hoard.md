[[Ada Pennock]] puts the bread down in front of the first of the party who looks like a Raveler, or a soldier, or just hungry, and says, in the voice of a woman reading a bill, "I don't suppose any of you is going down the culvert." She is, as it happens, hoping not to be told no. She puts it on the slate and makes it a joke.

She does not want the money. There is money: a baker's takings, in a tin, in an oven in a bakery under the river. She wants the thing in the middle of it.

### What Ada says
- Her husband Tom kept the day's takings in a tin on the shelf by the oven, and took his wedding ring off every morning to knead and dropped it in with them, because a ring in the dough is a ring in a loaf.
- The night of Cinders he went to the chapel at the end of the High Street, with the street, to keep the door, and left the tin where it was. The sorcery took the bakery into the glass with everything in it.
- The Glassers say the ovens are standing, because Tom built them to stand. She has asked every one of them whether the tin was in it. None would say.
- "I want the ring. The rest is yours. Don't tell Fennick I said so."
- She will chalk the party's bread to the end of the campaign for it.

### Leads
- The bakery was on the old High Street, which is under the river now: the Barrow, by the Flooded Street, if the Glassers are right.
- Ada asks in the morning, over bread on credit, before the muster on 14 Ashfall.

:::gm
**The steps.**
1. **Hear it.** Ada tells it at the counter at dawn on 14 Ashfall (Coinday), over bread on credit, before the muster.
2. **Find the bakery.** In session 1's Barrow, it is area 3 on the Flooded Street, on the left, half way down: a collapsed front and three brick oven-mouths at the back. Nobody needs to go in here. Anyone who does has a reason now.
3. **Get past the spiders.** The Collapsed Bakery has two glass-spiders in the ovens and webs at knee and neck (see [[The Glass Barrow]]). A hooded light, a thrown stone, a retreat to the street and a fight at the door, or a Stealth check across the room will each do.
4. **Bring the lump up.** Fused into one mass in the middle oven: 23 silver and 140 coppers, the size of a small loaf, heavy as a brick. A DC 14 Investigation, or a lantern held behind it, shows a thin band of gold in the glass at one side.
5. **Have it cut.** [[Ninefingers Forge]] opens fused coin-lumps with a chisel for 1 sp. Hurkul will do it in a minute and say nothing, and set the ring in a clean cloth on the anvil.
6. **Return it.** Ada is at the counter. She does not look at it. She looks at the cloth, and then at the party.

**Complications.**
- **Skelt claims the salvage.** The Glasser who sells charts at Gutter Market (see [[Skelt]]) says that everything in the glasslands belongs to whoever digs it out, and that the Barrow is glasslands. He held Dannic's rope on the 3rd and he dug the Barrow's one shaft in his time. The party, having dug it out, can quote the Treaty back at him. He will settle for a cut of the metal and a drink.
- **Fennick wants the 4 gp.** At Gutter Market the lump is worth its weight, 4 gp, or 40 silver. [[Fennick Bottlebrook]] owes Ada nine days of the followers' loaves, 63 silver, and says the company's need is greater than hers. He is not wrong. He is also embarrassed that he is arguing with a PC over a dead man's wage.
- **The stone loaf** in the ovens, warm and perfectly detailed. A PC who takes it has taken Tom's last Hearthday plait. Ada will want to see it, and will not ask for it.
- **The ring is not the only thing in the tin.** A folded twist of paper, fused flat: a baker's receipt for flour, signed *T. Pennock*, dated the 13th of Ashfall 3002. The writing is perfectly legible.

**Reward.**
- The ring, back in Ada's hand: bread for the party for the rest of the campaign, a loaf a head a day, never chalked, never mentioned.
- Fennick's nine-day debt of 63 silver wiped from the slate, which turns the company's bread contract into an ordinary one and takes a small weight off him.
- **Tom's words.** In the Glass Chapel (area 4), the forty fused in the pews all say the same five words; that is the sentence the dying have been whispering to Quorl. Tom is in the fourth pew, aisle side, cap on, with his head turned a little to the door. A rite (DC 12 Religion) asks him for his own: it is a short line, said once. Quorl, who can speak in any voice he has heard, says it to Ada in Tom's voice, if she asks: *"Ada, love, I've left the oven on. I'm just nipping out."*

**How it ends.** Ada laughs. It is the entire laugh: head back, shoulders going, tears; and then she is not laughing, and does not say so, and the party leaves her in the oven-room with the door shut. The next morning the first loaf is on the step, warm, for the one who fetched it, and the shop's slate has a line through the party's name.

**The GM-only truth.** Tom did leave the oven on. The Hearthday plait was still in it when he hung up his apron and went out to keep the door, and the stone loaf in area 3 is that loaf, fused as it stood: it is warm because the oven was lit when the glass came down and the glass has never let the heat go. He did not take his cap off. He knew, or half knew, that he would not be back, and said so in the only way he could.

Tom's words are not accidental. He went to the chapel with the whole street because the priest of Hathe had told them that the door was in danger, and that was the night Hathe died: a Hathe clue. The forty sit facing a door in the altar that was to have been held open, and they are saying the sentence the dead whisper because they were the ones who kept it from shutting. Quorl's rite on them is also the cleanest way for a player to learn that the forty are not haunting anything; they are waiting.

If [[Keld Owes-Nothing]] is told about the apron (she will not leave Sedgemere, but she will send it), Kettle's cousins bring it up the river in a parcel tied with eel-twine, a river-pebble on the top. If Ada gets both the ring and the apron, she shuts the shop for a day. It is the only day it has ever shut except the first of the year.

**Failure paths.** Take the lump to Gutter Market and the metal goes to a Concord factor, and the ring with it; Ada will say nothing, serve the party bread, and charge them for it. Leave the lump to Skelt and he will cut it himself, badly, and the ring will be a bent gold wire. In both cases the chapel scene and Tom's words are still available, and are the better reward.
:::

== GM notes ==

Run this as the warm thing that makes the Barrow personal. The party goes in for the oil and the deserter; Ada gives them a second reason, and the second reason is the one that makes the congregation in the pews into people. Do not let it distract from the tin of oil: the lump is a pocket-sized detour, five minutes of play, a spider fight the party was going to have anyway, and a payoff that comes back with the morning's bread. If the party skips the bakery, the quest stays open all the way to session 5; Ada does not nag, but the bread is thinner.
