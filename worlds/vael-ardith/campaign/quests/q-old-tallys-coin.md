There is a coin on Old Tally's grave at [[Tally's Cut]], and it is always a coin struck before 3002, and the diggers think the dates are a map. [[Ferrow Gant]], who keeps the grave and the Ripe One, will tell you after the second thimble that they are not, and then, if you have been decent about the grave, what he actually wants.

When the 11th Foot's pay-chest came out of the glass in 3008 there was more in it than silver: the quartermaster's roll of company seals, lead discs on ribbons, the paper of a dead regiment. [[Vexa Glassjaw]] has them in her vault at [[Fort Mercy]] and has never found a use for them. Ferrow has. A widow of the 11th, Bethan Gant, keeps the back room at [[The Sunburst]] at Whitepost, two days west on the Fallowden road, and has drawn no pension in nine years because the Regency has no record that her husband is dead. The pensions seal, in her hand, is the record. Ferrow cannot leave the Cut, cannot climb to the fort with his chest, and cannot ask Vexa, because asking Vexa for anything costs a split. He is asking you, and he is asking you not to say who sent it.

Twenty silver, which in the Ashenreach this month is real money, and a bottle of Mercy put by.

### Steps
1. **Learn who leaves the coins**, or do not; Ferrow will tell you himself if you ask kindly, and it is the key to his trust.
2. **Learn where the seals are**: the records vault under Vexa's tower, beside a box of half-burned imperial papers that [[Ottoline Crane]] reads at night.
3. **Get one.** Read the cellar writing for Vexa and ask for the roll as part of the price; trade for it; or steal it (the vault door is iron, DC 15 to pick, and Ottoline sleeps across the threshold).
4. **Carry it**, to Whitepost, or send it west by a hand you trust, or keep it a while, because of what it turns out to be.

### Complications
- The Concord factor, [[Cousin Pennamund]], has asked Vexa for the roll twice and been told the price twice.
- [[Jaspin Sweet]] at Gutter Market offers fifty silver a seal, no questions, and will go to a hundred.
- The roll has forty-one seals on it, and the party will be asked by Ferrow for one, and will have to decide what to do with forty.
- Vexa does not part with anything she has not been paid for, and does not know what the seals are worth, and will know the moment the party asks for them by name.

### What the seal is
Cousin Pennamund knows what the roll is for, which is why he asked. Under [[The First Concord]]'s ninth article, read by nobody, "the debts of the dead pass to the land they died on": the clause [[Tollamund & Daughters]] has used for nine years to buy up the Ashenreach's dead at forty in the hundred. It cuts both ways. A regiment's seal is its signature; the holder of the 11th Foot's pensions seal, standing on the land the 11th died on, is the 11th for the purposes of the article, and the 11th is owed nine years of dead-pay for six hundred men by whoever holds the Regency's paper on the March. That is Tollamund, layer on layer, from the 2650 lien on the marches along the Cinder to the garrison wages she bought in 3009 "at four in ten, the same afternoon". She has never once had Article Nine cited back at her, and she has never had to explain to the Principals at Sevenhaven that the paper she bought carries a dead regiment's credit as well as a living company's debt.

It is not a winning argument. The Treaty's page seven is the winning argument: the lamp is not collateral; the company is. It is a lever. A PC who lays the seal on the table in session 7, when the Grandmother is at the Keep gate with her lawyers and the Reeve, gets this: she goes still, for longer than usual, and then she says, very slowly, "Where ... did you get ... that," and one of the two lawyers leaves to send a letter, and the party has bought the company a day. Offer it in exchange for something concrete: the loan-chit's third line read aloud in front of the Reeve, a day's grace, Fennick's personal paper torn up, or Odile's unsealed charter put back in her hands. Tollamund will pay to make Article Nine go away quietly; she will not pay to be shown it in a court.

A Corvex auditor (Brother Audit on Temple Row, or a priest of Corvex in session 8's crowd at the lip) can confirm the reading in a day; it will make him very busy and very frightened. Ferrow does not know any of this; he wants a widow paid.

### The widow
Bethan Gant is real, seventy-one, in the back room of [[The Sunburst]], and she is Old Tally's widow and Ferrow's mother: Quartermaster-Sergeant Harl Gant of the 11th was his father, and Bethan believes her son died with the regiment, because Ferrow has let her believe it for nine years (see [[Ferrow Gant]]). The seal in her hand draws a pension from a Regency Office of Pensions that has not paid a garrison in two years; it will be filed, stamped and deferred. She will cry anyway, and she will tell the party about her husband in the ash, and her son, if they buy the second cup. If the party carries it there (prologue, post-session 8, or by a hand they trust in session 4), Ferrow hears by letter and gives them the chart of the southern glass and does not say anything about it, ever. If they tell her he is alive, see his page.

### How it ends
- The seal goes to Whitepost: Ferrow's friendship, the chart, and the Cut's water free for the campaign. Tollamund never learns the roll exists.
- The seal goes on the table in session 7: a day, a concession, a Shellback's full attention for the rest of the party's lives.
- The roll goes to Jaspin Sweet: 50 sp a seal, 2,050 silver for forty-one, and within a week Cousin Pennamund has them all and the Concord has quietly closed a door it did not know was open. Ferrow does not forgive it.
- The roll goes to Pell Quillon: the Assessor files Form 44-A on behalf of the 11th Foot, deceased, and the Regency's Treasury has a second line in its ledger it cannot explain. Vell's office notices. The Quiet Office clock ticks.

== GM notes ==

A small kindness that turns out to be a lever, which is the shape of most of this campaign's side quests. Start it at the Ripe One on the road to Fort Mercy in session 2, let the seals sit in the party's pack until the Grandmother is at the gate, and then watch a player remember. Keep the law thin and the stillness long.
