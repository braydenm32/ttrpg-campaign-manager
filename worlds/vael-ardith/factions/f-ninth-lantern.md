The Ninth Lantern holds [[Cinderford]] and the road through [[The Ashenreach]]. About 140 soldiers, orcs and humans and dwarves and halflings and anyone else the war left over, quarter in [[The Lantern Keep]] on [[Lantern Hill]]. They have not been paid since the first of Hollowfrost, 3009. They are still here, which is the first thing to understand about them.

### The legend
Everyone in Cinderford tells it and no two tellings match. This is the company's.

Eight companies swore to hold the walls on the night of the Battle of Cinders: the Salt Guard, the Iron Tithe, Greywake's Spears, the Ruddy Hundred, and four more whose names the war ate. Each hung a lantern on the wall so the others would know it still stood. Then the sorcery came and the plain burned, and by dawn all eight lanterns were dark.

A ninth burned on. Nobody had counted it. It was lit from the cooks' last coal, at the cooks' camp in what is now [[The Weeping Orchard]], by Mother Hennet, who ran the Ruddy Hundred's kitchen. A deserter named Pike, who had come back across the ash because he could not stand being the only one alive in a ditch, carried it up to the Keep, and a boy of the walking wounded with a burnt hand and no company of his own followed it. They had no banner and no oath. They wanted a light in the window for the ones still coming home. At dawn the survivors of eight broken companies found one flame and followed it. The treaty clerks wrote down a ninth company because there was a ninth lantern.

Mother Hennet lost an arm that night and keeps [[The Last Candle]] now. Pike, the story goes, walked into the Rent three years later and was not seen again. The boy with the burnt hand is [[Brannoc Saltash]], who cooks for the company and will show you the scar if you ask, and not if you don't.

The contract over the hall door says: *"The company shall hold the Keep and the ford for the signatories, and shall stand down if the lantern ever goes out."* It is read to every recruit. The soldiers' version is shorter: "We go when it goes." It has not gone out in nine years. Soldiers touch the cage for luck, and swear on it when they mean it.

:::gm
The legend is true and incomplete. The coal did not light the flame. A splinter of the Ninth Stone, blown loose in the blast, lay in Mother Hennet's cookfire and set the coal alight, and the lantern's flame has burned on that splinter ever since ([[The Ward Shard]]). Hennet carried the splinter up to the Keep that night in a soup-pot, and [[Brother Tallow]] has kept it under the lantern's cage since. The flame is part of what holds the crack in the Ninth Ward shut. Nobody in the company knows what the stone is, and Tallow only suspects. The contract is therefore a trap: if the lantern goes out, the company stands down and the crack opens.

Pike never went into the Rent. He walked as far as its lip, lost his nerve, and came back two years later with a beard and a limp to sign on under his own name, Halloran Pike; there are a great many Pikes, and nobody looked twice. Hennet knew him the first night and has never said, and he drinks free at the Candle. He is the old soldier who dies in his cot in session 5 and sits up at his own funeral ([[The Funeral That Would Not End]]), and his confession tells the rest.
:::

### Structure and ranks
Ten soldiers make a **tally**; fourteen tallies make the company. New recruits are **wicks** until they stand a night watch beside the cage, when they are given a numbered lantern token and count as soldiers. Above them sit corporals, sergeants, lieutenants, the **Second Blade** (the captain's deputy, by orc custom) and the captain. The **lamp-keeper** answers to nobody but the flame, and the quartermaster answers to everybody.

Every wick is given a hooded tin wick-lamp the size of a fist, lit from the Lantern by the lamp-keeper the morning they sign on; that is why recruits are wicks. Let it go out and you climb to the cage at dawn and ask for a light, to general mockery.

### Officers
- [[Captain Marrow]]: captain "by attrition", the last officer who could count.
- [[Sergeant Vosk]]: Second Blade, gallows wit, keeps recruits alive.
- [[Brother Tallow]]: lamp-keeper, going blind.
- [[Fennick Bottlebrook]]: quartermaster, balances books that cannot balance.
- [[Garrick Sallow]]: lieutenant, ambitious, popular with the hungry.
- [[Spoons Aldane]]: corporal, runs the betting pool on when the lantern goes out.

### Others you will meet
- [[Skarra]]: orc sapper, burnt brows, builds the Keep's walls and mends them again.
- [[Dunstan Bray]]: Sallow's corporal, big and affable, everybody's friend and the lieutenant's man.
- [[Jory Dunmore]] and [[Jessom Dunmore]]: twin crossbows, who never agree about anything.
- [[Brannoc Saltash]]: the cook, and the boy with the burnt hand.
- [[Gerda Hollin]]: surface-dwarf corporal at the gatehouse, who reveres the Lithic and watches the road.
- [[Noume Flarewick]]: Vesh signaller, who talks to the far patrols in lamp-code and cannot stop looking at the cage.

### What they want
Food, pay, and not to be the company that lost the lantern. Failing those, a reason to stay.

### How they treat the party
The recruits are wicks: carried, bullied, fed last. A wick who does something brave gets a nickname inside a week. A wick who runs gets the same nickname, but it is less kind.

### Resources
The Keep, the gatehouse at the bridge-head in [[The Ford]] (the company keeps the gate; the toll belongs to the Reeve's son), a quartermaster's store that is mostly rumour, and 140 people who can fight. Debts to [[Tollamund & Daughters]] that grow each Coinday.

### How they act
- **Pay-day pools:** Spoons posts new odds; the mood of the Keep shifts with them.
- **Short rations:** Fennick cuts the ration and Sallow notices out loud.
- **Desertions:** a tally goes quiet in the night. The lantern gets a little dimmer in the morning, or seems to.
- **A patrol returns short.** Someone has to write the letters.

:::gm
**The eight dead companies.** The names are public; the ordinals are how veterans talk. First: the Salt Guard (Marrow's old company; its lantern went dark in the second hour). Second: Greywake's Spears. Fourth: the Iron Tithe (Ismere's; dark in the first hour). Seventh: the Ruddy Hundred (Hennet, Hesk and Brannoc; the Candle's crow; the Cold Captain). Third, Fifth, Sixth and Eighth: names eaten by the war. The "new Salt Guard" in gossip is a Concord-paid paper company at Fallowden.

**The lanterns.** Seven real dark lanterns hang in the Keep hall beside one empty hook. The eighth, the Seventh's, hangs over the bar of the Last Candle, where Hennet carried it. At Corvel Rise the Field of Lanterns has eight iron copies on poles and a ninth pole, empty.

**Kitchen and stores.** Hennet ran the Ninth's kitchen in the first winters and was never quartermaster. An unnamed man held the post 3003 to 3006 and was flogged out for selling the winter boots; Fennick has had it since 3006.

**Mutiny (6 segments, starts at 2).** Ticks: the lantern gutters (end of S1: 3); pay fails; rations drop to half; a popular soldier dies pointlessly; Sallow wins an argument; the Concord at the gate (S7). Rolls back: someone feeds the company; the pay coin is spent on wages (-1); Marrow's savings come out (-2); Sallow is exposed with proof (-1). At 6 Sallow's mutiny breaks out openly, S7 at the latest. Strength that marches in S8: clock 0 to 2, about 120; 3 to 4, about 100; 5 to 6, about 60, with Sallow's diehards on the road.
:::

== GM notes ==

Run the company as a family under strain: every scene should show somebody being kind to somebody else in a way that costs them. Show the Mutiny clock in the room before you tick it: a quieter mess hall, a tally that stops saluting. When it fills, the company splits three ways: those loyal to the flame, those loyal to Sallow, and a long night in the Keep.
