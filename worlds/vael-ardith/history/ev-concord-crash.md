On the morning of 22 Seedwake 3010 AW a rumour went round the bottle-necked harbour of [[Gilt]] that House Ferrowyn, the city's oldest bank, had lent too deeply to the Regency. The best account of what followed is the day-book of a Ferrowyn counting-clerk, which survives with a thumb-print in the margin.

> *22nd. Opened at the usual hour. Queue to the corner by the second bell, to the chain-lockers by the fourth. Paid every depositor in full. Sevenhaven desk refuses our Regency drafts, and will not say why.*
>
> *23rd. Merchants selling whole cargoes at a third to raise cash. Doors shut at noon, for the first time in two hundred years. The younger clerks are crying on the steps. I have told them to stop, and have not stopped myself.*
>
> *25th. Balanced. Do not understand how.*

The rescue arrived on the fourth morning, slowly, with a cart. [[Grandmother Tollamund]]'s family, it was said, paid Ferrowyn's depositors in full and took, in return, its entire portfolio of Regency debt, including, in the small print, most of the Ashenreach's. The other Concord houses had watched and not intervened.

It left a Concord richer in its debts and poorer in its trust, and a Gilt that no longer boasts about its banks. It left the Ferrowyn steps, scrubbed twice a year and still darker where the clerks wept, and a notary's drawer in Gilt full of IOUs with the ink gone soft. And it left a quiet shift in the balance of power: the Shellbacks, who had always been the Concord's bankers, were now its owners in all but title. In Gilt it is told as a lesson in prudence, in Sevenhaven as a bargain, and by the priests of [[Corvex]] as a case study in how a broken promise is theft and a rescue is a purchase.

:::gm
The Shellbacks engineered the run. The rumour was theirs. The rescue let them buy the Ashenreach's debts at a fraction of their worth, and that is the whole of their plan: to own the land the lantern stands on, and then to own the lantern.
:::
