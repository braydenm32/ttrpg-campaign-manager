The siege ended with a town in ruins and a victory no one could pay for. The Regency had no coin for the three companies who broke it, so it issued paper instead:

> ARDMERE RECEIPT No. 0412. The bearer is owed, for service in the relief of Ardmere, the sum of thirty-one crowns, payable upon the settlement of the realm. Stamped, signed, numbered.

A soldier could not eat one, and several tried.

In the thaw of 2995 a Concord broker arrived with a cart and a clerk and offered the companies forty crowns in the hundred for their receipts, in coin, on the spot. Most took it. By spring every receipt was in a Concord strongroom, and the Concord had learned something it would never forget: that a promise from a shattered state could be bought cheap and sold dear.

What it left was the Concord's appetite for the Regency's debt, and the companies' lasting suspicion of any paper that was not coin. It left Ardmere, rebuilt and run half by a company, half by a council and wholly by a bankers' guild. And it left a sentence you hear in any camp, *"I'll take it in Ardmere Receipts"*, which means *I do not believe a word of it*. The companies tell the story as the day they learned never to take paper. The Concord tells it as the day paper first became useful. Neither is wrong, and the broker is still counting.

:::gm
The broker who bought the Receipts was an agent of Tollamund & Daughters. The same house is now buying the Ashenreach's debts on the same terms, and for the same purpose: a cheap claim on a land that cannot pay.
:::
