Hathe died on the night of Cinders. The Ravelers say his door shut that night. Ilyra of Halcyra walked out of the same fire unburnt.

Since then the dead do not always pass. Raveler priests keep his doors anyway, and open one at every burial, and say the words to a god who cannot hear them.

:::gm
Publicly, [[Hathe]], Keeper of Doors, died at the Battle of Cinders because a god's threshold cannot survive a field of unmaking. Everyone accepts this as a tragedy of the war. The truth is narrower and worse.

**What happened.** In the heart of the fire, a camp healer named [[Ilyra of Halcyra|Ilyra]] stood among the dying. The Keeper came, as he comes to every death, to open the door for the souls of the battlefield. There were too many. The battle sorcery that cracked the Ninth Ward broke him open with it, and by the third hour there was too little left of him.

Hathe died of the battle sorcery; Ilyra took what was left. She did not choose to. She did what a healer does with a dying thing, and took its weight, and the god's power, long meant to carry the dead, poured through her and left her unburnt. She walked out of the fire into the morning, and a legion of dead souls found a door that no longer opened.

**What it left.** A world in which the dead linger. A woman who is becoming a goddess. A church that claims her miracle and calls it a bequest. And a priesthood of Ravelers, still guarding doors that nobody walks through.

**What survives.** A key: [[Hathe's Door-Key]], one of his keys, dropped in the fire, and it still turns.

Ilyra is ascending on Hathe's power and her church wants the Ninth Ward's crack to finish the job. [[Quorl Many-Words]] has heard seven dying soldiers whisper the same sentence, and the dead who cannot pass are the ones saying it.
:::

== GM notes ==

The public paragraph is for the players. Keep the rest off the table until Session 5 or later. The easiest thread to pull: the dead who linger near Cinderford. They are not haunting the place. They are waiting at a door that is not there.
