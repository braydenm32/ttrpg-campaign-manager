A camp-kettle of black iron the size of a small drum, with a bail handle wrapped in old rag and a dent in one side you could rest a fist in. The rim is worn bright where a ladle has knocked it ten thousand times. The inside is scrubbed clean and dry. It hangs on its own hook in the corner of [[The Last Candle]]'s kitchen, the cold corner, as far from the fire as the room allows.

### Its story, as the Candle tells it
It was the cook-pot of the Ruddy Hundred, the Seventh Company, and [[Mother Hennet]] carried it from the Seventh's wagons to the cooks' camp in [[The Weeping Orchard]] on the night of Cinders, and carried it out again in the morning, when there was no Seventh left to cook for. The company's story is that she lit the ninth lantern; hers is that it was the soup that did it. Whatever was in the pot that night, she has not cooked in it since. She scrubs it every Restday, hangs it back on its hook, and does not look at it on the way past.

The old Seventh hands at the Candle touch it on their way to the privy, the way the Ninth's veterans touch the cage on Cinders night. Hennet lets them, and says so: "Touch my pot and you're washing it."

She will not sell it. She has refused, in order: a Glasser who offered a pound of salvage, a Lamp-Warden from the House of Unburnt Light who offered a blessing, an elf who would not give her name and offered gold, and a Shellback clerk who offered to weigh it first.

### Quirks
- It is never quite as cold as the iron round it. Nobody has thought that strange, because it hangs in a kitchen.
- The ladle that goes with it is lost. Hennet says it went into the Rent with a man who deserved it.
- It holds nine pints. Hennet says that is a company's soup for a night's march, which is nonsense, and nobody has ever argued.

:::gm
**What it carried.** On the night of Cinders a splinter of the Ninth Stone fell into the Ruddy Hundred's cookfire. Hennet lit the ninth lantern from that fire, [[Halloran Pike]] carried the lantern up to the Keep, and Hennet carried [[The Ward Shard]] after him in this pot, with the boy [[Brannoc Saltash]] howling behind; at the Keep she tipped the stone out of the pot into [[Brother Tallow]]'s hands. The black ring in the orchard's fire-pit is where the pot sat on the burning stone before she lifted the shard into it. [[Aurelio Bask]], hiding in the orchard that night, saw the pot go past glowing from the inside like a lantern with a lid on, and has never said so.

**What it remembers.** The Shard sat in it for an hour and something stayed. The pot is faintly warm on the nights the lantern gutters (end of session 1, midnight in session 3); Hennet knows, and sat up with her hand on it on the night of the 14th. Within ten feet of the cage it rings once, very faintly, like a bell struck in another room. Ysse, if she holds it, hears the Ninth's voice a little clearer for a breath ("You came.").

**In play.**
- **Session 5:** after Pike's confession, Hennet, asked at the Candle, says "Took you long enough," takes the pot off its hook and puts it in a PC's arms (or gives them Pike's chart, if Vexa's was missed). Set the pot in the orchard's ring and it fits to a hair; that is the clue landing.
- **Session 8, the dawn of the march:** Hennet asks for it back. She cooks in it for the first time in nine years: the night-of-Cinders soup, barley, salt mutton, onions and the last dried pears from the pear country, and ladles it into every soldier who marches. The 5e effect above applies to all of them. She does not march.
- **If the cage is broken** on the road or at the Rent, the pot is the vessel that carried the Shard once without harm and will again: whoever carries the pot is not holding the stone, and the Sleeper does not hear them.
:::

== GM notes ==

Hang it in the background in session 1 (one line in the Candle's kitchen) and never mention it again until session 5. A relic nobody guards because nobody knows it is one. If a player asks what is in it, the answer is "nothing, and it's clean", which is true.
