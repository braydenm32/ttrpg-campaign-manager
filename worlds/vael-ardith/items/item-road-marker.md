A squat stone the size and shape of a loaf-end, grey, smooth as a river pebble on every face but one, where a column of tall thin marks is cut top to bottom with a precision no mason now alive could match. The marks are the script of [[Ul-Tarran]], and nobody but a Lithic reads it. It is cold: not cellar-cold but the cold of the Barrow's stair, a cold that gets into the bones of the hand. Set it down flat on a table and wait, and it turns, slowly, a quarter-turn or less, with a faint grinding, until the script faces east, and stops.

### What it is
The Lithic of Ul-Tarran built nine roads, one to each of the Great Wards, wide enough for two biers and a priest, so that those who felt the stone coming could be carried to their place and stood with honour; and at every waystation on every road they set a marker that knew which way the ward lay, so that a bearer in the dark, or the ash, or the end of a long life, could not lose the road. The Ninth Road runs under the March of Cinders: under the ford, where the Barrow's stair is its throat; under the Burned Mill; under Tally's Cut; under Ambercote's square, where this marker stood for three thousand years as a waist-high post the villagers called the Old Post and tied their goats to. The war broke the post off at the ground. The piece with the writing on it is this.

### What it does
Laid flat, it points along the Ninth Road toward the Ninth Stone, wherever you are in the March: not as the crow flies but as the road runs, under the ground, which in the open ash is near enough the same and in the dark under Fort Mercy is the difference between the Cold Seam and a grave. It is a route-finder for the covered ways, and a compass in a greywind. And it warms when a spell is cast near it, a little for a cantrip, like a hand held near a fire for anything larger: it is feeling the ward being drawn on, and it does not like it.

### Quirks
Gerda Hollin, if she sees it, will not touch it and will not let anyone else touch it in her sight, and will ask, with real anger, who took it off its road. A Lithic, if the party ever meets one, would read the script as the name of the road, the waystation's name, and the rota: who stood at Ambercote's station, and when, and the last line, which is blank.

:::gm
[[Warden Uthrem]], shown it in session 6, says the station's name aloud, which nobody has heard spoken for a thousand years, and then asks, very slowly, to have it put back.

The marker is the key to session 6's underground route: laid on the floor of Fort Mercy's black-glass chamber it points down the Cold Seam, and at every fork in the covered way it turns toward the Stone, so a party that goes under the glass instead of over it does not get lost. It is also a ward-cost meter that a player can hold, which makes the mile-rule of [[Magic and Its Cost]] something they can see: when it gets hot in the Rent, stop casting. [[Aelith Sorn]] wants it because a Remembrancer who can read a road-marker's rota can read the Fourth Ward's, and because the station's name is a word of the script she lacks. [[Grandmother Tollamund]], if she learns of it, wants it for the same reason she wants everything that knows the way to a ward.

It should go back to Ambercote when the campaign ends. Jeb will ask, politely, once.
:::

A compass that points at the campaign's ending and warms when you do the thing you should not. Give it to the party through Jeb, as a reward for not touching anybody. It is not a weapon and should never roll dice.
