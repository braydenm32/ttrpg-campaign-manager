A pear, perfect, the size of a fist, grey, with the stalk on and a leaf still attached, and every part of it stone. The skin has the grain of river-pebble. Hold it to the light and the shape of the flesh shows faintly through, the way a leaf's veins show through the stone leaves in the Orchard. It is cold, most of the time. Three nights either side of the red moon's full it is warm, like something that has been in a pocket.

### Where it comes from
[[The Weeping Orchard]], two miles out along the orchard lane: four hundred and eleven trees that turned to stone in a night, in full fruit, and have borne that fruit ever since. The Glassers knock them down with a stick and sell them by the blanket at [[Gutter Market]], a penny each, to pilgrims, children and soldiers who want something from the Orchard that is not the sap. The trees are never emptier for it. Nobody has counted the fruit and got the same number twice.

### What it is used for
- **Marbles.** The Hollowborn children of the Smokes play a game with them in the lanes that has rules nobody else can follow, and the pears are laid out in rings, and the children stop the game to let someone invisible pass, and start again.
- **A sign.** A stone pear on a grave at [[Corvel Rise]] means the Ravelers asked the dead for leave to take something and were given it. A stone pear on a doorstep in the Smokes means the Ravelers are coming for somebody inside. People move house.
- **A ribbon's answer.** Tie a ribbon on the dead tree on Temple Row and come back the next day: the ribbon is gone and a stone pear lies in its place. Nobody has seen who makes the exchange.
- **A doorstop, a paperweight, a sling-stone.** The company has a barrel of them at the gatehouse for the crows.
- **Eating.** "Sweet if you can bite them," the Glassers say, and sell you one, and watch.

### Rumours
- Bite one and your teeth turn to stone from the inside.
- At the Reseal the pears will fall, all at once, like a hailstorm, and the Orchard will be bare.
- One pear in the Orchard is real, and whoever finds it may eat it, and then they will be like the Hollowborn.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False, though Maud Severin has pulled two cracked ones this year and charged for the lecture. 2. Nobody knows; it is what the Hollowborn children say. At the Reseal ending the Orchard stops weeping, and whether it also drops its fruit is the GM's picture to paint. 3. Backwards, nearly.
:::

:::gm
One stone pear in a hundred is hollow, and inside it is a seed, real, dry, the size of a barleycorn, with a grain to it like a fingerprint. A PC who finds one (the pear is a little lighter; Investigation DC 15 hears the seed shift) has something nobody in the March knows exists. Planted in the Smokes, or anywhere in Cinderford, it will not grow. Planted at the lip of the Rent, within sight of the Ninth Stone, it would: a pear tree of grey stone, alive, that weeps at red moon from the day it breaks the ground, which is the ward putting down a root. That is a hook for a campaign after this one, or a very strange gift for Uthrem.

The seed in the carved hand of Ketterin's shattered statue, which the Glassers have and Kettle carries in the warm crate ([[The Glass-Fused Hand]]), is a carving of this exact seed, three thousand years older, with the first word of the Oath cut into it. A PC who holds both knows the Orchard is not an accident of the blast. It is what the Ninth Stone grows when it is broken.
:::

== GM notes ==

Put a stone pear in a PC's pocket early, at a penny, and let it be the thing that goes warm on the night of session 3 before anyone else has noticed the moon.
