A thread of old hemp, a finger long, brown going black, worn tucked in a hat-band or wound once round a button. It is not knotted. That is the difference between a strand and the charm the sergeants wear: a strand is a prayer you have not finished making yet.

### Where it comes from
Two places. The rope on the nail behind the bar at [[The Last Candle]], which the soldiers put up themselves and which Hennet replaces every month because there is nothing left of it by the thirtieth; and the noose at [[The Corporal's Gibbet]] at the end of Temple Row, which is replaced by hands nobody has seen, and from which pulling a strand is considered braver, or stupider, by the same people. You put a penny in the tin at the Candle. At the Gibbet you say what you will not do, aloud, and that is the price.

### How it is worn
In the hat-band, where the sergeant can see it and pretend not to. Round a button. In a wick-lamp's hood, by wicks who do not yet have a hat. Thistle Weirford has one on every drumstick. [[Mirrel Vosk]], who wears two of the real knotted charms ([[The Hanged Corporal's Noose-Knot Charm]]) and will deny it, has told the recruits that strands are for children. Three of the recruits have seen one in his hat.

### What it does
Nothing, the soldiers will tell you. Then, lower: the morning a man wears one is the morning the sergeant forgets why he came over. It is luck, the kind you do not thank anyone for, because thanking the Corporal is the one thing he is known not to like.

:::gm
The strand is a small true thing. [[The Hanged Corporal]] is ascending on refusal, and a strand of the real rope, worn by someone who refuses an order or a bargain at real cost, is a prayer he answers once: advantage on the roll that follows, and the rope goes limp and is done. The Candle's nail and the Gibbet's noose are both "real" in the sense that matters, which is that soldiers put them there meaning it; the rope at Dunmeer that [[Mab Lennard]] cuts into strands for pilgrims at a penny each is new rope she buys by the coil, and works one time in five, which is more than she expects and less than she charges for.

The best moment for it is a PC saying no to Sallow, Seraphine, Tollamund or the Regency with something to lose, and the GM saying, before the roll, "the strand in your hat goes slack." Never more than once per strand; a PC who takes a new one every session is praying by rote, and the Corporal knows the difference. He has never answered a strand pulled for profit.
:::

== GM notes ==

Hand them out as texture, a penny in the tin, and let players forget them until a refusal comes. The strand is how the campaign teaches the table that saying no is a mechanic in this world, before the god who makes it so has to be explained.
