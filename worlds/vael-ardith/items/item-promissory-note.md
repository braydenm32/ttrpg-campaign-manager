A single sheet of heavy Concord paper, watermarked with seven coins and a scale. It is signed by Captain Ilse Marrow on behalf of the Ninth Lantern, countersigned by Fennick Bottlebrook, and sealed with the House of Tollamund. The terms are in a small, perfectly neat hand.

### What it says
The Ninth Lantern borrows money for rations, powder and the rebuilding of the Keep walls. Payment is due on the quarter-day, with interest at a rate that looks reasonable until the third line. If the company fails to pay, the Concord may claim "the company's possessions, contracts and standing" in satisfaction.

### Who holds it
[[Grandmother Tollamund]] keeps it in a small locked box under her desk. She reads it every morning, slowly, as other people read scripture.

:::gm
The note was written by Tollamund herself. The credit line stood at 4,200 gold, interest added, at a tenth per quarter, when the House stopped lending in Reaping 3010; in Ashfall the House advanced 600 more under the third line (the pay-wagon coin) and booked it to the company, so the note now stands at 4,800. The quarter's interest, 480, fell due on 14 Ashfall and was not paid. Concord grace is twenty days: it ends at sunset on 4 Leafrot, and Tollamund collects on 5 Leafrot (S7). Nothing compounds inside the campaign.

The collateral is the company's "possessions, contracts and standing, including the lantern", the last two words underlined. Marrow saw it and signed anyway, because the company was starving. The clause is void as to the lantern by the Treaty's page seven: the lamp is a Treaty fixture, never the company's property. The winning argument is that the lamp is not collateral; the company is. Fennick has been juggling the books to keep the due date from falling on a day the company cannot cover. The Debt clock ticks on this paper.
:::

== GM notes ==

A prop for a pivotal scene: Tollamund, speaking very slowly, reading the clause aloud. Make the party wait through the whole sentence.
