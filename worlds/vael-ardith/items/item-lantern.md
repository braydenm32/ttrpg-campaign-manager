In an open niche on the outer east wall of [[The Lantern Keep]], twenty feet up, hangs a plain iron cage the size of a beehive, and inside it burns a flame about the size of a man's hand, fist-sized on nights it flares. It is gold at the heart, white at the edges, and it does not smoke. It has not gone out in nine years.

### What it looks like
The cage is black iron, riveted, with a hinged door and three locks ([[The Lantern's Cage Keys]] open them). Inside sits a squat brass lamp, dented, patched with solder, and once a cook's. The flame stands above it without a wick, the way a candle might if it believed in itself. There is no oil in it that anyone has seen, though the company pours a measure into the bowl each dawn, and the bowl is always dry by evening.

### What the company says
Soldiers touch the cage for luck. Recruits are made to hold their hand near it, to feel the warmth. Veterans swear on it. The old orcs will not look at it directly. The contract over the hall door says the company shall hold the Keep and the ford, and shall stand down if the lantern ever goes out. Every soldier knows what that means: if it dies, they are free, and nobody can say whether that is a promise or a threat.

### What it does
Quietly, it comforts. People who sleep near it sleep badly but wake with strange clarity. Animals avoid it. Ash will not settle on the cage. Anyone who stares into it long enough hears something under the crackle, like a breath held by someone far below.

:::gm
The flame burns on a splinter of the Ninth Stone ([[The Ward Shard]]), lying in the lamp's bowl under the flame, long since fused to it. Mother Hennet carried it up from the orchard in a soup-pot the night the lantern was lit, and Brother Tallow has kept it there since. The splinter keeps the crack in the Ninth Ward from widening. That is why the flame needs no oil, and why the ash-cut oil that Dannic Rook swapped into the store was killing it: the ash fouled the bowl, and the flame guttered because the stone behind it was dimming.

The Sleeper Below hears the flame. Anyone who holds it for long, or who is called by it, begins to hear the Sleeper in dreams, and slowly to become its voice. Ysse feels this already. The Saint wants the flame to feed on. The Concord wants it as a key to the Ness. The Quiet Office wants it for the Regency. The Mothers want it left alone.

If the flame goes out, the crack opens; the dead walk. If the flame is carried away from the stone, it dims. If it is taken by someone willing to bear it, they become a leash on the Sleeper, and the Sleeper's mouthpiece in the same moment.

Running the flame: it gutters when the Ember is full, when a Hollowborn dies near it, when the oil is poisoned, and when the company loses hope. It brightens when someone tells the truth in the Keep. It never goes out during play unless the PCs choose an ending that demands it.
:::

== GM notes ==

This is the heart of the arc. Show it in every session: Tallow tending it, a sentry resting a hand on the cage, a stray draught that does nothing. When it gutters (first at the end of session 1), do it plainly: the shadows lean, the room goes cold, Tallow stops mid-sentence. Never explain it. The three possible endings all turn on what a PC does when they stand in front of it.
