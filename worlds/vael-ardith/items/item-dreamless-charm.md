A knot of faded ribbon the length of a finger, red once, with a twist of grey salt tied into the middle of it and a loop to tie it to the wrist or the bedpost, or sew inside a shirt. It smells, faintly, of pears. Nell Harrow sells them from a jar on the counter at [[Harrow's Simples]] on Ford Street, a silver each, and does not say what they are made of, and does not need to: everybody in Cinderford knows what they do, which is that you do not dream.

### Where it comes from
Nell Harrow is the charm-woman the company gossip means, the one in the Smokes who sells charms against bad dreams and whom half the Keep visits on Restday, and her shop has two doors, one on Ford Street and one in the Smokes, for the two kinds of customer. She started tying the knots the winter after Stillwater drowned, for the mothers of the Smokes, and the company found out the first red moon it slept badly. She has never raised the price for soldiers. She has raised it for the Church.

### What it does
You sleep, and you wake, and there was nothing in between. On an ordinary night that is a kindness. Three nights either side of a full Ember, when a third of the town dreams the same dream and wakes sweating, it is the difference between a soldier who can stand a watch and one who cannot, and the Keep's sergeants have stopped pretending they do not know who wears one.

The Sleepless hate them. A Sleepless Caller who finds a dreamless on a sleeping soldier cuts it off with a knife and leaves the knot on the pillow, and more than one soldier has woken with the cord cut and the dream already begun. The Dreaming Widow calls the charms "blinding" and preaches against them by name. Nell Harrow's shop window is smashed every red moon. She has it glazed on the Ironday after, every time, by the same glazier, who does not charge her.

### Quirks
A dreamless that has been worn through a full Ember is grey by morning, and the ribbon comes apart like ash when touched, and that is the end of it. Nell says that is the dream going into the knot instead of you, and will not say where it goes after. A Hollowborn child made to wear one by a frightened parent stops seeing the dead for the night and screams when the sun comes up.

:::gm
The ribbons are cut from the ones tied to the dead tree on Temple Row, which is a cutting from [[The Weeping Orchard]] and is not entirely dead, and the knot is blessed there at dusk; the salt is Stillwater salt, from the shore of the lake that does not ripple. Nell knows exactly what she has made and has decided it is worth it. The charm is hedge-craft that works by shutting the one door the Sleeper uses, and its cost is the plainest in the campaign: not pain, not years, but **what the wearer would have learned**. A PC who wears one through the session 3 full sleeps soundly and has nothing to compare with the others in the morning; a Hollowborn PC who wears one loses the true dream, which is Tick's mother at his shoulder, and the Widow's word "blinding" is, for once, exactly right. The Widow preaches against the charms because they work, and because her sister would have worn one.

Nell dosed her own Hollowborn son with charms for five years; he is Sleepless now, in Ambry Coyle's cell, and will not come home. She will tell a PC this, once, if they ask why the window gets smashed and she keeps selling. See [[Sleeper Dreams]] for what the charm is keeping out.
:::

== GM notes ==

Offer the charm in session 2 at a silver and let the players argue about whether to wear it on the 16th. Whatever they decide is right, and whatever they decide costs something. The soldier who sold his to a Hollowborn mother for the price of a round is a Mutiny-clock scene waiting to happen, and so is the one who did not.
