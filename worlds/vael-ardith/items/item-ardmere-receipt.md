A slip of Regency paper the size of a hand, foxed brown, folded and refolded until the creases have gone soft as cloth. Printed: *ARDMERE RECEIPT No. ____. The bearer is owed, for service in the relief of Ardmere, the sum of ____ crowns, payable upon the settlement of the realm.* Stamped, signed, numbered, in a clerk's hand that was in a hurry. Most say thirty-one crowns, because that was a season's pay for a ranker and the Regency had worked out exactly how little it could promise.

### Where it comes from
The siege of 2994 ended with three mercenary companies owed a fortune and a Regency with no coin, so the Regency printed these instead ([[The Ardmere Receipts]]). In the thaw a Concord broker bought nearly all of them at forty in the hundred, in coin, on the spot, and the Concord learned what a broken state's promise was worth to the people who hold it. The soldiers who refused to sell kept their paper out of spite, and the paper has kept them company since.

### What it is worth
Nothing, officially, until the realm is settled, which is to say never. [[Fennick Bottlebrook]] takes them at face from anyone who hands him one, as a joke, and enters them in the company's ledger under *Receipts, Ardmere, for the record*, and the column is longer than it should be. "I'll take it in Ardmere Receipts" is what any soldier says to any promise with no coin behind it, and Fennick says it aloud to the Regency's pay notice every quarter.

### Who has one
[[Spoons Aldane]], who was the Long Spoons' pot-boy at the water-gate and was handed one at twelve; it is in his book, pressed flat, and he shows it to anyone who asks what the odds on the Regency are. Captain Marrow has one in the lining of her coat and has never said whose it was. Old men by the fire in every tavern on the Ardis, who were there.

:::gm
Two places in Cinderford will pay for a Receipt, and the difference is the point. [[Tollamund & Daughters]] honours one at forty in the hundred without a word or a question, exactly the broker's price of 2995, because the broker was theirs and a Shellback does not change a rate it has once set; a PC who walks in with a thirty-one-crown Receipt walks out with twelve gold and four silver in shells and has learned, cheaply, how the Concord thinks about the Regency's paper, which is the whole of the campaign's debt plot in one transaction. [[The Hock]] on Ford Street will give a copper for the frame and laugh.

Marrow's Receipt is not hers. She was inside the walls of Ardmere for the whole siege as the garrison's supply clerk and was owed nothing by anyone; a Long Spoons sergeant who came through the water-gate pressed his own into her hand for keeping the garrison's stores alive, and died that week, and she has carried it since as the only pay she ever took that she did not earn, which is how she thinks of it and is wrong. If a PC ever sees it, Insight DC 14 reads it on her face: this is the thing she will not sell, and the Concord's note had better not ask for it.
:::

== GM notes ==

A small prop for a big idea. Let Fennick enter a PC's Receipt in his ledger with great ceremony in session 1, and let Tollamund's clerk pay out on one in session 4 without looking up. Players who connect the two understand the Concord.
