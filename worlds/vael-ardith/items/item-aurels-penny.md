A copper penny of the old empire, worn thin, with the last Emperor in profile on the face: the long nose, the high forehead, the slight frown, as though he were listening to something. Soldiers call it a **listener**. The reverse is the sunburst, mostly rubbed away by thumbs.

### Where it comes from
The Mint at [[Caer Ardith]] struck them for twenty-two years and stopped the week [[Aurel IX]] died; the new pence carry the sun on both sides. The old ones are still everywhere, because nobody spends a listener if they can spend anything else. Gutter Market's fused coin-lumps are mostly listeners, and a Glasser who chips one out whole keeps it.

### How it is used
In the hat-band, in the boot, under the tongue before a fight. A soldier rubs it with a thumb when waiting, which is most of soldiering, and swears the frown is deeper on bad days. "A penny for the Emperor" is laid on a dead man's eye at a wake so that he has something to pay the first toll with, and the Ravelers leave it there, which is the only coin they do leave. In the Ninth, a wick is given a listener by the oldest soldier in the tally the first night they stand by the cage, and told not to spend it, and most do not.

### Quirks
Rub it a while and one edge goes cold. Soldiers know this and call it the Emperor's draught; they do not agree on what it means, and the usual explanation is that the metal remembers the Mint's cellar. In Cinderford the cold edge points east, toward the Rent, which everyone has noticed and nobody finds odd, because everything in Cinderford points east.

:::gm
The listener is ward-sensitive, very slightly. The imperial Mint stands over a stone the old magi used to tune their dies, a thing that was never a Great Ward but was cut from the same quarry as one, and twelve centuries of striking there left the metal with a faint habit of the wards. Rubbed, it chills toward the nearest Great Ward and warms near [[The Ward Shard]] or [[The Ninth Stone]]: a compass that points at the thing the campaign is about, carried in the pocket of every soldier in the March, and used a hundred times a day for luck. The cost is a breath of the ward each time, which is nothing, exactly as a cantrip is nothing, until a hundred soldiers of the Ninth stand on the lip of the Rent in session 8 and every one of them, waiting, rubs his penny. Let the frost bloom on the glass under their boots and let a Vesh PC see why. See [[Magic and Its Cost]].

A PC who works out what the penny is doing has a clue-finder for the Barrow, the Cold Seam and the Orchard, and a reason to tell the company to keep its hands in its pockets. Tesh, who is of the imperial blood, cannot hold one for long: it goes warm in her hand wherever she is.
:::

== GM notes ==

Give one to each PC in session 1 from a veteran, as a kindness, and say nothing. Let a player discover the cold edge by rubbing it while they think. The reveal belongs to them, not to an NPC.
