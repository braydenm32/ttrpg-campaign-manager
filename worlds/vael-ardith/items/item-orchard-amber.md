A bead of dark red glass the size of a pea, warm in a cold hand and cold in a warm one, with a pale thread running through it like the vein in a leaf. Hold it to the light and the thread moves. Press it to your tongue and it tastes, faintly, of pears.

### What it is
At the full of the Ember the stone trees of [[The Weeping Orchard]] weep red sap from the forks of their branches and the stems of their stone fruit, and by morning it has set into beads on the bark. The Glassers are there at first light with knives, because the beads sell: to the kiln-masters, who have no idea what they are; to the Saint's mission, for its infirmary lamps; to Hesk Varro the chandler, who buys the raw sap by the vial for his own blend and has never asked what it does; and to anyone at [[Gutter Market]] with two silver and a reason. The Glassers call it Orchard amber. The Hollowborn of the Smokes, who sit under the trees at red moon and drink the sap, call the beads "tears" and will not sell them.

### What it does
Dissolve a bead in a pint of lamp-oil and the lamp burns white, clean and tall all night, and does not gutter in a wind, and for an hour after it is lit anyone standing in its light sees what a Hollowborn sees: the dead who did not pass, standing in the corners of rooms, at the ends of lanes, in the shallows under the third pier. The Saint's wardens use it in the infirmary at the House of Unburnt Light to see what is waiting by a bed. Soldiers of the Ninth who buy it do so, mostly, once.

Eaten, it is the sap, slower: a true dream for a Hollowborn, a short bad one for anyone else.

### Quirks
A bead held near the lantern on the hill grows warm and then hot. Hesk's blend smells of green, sweet fruit; the beads smell of nothing until they burn, when the whole room smells of an orchard that is not there. Nan Crupp at the ferry will take a bead instead of a copper and will not say what she does with them.

:::gm
The sap is the ward's own substance leaking through the Orchard's Lithic trees at the moon's pull, and a bead is a little of the Ninth Ward in a pocket. In the lantern it is medicine: Hesk's accidental drop a tin is part of what kept the flame tall for eight years, and [[Sap from the Weeping Orchard]] is Tallow's cure for the ash-cut oil. Burned anywhere else within a mile of [[The Lantern of the Ninth]] it is a cost: the ward's substance calls to itself, and the flame in the cage leans toward every bead-lamp lit in Cinderford, visibly, the way a candle leans to a draught. One or two in a night the flame can bear. Ten in a night, or three in one room on Lantern Hill, is a guttering, and should be run as one: the flame thins to a blue bead for a breath, and the wick-lamps dim, and Tallow or Ysse feels it. Whether the Saint's mission knows this is yours to decide: Seraphine says not, and the Lamp-Wardens may truly not, while Ilyra, looking through her, might. Either way the House of Unburnt Light buys every bead the Glassers bring, its infirmary lamps burn white on the nights before a red moon, and a party that counts the House's lamps from the Ash Road on 15 Ashfall will count more than thirty beds need.

A Sleepless with a bead-lamp sees the dead and does not need the Hollowborn to tell them where the door is. Ambry Coyle has one.
:::
