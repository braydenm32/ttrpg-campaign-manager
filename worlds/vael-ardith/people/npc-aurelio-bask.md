### Look and voice
Forty-five, round-shouldered, soft-bellied, with a long sad face and a mouth that keeps starting to smile and thinking better of it. He sits cross-legged on a table, not a chair, from a habit of the campaigns. His hands are the one fine thing about him: small, quick and clean, a tailor's. The tabard he wears is blue-and-white of Greywake's Spears, on inside out: the pale lining shows, and the badge sits on his back where only the wall can read it.

He apologises before he disagrees and after, and he is funnier than he knows.

> "Turncoat, they call me. I tell them: only the one side, mind. You'd be surprised how many want the other."

> "I'm not hiding. I'm just wearing the honest side out."

### Who he is
A Greywake's Spear, the Second Company on the wall at Cinders, who ran. At the first sound of the sorcery he threw down his spear, found a ditch, and spent the night with the cooks and the walking wounded in the stone orchard, where he held a bucket and did not look at anything. In the morning eight companies were dark and he was alive. He came back to Cinderford in 3004 and asked the Ninth to take him; Vosk looked at him for a long time and said no, and the company let the no stand. He bought a vat, rented a shopfront, and has spent seven years turning the dead's tabards into the living's coats. He has never stopped wearing his own, inside out. It is not penance. It is a way of saying, in a town of soldiers, *I ran, and I know what it looks like.*

He makes the best coats in the March and the worst conversation, and he knows which is which.

### What he wants
To be addressed once, by a soldier of the Ninth, as someone who stayed alive. To buy the Ninth's winter boots back from himself before Fennick has to. To say what he saw that night to somebody who will not think less of him.

### What he knows
Every cloth in the March by weight and dye. Which soldiers have sold their tabards and which have kept them. Which deserters in the Ashenreach are the old kind and which are new. That the Concord's tallymen buy Salt Guard tabards by the dozen. He has said none of it, because nobody has asked him a thing.

### How he treats the PCs
Tenderly, if he thinks they are young, with an exact eye for the fit. He measures them without being asked and charges them nothing for it. He will say a good deal about thread and nothing about the tabard, and a PC who understands the difference gets a coat at cost. He is very attentive to anyone who wears the Ninth's lantern, and a little afraid of them.

:::gm
**Secret.** On the night of Cinders he was in the cooks' camp in the Weeping Orchard, and he saw [[Mother Hennet]] lift a soup-pot out of her fire with her one hand and tip something into it that glowed. *He saw the glow through the steam. She did not look at it.* A man he did not know ran past a moment after with a lantern that had not been lit a minute before, and a woman's voice shouting after him. Years later, in 3004, he stood at the Keep gate being turned away, and an old man with a beard and a limp was waved through behind him, and Bask knew the face. He has said nothing for seven years. It is the one oath he has kept.

In session 5, when Pike dies, Bask will say what he saw if asked in front of a priest, or by Hennet, or by Marrow: he is the second witness to the soup-pot, and the only one who is not part of the company's legend. A PC who has been kind to him is told first. If Hennet is asked and refuses, he tells; if she answers, he is not needed, and he knows that, and goes back to his vat and lets the colours run.

The six lengths of white-and-gold are a quiet clue: they are for six Lamp-Wardens, new tabards, for 6 Leafrot.
:::

== GM notes ==

Make him easy to love in session 1 or 2: the shop is warm, the coat fits, the man is kind. Give him a line in each session that is a small act of decency, and let him fail at the one thing that matters, speaking up, until session 5. If a PC is a deserter or an ex-mercenary, he knows it in a glance and pretends not to. If the party reaches the Orchard before session 5 he will go quiet if asked what he did that night, and the table should notice.
