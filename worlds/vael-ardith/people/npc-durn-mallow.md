### Look and voice
A hundred and forty, which for a dwarf is the thick of life, and he looks it: broad as a door, arms like hawsers, a plait tied with the Underlook's red wire gone black with forge-scale, beard cut close in the Returned way. Burns on both forearms in a pattern that, if you look, is the same shape over and over: the bow of a key. He stands in forge-light with nothing on the floor behind him and has stopped noticing that people notice. His eyes are small and very steady and do not quite focus on the person he is talking to; they focus on something about a foot behind them.

He talks the way the dark side of the hall talks, which is barely, and in a voice worn flat by the hammer. When he says the thing he always says, he says it the same way every time.

> "I don't know why. I make it and it's wrong and I melt it. Next week I make it again."

> "You want to know what's down there. Everyone does. Here's what's down there: it's warm. That's all I'll say. It's warm, and it was glad to see us."

> "Hathe's? No. Never heard of him. Doors? ... Doors. Yes. Doors, I'd believe."

### Who he is
Durn Mallow was a smith on the lit side of [[The Underlook Forge]] before the war, with a shadow and a wife and a temper, and when the hold went under the Grimmark in the winter of 2993 he went with the tenth that went down the Sealed Stair. He came up in the first column in 3004, without the wife, the temper or the shadow, took the second name Came-Back because it was the plainest one going, and asked the Forge-Warden for the forge nearest the rock. Harrek Underlook gave it to him. He has not asked for anything since.

In Emberwane 3004, a month after the Return, he made a key. Hand-long, black iron, the bow shaped like a shut door, the teeth in a pattern no lock in Hollowhall has ever matched. He looked at it, said it was wrong, and melted it. He has made it, by the Warden's slate, three hundred and forty-six times since, about one a week, and melted every one, and the hall has stopped watching, and the surface clans tell it as a joke, and the Returned on the dark side do not laugh, and neither does he.

### What he wants
To stop. He says this plainly to anyone who asks, and it is the only plain thing he says. He would like to make a hinge, a hoop, a chisel, and go to the Cup, and sleep. He has tried not making the key. By the ninth day his hands shake and the forge-note under the hall gets into his teeth and he makes it, and it is wrong, and he melts it.

### What he knows
Every tooth of the key, which he could cut blind. That the thing under the mountain is warm and glad and wants, and that wanting is the worst of the three. That Mardle Ock, who was a boy at his forge before the war, went south to dig in the ash because the ash is the only other place where the ground wants something, and that Mardle hums three notes when he works, and Durn knows the tune, and has never hummed it in his life.

### How he treats the PCs
As a nuisance until they say the word "door" or "ward" or "Borrin", and then with a stillness that is worse than anger. If one of them is Returned he will look at the floor behind them, once, which no Returned ever does, and then at their face. If one of them has stood near the Ninth Stone he can smell it on them, he says, "like a forge that's gone out", and he will take them to the Cup and buy the thimble and ask them what the crack sounds like, and listen to the answer with his eyes shut.

:::gm
**Secret.** Durn knows why. Under the Grimmark the dwarves found the Eighth Ward breached and a thing on the far side that took their shadows as the price of passage ([[The Nine Great Wards]]). When the Returned came up in 3004 they shut the door at the bottom of the Sealed Stair behind them, from below, with a key whose shape the thing gave them, so that they could lock it and so that they could never forget it; and the thing dreams through the shadows it keeps, and in Durn, who was a smith, it dreams that key ([[Why does the Returned smith forge the same key?]]). It is also, tooth for tooth, the key to the Lithic doors on the ward-road under the March, which the standing dead shut and which [[Hathe's Door-Key]] turns because Hathe's key was cut for the dead; Durn's turns them because it was cut *from the other side*. The Red Seam door under the Glass Barrow is one of those doors. The Cold Seam's landing is another. The Ninth Stone's root is the third. The thing under the Eighth wants them open, from below, so that what is under all nine stones can reach each other, and it has found a dwarf who makes keys.

He melts the key because the moment it is finished he hears, through the floor, the same voice that asked for his shadow, asking for the key, warmly, and he will not give it a thing it asks for. That is the whole of his resistance and he has kept it up three hundred and forty-six times. He does not let himself know what the key opens. He knows that the thing wants it, and that is enough. Tam Hollowell, in the Smokes at Cinderford, carves the same key in slag and wood and burns it, for the same reason, without ever having met Durn.

**The rite.** In Leafrot 3010, after the three-hundredth key, Durn went up to the gate-shrine and asked [[Hold-Mother Greta Holm]] to say the Standing rite over him, the one for a Lithic about to stop, because he wanted to stop. She said it, because he asked, and because she is the only priest in the Grimmark who still knows it. It did not take: a dwarf is not stone. But since that night the keys come out of the quench warm, and stay warm until he melts them, and he has not told her, and she has guessed.

**The next key.** Two things make him stop melting. One is the Reseal: the night the Ninth's seal holds (session 8), the voice under the floor goes quiet for the first time in seven years, and Durn finishes a key and does not melt it, and does not know why that either. The other is the words: if someone brings him the line from the Glass Barrow's cold stair, *what we buried is not at rest*, in the Lithic hand (a rubbing will do), he reads it, understands for the first time which side of the door he has been cutting for, and the next key he makes he keeps. Either way he gives it to whoever is standing there, with one instruction: "Don't open anything from underneath." The key is a campaign-two object: it opens the Red Seam door, which the party was told in session 2 to leave alone, and what it opens from the other side is the next campaign. With it out of the forge, Durn sleeps, and the thing under the mountain turns its attention to whoever is holding it. If the Ninth is used instead of sealed, his keys start coming out of the quench with a second ward's mark on the bow, and he melts those faster.

**Borrin.** The thing that wears [[Borrin Halt]]'s shadow is the same thing, or a hand of it. When Borrin and Durn are in the same room the key on the anvil, if there is one, rings once, untouched. Borrin's shadow stands up behind Durn, not Borrin, for the length of a breath. Durn sees it. He says, "There you are," and goes back to work. The thread is [[Why does the Returned smith forge the same key?]].
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** flat, few words, the forge in it; "I don't know why" said exactly the same way each time until the players stop believing it, which should take about three repetitions. He is not haunted so much as employed by something he refuses to work for, and his dignity is in the melting.

**Use.** Post-session 8, or by Gerda Hollin's letter home before. The scene is the forge: the key coming out of the quench warm, Durn looking at it, the players realising he is about to melt it, and someone saying "wait". Let them talk him into or out of it. If they bring the Barrow's words, give them the key and the instruction, and start the next campaign's clock.
