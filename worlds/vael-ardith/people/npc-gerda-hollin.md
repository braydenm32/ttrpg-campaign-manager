Short, square, grey-braided and immovable, Gerda commands a tally at the gatehouse and holds a shield wall like a cliff holds the sea. Like most surface dwarves she reveres [[The Lithic]] as holy ancestors made stone, and she makes a pilgrimage to the [[The Treaty Stone|Treaty Stone]] every Restday to leave a pebble "for the ones who stood". Now and then she walks the three miles further to [[The Burned Mill]] with a loaf for the widow there, and talks to the pear tree before she talks to the widow. On Shadow-Count, 2 Emberwane, she stands the gatehouse's dwarves in the noon sun and writes "all present" whatever the shadows say.

She does not like [[Borrin Halt]]. She makes the sign of [[Tharos]] whenever he passes, and he has started making it back, which infuriates her.

> "Nothing that walks out of the deep without its shadow walks out alone, boy. My grandmother told me that. She was right about everything else."

That grandmother is [[Hold-Mother Greta Holm]], hold-priestess of [[Tharos]] at Hollowhall's gate, and the line is hers.

:::gm
Gerda is right, which is the worst part. A few mornings before session 1 she saw Borrin standing at the ford rail looking down into the water, an hour after she had watched him go to his bunk. In session 1 she says it aloud, in the yard, to his face: "I saw you at the rail this morning, boy, and you were not looking at the water." If Borrin's shadow-thing acts again before session 6, Gerda is the one who sees it, and the company will believe her. Win her over (save her tally, show her the respect due a Lithic shrine) and she becomes Borrin's fiercest defender instead. Her scrap on [[The Gatehouse Board]] reads FOUND: a shadow. Apply within. It is about Borrin, and she does not think it is funny.
:::
