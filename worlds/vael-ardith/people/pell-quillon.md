### Look and voice
Thin, stooped, thirty-eight going on sixty, with a long earnest face, ink to the wrists and a Regency official's black coat gone green at the seams. He carries a satchel of forms the size of a small dog and an inkpot on a chain round his neck, "for emergencies". He apologises to doors.

Every sentence arrives fully dressed, with its clauses in order and its sub-clauses buttoned, and he runs short of breath before he runs short of regulation.

> "I'm terribly sorry, Captain. I'm afraid I must ask you to complete this form in triplicate. The third copy is for the fire. Should there be a fire. There usually is."

> "Under section nine, subsection four, a company unpaid for more than two years is still liable for the Hearth Levy. I agree it's monstrous. I'm only the collector."

### Who he is
**Assessor Pell Quillon**, Fourth Assistant Under-Collector of the Regency's Office of Revenue for the March of Cinders, has been sent to [[Cinderford]] to assess the Ninth Lantern for back taxes: the Hearth Levy, the Garrison Tithe, the Tax on Lamps and Lights (which he calls "the Lantern Duty": passed in 2997 and never repealed), the Fee for the Assessment of Fees ("the Fee for Fees") and the cost of preparing the notice. He arrives at dawn on 15 Ashfall, takes over [[The Assessor's Office]], and finds it already occupied by forms and a clerk who got there first. He sleeps at the office, and on Crownfast he keeps the day alone with one candle, wherever he is posted.

The notice itself is the Tax Notice, in Triplicate. The company can dispute it on Form 9-K (Declaration of Wages Owed But Not Payable, Countersigned), and claim its arrears on Form 44-A (Declaration of Non-Receipt of Imperial Funds). He will explain both to anyone who stands still.

### What he wants
To complete his forms. All of them. Correctly. He is the only honest Regency man in the [[The Ashenreach|Ashenreach]], and he does not know it, because it hasn't occurred to him that the others aren't.

### What he knows
The Regency's tax law, in detail. The Regency's own absurd ledger of "wages intended", on which the Hearth Levy is assessed, and which he cannot explain either. That his clerk, [[Ansel Mote]], is a treasure, and that [[Hester Pruitt|Widow Pruitt]], who does the sums, is a terror. That [[Lusk Pardew|Examiner Pardew]], who trained him at Whitepost, has never once been wrong about a cart.

### How he treats the PCs
With nervous courtesy, then growing horror as he realises the Regency owes the company far more than the company owes the Regency. He is easily bullied and impossible to bribe (he would have to log it). Treat him kindly and he will fight for them in the only way he knows: on paper.

:::gm
**Secret.** He has none. That is the joke and the tragedy. Pell is harmless, honest, and being used: the Quiet Office sent him to the March as cover for Ansel Mote, chosen precisely because nobody would ever suspect him of anything.

**What he finds.** The Council votes the Ninth's wages every quarter, and the Treasury signs them out "to Account Deferred, by order of the Regent-Chancellor". Pell comes across the entry in sessions 4 and 5, and it frightens him more than anything he has ever audited.

**His payoff:** once Pell finds the pay was signed out and diverted, he files a formal Claim of Arrears on the company's behalf against his own government, in triplicate. In session 7 his paperwork can prove the Regency diverted the pay and that the order came from the Regent-Chancellor's own office. If Mote learns of it, Pell is in danger.
:::
