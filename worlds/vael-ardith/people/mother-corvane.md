### Look and voice
Small, hunched and very old, her black feathers gone white-streaked about the face like frost on a hedge. One eye is bright black; the other is clouded and, people say, sees further. She wears layer on layer of grey shawls hung with keys, bones, buttons, coins and teeth, all of which rattle when she laughs, which is often. Her beak has a crack down it mended with a silver rivet.

She talks like a market trader who happens to be an undertaker: quick, cheerful, morbid, haggling.

> "Last words are cheap, dearie. It's the second-to-last ones that cost."

> "Hathe's dead and I'm still keeping his doors. Somebody's got to. Bit like being married."

### Who she is
Mother Corvane is the head of [[The Rook-Court]] in [[Cinderford]]: the [[Ravelers|Raveler]] undertakers who buried the dead of [[The Battle of Cinders]] and have buried everyone since. She keeps [[The Doorkeeper's Chapel]] on [[Temple Row]], the only shrine in town to a dead god, and the rookery and burial grounds at [[Corvel Rise]]. She taught [[Quorl Many-Words]] his trade.

She is unbribable. She simply charges more. She has not used her deed-name in forty years, and nobody dares ask it. These days she keeps to the rookery at Corvel Rise and has not come down to town in a year; her seat in the Chapel's upstairs room is kept empty, and [[Skrell]] runs her errands and carries her bodies. She keeps [[Hathe's Door-Key]] in the reliquary there and lends it for funerals.

### What she wants
To decode the last words. Every word spoken by the dying since the war goes into the Rook-Court's great ledger, and Corvane means to read them all, one way or another.

### What she knows
The last words of almost everyone who has died in the March since 3002. Which dead have not passed. That the dead don't pass since Hathe fell, and roughly how many walk the ash at full red moon.

### How she treats the PCs
As customers and as future customers. She is helpful, ghoulish and expensive. She will sell them a dead man's last words for a living man's secret. She is fond of Quorl and will be fond of anyone he trusts.

:::gm
**Secret.** For nine years the Ravelers have noticed that the last words, set side by side, are not random. Corvane has built four of the five words from the Court's ledgers and a Hask priestess's letter: **"the lamp is the ..."**. She lacks the last until [[Halloran Pike]] says it at his funeral in session 5 and she will be sure. She has not told the Rook-Court elders, or Quorl. She also knows [[Brother Tallow]] is frightened of his own lamp.

Her sister Keld, the Court's first watcher of the lantern, bet "never" on [[Spoons]]'s pool in 3003 and vanished the next day; Corvane has not forgiven the lantern for that either.

And she has drawn the wrong conclusion. She believes the lamp is what's locking **Hathe's doors**: that since the lantern was lit, the dead can't pass, and that if it went out, the doors would open and the dead could rest. It is half true: the doors are troubled because Hathe is dead and his power is in [[Ilyra of Halcyra]]. But putting out the lamp would open the crack, not the doors.

She loves the dead more than the living. If she becomes certain, she will help put the lantern out. That is why the dead warn Quorl: "Don't let them put it out."
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** quick, chatty, haggling, rattling shawls; cackles.

**Across the arc:**
1. Receives the drowned man's body from the ford-watch, by Skrell's hands. It gets up on her table. She is delighted.
2–3. On the red moon night, she counts the walking dead from the chapel roof and sends Quorl to tally the Smokes.
4. Asks Quorl, casually, whether anyone has said anything "odd" lately.
5. **Last Words**: lends [[Hathe's Door-Key]] to finish old [[Halloran Pike]]'s jammed funeral rite ([[The Funeral That Would Not End]]). Opens the ledger to the PCs (for a price). Reads them the message. Explains her theory, earnestly.
6. If she learns what the PCs found at the Ninth Stone, she must choose between her theory and the truth. She is old and stubborn.
7. She can be an ally in resealing the ward (her undertakers know the Rent's dead roads) or a knife in the dark at the last moment (she'll try to snuff the flame "to let them rest"). Let the PCs talk her round, or not.
8. She is **the friend at the lip**: among the priests above the chasm when the cart reaches the Stone.
