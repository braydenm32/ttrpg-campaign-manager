### Look and voice
Fifty-five, broad and weathered, with a farmer's forearms and a magistrate's chain she wears over a waxed coat because she is usually somewhere wet. Iron-grey hair under a hat: a different hat every day, which the soldiers bet on. Clear blue eyes that are always counting heads.

She finishes other people's sentences, usually more bluntly than they would have, and holds court on Wardday with the same economy: three questions, one ruling, next.

> "Companies come and go, Captain. Cinderford stays. That's what a town is: the thing that's left over."

> "I don't care who's right. I care who's paying for the roof."

### Who she is
Odile Fairweather came to [[Cinderford]] in 3003, a widow of forty-seven, the year after [[The Battle of Cinders]], with a wagon, two sons and a charter from the Treaty commissioners to rebuild a town that everyone else had given up for ash. She rebuilt it. Grey stone, slate roofs, wells sunk below the ash line, a toll bridge at [[The Ford]] and the bell-ward's stroke to shut it. One of her sons died of the glass-lung in 3006; the other holds the toll, with [[Mistress Hebbe]] as his clerk at the company gatehouse. She has been Reeve for eight years, elected three times, never opposed, and has outlasted three captains. She keeps the only key to the locked shrine of Aurelan on Temple Row, and will not say why.

### What she wants
A town that outlives [[The Ninth Lantern]]. A charter nobody can tear up. Grain stores for two winters. Roads. She thinks of the company the way a farmer thinks of weather: necessary, unreliable, and not to be relied upon for the harvest.

### What she knows
Every family in town and what they owe. That the company can't pay the town for its bread much longer. That the Concord's banks are buying up the March's debts, and what they're offering. That the Sleepless are growing in [[The Smokes]], and that half the people who go to their meetings are her own tanners, who are simply very tired.

### How she treats the PCs
Courteous, brisk and practical: they are the company's, so they are useful to her but not hers. She will pay them for jobs the company won't do, and she pays on time, which makes her the only employer in the March who does.

**The Reeve's board** (four jobs, at the Reeve's rates):
1. A child who has walked toward the Rent in the night, and keeps doing it. Her mother says she was sleep-walking; the Reeve thinks she is Hollowborn.
2. A cellar on Tanners' Lane flooded with water so cold it has a skin of ice in Ashfall.
3. A quarrel on the Low Quay between Kettle's cousins and a Glasser, over a crate nobody will name.
4. A coin that rang wrong in [[Mistress Hebbe]]'s hand this month, and the question of where it came from.

:::gm
**Secret.** Odile has quietly agreed to sell Cinderford's charter to [[The Concord Houses]], through [[Grandmother Tollamund]]. In return the Concord will pay the town's debts, guarantee its grain, and garrison it with Concord-paid soldiers if the Ninth Lantern stands down. The papers are drafted and waiting at [[Tollamund & Daughters]]. She signs them between sessions 3 and 7, but she has not sealed them. She tells herself she's waiting for the right terms. Really she's waiting to see if the company survives.

Tollamund also holds the mortgage on the toll bridge her surviving son collects.

She doesn't know that the Concord wants the lantern far more than the town, or that the charter's fine print gives Tollamund the Keep and "all fixtures and fittings thereof".
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** brisk, blunt, warm underneath; finishes sentences.

**Across the arc:**
1. Comes up to the Keep on payday to ask, politely, who's paying for the bread now.
2. Gives the Assessor a room and a very long look.
3. After the red moon, she's frightened: a third of the town dreamed the same dream. She asks the PCs what it means.
4. Tollamund presses her to sign, and by the end of the session she has signed, unsealed. If the PCs learn about the charter, she defends it fiercely: "And what's your plan, soldier? Starve politely?"
5–6. If the PCs have earned her trust, she shows them the fine print and asks what "fixtures and fittings" includes.
7. **Three Knives**: the Concord's move; it is court day, and she stands beside Tollamund's chair at the Keep gate. She seals it (and the Concord's people walk into the Keep), or she tears it up publicly (and the town has to face winter alone). The PCs' choices decide.
8. Whatever happens, she rings the Treaty Bell at dawn and starts organising the clean-up.
