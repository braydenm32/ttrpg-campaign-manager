### Look and voice
A hundred and ninety, which is old for a dwarf and the right age for a hold-priest, who are chosen by sitting longest in silence. Short even for her people, square, slow, with a white plait tied in the Underlook's red wire and a face like a dry riverbed. She wears a grey robe with Tharos's fist on the breast and chain under it, because the gate is the gate, and carries a mace she has not used since the war and a leather bag of pebbles she has. She sits on the shrine's bench by the great gate with her back to the carved faces and her hands in her lap and does not get up for weather.

She says little, and what she says is in the old hold cadence, every sentence set down like a stone on a stone.

> "Nothing that walks out of the deep without its shadow walks out alone. I told my granddaughter that. She tells everyone. She's not wrong. She's not asking the right question either."

> "The rite isn't words. The words are for the ones watching. The rite is: you sit with them until they stop, and you don't look away, and you don't help."

> "Tharos does not answer. That's not news. He hasn't answered since the Nine. The Returned stand at his door like he's going to open it. I don't tell them he won't. They'd only stand harder."

### Who she is
Greta Holm has kept the pebble shrine at [[Hollowhall]]'s gate for sixty years, under the hundred carved Lithic faces, and is the hold-priestess of [[Tharos]] for the Underlook, which means she is the one who sat longest in silence when the old priest died and was given his bag. She keeps the stone-oaths, the mine-rites and the long list of the dead in the deep, which has got much shorter since 3004 and much stranger. On Wardday, when nothing is dug or built, she walks to the nearest standing Lithic, which for Hollowhall is the worn figure the Skarn call the Moot Stone, three days away, or the gate-faces, which are nearer and do not blink, and sits with it.

She is also the last priest in the Grimmark who knows the Standing rite entire: the words and the sitting the Lithic taught the first carvers in 412, for a Lithic who has walked to a ward and is about to stop. The surface clans revere the Lithic as ancestors made stone and tend every statue they find; the rite is the reason. Nobody has asked her to say it in forty years, because no Lithic has come to the Grimmark to Stand in forty years, because there are so few Lithic left that the ones who remain go to the wards and not to the dwarves. She has it anyway. She says it to herself on Wardday so as not to lose the order of it.

Her granddaughter is [[Gerda Hollin]], corporal of a tally at the Lantern Keep in Cinderford, who writes home every quarter and whose letters since the spring have all been about a Returned recruit with no shadow who makes the sign of Tharos back at her.

### What she wants
For the Returned to stop standing at the shrine. For Tharos to answer, which she has stopped expecting. For somebody, before she dies, to need the rite for what it is for.

### What she knows
The rite, entire. That a Lithic who Stands at a ward strengthens it, and that fewer are born each generation, and what that arithmetic means, which she has never said aloud. That the Returned stand at Tharos's shrines and do not pray because they are waiting for a door, and that Tharos has never once opened one. That the Deep Stone is under her feet and the Stair That Waits is shut from below, which she knows because she has put her hand on the stone of the lowest hall on Wardday for sixty years and it has been warm since 3004.

### How she treats the PCs
As Gerda's, if they are from the Ninth Lantern, which means she already knows their names and their faults. She is grave, unhurried, kind in the way a wall is kind, and she asks each of them one question and waits. A surface-dwarf PC she treats as kin and tests. A Returned PC she looks at straight, which almost nobody does, and asks whether it was warm. A Vesh she asks what the stone looks like to them. She will come south if asked. Nobody has asked.

:::gm
**Secret.** She has said the rite once, for a dwarf, not a Lithic: [[Durn Mallow]], the Returned key-smith, in Leafrot 3010, who came up to the gate after his three-hundredth key and asked her for it because he wanted to stop. She said it, all of it, through the night, with her hand on his head, because he asked and because a hold-priest does not refuse the one rite she has. It did not take: a dwarf is not stone, and he walked back down to the forge in the morning. But she felt something *under* him answer the words, the way a bell answers a bell, and she has not slept well since. She will not say who. If the party learns it from Durn she will not deny it; she will ask what his keys do now.

**The rite and the Reseal.** The Standing rite is the Reseal ending's price said properly ([[Ending: Reseal the Ward]]): a heart that chooses, not one that is taken, and someone who sits with the giver until they stop and does not look away. [[Warden Uthrem]] knows it and is too far gone to say it for himself. If the party brings Greta to the Ninth Stone for session 8 (she needs a fortnight's warning and a barge or a horse, and will take either), she says it over whoever goes into the heart-socket, Uthrem, a PC or Ysse, and the seal holds: skip the epilogue's d6 for a seal that fails. If she is not there, Uthrem can manage the words and nobody the sitting, and the roll stands. She is the hold-priest the god-tharos page promises, "who knows the rites for a Lithic who is about to finish turning, and nobody has asked her to perform one in forty years".

**Borrin.** She sees what wears his shadow the moment he stands in the gate-light, and says nothing to him, and takes her granddaughter's hand if Gerda is there. Asked, she says: "It came up the Stair with him. The Stair's shut. So it didn't come *through*. It came *inside*." She knows the thing under the Eighth is not Tharos, and has known since 3004, and has told nobody, because the surface clans' whole faith is that it was.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** slow, stone on stone, no wasted words; she asks one question and waits, and the silence is the priesthood. Her humour is the dryness of someone who has sat with the gate-faces for sixty years and knows they are listening.

**Use.** By Gerda's letter from session 3 or 4, if a dwarf PC or Gerda asks what the Standing costs; in person for session 8 if the party fetches her, which is a strong late-arc move and worth a session's travel. Post-session 8 she is the one who can tell a party going after the Eighth what the rite will not do for a dwarf, and why Durn's keys are warm.
