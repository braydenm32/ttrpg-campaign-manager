### Look and voice
Eighty-one, which is old for a halfling and ancient for a bargewoman, small and brown and square as a cask, with white hair cropped to the skull and a face so lined it reads like a chart. Oiled wool, a stern-bench cushion sewn from forty flags, and round her neck on a cord a brass thimble worn through at the tip: her mother's, and the reason for the name. Her hands are the hands of someone who has hauled rope for seventy years and signed treaties with the same fingers. She sits at the stern of the *Sixpence* and does not get up for anyone, including Shellbacks, who respect it.

She speaks slowly for a halfling, which is to say at the speed of a human, and never asks a question she does not already know the answer to.

> "I carry the Concord's letters. I carry the Regency's. I carried the Mothers of the Iron's for thirty years, which nobody else would. I have never once opened one and I have never once not known what was in it. That's the trade."

> "Kettle Brindlewake is the best skipper on the Cinder and the worst halfling on the water, and she's mine, and if she hangs I'll cut her down myself and then I'll drown her."

> "Ask me one thing. I will answer it truthfully. Then you will have to be a good deal cleverer than you look, because I do not do it twice."

### Who she is
Thimble Fairlock is Mother of the Water of the Fairlock flotilla, forty-one boats on the Ardis from [[Sevenhaven]] to [[Ardmere]], and the senior of all the Mothers and Fathers of the Water on the river. It is she who sends out the call for the Quay-Moot each Turning, 7 Bloomtide, when every Mother on the continent who can reach Sevenhaven ties up at the post-stair under the causeway and the Clans decide, in a day, what the continent will know for the next year. [[The Barge Clans]] have no ruler. They have Thimble, which they say is better, because a ruler can be bought.

She cannot be. Not because she is virtuous: because she carries the Concord's letters, the Regency's, the Church's and the Mothers of the Iron's, and a courier who takes a side is a courier nobody uses, and the Clans would be sunk without the trade. She has kept that neutrality for sixty years with the ferocity of a woman defending a hull. [[Kettle Brindlewake]] is her junior, trained on her deck, and Barge-Mother Posy Reedmarsh of the Cinder's lower reach is her cousin and four years her elder; both report to the *Sixpence* at the Turning and neither tells her everything, which she knows, and allows.

Under her stern bench, in a lead tube, is the Clans' own copy of the Charter of the Open River, the paper that ended the Toll War in 1255 and gives every barge the right of passage on every river in Vael Ardith. She has shown it to three people. One was a Shellback. One was an emperor. One was Kettle, aged nine, who asked.

### What she wants
The rivers open, the tolls low, the letters moving. For the Clans to come out of the ash country's trouble owing nobody and owed by everyone, which is the only safe position on a river. And, since Ashfall, an answer to a question she has not asked anyone, because there is nobody above her to ask.

### What she knows
Everything that has gone up or down the Ardis in sixty years, and most of what was in it. That the Clans' neutrality rests on her never being seen to read what she carries, and on her never once being surprised by it.

### How she treats the PCs
As Kettle's, which means she has heard about them, which means she is already sure what they will cost. She is courteous, unhurried, and gives nothing away for free except the one thing she gives everyone: a true answer to a question honestly asked, once. Ask the right one. She likes soldiers who know what they are owed and sailors who know what they owe.

:::gm
**What she knows, in full.** The Concord bought the garrison's Regency paper the afternoon the Regency stopped paying, and Thimble knows because she carried the letters that did it, both ways, and knew what was in them. She knows which boats were at the Cinder's mouth in the first week of Ashfall, who hired drivers there, and what the drivers said in the Seventh Chair afterwards. A sergeant of the Ninth Lantern sends a letter north to the Grimmark every month; she knows who carries it, and has never asked what is in it, and does not need to.

**The lamp.** She keeps one quote for herself and does not give it to the table: "The Grandmother asked me to carry a lamp. Not a lantern. A *lamp*. In Leafrot. To Gilt. She wrote it the way you'd write *a barrel of eels*. I have not answered. I have never not answered a letter in my life."

**Secret.** On 20 Ashfall, by a Fairlock boat up from the Cinder, Thimble received a letter from [[Grandmother Tollamund]] under the house-mark, asking her to "carry a lamp, with its keeper if the keeper will come, from Cinderford in Leafrot, to the House's vault at Gilt, by water, unopened, and to name her fee". The letter did not say which lamp. It did not need to. Tollamund expects to own the Ninth Lantern in law from 5 Leafrot and has arranged its shipping: down the Cinder and the Ardis to Sevenhaven, nine days, then the coast to [[Gilt]], where her vault already holds a year of cold, humming Glasser crates from the Rent's edge. Thimble has not answered. It is the first letter in her life she has not answered, and the whole river knows something is wrong with the Mother because the *Sixpence* has not moved from the post-stair since.

She also knows, from the Fairlock boat moored at the Cinder's mouth in the first week of Ashfall, that the wagon's escort was paid off at the waystation two days west and that Tollamund's own drivers took it on, because the drivers were hired at the post-stair and talked; and she has worked out, from a year of Skarra Burnbrow's monthly letters north, who the letters are really about, and has carried them since without a word to anyone, including the Mothers.

She has not refused because refusing the Concord is taking a side. She has not accepted because the Fairlocks have carried the Mothers of the Iron's letters for thirty years and she knows what Grashka did to keep a ward shut, and because her own boats have refused the Gilt run since Ashfall: the Gilt Water, the skippers say, is still as a bowl and smells of hot stone, and the gulls will not land on it. She would like someone to make the decision for her. If the party asks her the question honestly (does carrying a thing take a side?), she will tell them what the letter said, and that is the Concord's plan for the lantern laid bare a fortnight before session 7.

**Posy and Kettle.** Kettle's free passage (session 7 or 8) is Thimble's boats as much as the *Grey Dumpling*'s; if the party needs to move the lantern, a person or themselves by water in those sessions, the Fairlocks can do it from Cinderford to Sevenhaven in nine days, and will, if the Mother says so, and the Mother has not said so, and is waiting to be asked.

**Post-session 8.** If the lantern is resealed and out, Thimble answers Tollamund's letter at last, in one line: *There is no lamp. Fee waived.* If the lantern is taken by anyone, the Fairlocks carry nothing for them, and the Clans follow the Mother, and the river closes to the lantern's holder for a season, which is the one thing on the continent the Concord cannot buy back.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** slow, square, certain; the only halfling in the setting who makes the players wait. She calls Kettle "that one". Her humour is dry and all about hanging. She is a treaty with a face: play the Charter in its lead tube as her holy book, and the unanswered letter as the one crack in sixty years of knowing exactly what to do.

**Use.** By letter through Kettle from session 4; in person at the post-stair after session 8, or in session 7 if the party goes downriver for help. A fine scene: the players ask her to carry the lantern to the Stone, and she says "That's not water", and then thinks, and then says what she will do instead.
