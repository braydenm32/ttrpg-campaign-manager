### Look and voice
Three hundred, tall, long-handed, and written over: from collarbone to fingertip his skin is a grey field of script in six hands, the oldest faded to the colour of the skin itself, the newest (his own, in the small square hand he teaches) still black. His face is bare, as all elves' are, and unreadable in the other sense: calm, courteous, attentive, with eyes that have read ten thousand skins and give nothing back. Sleeves rolled up at work, rolled down the moment he stops. He smells of oak-gall and lamp-oil. He has a way of taking a visitor's wrist to say good-day that is also, if you know the custom, asking permission to read.

He speaks in short sentences and repeats the last words of each, once, quietly, as a tutor drills a script: "The hand is old. Hand is old. You will learn it." The warmth is in the repeating; he never repeats a thing he does not mean you to keep.

> "I say it. You say it back. Then it is yours. That is the whole of the art. The needle is only ink."

> "There are two kinds of forgetting, child. The kind the century does to us. And the kind we did to ourselves. The first is grief. The second we called mercy. We were wrong about the second."

> "Aelith was my best pupil. I wrote that on her. I would like to have it back."

### Who he is
Caelo once-Mar is the Inkwright of [[The Inkwright's]], the old ink-house under the Archive of Skins at [[Ilmareth]], and has been for a hundred and ten years. He crossed his third century mark in the Saelwood's closing year and lost, as elves do, most of a hundred years; what survived he had already written on himself, which is the trade. He is a tutor of Readers, which is the highest thing an Inkwright can be, and half the Readers in the Archive learned the old scripts with their hand on his wrist.

Two people matter to him in the March. A century and a half ago, a quick, gracious Unlettered called [[Aelith Sorn]] learned the pre-1200 hands at his couch, and he wrote her Reader's lines himself and told her she was the best he had taught. And eighty years ago a young priestess of [[Isaurana]] called [[Sorrel Vey]] came to his room with her back covered in a script her dead grandmother had taught her to call decorative, and asked him to read it. He told her he could not. She left Sael soon after, and he has never said what passed. Both women are in [[Cinderford]] now.

### What he wants
The record copied, not taken: a thing read aloud and set down on parchment may be kept, and a thing cut out of someone is only stolen. He has spent his life inking what people wanted kept and watching the century take the rest. He wants Aelith to come home before she becomes what the Circle sent her to be. He is three hundred and he is not sure he has time for that.

### What he knows
The old scripts, every hand before 1200, which almost nobody else alive can read. Every Reader in the Archive by their first line. That "there was a fire" is the Remembrancers' way of ending a conversation. What Sorrel Vey's back looks like, because he saw it once, eighty years ago, and has never said what he saw.

### How he treats the PCs
With the Inkwright's courtesy, which is total and tells you nothing. He takes wrists. He asks an elf PC whether their third tattoo has ever been read; he asks a human what they would want kept if they had only one line; he asks anyone from the March, after the second question, whether Madame Vey is well, in a voice that is suddenly not the Inkwright's at all. If they are Aelith's friends he is kind to them and watches. If they are Sorrel's he will tell them, eventually, in his room with the door shut, what he did eighty years ago, and ask them to help him do the rest.

:::gm
**Secret.** He can read all of it, and he carries it: the whole of Sorrel's back is on the inside of his own left arm, in a cipher of his own, under his sleeve, *We came because called*, the eleven names, the Fourth Ward's sign and the rest, and he has told no one. He knows the Archive's empty racks between 1050 and 1200 held [[The Saelish Trespass]], that the Circle lit the fire, and that not every skin burned; he knows the Saelish black burns at the red moon because the Old Tree's sap is in it and the Old Tree is the Fourth Ward's dressing, and that the Circle would not stop using it if told. Eighty years ago he read Sorrel's back entire with her hand on his wrist, recognised the Fourth Ward's sign and the Trespass dead, and understood in one night what Sael had done and that the girl in front of him was carrying the only copy. He told her he could not read it. He told her the script was very old, that the Circle would want to see it, and that she should leave Sael by the coast road before the Circle asked, and he gave her the name of a Shellback pilot. That is why Sorrel Vey left Sael eighty years ago and never went back, and she has never known it was a kindness; she thinks he was embarrassed. He has lied to the Circle about her ink in every census since, which for an Inkwright is the one unforgivable thing, and he would do it again.

**What he wants, in full.** Sael chose to forget the Trespass, and Caelo, who was born three centuries after, has come to believe that a people who burned their own memory have no right to cut it out of someone else's back to get it back. He wants Sorrel alive and willing in his room, her hand on his wrist, and every line of her read aloud and set on parchment, and then on skin that is nobody's, and then given to the Circle to do with as it dares. Under that, he wants Aelith home. One of his two pupils is hunting the other, and the thing being hunted is the one skin Caelo has ever lied about.

When the empty rack's label came down from the Circle in Thawmere, unsigned, Caelo wrote it, as ordered, in the Archive hand, and then went to the Unlettered and drank green wine until the branch healed. Aelith, then six years in Caer Ardith, was on the road within the week. He knows what Aelith's lead case is for, because the Circle's note asked him to specify the salt and the lining, and he did, because an Inkwright who refuses an order is replaced by one who does not ask why.

**Post-session 8.** If Sorrel comes to Ilmareth alive, Caelo reads her in his room and copies her onto parchment and then onto a clean skin, and Sael gets the Trespass back without a cut; Caelo then hands the copy to the Circle and his own left arm with it, and is expelled, and goes to live in the Unlettered, and does not mind. If Aelith comes home with the lead case and what is in it, Caelo reads that too, because he must, and then he puts his needles down and does not pick them up again, and the Circle has its memory and no Inkwright. If Sorrel burned her own skin in session 8, as her page allows, Caelo's left arm is the last copy, and he has to decide whether to show it, and the players can be the ones he asks.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** short sentences, the last words said twice, quietly, as a tutor drills a script; takes wrists; never raises his voice; the warmth is in what he asks, not how. His humour is dry and about forgetting ("I would remember that, if I remembered things. Remembered things.").

**Use.** A post-session-8 face for Ilmareth, and a letter before that: in session 5 or 6, if Sorrel is threatened, a note reaches the Velvet Ledger by Kettle's barge in an old square hand: *I could read it. I am sorry. Come home, or do not; but do not let her cut it. C.* Sorrel reads it and does not understand for a day, and then does. That is the one moment Caelo gets to be in the campaign before it ends, and it should land.
