### Look and voice
A round, red-faced woman of sixty in a brown apron over a brown robe, forearms scalded pink from forty years of kettles, grey hair under a cloth. She moves at one speed, which is steady, and talks at one volume, which is kind. The hearth-house is a room with a fire, a kettle the size of a font, a shelf of bowls and a bench, and she is never more than two steps from the ladle. On Hearthday she bakes, and the Row smells of it.

> "Sit. Eat. Then tell me. Nobody ever told me anything true on an empty stomach."

> "Maelis doesn't want your prayers, love. She wants you to wash the bowl after."

### Who she is
Mother Brisk has kept the hearth-house of [[Maelis]] on [[Temple Row]] since the Row was two shrines and a mud lane. She came east with the refugees in 3003, found the old hearth-mother's kettle in the ash where the village chapel had stood, scoured it, and lit a fire under it, and has not let the fire out since. Soup for anyone, no questions, one bowl, wash it after. Half the Smokes eats here once a week. The company's recruits eat here when the Keep's rations thin, and she does not tell Fennick.

She is also, by town custom, the keeper of the birth-rolls: every child born in Cinderford since the rebuilding is written in a book she keeps in a chest under the kettle-shelf, and anyone who stirs the soup may read it.

### What she wants
Everyone fed. Everyone home. The crack left alone, which she says plainly to any priest on the Row who starts talking about mercy, and does.

### What she knows
Who is hungry, which is to say who is in trouble, before anyone else in town. The names and dates of every child born here in eight years, and which six were born the night of Cinders. That [[Ottilie Marlowe]] has been writing heights in the margin beside those six every red moon, and that the heights have stopped. That every other priest on the Row has started saying the same thing about the Rent in different words, and she does not like it.

### How she treats the PCs
As mouths first and soldiers second. She feeds them, tells them to come back when they are hungry rather than when they have questions, and answers the questions anyway while they eat. [[Tibbet Marlowe]] she has fed since he could hold a bowl; she calls him "the fourth" when she forgets and "Tibbet" when she remembers.

:::gm
**The rolls.** The chest under the kettle-shelf holds the town birth-rolls from Seedwake 3003 and, copied in at the front from Ottilie's hand, the births of the war years. Under 14 Ashfall 3002: six names, and Tick's is the fourth. In the margin beside each of the six, a column of heights in Ottilie's hand, dated at every full Ember, climbing in steps and stopping two years ago (see [[Why did the Cinders-born grow up so fast?]]). Brisk has read the column and understood none of it and all of it. She shows the book to anyone who stirs the soup; she talks about the six only to Ottilie, Tick, or someone Tick brings.

**Session 8.** She is among the priests at the lip, the one without a reason. She has walked sixty miles to say that Maelis wants the crack shut, and to feed whoever is left after. If the ending is Let It Crack, she is the last person on the lip still handing out bread as the dead turn round.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** steady, kind, unhurried, a little deaf when it suits her. Feeds first. Never raises her voice; lowers it instead.

Use her as the town's quiet counterweight to the Row: every other shrine is nudging toward the crack opening, and Brisk is the one who has noticed and says so. A party that has eaten her soup three times has a friend who will hide them in session 7.
