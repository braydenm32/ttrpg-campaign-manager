### Look and voice
Thirty-eight, lean, straight-backed, with a surveyor's squint and ink to the second knuckle of both hands. She wears the same grey coat in all weathers, with a pair of dividers in the breast pocket like a pen, and her hair cut short and square because she measured it once and decided it was a waste of an inch. She stands, when listening, with her boots exactly shoulder-width apart.

She speaks in plain figures. A mile is a mile, a pace is a pace, a lie is "about eleven paces out", and she says so to your face, quite kindly, as if telling you the time.

> "It is six hundred and eleven paces from the West Gate to the square, and the goat adds four. I have counted them. You may count them too, but I would rather you did not."

> "No, I will not draw you a chart of the Barrow. A chart of a place nobody has seen whole is a lie with a border round it. I have a great respect for borders. I do not lie within them."

### Who she is
Daughter of Helmar Quist, the Regency commander who held Ardmere for 211 days against Ostrey's Free Host and whose weekly letters for relief were answered with forms. She grew up in his barracks, learned to chain and level before she could read, and followed him to the March in 3003 as a survey-hand to the treaty commissioners. When they went home, he stayed; when he died, in the winter of 3009, she stayed. She measured the Rent for the company four times and cut the figure on the slate at its western end each time. The company can no longer pay her. The slate for 3011 is blank, and she thinks about it as another woman might think about an unpaid debt to a friend.

She is not warm. She is reliable. The two are different, and she knows which she was built for.

### What she wants
To measure the Rent a fifth time before the figure changes again, which it will. To be paid for it, though that is a lesser matter. To put her father's papers into the right hands.

### What she knows
The ground: every stake on the Ash Road, every boundary in town, and the shape of the Rent from the west end to the Stone, better than any Glasser. That the Rent is growing, and that she is the only one who has the figures to say how fast.

### How she treats the PCs
As survey-hands: she assigns, measures, corrects and, at the end of the day, tells them to the pace how far they came. A PC who counts steps gets her respect. A PC who lies gets the figure. She will hire an escort to the cairn for 3 gp and a copy of the sheet, and hold to the bargain to the letter.

:::gm
**Secret.** She holds **Helmar Quist's ledger** of promises broken at Ardmere (see [[Quist & Daughter, Surveyors]]). It names Ilse Marrow, as the clerk who told Quist the truth about the stores on day ninety and was not believed, and the Long Spoons, who were promised forty acres a head on the Ardmere bend. Spoons was their pot-boy. Orla has not told either of them, because she does not know whether they would thank her. If asked straight by either, she gives it up, and tells the truth about how she read it.

She knows the Rent is accelerating: 29, 30, 30, 31, and her own pacing last winter of the near cairn says *at least thirty-three*, which is the Glassers' figure, which she called a lie in print. She is afraid of being right.

If the party escorts her to the cairn she measures 33 and says nothing for a mile, then says, "I owe a Glasser an apology," and hands the party the sheet.
:::

== GM notes ==

A good second "specialist" NPC: someone who can say a thing is true because she measured it. Her ledger is a late-game gift, more than a clue: a way to give Marrow or Spoons a quiet scene in the Ardmere past before session 7's crisis. If a PC is Spoons's friend or Marrow's, put Orla in the way in session 4. Her physical comedy is that she stands too straight; her moral weight is that she is the one person in Cinderford who has never made a number say what it wanted.
