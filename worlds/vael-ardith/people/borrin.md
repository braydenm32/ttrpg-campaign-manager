### Who he is
Borrin Halt went down under the Grimmark three and a half years ago with a deep-road party out of [[Skarn]], to look for the kin who had gone under in the war and were not among the first Returned to come back up. He remembers the first week. He remembers coming up through the gate of [[Hollowhall]] six months ago, blinking at a sun he hadn't seen in three years. He does not remember anything in between, and he casts no shadow.

The highland folk have a name for dwarves like him: [[the Returned]]. In the dwarf way a Returned takes a second name, and his is the one the soldiers gave him: **Borrin Halt Shadowless**. He answers to it, because he has stopped expecting better. To the company, he's a wick like any other.

### Look
Broad and short and weathered, beard cut close (Returned dwarves often cut their beards; nobody knows why, and they don't say), with a pale band across his eyes as though he'd worn a blindfold a long time. Grey-green eyes that don't blink quite enough. A longbow of black yew that he did not take down with him and cannot explain. In sunlight he stands in the middle of a lit yard and the ground around his boots is simply bright, all the way round. Now and then, in lantern-light, something dark follows him a beat late, and not always where he walks.

### Manner
Patient, watchful, dry. Counts things: steps, coins, people in a room, exits. Sleeps with his back to a wall and wakes before anyone else. Has a habit of stopping mid-sentence as if listening to someone speak behind him.

> "It's a good wall, that. Thick. Dry. Nothing's been through it since... No. Sorry. Where was I?"

> "Bastard would have liked you. He liked nobody. It's the highest compliment I know." Surface dwarves make the sign of [[Tharos]] when he passes; he has started making it back, which annoys them.

### Background
He was a hunter and pathfinder in the high passes before the war, a good one, with a wife in [[Skarn]] and a dog called Bastard. When he came back, the wife had remarried (she had held his funeral, which he attended, eventually, and stood at the back of) and the dog had died. The clan was polite. Politeness among dwarves is a kind of exile.

He drifted south, taking scout work with caravans, until a caravan master told him that in the [[The Ashenreach|Ashenreach]] nobody minds what you are as long as you can find water. That was nearly true.

### Why you're in Cinderford
The Ninth Lantern needed scouts who could read the ash and see in the dark. He needed somewhere that didn't look at his feet. And there is a dream he keeps having, of a stone with a crack in it and a light at its lip. The first time he saw [[Lantern Hill]] from the road, he knew the light.

### Ties
- **[[Varra Nine-Knuckles]]** signed the roll the same morning. Both outcast from their peoples; neither asks about it. She is the first person in six months to sit beside him without glancing down.
- **[[Quorl Many-Words]]** wants Borrin's last words recorded now, "while you've still got all of them". Borrin keeps asking him, half joking, whether a Raveler can tell if a man is alive.
- **[[Ysse Lampwright]]** looks at the ground near him too long when she thinks he isn't watching.
- **[[Kettle Brindlewake]]** buys his trail maps of the glasslands and pays fair.
- At [[The Shadowless Cup]] in Hollowhall a place is set every night for the Skarn party he went down with. Two bowls have come off it. One was his.

### Playing him
A man who lost three years and part of himself and is trying to live in the time he has left. Quiet competence, gallows humour, and the growing suspicion that something remembers those three years for him.

:::for Borrin Halt
**Your secret.** Something followed you up. You know it the way you know someone is standing behind you in a dark room. Three times now, soldiers have told you they saw you somewhere you weren't: Gerda Hollin at the rail of the ford, looking down into the water; Jory Dunmore on the east wall at night; Noume Flarewick, from the signal tower over the Smokes, standing in the street below. Once you woke with ash on your boots and no memory of walking.

You think it is your shadow. You think it is wearing your shadow, the way you'd wear a borrowed coat. And when you dream of the stone with the crack in it, you are not the one doing the dreaming.
:::

:::gm
**How his secret pays off.**
- Under the Grimmark the deep-road party reached the breached Eighth Ward. Something there took their shadows as the price of passage. Borrin's was taken by a thing that wanted to come up, and it did, inside the shadow: a door-warden of the Eighth Ward. It is not evil exactly; it is curious, hungry, and very old, and it wants to reach the Ninth Ward's crack to see what's on the other side.
- Plant sightings: [[Gerda Hollin]] at the ford (she says it aloud in session 1); [[Jory Dunmore]] on the east wall; [[Noume Flarewick]] from the signal tower; old [[Nan Crupp]] at the ferry saw "Borrin" standing on the ford at midnight a week before session 1, while he was in his bunk; [[Mardle Ock]], the Returned dwarf at Fort Mercy, swears he met Borrin in the deep "two winters back" (session 2); and [[Tam Hollowell]], a Returned mason of the Smokes who went down in the same party, is the second witness and the better one: he knows it was not Borrin, and he cannot be in a room with him.
- At **Into the Rent** (session 6), the shadow acts on its own at the foot of the Ninth Stone as the party turns to leave: it stands up, bows to the Stone, and says "Not yet." [[Warden Uthrem]], who looked past Borrin once on their arrival and said nothing, names it and is afraid of it: a door-warden of the Eighth Ward, one of the forgotten.
- In the finale, the shadow can be bargained with, fought, or sent back through the crack, and if Borrin wants it back he may get it, changed.
:::

== GM notes ==

Never describe Borrin's missing shadow as a gimmick: describe what it does to everyone else. Lanterns throw the party's shadows long across the ash, four of them. Let the players count. When the shadow is off doing something, give the GM a quiet beat: "Borrin, roll a Wisdom save. You don't need to know why."
