### Look and voice
Thirty, pale, tall for a clerk, with the Regency's long nose and ink on the inside of her right wrist where the cuff rides. Brown hair pinned flat, a black coat two grades too good for her because it was her mother's, a Treasury-blue ribbon on her satchel that she touches when she lies, which is why she does not lie. She writes a hand so clean that the Office uses her for the notices that will be read aloud, and that is how her name came to be on a sheet pinned to a Keep gate three hundred miles east.

She speaks fast and low, with the sentence already finished in her head, and stops dead if anyone in a good coat comes within three tables.

> "I signed it. It was my hand. It said nought, and I wrote nought, and the form said to keep a copy, so I kept a copy. That is all I did. That is the whole of what I did."

> "Aunt Hester writes that the Assessor is a good man. She has never called anyone a good man in her life. I had to read it twice."

> "You don't understand how the Hall works. Nothing is stolen. Everything is *filed*."

### Who she is
Edda Haverleigh signs the quarterly pay notice to the Company of the Ninth Lantern: the sheet that arrives at [[Cinderford]] by post-rider, under the Regent-Chancellor's seal, and says 0 silver in four places ([[The Regency Stops Paying]]). She has signed eleven of them. She was Fourth Grade on the first of Hollowfrost 3009 and Third Grade by the sixth, because the Third Grade clerk above her was found a post in the Marches, and Edda, who was told nothing and asked nothing, was given his desk and his pen. She has been afraid every Coinday since.

She is [[Hester Pruitt|Widow Pruitt]]'s niece, her dead mother's sister's girl, and the two write monthly: Pruitt's letters ask the capital for help and get forms; Edda's letters say the forms are coming and ask after the Assessor's health. Neither has ever written the word "deferred". Both know the other knows.

### What she wants
To have done nothing wrong, which is the one thing she can no longer have. Failing that, to give what she knows to someone who will not get her sent to the Marches for it, and to see her aunt, whom she has not seen since she was nineteen, before one of them is posted somewhere worse.

### What she knows
The notices, all eleven, and the ledger they are drawn from. What "arrears deferred" means to the Treasury, which is not what it means in [[Cinderford]]. That the Concord's factors began buying the garrison's unpaid paper at forty in the hundred the afternoon the first notice went out, which she thinks cannot be a coincidence and cannot prove is not.

She knows [[Pell Quillon]]'s reports, which come in by the same post that takes the notices out, and which nobody above her reads.

### How she treats the PCs
Like a woman being handed a knife she has been looking for and did not want to find. If they are from the March she is frightened of them and cannot stop asking questions: is her aunt eating, is the Assessor really as kind as the letters say, is it true about the lantern. She will not be seen with them in the Hall. At [[The Nine Generations]], at the end of the long table, after the second cup, she will talk, if they talk first and nobody in a good coat is near.

:::gm
**The mechanism.** Every quarter the Council votes the Ninth Lantern's wages in full, in open session, and minutes the vote; the notice still says nought, and nobody in the Hall has ever asked Edda how both can be true. The Treasury signs the sum out of the Martial account, and Edda's office draws the notice. Since Hollowfrost 3009 the sum has been signed out "to Account Deferred, by order of the Regent-Chancellor", and the notice has said nought. Account Deferred is not a strongroom. It is a ledger line, numbered, in the Chair's own office, countersigned each quarter by the Chair's private secretary, and it has taken in eleven quarters of a garrison's wages and paid out, to the Treasury's knowledge, nothing. This is what [[Pell Quillon]] finds in sessions 4 and 5; Edda has known it since the sixth of Hollowfrost 3009 and has told nobody, because the last clerk who asked was posted to the Marches.

**Secret.** The notice form, 7-N, instructs the signing clerk to "retain a true copy for the Office". Edda has done so, every quarter, because the form said to, and has kept them not in the Office but in the lining of her mother's coat, because the Office's copies are collected monthly by a courier from the Chair's office and she has never seen one again. Her eleven copies carry what the garrison's notice does not: the Treasury's own counterfoil, with the sum voted (the whole of the ranks' and officers' wages on the notice, near six hundred gold a quarter), the account it was signed out to (Account Deferred, Office of the Chair, ledger line 44-A-9, the number is a joke somebody in the Chair's office made and nobody caught), and the countersignature of Vell's private secretary. It is the paper that proves, in the Regency's own hand, that the Ninth Lantern's pay was voted, drawn and diverted to the Regent-Chancellor's office every quarter for nearly three years.

Shown to Pell, it turns his Claim of Arrears from a brave gesture into a case. And in the weeks after session 8 a very different paper is drawn at her desk, a "pardon list" for the deserters of 3002 with four names on it, one of which was never a soldier ([[Who is on the Crownfast pardon list?]]); she copies that too, because the form says to. Shown to a Concord lawyer on 5 Leafrot (session 7) it proves the Regency defaulted on purpose, which is not a defence against the note but is a very good reason for the Treaty's other signatories to be angry.

**Danger.** The courier from the Chair's office who collects the Office's copies is the Quiet Office. If Edda is seen with the party, or if the party asks after Account Deferred by name at the Hall, she is posted to the Marches within the week, which is to say she disappears on the road. Tick the Quiet Office clock. If the party gets her out of the capital (Kettle's barges go up as well as down), she turns up at the Assessor's Office with her coat and her aunt is, for the first time anyone can remember, speechless.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** quick, low, exact, with the sentence already finished; she touches the blue ribbon when she is about to lie and then does not lie. Not a coward: a clerk, which is braver and slower.

**Use.** Edda is a post-session-8 face for Caer Ardith, and a letter before that. If the party writes to her through Pruitt (sessions 4 to 7), her reply is three lines about the weather and a copy of the counterfoil folded inside the second sheet, unsigned. If they go to the capital, she is at the end of the long table under the Hall, and the scene is the two of them looking at each other across a bare table while a well-shaved man buys the next grade a round. She and Orrin Mallory have never spoken; they drink in the same room every night. Introduce them and the Regency has a problem.
