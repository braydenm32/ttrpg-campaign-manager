### Look and voice
Broad, sun-browned, floury to the elbow and cheesy to the other, with white hair under a grey kerchief and a face that has had a great many conversations with a great many officers and won them. Sixty. Eyes like a hawk's, with a countrywoman's patience. She wears a loop of cord on her left wrist, knotted, which she touches when she talks about her brother and does not otherwise.

She sells cheese like a woman handing out judgements. She is dry, direct, and does not like being thanked.

> "He gave it up. I'm not giving it back."

> "Prayers? About one in five answered. Which is better than the Regency's record, and I've seen both."

> "The rope's new, love. A copper a strand. I'll tell you it's new and I'll tell you it works, and you may decide which part to believe."

### Who she is
Mab Lennard is the elder sister of a cheesemaker's son who put down a torch on 12 Frostcall 2996. She was forty-five. She walked at the back of the line of eleven old people and forty children that went out of the east gate past the Tree on the day her brother was hanged, counting heads, and she is the last of the grown ones who walked it. She kept the cheese-farm, rebuilt it on its own ashes, and keeps the shrine at the Tree. She sells a strand of rope ([[A Strand of the Corporal's Rope]]) to every pilgrim for a copper, from a basket at the shop counter, and says that it is new rope, because it is, and that the real one is "in the Candle", which is a lie and a joke, and she tells both.

The soldiers call her brother Dask, or Hob, or Little Fenn. She has never called him anything but his name, and has not said it aloud since the day, and will not. "He gave it up. I'm not giving it back." She hears her brother's prayers answered, in her estimate, about one in five.

### What she wants
For the village to be left alone to remember properly. For the Regency to stop banning rope. For her brother to be a soldier's saint and not a joke. For somebody to say what happened in the four hours.

### What she knows
The Dunmeer story, whole. The roll of the forty, one by one, in the order they walked. Who comes to the Tree and what they want. That the levy marched under the Salt Guard; that the lieutenant demanded a rope; that a young clerk brought tent-line, and the line broke, and a second coil, and that broke too. She remembers the clerk's face. She has thought about it for fifteen years.

### How she treats the PCs
As customers, kindly, and as soldiers' children, more kindly. She looks at their boots, and their wrists, and what they carry, and asks nothing. She will give a strand free to a soldier who says "No, sir" with a straight face. She will sell a cheese to a Regency officer and give him change in a very small coin.

:::gm
**Secret.** The first rope was the clerk's. The Salt Guard's supply clerk, a young woman with a quill behind her ear and a very white face, brought the lieutenant tent-line from the worst coil in her stores, rotten with years of damp. It broke. A second coil: it broke too. The third rope was a halter taken from the Lennard byre, and Mab handed it over herself, because the line had broken twice and the children were watching, and she wanted it over. She has never forgiven herself. She has never forgiven the clerk either, and does not know whether to. She knew the line was rotten at the first touch, and saw the clerk's face when it broke the second time: a face of someone who had meant it and was afraid of what the meaning had done.

The clerk was **Ilse Marrow**. She does not know what her four hours bought. Mab has never told anyone it was the Ninth Lantern's captain, because Mab does not know; she knows only the face, and has never seen the Ninth's captain. If Marrow ever comes to Dunmeer (post-S8, Rope Day) Mab will see it at once. "It's you." "It is." "Come in. I've cheese."

**His first name.** She will give it, once, to a person who has said no to an order at real cost and can say what it cost. It is **Hamon**. The Ninth's "Dask" comes from nowhere she can trace. The orc companies' "Hob" is a corruption of nothing. She finds it all very funny.

**Optional prop.** The halter went back to the Salt Guard's stores with the levy, logged by a clerk. Marrow kept it. It is in a chest in her room at the Keep, with her mother's shawl. She has never been able to say why.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** dry, countrywoman, decisive. Hand-gestures with a cheese-knife. She is neither a mourner nor a keeper of a flame; she is a woman who runs a shop and has outlived a war. Make the grief practical.
