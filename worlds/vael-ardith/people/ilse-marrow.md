### Look and voice
Forty-four, lean as a fence post, with grey-shot hair cropped short so it won't need doing and a quartermaster's ink stains that never quite washed out. She wears a good officer's coat, brushed every morning, patched at both elbows. Reading spectacles she takes off whenever anyone comes in, and loses daily. Round her neck on a cord, the second of the three keys to the lantern's cage.

She never raises her voice; she lowers it, and a drill yard goes quiet to catch it. It is dry, and very tired.

> "Captain? By attrition. The first three died of an arrow, the flux and a horse. I was the last one left who could count. Turns out that's the job."

> "I'm not asking you to die for the company. I'm asking you to be on time. The dying is optional."

### Who she is
Ilse Marrow was a supply clerk with the Salt Guard, one of the eight companies that swore to hold Cinderford's walls in 3002. Eight years before that, aged twenty-seven, she was a mercenary supply clerk inside the walls of Ardmere for the whole of the siege, counting out what was left of the grain; she has never again thrown away a crust, and she remembers the first hot pot that came through the water-gate on the last day better than she remembers the faces. Her company's lantern went dark in the second hour of [[The Battle of Cinders]]. She walked out of the burning with a ledger under one arm and a wounded boy under the other, and found herself among the cooks and deserters who lit the ninth lantern. She has been with [[The Ninth Lantern]] ever since, and its captain since 3007.

### What she wants
Her people fed, paid if possible, alive if not. A winter without a funeral. A contract that means something. She would very much like an evening off.

### What she knows
The real state of the company's money, which is none. That the Regency has paid nothing since Hollowfrost 3009 ([[The Regency Stops Paying]]); that [[Tollamund & Daughters]]' credit line kept the company in rations and powder after that; and that the Concord has been buying up the debt. That [[Garrick Sallow]] wants her job. That the lantern matters more than anyone in the company understands, though she couldn't say why; she just knows she sleeps worse when it flickers.

### How she treats the PCs
New, which means not yet in anyone's pocket, which means useful. She is blunt with them, fair, and quietly kind: she learns their names on day one and uses them. She gives them the jobs she can't trust to anyone with a history: following the drowned man, chasing the pay, watching the oil.

:::gm
**Secret.** For the last year Marrow has paid the company out of her own savings, coin by coin, everything she had put by for a farm she was never going to buy. It ran out in Reaping. The day the pay doesn't come is the first day she has nothing left to cover it with. She has told no one; she thinks not even [[Fennick Bottlebrook]] knows. Fennick noticed by arithmetic and has been hiding it in the books.

She is also the lover of [[Madame Vey]] of [[The Velvet Ledger]]. The whole town knows the Captain uses the Ledger's back stair and pretends not to; most assume it's business, or comfort. It is love. It began as a negotiation for the company's credit and stopped being one very quickly. Sorrel is the only person she lets see her tired. She does not know what Sorrel's tattoos record, or how much of what she tells Sorrel goes into the Ledger's book. Nor does she know that the anonymous "friend of the company" credit in Fennick's books every Coinday is Sorrel's, after Marrow refused her money twice.

She was at Dunmeer in 2996 with the Salt Guard, the winter of the Hanged Corporal, and does not speak of it. There she issued the lieutenant's rope: tent-line from her worst coil, twice, and it broke twice (the first time on purpose). Those four hours were the hours [[Dunmeer]] used to walk its children out. She has never learned what they bought, and she keeps the halter that was finally used in a chest in her room. [[Mab Lennard]] remembers her face. If Marrow is told, post-S8 on Rope Day, give them the table to themselves.

Two of the standing in [[Ambercote]]'s square wear Salt Guard tabards: Corwen Hale and a boy called Pettit, both of her own supply detail, listed by the Salt Guard as dead on the wall. Shown their faces she names them after a long silence and asks for them to go in the ledger as Salt Guard, not as deserters. She holds the **second cage key**, and her mother's line is one of the ward-sensitive bloodlines: she feels the cage's Rent-facing side run warm when no one else does, and has never told anyone that either.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** short sentences; dry; she thinks in numbers ("Forty-one rations, a hundred and forty mouths"). Takes off her spectacles to deliver bad news.

**Across the arc:**
1. **Payday**: announces the pay hasn't come; holds the yard by force of will; sends the PCs after the drowned man.
2. **The Assessor**: has to host [[Pell Quillon]] while he taxes her unpaid company; sends the PCs after the pay wagon.
3. **Red Moon Rising**: the lantern gutters and [[Brother Tallow]] falls; she stands on the wall all night and doesn't sleep.
4. **The House of Unburnt Light**: [[Sister Seraphine]] courts her openly and offers the Saint's coin for the flame. She is tempted, badly. If the PCs learn about Sorrel, they can use it, or protect it.
5. **Last Words**: learns where the lantern was really lit; starts to understand what she's guarding.
6. **Into the Rent**: stays behind to hold the town.
7. **Three Knives**: Sallow's mutiny breaks. She will be deposed, locked up or killed unless the PCs act. If she falls, Vosk or a PC takes command.
8. **The Lantern Gutters**: the contract says the company ends if the lantern goes out. She will ask the PCs to choose the ending knowing it may end her company. She will not order them.
