### Look and voice
Forty, lean, brown and dry as the plateau, with the Saint's flame on a cord at his throat like every Halcyran and a shiny burn across the back of his left hand from the coals, which he does not hide. Black hair going grey at the sides, a face that was handsome before it got tired, and a voice worn down to a rasp by twenty tellings a day for four years. An apron of oiled cloth with lamp-soot on it. He sits on a stool behind the trestle with the Steps going up behind him into the light and he never, in the whole of the story, turns round to look at them.

He speaks the plateau's way, soft-ended, and tells the story in the same words every time, with one difference the second time.

> "She was a camp healer. Twenty-five. Plain as bread, my brother said, and he said it like a man saying *gold*. She went into the fire for three dying men and came out with all three, and her hair wasn't singed. That's the story. That's the whole of the story. Two pence."

> "Mother, sister, lover. All three up there, behind the lattice, in the light. None of them has written. She doesn't need letters, the Synod says. Well. *They* might."

> "The unburnt wick? One shilling. Don't ask me if it's true. Ask me what it's for."

### Who he is
Dovan Ilm keeps [[The Lamp at the Foot]], the lamp-stall where the Thousand Steps meet the square at [[Halcyra]], as his father and grandfather did, and tells the Saint's story to everyone who buys a lamp, which is everyone, because nobody climbs the Steps without one. He is good at it. He has told it perhaps thirty thousand times. The Synod does not require the story; the plateau expects it, the way it expects the lamps, and a Lamp-Warden stands in the square every day to be sure he tells it right.

His brother Tamas was [[Ilyra of Halcyra]]'s lover before she was anyone's saint: a potter's son and a healer's apprentice, two years older than she was, who followed the companies east with her in 3002 as a stretcher-bearer and came home without her, and waited. For five years she prayed on the plateau, unseen, and he kept her room over the stall and sold lamps beside Dovan and did not marry. On 1 Highsun 3007, the morning the Synod declared her, Tamas climbed the Steps with a white lamp and went in at the Lamp Court's gate with her mother and her sister, and the gate shut, and no one of the three has written since. The Synod says the Saint's household does not need letters. Dovan sells lamps under the Steps his brother went up.

### What he wants
A letter. Any letter. Failing that, to know whether what went in at the gate in 3007 is still his brother, which is the question the second telling of the story is for: he tells it the second time with Tamas in it, and watches the buyer's face, and has learned to tell grief from the Synod by what the face does at the name.

He would also, if anyone asked, like to stop selling the unburnt wick.

### What he knows
The story, both versions. The Steps: every Vesh on them, every landing, which lamps gutter in a west wind and which Wardens look away. Who buys lamps and who does not: the Synod's people never do, and a pilgrim who does not weep at the story is either plateau-born or watching him. That [[Seraphine Ashe]] bought a white lamp here the morning she left on foot in 3008 and listened to the whole story and said "I know", and that she had the look the envoys get, the one that does not fade, before she had ever gone west. That [[Brother Iosune]] bought a red lamp here at fourteen with hands that shook, and burned on the coals the next year, and went west anyway.

### How he treats the PCs
As buyers, first. Two pence, the story, the next. If they come back for a second lamp he looks at them properly and tells it with Tamas in it and watches. If they are from the March of Cinders he will ask, in the plateau's soft way, whether it is true the lamp there is failing, and whether the Saint's envoy is well, and whether she still says "I know" before people have finished. If they stay to the small hours, when the square is lamps and nothing else, he will sit on the bench and talk about his brother the way a man talks about a limb.

:::gm
**Secret.** There has been one letter. In Emberwane 3009 a Vesh lamp-keeper dismissed from the Lamp Court's own lattice-lamps came down the Steps at night, bought a lamp, and put a folded paper under it: a half-sheet in Tamas's hand, which Dovan would know anywhere, with six words on it and no name. *She is not hungry yet. Tamas.* Nothing else. Dovan keeps it in the lining of his apron and has shown it to nobody, because the Vesh who carried it was found at the foot of the Steps the next morning with her neck broken and the Synod called it a fall. He does not know what "hungry" means. The GM does: Ilyra is ascending on dead Hathe's power and will finish on the Ninth Ward's leak ([[The Unburnt Saint]]), and the three people who knew her before are kept in the light behind the lattice as what she was, which she visits, and does not eat, yet. Tamas is alive. So are the mother and the sister. What they are *for* is the Church's secret and Ilyra's, and the letter is the only word out of the Lamp Court in nine years that was not the Synod's.

**The wick.** The unburnt wicks are real: cotton passed through the Saint's flame at the lattice by the Synod's own hands and not consumed, sold to the stall by the gross with instructions to charge a shilling and look doubtful, because a pilgrim who thinks he has bought a cheat and climbs anyway is a pilgrim who has chosen the Saint over his own good sense, and the Synod counts those. Dovan worked this out in his second year and has sold them since, looking at the ground, because the Lamp-Warden in the square counts his stock. A wick held near [[The Lantern of the Ninth]] catches at once and burns white; held near the House of Unburnt Light's altar-lamp it does not catch at all. That is a small, usable clue to what the two flames are.

**Post-session 8.** If the Ninth is resealed and the Saint's ascent stalls, Dovan is the one Halcyran the party can trust with what they did, and the letter is what he gives them in return, and the next campaign's question is whether the three behind the lattice can be got out. If the Saint ascends, the Lamp Court's gate opens on the night of the red moon and three people walk out of it into the square, unburnt, and Dovan is at his stall, and one of them buys a lamp.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** soft-ended, worn, the story in the same words every time; he never looks at the Steps. His humour is a stall-keeper's: prices, and "don't ask me if it's true, ask me what it's for". The second telling is the scene: let the players notice the brother go into the story and the man watching their faces.

**Use.** Post-session 8 at the stall, or by Iosune's memory before: Iosune can say, in session 4, "the lamp-man at the foot told me his brother went in and never wrote; I thought it was a story for pilgrims." The letter is a single handout line and should be shown, not read: six words in a hand the players have never seen, and a lamp-seller's thumb holding the paper flat.
