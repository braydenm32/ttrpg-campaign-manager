### Look and voice
Broad, weathered, floury to the elbow, with a plait of iron-grey hair wound like a ship's cable round her head and a goat-hook that has not been put down since the first Renewal. Fifty, with the stance of a woman who has outstared a great many animals and some officials. At the upper window of the house she counts the goats at dawn aloud, in a carrying voice, so that the rest of the holding can hear that they are all still there.

Her counting is a sort of prayer. "Thirty-eight. Thirty-nine. Forty. No, that is Hobart's. Thirty-nine."

> "A goat that has been counted is a goat that has been promised, and I do not promise lightly."

> "Two pennies a beast to cross a bridge with a goat. I have asked Mistress Hebbe which end of the goat the beast is."

### Who she is
Marn Tolliver took the north holding at Bellwether the spring after the battle with a husband, two sons and a daughter. The husband died of the cough in 3005. One son went east with a Glasser crew and did not come back, and the other drowned at the weir at Sedgemere, and the daughter, Linnet, died of a fever in 3009 and left her a granddaughter of five, **Ember**, now seven, born in Marn's own kitchen with one eye grey as the lake and the other brown. (She is not the Hollins girl in the Smokes, who has the same eyes and is also seven, and the two have never met; Marn thinks it is a thing the ash does.) Ember is a Hollowborn child, small for her age and with a grave stare, who will not come indoors when the bells ring and plays in the byre until they stop.

Marn keeps the dairy, the forty goats, and the tally on the window-frame. The tally is knots, one a red moon, with the number of lanterns that went up the lane. She is proud of it, and a little afraid of it, and has never thrown a knot away.

### What she wants
To keep the holding and the child. For the bridge toll to stop catching her goats. For the lantern to stay lit, because the company buys her cheese. For Ember to sleep through a night.

### What she knows
Everything on the orchard lane after dark, in knots. The goats, to the head. That the pale ash on the east side of her wall is not weather. She will say what she has seen if she is asked about what she has seen, but she must be asked: she thinks it is none of her business and also that it is everyone's.

### How she treats the PCs
Like customers, until they ring the bell correctly, and then like family she has not yet decided to like. She feeds them cheese. She will talk about tolls for an hour. A PC who offers to speak to the Reeve about the goats is promoted at once from customer to ally.

:::gm
**Secret.** She holds the **jars**. Since Reaping, sealed clay jars of pale ash have been left in her goat-shed by Smokes families coming back from Stillwater, for a copper a jar from [[Ambry Coyle]], on the Dreaming Widow's word. At dusk on Coinday a boy from the Assessor's Office, [[Nib]], takes them away in a handcart. She was told it was blotting-sand for the Office. She thought nothing of it until the lantern guttered on the 14th, and the whole March said "ash", and she remembered the smell of the jars. She has not told a soul. She is scared of what she has been selling.

She kept one jar back, under the straw in the third byre, unopened. Whoever asks about the jars and promises that the company will forgive the next season's goats (forty beasts, about forty silver in toll and tithe) gets the jar, the dates, and Ambry's name. If a PC mentions Ember kindly, she will give them the first two for nothing.

She does not know the buyer is [[Ansel Mote]], or that Nib's blotting-sand is what is thinning the flame. She knows only that it comes from Stillwater and goes to the Assessor. If asked what she thinks, she says: "I think a man who pays for sand in jars with the Regency's sun on the cart is not blotting a thing."
:::
