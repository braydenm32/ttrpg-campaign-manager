### Look and voice
A broad bald man of sixty with black hands. Not dirty: black, ink to the elbows, worked into the creases so deep it will go into the ground with him. He wears a leather apron, a pencil in each ear and an expression of permanent professional interest, as if every face he meets might need setting in type. Thirty years beside a press have left him a little deaf and very loud.

> "Ten silver! Ten! The first paid work the Regency's put through my door in two years, and it's a deserter. I set the A.M. myself. I didn't know what he meant by it. Still don't. Nice clean initials, though."

> "Every wanted man in the March has the same face, friend. It's my brother-in-law's. He was never wanted for anything in his life, gods rest him, and look at him now."

### Who he is
Ottun Fray was a printer's devil in Fallowden until his master's shop was seized in 2999 for running off a claimant's broadside, and walked east with the only thing worth saving: a hand-press, second-hand even then, lashed in the bed of a borrowed cart. He set it up on [[Temple Row]] in 3004, between the gods, on the principle that people who pray also want things written down. He was right.

He prints whatever pays. Wanted bills, from a single woodcut face he cut in his first winter and has used on every bill since. [[Spoons Aldane]]'s weekly odds-sheet, THE BOOK, with the lantern pool at the top in heavy type. The Reeve's ordinances, which [[Odile Fairweather]] pays for in hats she has finished with. Banns, funeral cards, a broadside on the price of coal, and prayer-cards for the House of Unburnt Light with the Saint's lamp on one side and a blank on the other "for your intention". He has printed for everyone in Cinderford except the Sleepless, who write nothing down, and the Ravelers, who write everything down by hand and regard print as a kind of lying.

When [[Pell Quillon]] nailed the Tax Notice to the Keep gate, Fray ran off fifty copies at cost and pasted them up from the West Gate to the Smokes, "so the whole town can see what the Regency thinks a lamp is worth". Pell came round to thank him and left looking unwell.

He has not stopped talking about the wanted bill since the morning he set it. It was the Assessor's people's money, counted out across his counter, ten silver offered for the man "alive", and he is prouder of the clean type on that bill than of anything he has printed in the March.

### What he wants
Paying work, a new set of capitals (his Q has been broken since 3006 and he sets it as an O with a comma), and to be thought of as the town's memory, which he quietly believes he is: every job he has set since 3004 is on the spike behind the press.

### What he knows
Who has posted what, and when. What the Reeve's next ordinance says, two days before she reads it. Which of Spoons's odds were changed at the last minute, and by how much. The poster trail of Cinderford, in order.

### How he treats the PCs
As customers and as an audience. He will print anything for them (a handbill, 2 cp; a hundred, 1 sp; a wanted bill with his brother-in-law's face, 3 sp) and tell them all about the last job while he does it. He is no good at secrets and knows it, and tells people so as a kind of warning.

:::gm
**Secret.** Mote asked him to break up the type after the run, and he did. But printers keep copy: it is how you prove you set what you were given. The copy-text for the wanted bill is on the spike behind the press, in [[Ansel Mote]]'s small, neat clerk's hand, written on the back of a spoiled Form 9-K blank. Mote writes on the backs of 9-K blanks out of habit, which is where his cipher is too. Anyone holding the copy beside the back of [[The Assessor's Lost Form]] sees the same hand at once; it is the handwriting payoff of [[Why is Dannic's wanted poster dated after his death?]] a session early, if the party thinks to ask a printer.

What Fray tells everyone is that "the Assessor's people" paid. What he saw, and will say if pressed, is that the Widow Pruitt counted the coins out and a mousy little clerk with a cold stood in the doorway the whole time and never came in. The coins were new Regency silver, Caer Ardith mint, this year's date, and Fray kept one to show off: after the one [[Mother Hennet]] keeps in a cup under her bar, it is the second new Regency coin anyone in town has seen in two years. He bites it for customers.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** loud, happy, interrupting himself; every story starts in the middle and ends with the price.

**Uses:** the poster trail in one stop (session 1 or 2); the cheapest way to get Mote's handwriting before Mote knows anyone is looking; a printer for anything the party wants the town to read (a broadside in session 7 telling the company Marrow fed them from her own purse is a strong move against the mutiny, and Fray will print it overnight for nothing, because it is a story).

If Mote learns the copy survived, Fray's press-room burns on a Wardday night. Keep him alive if you can; he is the only man in the March who will set the truth in type for free.
