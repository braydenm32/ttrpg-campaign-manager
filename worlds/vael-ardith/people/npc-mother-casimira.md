### Look and voice
Fifty, small, round, brown, in the Saint's white and saffron with the sleeves pinned back and the hands bare, because they are unmarked and the unmarked hands are the Church's argument. Black hair gone iron under a white cap, a face made for smiling that smiles a great deal, and eyes that count. A ring of keys at her waist, one of them a long brass harbour key that does not belong to any door in the House. She moves fast for a priest and writes faster, in a square plain hand, in rolls that she signs at the bottom of every page with a small flame.

Her voice is warm, quick, exact, and uses your name within a minute of learning it.

> "Taken in: the day, the name, the ailment, the eye if there is one. Seen again: the day, and where. I write both. I have never once written a lie in this roll. I would like you to notice that I said *in this roll*."

> "She asks nothing of you. She asks you to stop hurting. People say that as if it were a small thing. It is the largest thing anyone has ever asked of me, and I have been doing it for thirty years, and I am tired, and I still believe it."

> "Forty-one names on that wall. I chose nine of them. I would choose them again. Ask me if I'd sail."

### Who she is
Casimira walked the coals at fifteen, in 2976, under the old gods, and did not burn, and the temple took her in as a healer, and when the plateau rose in 3002 and the camp healer walked out of the fire at Cinders, Casimira was already a Mother of a house of the sick and became, without changing a single habit, a Lamp-Mother of [[The Church of the Unburnt Saint]]. She has kept the Lower Mercy House, the biggest of the houses below the Lamp Court at [[Halcyra]], since 3004. It takes in the sick, the poor and the Hollowborn, feeds them, heals them, lights a lamp for them, and is famous for its kindness, and the fame is deserved; she sees to it.

She signs the takings-rolls. Every soul who enters a Mercy House in Halcyra is written in at the door by the House's takings-girls, and every page of the roll is signed by its Lamp-Mother, and the Lower House's rolls are in Casimira's hand: *taken in*, with the day, the name, the ailment, and the eye if there is one; and, for those who go on up the hill to the Upper House or the Lamp Court, *seen again*, with the day and where. Not everyone taken in is seen again. Nobody who asks about it is answered unkindly. Casimira answers, kindly and exactly: the rolls are the Church's and the Synod's, and the Synod does not show them, and she does not lie.

She also chooses, from the Hollowborn on her rolls, the ones who go to [[Qerath]] every Seedwake to sail for [[Ostrakai]]. She has chosen nine. None has written. She paints none of the names on the harbourmaster's wall; she goes down to Qerath each Seedwake to watch the ship out, and comes back up the Spine, and signs the next page.

### What she wants
For the Saint to be what she believes the Saint is: mercy, with a face, made large enough to stop the world hurting. She has believed it for nine years with her whole exact heart and she has a letter in her desk that says it is not true, and she wants, more than she has wanted anything since the coals, for the letter to be wrong.

Under that, and not said: for the forty-first name to stay off the wall.

### What she knows
The rolls, every page: who was taken in, who was seen again, who was not. Which of the Hollowborn on her rolls went up the hill first, and in what order, which she has never been told the reason for and has worked out. That [[Brother Iosune]] burned on the coals and went west anyway and she wrote *worthy* on his page. That [[Seraphine Ashe]] was chosen by the Saint's own hand and that nobody chosen that way has come back to Halcyra the same, or at all. That a lamp-maker called Tavan Soran, brother to two sisters who kept a bathhouse on the lower terrace and now keep one in the ash country, came into the Lower House in 3007 "to rest" and is on her rolls as *seen again*, and that his sisters' letters are answered by the Synod's clerk and not by her, because she is not allowed.

### How she treats the PCs
With the Mercy House's whole kindness, which is real: a meal, a bed, a lamp lit for whoever they have lost, a hand on the wrist that takes the pain out of a wound, and their names used warmly from the first minute. She asks what they dream. If one of them is Hollowborn she looks at the grey eye and then, deliberately, at the other one, and writes nothing down in front of them, which is a courtesy she extends to no one else. If they are from the March she asks after the Soran sisters by name, and the House, and the lamp, in that order, and the order is the tell.

:::gm
**The hill.** The Hollowborn whose dreams are clearest at the full Ember go up to the Upper House first, where the Mothers listen to them sleep with wax tablets, and from there to the Lamp Court; the rolls write *seen again* and mean it kindly. Casimira has worked this out and signs the pages anyway.

**Secret.** The forty-first name on the Qerath harbourmaster's wall was Ilka Dunne, called Moth: a Hollowborn girl born in the Ardmere siege, raised on Tanners' Lane in the Smokes of Cinderford, brought to Halcyra by preachers in Reaping 3006 at twelve, taken in by Casimira, kept four years, and sent down to Qerath in Frostcall 3010 to sail on the Seedwake 3011 ship because her dreams at the full Ember were the clearest on the rolls and the Upper House wanted her and Casimira, who had come to love her, thought the sea was the kinder of the two. Casimira has a key to the harbourmaster's office because the Mercy Houses provision the missionaries; in Reaping this year she went down the Spine, let herself in at night, and painted the name over, carefully, with the harbourmaster's own white, because the girl is alive. A letter had come. An Ostrakai trader off a mirror-ship put it into the Lower House's alms-box in Emberwane, addressed in Ilka's round Smokes hand to "Mother Casimira, who was kind", and it is the only letter from Ostrakai that exists.

**What the letter says** (a handout, if the party earns it): that the Ostrakai did not kill their gods; they stopped feeding them, and the gods starved, and the people lived. That the Ostrakai priests looked at her grey eye and knew it, and showed her a temple that is kept swept and empty and told her whose it was, and what was under it, and that it is asleep and that they mean to keep it that way by never once saying its name. That she has told them about the Saint, and they were sorry for her. That the Saint is being fed, and will die of it when the feeding stops, the way theirs did, and that she thinks the Saint knows. And a last line, for Casimira only: *You were kind. That was real. Don't go up the hill.*

Casimira has read it forty times and told no one. She believes the Saint and she believes the girl and she cannot make them fit. She painted the name off the wall because a living girl's name does not belong on a list of the lost, and because if the Synod knew a missionary had written they would want the letter, and the letter says the Saint can be starved. She has not decided what she will do with it. If the party reaches her after session 8 having resealed the Ninth, she gives it to them, because it is the proof that what they did can hold ([[Ostrakai]]: a buried god can be starved and the living survive it), and that is the next campaign's thesis in a Hollowborn girl's hand. If the Saint has ascended, she burns it, and herself, in the House's own lamp, and the rolls with her.

**Tavan Soran** (the brother of the sisters at [[The Salt Lake]]): taken in 3007 "to rest", seen again Bloomtide 3008, Upper House, where he makes lamps for the lattice and is listened to at every full Ember; alive; not coming home. Casimira will tell the sisters if asked by someone who is not the Synod, which is against the rule, and she has never broken the rule, and she is going to.

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** quick, warm, exact, uses names; the kindness is not a mask and must not be played as one. She is the Church's best argument and the proof of its cost in one woman, and the players should like her and be afraid for her. Her humour is an administrator's: she is proud of her rolls and says so.

**Use.** Post-session 8 in person; before that she is a name on Iosune's lips and the Soran sisters' letters, and the painted-over name at Qerath. The letter from Ostrakai is the single most important piece of paper in the world after the Treaty, and it is in a desk in a hospital, under a lamp, and the woman who has it prays every night that it is wrong.
