### Look and voice
Seventy and looks it: a beard like a hedge in winter, a limp he came back with, hands that shake until there is a cup in them. He has the corner of the Candle hearth that nobody else sits in, a pipe that [[Dunny Pike]] brings him lit, and a coat with a Ninth Lantern token sewn where a button should be. He talks like a man telling a story he has told a thousand times, and he tells it a little differently every time, on purpose, and watches to see who notices.

> "Then the deserter walked into the Rent and was never seen again. Which is more than you can say for most people who walk into things."

> "Don't tell me the real story, son. I was there. That's why I don't tell it."

### Who he is
Old Pike has sat by the Candle fire for seven years, since he signed on in 3004 at sixty-three, too old to carry a pike and too stubborn to be told so. He stood two years of gate watches and then they let him be. He has not paid for a drink in all that time, which the recruits think is a kindness and the veterans do not discuss. He taught Dunny Pike the bell codes when the boy's father died, and is forever saying he is no relation: "There are a great many Pikes. Spear and fish both."

He touches the lantern cage three times when he passes under it, and the soldiers say he is the one who puts the ring of salt round its foot. He has never denied it.

### What he wants
To die in his cot with nobody owing him anything, and to tell the real story once, to the right person, and only once.

### What he knows
What the ash plains were like the first winter, before the Ash Road was staked. Every verse of the Corporal's song, including the three that were cut. Which of the Candle's stories about the night of Cinders are wrong and exactly how. Something about the Rent that makes him stop talking when the Glassers come in.

### How he treats the PCs
As an audience. He tells them the lantern legend their first night, wrong in one place, and watches to see who corrects him; whoever does gets the corner of the hearth next to him and a different wrong version every night after. He is kind to Ysse and will not look at the lantern when she is near it. He calls Borrin "the other one who came back".

:::gm
**Secret.** He is the deserter of the company's legend. On the night of Cinders he was running, and he ran through the cooks' camp in [[The Weeping Orchard]], and [[Mother Hennet]] put a lit lantern in his hands and told him to carry it up to the Keep, and for the first time that night he did what he was told. He carried the ninth lantern up Lantern Hill with a one-armed cook behind him and a boy with a burnt hand behind her, and when it was hung he kept walking, east, two days, to the lip of [[The Rent]]. He stood there a night. He lost his nerve. He walked west again and worked barges on the Ardis for two years, then came back to Cinderford under his own name, with a beard and a limp, and signed on as a wick. Nobody looked twice at another Pike. Hennet looked once.

In 3008 and 3009 he drew [[A Glasser's Chart]] for [[Vexa Glassjaw]]: the north lip from what he saw that night, the rest from what her crews told him when they came back up. He signed it "-P" and told her it was the last time. Hennet keeps his own copy under the bar.

**Session 5.** He dies in his cot in the followers' lines on the night of 24 Ashfall, of nothing much. At his funeral at [[Corvel Rise]] on the 25th the door will not open, and he sits up on his bier, spits out the coin, and apologises. Before [[Hathe's Door-Key]] is brought he confesses, with great dignity and in order: the running, the cook, the pot, the lip. He is the eighth voice; when the door finally opens his last words are "The lamp is the lock," and [[Mother Corvane]] goes very still.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** a storyteller who has decided the truth is nobody's business but his. Slow, dry, a little pleased with himself. He never lies about anything but the one thing.

**Across the arc:** one line a session by the fire, so the players know him before they bury him.
1. Tells the legend with the deserter walking into the Rent "three years later". Hennet, passing, says "Two," and keeps walking.
2. As the party leaves for Fort Mercy: "If Vexa shows you a chart, the Choir's real. The rest is her crews showing off."
3. The night of the vigil he is not by the fire. He is at the foot of the cage, with salt.
4. Watches Sallow buy a round and says, to nobody: "Seventh had a lieutenant like that. Generous man. We buried him first."
5. The funeral. Give the confession its full weight; it is what sends the party to the Orchard, and the Candle shuts behind them.
