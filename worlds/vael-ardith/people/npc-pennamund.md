A Shellback the size of a wheelbarrow, his shell still bright green-brown and only lightly ringed, with spectacles perched on his beak-like nose and a satchel of ledgers strapped to his back. **Cousin Pennamund** (his given name is Ommerrow; he's young enough to use it once a year) has come from [[Sevenhaven]] to see to the Concord's business in the March: its stalls at Gutter Market, its dealings with the Glassers at Fort Mercy and, quietly, Tollamund's purchases. By Shellback standards he is a reckless youth. He speaks in sentences of almost normal speed and walks nearly as fast as a slow human, which the old Shellbacks regard as vulgar.

> "One hundred and forty is a perfectly respectable age, thank you. I'm *mature*. I simply *finish my sentences*."

He is earnest, a little lonely, and thrilled to talk to anyone who isn't three hundred years old.

:::gm
The Concord Table sent Pennamund because they suspect Grandmother Tollamund is buying the March for reasons of her own. He's right, and he doesn't know what they are. He hired the wagon's escort and told [[Vexa Glassjaw]] to hold the strongbox "until collected", on Tollamund's written word; it was the first thing that made him suspicious, because he cannot see why she did not simply send it to the Keep. He can be an ally to the PCs against Tollamund's plan, if they help him find out what she wants with the lantern, or a rival if they get in the way of his enquiries. Perrin, one of Tollamund's three "daughters", quietly works for him ([[The Tollamund Daughters]]).

**Wants:** to learn what she wants with the lantern before the Table asks. **Fears:** that she is right and he is too quick to see why. **Beat:** in sessions 4 and 5 he finds the PCs at Gutter Market and offers a quiet word; in session 7 he stands behind the chair, or he does not.

He does not know the thirteenth barrels exist, and is horrified if shown one; Perrin knows ([[The Unmarked Barrel]]). On 20 Ashfall his clerks go back to the Last Candle to buy Mother Hennet's slate-book, with an offer to clear every tab on her slate: the book proves the House bought the garrison's wages in 3009 and, under its own Treaty clause, has been the company's defaulting paymaster since. He wants it for the Concord Table, not for the Keep gate ([[The Price Was an Arm]]). His letters to the Table are read first at [[The Ledger Island Assay]] by Grandfather Veramund, who has not told him.
:::
