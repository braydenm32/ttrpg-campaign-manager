### Look and voice
Sixty, thin, and so clean it looks like a condition: black coat brushed to a shine, white cuffs, nails pared, hair the colour of the dust he spends his life shaking out. He walks like a man carrying a full cup. Nine keys on a ring at his belt, worn smooth, and under his arm, always, a folded square of linen. He never sits with his back to a window and never sits down at all until he has looked at the floor.

He speaks quietly and precisely, the household voice, every sentence a small bow. He has said "Good morning, Majesty" to an empty chair fifteen thousand times and has started, he admits, to hear it answered.

> "There are nine of us on the sheet, back to the first. My father, his, and so on. I am the ninth. I notice I'm the ninth of everything lately."

> "It isn't a hum, exactly. A hum is a sound. This is more like a room that is about to say something and keeps not saying it."

> "I changed the sheet the morning after they examined the Fallowden boy. He'd screamed for three nights. The sheet was perfectly clean. That was the first morning I thought: it isn't him it wants."

### Who he is
The Mallorys have kept the keys of the throne room in the Sun Palace at [[Caer Ardith]] for nine generations, father to son, and the one duty the office has never dropped is the sheet. Every morning at the fifth bell Orrin Mallory unlocks the throne room, bows to the Empty Throne, lifts yesterday's dust sheet off it, shakes it once toward the east window, folds it, and lays a fresh one. He has done it since he was nineteen. He was doing it on the morning of 15 Frostcall 2981, the day after [[The Kingslaying]], when there was blood on the sheet and nobody to tell him whether to change it, and he changed it. He has done it through thirty years of [[The Regency Council]] swearing on Crownfast that it is only keeping the seat warm.

He has kept every sheet. So did his father, and his father's father, in the cellar of the house on Keyward Lane, in chests: nine generations of linen, each square dated in the corner in the hand of the man who lifted it. The household thinks it is a Mallory eccentricity. It is a record, the only one the Palace keeps that nobody has edited, and since the first of Ashfall the dust on the newest sheets is grey.

### What he wants
Out. He has a cousin with a vineyard in the Fallowden country and he would like to die somewhere with a floor that is only a floor. He cannot leave: the keys pass at death, his son died at Cinders with the Regency levies, and if he walks away there is no tenth and the Council will give the keys to a Quiet Office clerk, and Orrin, who has watched the Council for thirty years, would rather hear the floor himself than let that man hear it.

He will tell almost anything to someone who buys him the Examination at [[The Nine Generations]] and lets him talk until the gin is gone.

### What he knows
That the throne room floor has made a sound since the night of Cinders, 14 Ashfall 3002, which he heard begin: a change in the room like a held breath. That every claimant examined in the last nine years has begun to scream on the second or third night. That the Fallowden vintner's son said "a voice under the floor", and that this is wrong: it is not under the floor. It is the floor. What the Regent-Chancellor's workmen have done in the throne room, twice, with the doors shut, which he will say only after the gin.

### How he treats the PCs
With the household's courtesy, and then, if they are from the March, with a kind of hunger: they have stood near the other end of the thing he hears. He asks about the lantern the way a man asks about a letter from home. He is careful: he has lived thirty years near the Quiet Office and looks round before every third sentence. He will not be seen with them in daylight.

:::gm
**Secret.** The dust on the Empty Throne's sheet has been fine grey ash since 1 Ashfall 3011, the month the lantern began to fail in earnest, and it smells, very faintly, of lake water and burnt pears. Orrin has a stack of the dated sheets of it in his cellar, and the newest under his arm. It is the First Ward answering the Ninth: the Hearthstone is dulled, not cracked, and the ash is not coming through the floor so much as *appearing* on the one object in the realm that has sat over the Ward every day for three thousand years. If the Ninth is resealed (session 8), the dust on the morning of 10 Leafrot is dust again, and Orrin weeps on the sheet and changes it. If the Ninth cracks, the sheet on that morning is black.

**The floor.** [[Marius Vell]] has had the flagstones lifted twice, in 3005 and 3009, by workmen who were paid off and sent to the Marches; under them is older stone, grey, with a grain, the cap of the Hearthstone, and Vell stood on it alone for an hour with the doors shut, and came out and asked Orrin what he heard, and Orrin said "the river". Orrin has not been asked since and has not stopped being watched.

**Why the claimants scream.** Orrin has worked out what the genealogists know and the Council does not say: the imperial lines were bred to hear the wards, and a claimant of the blood on the throne hears [[The Sleeper Below]] through the Hearthstone within a night. He has also noticed the thing nobody else has, because nobody else is in the room at dawn: the hum *stops* while a claimant sits, and starts again when he is carried out. The Ward goes quiet when it is being listened to. He does not know what that means. The GM does: the Hearthstone's Ninth-kin is holding its breath so the claimant cannot hear *it*, and the claimant hears what is under it instead.

**Post-session 8.** If [[Tesh]] is ever brought to the capital (the Use It ending, or Vell's search for a pliable heir), Orrin is the first to see her sit, and the first to see that she does not scream. He will know what that means before Vell does, and he will have to decide who to tell.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** quiet, exact, the household bow in every line; "Majesty" slips out when he is tired. He is not mad and should never be played as mad: he is the sanest man in the Palace, which is why he drinks.

**Use.** Orrin is a post-session-8 face for the capital, or a letter: if the party writes to anyone in Caer Ardith about the floor, the letter finds its way to him. Buy him the Examination and he gives the players the First Ward in one speech, the claimants in another, and the grey dust last, laying the folded sheet on the table and opening it so they can see. That is the whole scene. The nine chests of sheets in his cellar are a handout waiting to happen: a dated record, nine generations of mornings long, of what the Ward has shed.
