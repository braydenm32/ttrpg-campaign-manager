### Look and voice
A tall, spare Raveler in her fifties, feathers gone slate-grey at the beak and iron-grey at the shoulders, wrapped in a river-brown shawl pinned with a stone pear and a bent nail. One wing droops. The tarred stilt-hut at the weir's end is hers; she is at its door at dawn and at dusk with a long pole and a shovel, working the stakes. She has the gift Ravelers have, of making a corpse feel looked after.

She speaks in short sentences and does not hurry to finish them. She says "yes" and "no" and sometimes "if you like." She laughs at funerals, quietly, at the right place.

> "Fee? No. The river paid. It paid first."

> "Everyone who comes down the Cinder has a last word. Most of them don't know it. I tell them it anyway."

### Who she is
Keld Owes-Nothing is the Raveler of the Sedgemere weir. She takes the drowned off the stakes at the end of the dead arm, washes the river off them, says the door-words of [[Hathe]] over whatever is left, and buries them in the little ground behind the tar-yard where the Ravelers of Sedgemere have always buried. The name is a deed-name, the kind a Raveler earns at a burial: hers she earned by refusing, at her first, to take the fee. The river had already paid her. She has kept to it since. People at Sedgemere leave her bread, eel, tar, and once a pair of boots, and she takes the bread.

She is also the keeper of **the slate**: a black board the length of a door, on the hut's wall, chalked in a small square hand, with every thing the weir has ever caught, from the year the stakes went in. She reads it aloud to anyone who asks and nobody does. She sends the last words of Sedgemere's dying, when she hears them, up to Corvel Rise by a crow trained for it; the words go in the Court's forty books under "the weir", with no name.

### What she wants
Quiet. To go on doing the work. For the river to keep bringing her people and for nobody to ask why she has not gone back to town.

### What she knows
The river. The slate is the best record in the March of what has come down the Cinder since the flood, and she knows which of its entries are lies by what the water did. She knows the cold water of the 8th and the 13th was not weather. She knows who she is, and who her sister is, and what a dying Salt Guard man whispered to her in the winter after Cinders, which is why she bet.

### How she treats the PCs
Courteously, from the doorway, standing. She will answer a question that is asked directly and respectfully. She will not volunteer. A PC who helps lift a body off the stakes without being asked, and does not flinch, is a person she will talk to for the length of a cup of tea. [[Quorl Many-Words]] gets a long look, and then nothing, and then, at dusk, one sentence she did not have to say.

**The slate** (read aloud, in the order she reads it; she begins in the middle):

> *A baker's apron, flour in the seams. 3003. The wearer not found.*
> *Two men in Salt Guard tabards, a bank slipped at the Rise. 3005. Bones in the cloth.*
> *A boot, good leather. 3005. Not a pair.*
> *A Glasser, face down, pick still in his belt. 3008.*
> *A child's shoe, red. 3009. I buried it.*
> *A goat. 3010. I did not.*
> *A stave, new oak, a Concord brand half burnt through, silver filings in the crack. Reaping 3011. I kept it.*
> *8 Ashfall: cold water, glass in it, a drowned smell, nobody.*
> *13 Ashfall: the same, colder. Nobody.*

:::gm
**Secret.** She is the Raveler woman who bet "never" with [[Spoons Aldane]] on the night of the First Renewal, 14 Ashfall 3003, and vanished the next day. For a year she had been the Rook-Court's first watcher of the lantern: set by [[Mother Corvane]], her elder sister, to sit by the Candle fire and the Keep's wall and listen to everything the dying of the March said near it. She walked nine miles down the towpath at dawn on the 15th and has not been back to Cinderford in eight years. The sisters quarrelled at the Rise the week before, over the same sentence.

In the winter after Cinders a Salt Guard man was dying in her arms in the Smokes, and gave her the whole sentence, a word at a time, with long gaps between; and she, who has remembered every last word she was ever given, put it next to what Corvane had been saying all winter and understood the lamp could not be put out. It could only be given back. She told Corvane the conclusion and kept the words. Corvane said: then put it out and let them rest. Keld said: then I will bet it never goes out. She did, and she left.

She has had all five words, in order, since 3002, and has never said them to a living soul. She sends the Court the river's last words and keeps back any that carry a piece of the sentence. She does not know how many she has withheld. She suspects her sister reads every crow and has guessed whose hand it is. Corvane has.

The slate's two entries for Ashfall are in her own hand and prove Dannic Rook was in the Barrow's water on the 8th and moving on the 13th. She knows what they mean (the Barrow's frost, the Cold Captain, something that has started walking) and has told nobody because nobody has asked her.

**Her price.** She will not go back, will not be hired, and will not be moved by an argument. The only thing that moves her: a person who comes to Sedgemere in person with the news that the lamp is to be *given back*, not put out, and means it. Then she will come to the Stone. If the party fetched her (S5 to S7) she is beside Corvane at the lip in S8, in her river shawl, and says one sentence to her sister that neither of them ever repeats.

**Spoons's strongbox** still holds her stake. If she sees the book on the Ninth's last night she will say: "Don't pay it."
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** short sentences, patient, a breath between them. Do not let her be mysterious on purpose. She is simply unused to being spoken to.

**Across the arc:**
1. Her slate is the clue the table can reach if Kettle mentions the cold weir. The first scene is the slate; the 8th is the line.
2. If the party arrives in S2 or S3, the last two lines of the slate are fresh, the chalk still on her fingers. She will not elaborate. Ask for a Perception check at the stakes at dusk: the water below them is a shade colder than it should be.
3. At the full Ember (S3) the cold comes again. She calls a crow.
4. Possible mid-session visit to the Barrel; she has soup.
5. **Payoff.** Mother Corvane says "that was my sister." The party goes to Sedgemere. Keld listens. She says nothing for a long time, and then asks: "Is she well? Does she still do the thing with her key?" She will tell them the fifth word, in order, if they give her something she can believe: a true account of the lamp.
8. At the lip, if fetched, beside her sister. Otherwise, a crow arrives at the Candle with a single word on its leg and nobody ever works out which.
