### Look and voice
Smaller than her chair, and the chair is a stool. Eighty-five, brown as a boot, in a wide hat of woven reed with a brim like a dinner plate and a river-green coat to her ankles with her clan's three wavy lines stitched down the hem. Her hands are knotted rope. She carries the chair herself from boat to boat, a low folding seat of black eel-skin and larch, and sits in it to receive anyone she means to see, and nobody has ever been seen to sit in it twice.

She speaks slowly, in whole paragraphs, as if each were being read into a record. She never raises her voice; she lowers it, and the room leans in.

> "The toll is what a town charges to be remembered. I do not care to be remembered by Cinderford. A penny is a question, and I am not answering it."

> "That girl will hang. But she will hang in a very good hat, and I am told it is stolen."

### Who she is
Posy Reedmarsh, Mother of the Water, is the eldest of the Mothers on the Cinder and the Barge-Clans' last word between the Ardis and the ash. The Reedmarsh Water is a flotilla of twelve boats; its Mother decides cargoes, marriages, and, in the phrase of the Clans, "which towns are no longer worth stopping at." She has not set foot in Cinderford in nine years, since the first bridge-penny on a halfling's boots, and has been to the Low Quay only by deputy. Her family name is the water she came from, and it is also the name of the halfling captain of 1255 who drafted the [[The Charter of the Open River|Charter of the Open River]] and made barges neutral; in the Reedmarsh Water the name is handed on, like the chair, and the present Posy has worn it for sixty years and believes that she is rather better at it than the first.

She is [[Kettle Brindlewake]]'s superior, in the way a river is superior to a fish. She is [[Nan Crupp]]'s cousin, and says so with a small, formal sigh.

### What she wants
The bridge-penny abolished for barge-folk. The Charter says no toll on a barge, her crew or her cargo "within a long rope's length of her"; the Reeve's toll-keeper says the Quay is seventy paces from the bridge-steps and a long rope is sixty. She is right and she cannot make anyone care, and the thing eats her. She wants the Clans to stay neutral; she wants the Reedmarsh Water to keep its winter mooring at Sedgemere; and she wants the Concord to stop being so polite about it.

### What she knows
The river. Every tow that goes under the Weir, every body that comes down, every letter that crosses Kettle's floor. She has known since Reaping that a man who was not a clerk was sending letters to the capital by clan post, and read the first, and has not read another. She knows what the Concord pays at the Weir, because she is paid it.

### How she treats the PCs
With perfect, unhurried courtesy, and she makes them wait for it. Visitors are brought tea, and given a stool, and asked for a true thing about the river. A PC who says something she did not know, even a small thing, is a person to whom she will say a second sentence. A PC who says anything about Cinderford's bridge-penny before she has raised it is a person she will not hear again until the following morning. She respects people who bring her something she cannot get from the water, and she is the best ally in the March for anyone willing to be slow.

:::gm
**Secret.** Posy took the Concord's tithe. When the salt-tows began, a year ago last Reaping, a polite man from [[Tollamund & Daughters]] offered her one coin in thirteen to let a thirteenth, unmarked barrel go through the Weir unopened. She named the tithe "the barrel's share", put it in the *Long Patience*'s ballast, and has been quietly ashamed of how much of it there is. She told herself it was the cost of keeping the winter mooring. It was not: the House, through a letter in Perrin's neat hand last Reaping, now wants the mooring itself. The Charter protects a barge from tolls. It says nothing about the bank it is tied to, and a land-buyer with a good lawyer could charge wharfage until the Clans left. She took their coin and may have sold them the ground.

She also holds the **lost pay order** of 3009. [[Kettle Brindlewake]] fished it from the post-stair box when the box flooded, read it, and gave it to Posy for safe-keeping on the day [[Ansel Mote]] arrived. Posy has not decided what it is worth and will trade it, not for coin, but for help: either a written waiver of the foot-penny from the Reeve, or the party's agreement to leave her tithe out of anything they bring to [[Mistress Hebbe]]. See [[The Lost Pay Order]] and [[The Unmarked Barrel]].

If the tithe comes out, a Quay-Moot can remove her. She knows it. She is eighty-five, and tired, and not at all sure it would be wrong.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** slow, whole paragraphs, no contractions when she is angry. She calls Cinderford "the toll", the Keep "the lamp", the Concord "our friends from the sea" and the Regency "the paperwork." She takes tea like a magistrate and thinks like a river.

**Across the arc:**
2. First meeting: at Sedgemere on the *Long Patience*. Courteous, closed. Asks a true thing about the river. Does not mention the barrel.
3. By message: asks Kettle to tell her how the party took the offer of tea. This is a test.
4. If the party is working on the barrels, she tells them, in a perfectly level voice, exactly what they already know, and adds nothing.
5. She lets the party see the slate-book (Keld's) if asked politely. She does not let them see her own ballast.
6. If asked, she lends a barge for the expedition upriver: a free favour from the Clans to a friend of Kettle's.
7. The Charter at the Reeve's court. If the party gets her the foot-penny, she brings the pay order to the Keep on her own legs, which she has not done in nine years, and sits in her chair outside the gate while the Concord is stopped.
8. Post-S8, she is the first to be asked for a quote on the Frostcall cargo (10 Leafrot): see [[What is the sealed cargo for Frostcall?]].
