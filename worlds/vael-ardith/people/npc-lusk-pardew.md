### Look and voice
Short, thickset, and brown as a loaf, with a veil of black gauze pinned to the brim of his hat (put up, not down; he keeps it for visitors) and hands that are all knuckle and old stings. Fifty-five. A Regency official's black coat gone the colour of weak tea, with a bee on the lapel that is, upon inspection, alive. He walks the carts in the morning with one hand on the wheel and an ear cocked, and says, to every driver, the same sentence.

> "Anything humming?"

He speaks with a countryman's care and an examiner's precision, and every simile in his mouth is a bee. "Customs is like swarming, madam. It only seems like chaos until you have done it forty years." "A forged seal is like a drone in a worker's hive: it will not last the hour, and everyone will have noticed."

### Who he is
Lusk Pardew has been Examiner of Excise at Whitepost for twenty years, and has not once been to Caer Ardith since the day he was posted, which he took, correctly, as a message. He was a promising clerk in the Office of Revenue through the war, and then in the winter of 2991 he did something very small and very honest (he refused to certify a duty-return that did not add up) and was given the finest posting the Regency could offer to a man it wished to forget. He took it, and installed forty hives.

He is honest, competent, and therefore posted here. The Regency has never discovered that he actually likes it. In 2997 the Office of Revenue sent a twenty-four-year-old called [[Pell Quillon]] to Whitepost for his first posting, with a perfectly straight back and a satchel of forms, and Pardew gave him the second desk and a very good reference. Pell is still in his debt for the reference. They have not corresponded in fourteen years, and each privately assumes the other has forgotten.

### What he wants
To keep the bees. To be allowed to do the job properly. To know, before he dies, who is on the other end of the orders he receives, and why a post as unimportant as his has been given the one clerk-seal in the Regency that never fails.

### What he knows
Every cart that has come through since 3001: its load, its weight, and what it sounded like. That the glass that comes out of the Rent hums at three paces if it is Lithic and at six if it is angry, and that nobody asks him why it is getting louder. That someone in the capital knew about humming glass before he did, because the line was drafted before he ever heard it.

### How he treats the PCs
Courteously and exactly. Every traveller is examined, every cart, every pack; he will not hold up a person for longer than the job needs and will not hurry them. He will look at your sword and ask you to wrap it. He will look at your face and ask nothing at all. If you say "Pell Quillon sent me" he will stop and say, "Is he well?" with an expression you did not expect from a man who examines glass.

:::gm
**Secret.** Pardew has kept a copy of every sealed order he was ever given, in a flat cedar box under the bed upstairs, in order, for twenty years: one hundred and forty-one, and the newest is the Office of Charters' standing instruction to pass **Tollamund & Daughters' crates unexamined and unrecorded**, a week after the Gilt Run, over a clerk-seal that has never failed. He has obeyed it. He does not like it. On 4 Ashfall he logged the pay wagon in the yard-book (the escort turned back, two House drivers took it on) *because it was sealed and not crated*, which is a loophole an honest man can find. He would give the box to Pell Quillon, and to nobody else, and to Pell only if Pell asked in his own hand. He has not offered, because nobody has asked.

The **Crownfast pardon list** also goes east once a year through his post, under seal, the Marches' copy; he reads it, because the seal is his to check, and he has kept a copy of this year's. See [[Who is on the Crownfast pardon list?]].

He is more afraid than he looks. A man who has kept the rules for twenty years in a place that does not want them kept has something precise to lose.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** slow, courteous, apiarist similes. "Anything humming?" at every contact. When he is truly alarmed he says nothing and checks the hives.

**Across the arc:**
Prologue: examines the PCs as they come from the west (or leave it), with the sentence.
4 to 7. By letter: a request to Pardew for the yard-book entry costs 5 sp and a reference to Pell. He writes back in a single exact paragraph: it is a good letter.
7. If Pell is in danger, a letter from Pardew arrives with a cedar box: one hundred and forty-one sealed orders and a note: "I thought someone should have the other half."
Post-S8. He is the first official to hear what happened at the Stone, because the glass goes quiet. He logs it. "Nothing humming."
