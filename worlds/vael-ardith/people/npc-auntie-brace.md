### Look and voice
Stout, fifty-five, sleeves rolled, one eye brown and the other milky grey, a ledger always open on her knee and a pencil behind her ear. She sits by the stove at [[The Ninepenny]] from dusk to dawn and sleeps, when she sleeps, sitting up. A pear-country voice, soft and broad, that carries the length of forty pallets without rising.

> "Nine for the bed, one for the blanket, and if you dream, you tell me what. That's the rule, and I've buried three men who thought it was a joke."

> "I don't mind the dreaming. It's the ones who sit up facing east with their eyes open and don't know they've done it. I write them down twice."

### Who she is
Auntie Brace was born in the pear country, when there was one, married a sergeant of the Ruddy Hundred, and followed the Seventh's baggage with a husband and two grown sons until 14 Ashfall 3002, when she lost all three; by morning her left eye had gone grey. She is late-eyed: a grown woman who took the Hollowborn's eye the night of [[The Battle of Cinders]] and sees what they see, which in the Smokes is a lot. She opened the Ninepenny in 3003 in a drying-shed because, she says, somebody had to count the sleepers, and she has counted them every night since: name, pennies and dream, in a book that is now nine volumes on the shelf over the stove.

[[Tibbet Marlowe]] slept here for a year when he was too big for Ottilie's boxroom and too young by the calendar for the Keep; she still calls him "my lodger" and keeps his pallet, the one by the door, for anyone she thinks needs it.

### What she wants
Her sleepers counted and alive in the morning. She has lost a few, at red moon, who walked out onto the plain, and she takes it personally.

### What she knows
What the Smokes dreams, night by night, for nine years. Which lodgers are Sleepless (the open eyes). Which soldiers of the Ninth come down to sleep here when they cannot sleep in the Keep, and there are more of them than the Captain knows. That three nights either side of a full Ember the entries start to match, word for word, in forty hands.

### How she treats the PCs
As lodgers, which is to say with a pallet, a blanket and a question. She likes anyone who tells her their dream honestly and dislikes anyone who makes one up, which she can tell. Tick gets his pallet. Borrin gets the room with the door, free, and a long look at the floor where his shadow should be.

:::gm
**Secret.** The dream-book's seventh volume, 3009 to 3010, holds Tick's dreams for a year, in Brace's hand, a line a night: a stone door, a lamp, a hand on the latch from inside, and from one red moon in Reaping on, "a woman standing behind me who won't turn round". Tick does not know she wrote them down. Brace has read them back since, and understood that his mother was already there.

The matching entries round the full Ember are the plainest evidence in town of what the Hollowborn dream, and a quiet way to feed the Sleeper Dreams table to the players. She lends the book to [[The Dreaming Widow]], who preaches from it, and does not approve, and lends it anyway, because the Widow was a lodger once too: at twelve, for one night, on her way to Stillwater.

At the session 3 full Ember the Ninepenny empties onto the plain at midnight, every sleeper walking; Brace follows with a lantern and the book, counting.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** soft, broad, unhurried, motherly in a way that has had all the softness used out of it. Writes while you talk.

Brace is the Smokes' census and conscience. Use her to show the players the dream before anyone explains it, and to count the company's sleepless: a soldier who turns up at the Ninepenny three nights running is one Sallow will reach first.
