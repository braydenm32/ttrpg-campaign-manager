### Look and voice
Small and thin and very young: seventeen, with a heart-shaped face, cropped ash-blonde hair and bare feet black with tannery soot. She wears a grey wedding dress three sizes too big, cut down from someone's grandmother's, its hem dragging. Her left eye is the milky Hollowborn grey; her right eye she keeps shut, always, "so it can't wake me", which is why the Sleepless chalk an open eye behind a closed lid on their doors. She is never seen to sleep.

She speaks softly and slowly, in a sing-song half-asleep voice, and she smiles at people as though she has dreamed about them.

> "Shh. Shh. Everyone's so tired. Aren't you tired? He'll let us rest. That's all he wants. That's all he ever wanted."

> "I married him in a dream, the night the moon caught fire. Then they told me he was dead. So I'm a widow. But I don't believe them."

### Who she is
The **Dreaming Widow**, the Widow of Stillwater, leads the Sleepless, the dream-cult of [[The Smokes]]: tanners, glass-dust workers, widows, families with a grey-eyed child, people too tired for hope. About sixty adults, plus their Hollowborn children, whom they call the Dreamers, meet in cellars and in the Widow's yard: Wakers keep the night watch, Callers bring in the lost, and the newest, the Drowsy, are still half asleep. They keep each other awake for days with bitter tea and songs, and wait for the red moon, when they all dream the same dream. The Widow tells them what it means. It means: soon.

Nobody in the Smokes knows her real name. She came up out of Stillwater the night it drowned, with the lake still on her, and preached her first sermon from the jetty there in 3006. She has preached in the Smokes ever since.

### What she wants
Rest. She preaches that the buried god under the world is not dead but sleeping, and that if he wakes he will end everything that hurts: hunger, grief, cold, debt, war, the long grey grind of the ash. Rest for everyone, forever. She believes it completely.

### What she knows
The dream, better than anyone alive, and what it asks of people who keep still. Who in the Smokes has started to dream it. That she has her own name for the lantern on the hill, and it is not the Keep's. That the charm-wife's knots work, which is why she calls them blinding ([[A Dreamless Charm]]).

### How she treats the PCs
As fellow sleepers who haven't woken yet. She is gentle, sad and sincere, and she wants to save them too.

:::gm
**The dream and the lantern.** In the dream she sees the vast shape under water, the eye the size of a lake opening, and hears the voice that says a sentence nobody can keep on waking. She calls the lantern on the hill "the stopper in the bottle".

**Secret.** She was born **Wenna Marlowe**, in the winter of 2993-4 at a pit-camp in the Grimmark foothills, the winter the dwarves went under the mountain and the breached Eighth Ward took her eye: a genuine older Hollowborn, not one of the Cinders-born. She believes the buried god, [[The Sleeper Below]], is the god of endings. She is not wrong about what he was. She is wrong about what he wants. She knows that the dead are restless and that her sister is one of them.

The Widow is the younger sister of [[Marla Marlowe]], [[Tick]]'s mother, seventeen years her junior, and the younger daughter of [[Ottilie Marlowe]], the Smokes midwife: Tick's aunt. The family fled south to [[Cinderford]] in the war. On the night of [[The Battle of Cinders]], eight-year-old Wenna sat in the cellar of Marlowe's holding her sister's hand while their mother delivered Tick and Marla died. She ran from the house at twelve, to Stillwater on its grey lake, and was one of the children who came up out of it alive the night it drowned ([[The Drowning of Stillwater]]); she came back to the Smokes' cellars with the dream. She knows exactly who Tick is. Her mother has not spoken to her in five years, and goes to the Sleepless meetings sometimes, at the back, just to look at her. Marla's last words were, "Wenna, hold his hand. Don't look up." Wenna looked up. The red moon was rising through the cellar grating, and the Sleeper looked back.

She supplies the Stillwater ash for the poisoned oil, gladly: lake-ash from [[Stillwater]], where the dream runs strongest, which chokes the flame. The Quiet Office thinks it is using her. She thinks the same of them. She has heard from her crews at Tally's Cut of a grey-eyed child at Ambercote who dreams wrong, and she would like very much to meet [[Cass Marrowick]].

She wants Tick very particularly. She believes Tick is the Sleeper's firstborn, the child of the night it dreamed hardest (she is not wholly wrong: it's why he grew so fast). She wants him at the lip of [[The Rent]] under a full red moon, holding the flame, so the god can wake through him.

She does not know her sister's ghost walks beside Tick. If she learns it, it will break her heart, or her faith.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** whisper-soft, sing-song, sleepy; "shh"; she pats people's hands. Never raise her voice. She is a frightened child who found a god, and a cult leader with sixty followers, a hundred with the children.

**Across the arc:**
1–2. Unseen. Sleepless chalk on the Smokes' doors: an open eye behind a closed lid. A Sleepless child ([[Sabe Hallows]]) draws the PCs a picture of the dream. The cut oil smells faintly of lake water.
3. **Red Moon Rising**: leads the Sleepless march through the Smokes; half the town sleepwalks behind her toward Lantern Hill. First meeting with the PCs, if they find her in the crowd. She calls Tick by name.
4–5. Sends [[Ambry Coyle]] to "invite" Tick to the vaults. If refused, tries to take him.
6. A few dozen of the Sleepless walk east behind the expedition to wait on the lip for the red moon; the Widow, Ambry Coyle and the rest stay in the Smokes.
7. In the chaos of the three knives, a handful of Sleepless under [[Ambry Coyle]] reach the cage ladder to snuff the lantern. Ambry can be talked down.
8. At the Rent under the full red moon, the Widow brings her followers to the lip. She can be stopped, saved (if Marla's ghost can be made to reach her, through Tick), or she can step off the edge. The crack is far below the lip; she does not reach it.
