### Look and voice
A tall Raveler in black that is actually black, not a Raveler's joke of it, with a grey beak polished like a boot and a single red lacquered feather behind one ear, the house's colour. Mourn stands inside the red door of [[The Velvet Ledger]] from noon till dawn, takes weapons with one long hand and names with the other, and laughs at the wrong things: a confession, a threat, a death. The laugh is a short dry caw and Mourn does not seem to hear it.

> "Knives in the basket, names in the book, or neither, your choice. The goddess doesn't mind. I do, a bit."

> "Oh, that's a *good* one. Sorry. Carry on. You were saying you'd kill me."

### Who they are
Mourn was Rook-Court once, a fledgling at Cinders carrying bodies with the rest, and is not Rook-Court now, and says so to anyone who asks and nobody who does not. They keep [[Sorrel Vey]]'s door, which means they have heard every secret that entered the house and every one that left it, and after Madame Vey they are the one the house obeys. The lute-boy is frightened of them. Tesh is not, which Mourn finds restful.

### What they want
To be the one who hears it. A Raveler who leaves the Court does not stop being a Raveler: Mourn remembers every last word spoken in that house, and there have been a few, and keeps them, and will not say for whom.

### What they know
Who uses the back stair, and when. Who paid for tea and asked nothing. Which of the house's visitors are afraid of the Captain and which of the Captain's friend. That the Assessor's clerk looked at Tesh for an hour and did not blink. What [[Mother Corvane]] is really after, which Mourn will not tell the Court and might tell a PC who makes them laugh at the right thing.

### How they treat the PCs
With amusement, which is worse than contempt. Mourn opens the door, takes the knives, writes down what the PCs call themselves, and corrects the spelling. Quorl gets a long look and "Many-Words. She still talking about you?" and no more unless he earns it. A PC who confesses something true gets in free.

:::gm
**Secret.** In 3003, in the first winter of the rookery at Corvel Rise, Mourn sold a dying soldier's last words to the woman he had said them about, for her pleasure rather than for the ledger, and Corvane put them out of the Court for it: last words are Hathe's, the Court says, and may be kept or traded for a secret of equal weight, never spent. Mourn thinks the Court is wrong and the goddess of wanting things is right, and has thought so for eight years at Vey's door. The quarrel is the whole of why a Raveler keeps the door of Isaurana's house, and why Corvane's upstairs seat at the Chapel is kept empty on the nights Mourn is seen on the Row.

Mourn knows that [[Ilse Marrow]] uses the back stair, has known since the first night, and has told no one, not even Madame Vey, who knows anyway. They know [[Ansel Mote]] is no clerk, because clerks fidget and he does not.

**Session 8.** At the lip, Mourn stands for [[Isaurana]]: the goddess of wanting wants the door open, because everything behind every door is hers. Mourn has walked there to see whether it is true. Keep them funny on the lip, right up to the moment the dead turn round.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** dry caw, long pauses, laughs on the wrong beat. Never lies in the house; lying is Vey's job.

Mourn is the Ledger's gatekeeper in both senses: the party reaches Vey through them in session 4, and Mourn's notice of Mote's tea, and of who sits with Tesh, is the house's clue to the Quiet Office. A Raveler outside the Court is also a lever for Quorl's loyalty: Corvane's rule, or the goddess's.
