### Look and voice
Sixty, broad as a doorframe and about as talkative, grey-skinned and grey-bearded under a leather apron scorched to the colour of a good loaf. One eye is black and steady; the other went white from a spark in the Iron Tithe's forge-wagon the winter before Cinders and wanders a little, so that people at his counter find themselves looking over their shoulder. He has ten fingers, all there, all thick, and the nickname is a lie he has never explained and will not.

He speaks in as few words as will carry the weight. He asks the price, tells you the price, and lets you find out whether you can afford it from his face.

> "Five sp a hand's-breadth. Not a hand's-breadth of mine. Yours. Sit."

> "One. Two. Three. Four. Five." *(He puts the hammer down.)* "Six. Seven. Eight. Nine." *(Silence. The shop waits for the rest and does not get it.)*

### Who he is
He was armourer to the Iron Tithe, [[Ismere once-Fenn]]'s company, and was two days behind the field at Cinders with the forge-wagon mending axles. The lantern of the Tithe went dark in the first hour. He came east when the ash was cool to find the wagon's owners' gear, found only a few, and stayed. He holds his smithy under a Mothers' writ, because an orc outside a company needs one, and he is careful with it. On Ironday at dusk he walks to the anvil under the tin roof on [[Temple Row]] and says [[Grum]]'s words over it ("Hold. Hold. Hold, you rusty bastards."). He is not a priest. He is the one the company looks at when it needs somebody to.

### What he wants
To keep his hands in work, his slate honest, and the ninth lantern's pattern on his wall for the day somebody comes to say what it is for. To see the Iron Tithe's name chalked somewhere other than the Field of Lanterns. To be asked, once, by Ismere, how the forge-wagon was.

### What he knows
Every horse and every blade in the Ninth, and which owners are not looking after them. That there are fewer soldiers on the hill than there were in Reaping: he re-points the spears, he counts the spears. The fused coin-lumps the Glassers bring in and which of them have something living in the middle.

### How he treats the PCs
Fairly, which is rarer than kindly. He prices each of them by the kit they walk in with and charges everyone the same rate. A PC who brings a glass-fused sword gets a long look and a quote. A wick with no coin gets a chair by the trough and a nail to hold. He tells [[Varra Nine-Knuckles]], flatly and without malice, that he cannot work for her, and watches how she takes it. He asks [[Tick]] what the cages are for and answers himself.

:::gm
**Secret.** He shod horses and sharpened blades in the Palace barracks forge in Caer Ardith in 2981, and on the evening of 14 Frostcall he was the smith a young Iron Guard named Mirrel Vosk handed his sword to, with the words "No notches. Never used." There were none. He has known ever since what the Second Blade of the Ninth Lantern did not do at the Gilded Gallery door. They have never spoken of it; Vosk comes every Ironday to have a dagger edged that does not need it.

He holds the pattern for the **ninth iron lantern** of the Field of Lanterns on Corvel Rise: he forged the eight copies in 3003, and kept the ninth's mould on his wall, in the shape of a cage. He will cast it the day someone who knows the day tells him (Reseal: the company gets home to a dark cage, and he forges it in a night and sets it up himself). He does not know why. He only knows he made eight, and the ninth pole stands empty.

His charm-nails (the heads struck as noose-knots) work, a little, without the charm. It started in Reaping and he has told only Vosk. He is not certain whether he is being thanked by Grum or poached by the Corporal.
:::

== GM notes ==

Run him as the party's first proof that the March has a spine. He never explains the nickname. If a player offers to guess, count aloud, slowly, to nine, and say "No." He is the best place to put a mid-session breather: a bench, a trough, a nail to hold, a rumour about the Glassers he has repaired for. His hostility to Varra is professional, not personal, and should read that way until she has done something unusual; the Mothers' nod is a plot lever for session 7 or after.
