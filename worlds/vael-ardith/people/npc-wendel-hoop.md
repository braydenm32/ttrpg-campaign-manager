### Look and voice
A wide, red-faced man of fifty with forearms like barrel staves and a leather apron scarred by the adze. Shavings in his beard, tar under his nails, a pencil behind one ear for marking staves. He talks while he works and works while he talks, and every sentence lands on the beat of the mallet.

> "Two silver a month. Since 3003. Paid prompt till 3009, and since then, nothing, and a letter saying payment is made *on receipt of the appropriate form*. I've asked for the form. They sent me a form for asking."

> "I don't listen. I don't have to. Pine boards and a stove right over my bench. I know what that office had for dinner."

### Who he is
Wendel Hoop was a cooper in the pear country before the war, making the casks the brandy went into. One of them is behind the bar of [[The Last Candle]] with thirty-odd measures left in it, and he goes in once a month to look at it, and Hennet lets him. When the town came back he took the yard on [[Ford Street]] opposite the bathhouse and has made casks there ever since: for the barges, for the Keep, for Hesk the chandler's oils, for anyone who needs a thing kept dry or kept in.

In 3003 the Regency rented the room over his yard for [[The Assessor's Office]] at two silver a month, and paid on the nail until the first day of 3009, when it stopped paying for anything. He has kept the lease out of stubbornness, and because the Widow Pruitt brings him a biscuit on Coinday, and because, as he says, a tenant who does not pay is still a tenant who does not burn the place down.

Then [[Pell Quillon]] arrived with his warrant, found out the rent was owing, went white, and paid a month out of his own purse on the spot. Wendel has defended him in every argument on the street since, with the adze if need be.

### What he wants
His rent: two silver a month, thirty-three months, six gold and six silver in all, and he has it chalked on the slate by his gate where the Regency's clerks have to walk past it. Failing that, the form. Failing that, a quiet tenant and a yard that does not burn.

### What he knows
Everything said in the room upstairs, more or less, because the floor is pine and he is under it. The Assessor's arguments with the stove. The Widow's opinion of the new clerk. Who comes up the outside stair after dark. And casks: whose they are, where they went, and what was in them, because a cooper knows the weight of a full one in his sleep.

### How he treats the PCs
Like neighbours who might be useful. Help him get his rent (or just get Pell to sign him a paper saying it is owed) and he will tell them what he hears through the ceiling. Insult Pell and he puts down the mallet and picks up the adze.

:::gm
**Secrets, in the order he will part with them.**
1. **The tenth crate.** On his first day in town [[Ansel Mote]] bought an empty hogshead "to keep the damp off the forms" and had it rolled round to the carter's yard where his wagon stood. The porters carried nine crates up the outside stair and he counted them up himself, loudly. Wendel carried the hogshead up last, that evening, nailed shut, and it was heavier than paper, and something in it slid and clinked. That is how nine crates arrived and ten are in the room: the tenth came up inside his cask. (It holds a crossbow, three vials and a Regency seal Pell does not have.) Mote gave him a silver for his back and another for his memory, in worn old coin, and Wendel has kept the second silver out of principle.
2. **The voices.** Restday nights in Reaping and early Ashfall, after the Widow had gone home and before Pell came, two voices up there late: the clerk's, soft and with no sniffle in it at all, and a gentleman's with a Fallowden-country vowel who said "the Captain" a great deal. Wendel would know the second voice if he heard it again. It is [[Garrick Sallow]]'s. The meetings stopped the night Pell moved in, because Pell sleeps there.
3. **The four casks.** Late in Reaping [[Fennick Bottlebrook]] hired his handcart and his boy for a night, "for empties going for repair", and the boy came back saying they were the four heaviest empties he had ever pushed, all the way to the Low Quay. They were the four full casks of the company's good oil that went aboard the *Grey Dumpling*. A useful red herring: it points at Fennick, which is where the oil plot wants the party to look.
4. **The oil on the stair.** On the night of 4 Leafrot he smells lamp-oil on the outside stair and thinks the Widow has spilled the office's; she finds the office's oil missing in session 7. It is Mote's preparation to burn the office and everything in it. Wendel's yard is shavings, tar and seasoned oak: if the office goes up, it takes the cooperage, and the cooperage takes the street.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** a man keeping time with a mallet; aggrieved, cheerful, exact about money.

**Uses:** the Assessor's Office from underneath. He is the reason the "ten crates" rumour exists, the cheapest witness to Sallow's night visits before Spoons cracks, and an alarm for session 7: if the party has befriended him, he comes running up the hill on the night of the 4th or 5th to say his stair smells of oil, and Mote's night begins with the party already in the street.

Settle his rent (a PC with Pell's help can draft a Form 44-A claim that includes it, which delights both of them) and he gives the party one free cask of anything, sealed, no questions, which in Cinderford is a kind of passport.
