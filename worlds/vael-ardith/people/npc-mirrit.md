### Look and voice
A tall young woman who has not finished getting used to being tall: long arms she keeps folded, a chandler's leather apron with burn-holes in the bib, hair cut short with the shop scissors so it stays out of the vats. One eye is brown and steady. The other is the milky grey of all Hollowborn eyes and rests on the space just behind your shoulder, politely, the way you might not stare at a stranger's limp. She smells of tallow and lye and, faintly, of green fruit.

She speaks quietly and finishes every sentence, as if each one had been costed.

> "Tallow dips, four for a penny. Beeswax is four each. The red ones are for the Ledger and you're not the Ledger."

> "No, I don't see anything. I sell candles."

### Who she is
Mirrit was born in a tanner's loft off Tanners' Lane on the night of [[The Battle of Cinders]], one of the six whose names stand first in the Maelis birth-rolls. Like the other five she grew in steps: walking at a few months, reading at two, selling the chandler's matches on the Low Quay at four by the calendar and looking seven. On a late-summer evening in 3006 she told a woman in the queue that her husband was standing behind her and wanted her to know the roof was fixed, and [[The Night of Open Eyes]] began. Her mother was one of the eleven dead.

[[Hesk's Chandlery|Hesk Varro]] found her at dawn sitting on his step with a tray of his matches, all unsold, and took her in without asking anyone. She has worked his counter ever since: the Vesh keeps dusk to midnight and sleeps upstairs by day, and Mirrit does the day trade with every lamp in the shop lit, which offends him on principle and which he allows. She mixes his blend, presses his grey wax with the moth stamp, and walks to [[The Weeping Orchard]] once a month on an errand for him that she does not discuss. She stopped growing two years ago. She has not told anyone what she sees since the riot, and she means to keep it that way.

On her evening off she sits by the door of [[The Last Candle]] for the noise, and leaves before she has finished her beer. The Candle is crowded in more ways than one. The dead do not drink, she says, if you push her. They come to check their tabs.

### What she wants
For Hesk to live, which he will not, and for the Keep to buy his oil again, which she thinks would make the lantern happier, though she could not tell you why she thinks a lantern can be happy. A room with a door. One day in which nothing stands behind anyone.

### What she knows
- The chandlery's order-book, which she keeps by heart and will not show anyone without a reason.
- That Hesk's blend burns cleaner than anyone else's, and that she is the only one who knows the recipe besides him.
- Most of the Smokes by sight, living and otherwise.

### How she treats the PCs
As customers: fair prices, exact change, no conversation she has not been paid for. She warms, slowly, to anyone who treats Hesk as a man rather than a cough, and to anyone who buys a lamp-trimmer's kit and knows what it is. With [[Tick]] she is courteous and flat. The six do not seek each other out; there is nothing one of them could tell the others. She is the one person in the Smokes he has never made laugh, and he keeps trying.

:::gm
**What the order-book says.** One sealed Keel tin to "the Assessor's Office, paid in advance", by a clerk with a cold, and eleven more since Reaping, the same way. She served him herself, by daylight, every time. She also knows how the moth seal is pressed, where the wax bin is, and that anyone who stood at the counter for a minute could reach both.

**Secret.** The clerk with a cold was [[Ansel Mote]], and Mirrit knows two things about him nobody else does.

First, the cold is false. The first time he came in he asked, very humbly, how the moth seal was made, and turned the brass stamp over in his gloved hands while she went into the back for his tin. Through the curtain she saw him stop sniffing the moment she was out of sight, and press the stamp into something in his palm. When she came back he was wiping his glove. That is how the cut tins came to carry Hesk's own seal. She worked it out the day the gossip reached the Smokes that the lantern's oil had been cut, and has told no one, because the stamp was in her hand before it was in his, and she believes it makes the thinning of the lantern partly hers.

Second, nothing stands behind him. Everyone in the March has someone at their shoulder: a husband, a sergeant, a child. Mote has no one, which is the rarest thing her grey eye has ever seen, and it frightens her more than any of the dead. She would know him across the square. She has seen him on Ford Street at dusk, buying nothing.

**The sap.** Her monthly errand is a vial of sap from the oldest tree in the cooks' camp, bought from whoever is sitting under it, a drop to every tin of Hesk's blend. Hesk thinks it is a preservative. Mirrit knows better than he does: when the sap goes in, the shop lamps flare white for a breath and the dead in the room step back. She thinks the lantern on the hill liked the blend for the same reason, and she is right (the flame has been without its accidental dose since the Keep stopped buying in Thawmere).

**The six.** She hears the voice in daylight, as the Cinders-born will: warm, patient, at the noon lull when the shop is empty, saying her name as if it had been waiting to. She keeps the lamps lit to drown it out. On every red-moon night she asks Hesk to tie her ankle to the counter-leg with wick-cord, and he sits up with her, coughing, until dawn.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** quiet, exact, priced; never volunteers; looks just past people. Her tell is folding her arms tighter when she sees something.

**Running her.** She is the order-book's living witness and the cheapest way to put a face on "a clerk with a cold". She will not give Mote up to a stranger. She gives him up to a PC who has helped Hesk, or who tells her plainly that the lantern is failing and the Keep is not to blame.

**Across the arc:**
- **S1:** at the chandlery counter if the party comes for a dark-lantern before the Barrow; she sells them the good one and tells them which wick.
- **S2:** if they come back with the [[The Cut Oil Tin|cut tin]], she knows the seal at a glance and goes white.
- **S3 (red moon):** the march passes the chandlery. Hesk and Mirrit are inside, awake, her ankle corded to the counter. She asks the party, very calmly, to check the knot.
- **S4 to S5:** her confession about the stamp, if earned. She can pick Mote out of any room.
- **S5:** when the sap makes the lantern burn white, she is the one person in town who is not surprised.
- **S7:** she is the one who sees [[Borrin Halt]]'s shadow move on its own: "Your shadow's late, dwarf. It's looking at the lamp."
- **S8:** she walks with the company as far as the Treaty Stone and turns back to mind the shop, because someone has to and Hesk cannot stand.

If the ward is resealed, the noon voice stops. She does not mention it to anyone; she simply starts letting the lamps go out by day.
