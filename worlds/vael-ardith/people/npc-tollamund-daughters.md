### Look and voice
Three humans in their forties in identical grey, hair scraped back, a Tollamund ledger-book on a cord at each belt. They are not sisters and are not alike, and after an hour at the counter you cannot tell them apart, which is the point. **Perrin** has the ink: she does the hands, the ledgers, the seals, the counting, and raises one eyebrow at a Concord coin as if it owed her money. **Odessa** has the voice: she says what the Grandmother would say if the Grandmother said things at a speed people could wait for, and she is never rude and never hurried. **Sal** has the legs: she walks the Grandmother's errands, carries her letters, stands behind the chair, and is the only one of the three the town has seen outside the mill. In the back, **Fennel**, a Vesh scribe with ink on all four hands, moving at a speed that in that building looks like panic.

> Odessa: "The Grandmother says she is pleased to see you, and that the answer is no, and that she would like you to sit down while it is no."

> Perrin: "That coin is Sevenhaven, this year, and it has been somewhere interesting. Where?"

> Sal: "I carry letters. I don't read them. I remember who they're for."

> Fennel: "Copied. Sealed. Filed. Next? There is no next? There's always a next."

### Who they are
They are [[Tollamund & Daughters]]' daughters, which is a title, not a kinship: the house has had three since before the Concord, and when one dies or retires another grey woman takes the ledger-book and the name. These three came east with [[Grandmother Tollamund]] in 3007. The town calls them the daughters because the house does; the town suspects they are not anyone's. They finish each other's sentences, know everything the house knows, and are, Odessa will tell you with a small smile, "three hundred years younger than our mother, give or take".

Fennel has been with the house since it opened, took the post because it was the only clerkship in the March that paid, and is paid in lamp-oil as often as coin, which suits a Vesh.

### What they want
What the Grandmother wants, before she says it. Each of them is also, privately, a person, and the party can find out which one wants what if they take the trouble: Perrin wants the books to balance, Odessa wants to be asked a question she has not already answered, Sal wants to see Sevenhaven again, and Fennel wants to be paid on time.

### What they know
The debts of everyone in the March to the shilling. Who came to the mill after dark and how often. Which strongboxes in the vault are whose.

:::gm
They also know what the Grandmother wants with the lantern, which they will never say, and which only Odessa has fully understood.
:::

### How they treat the PCs
As entries. Courteously. A PC changing a coin gets Perrin's eyebrow; a PC asking a question gets Odessa's answer, which is the Grandmother's; a PC who follows Sal through town is noticed by the third corner and is not minded. Fennel can be bribed in lamp-oil, knows it, and is a little ashamed.

:::gm
**Secret.** Perrin works, quietly, for [[Cousin Pennamund]]: the Concord Table sent him to find out why the Grandmother is buying the March, and Perrin, who wants the books to balance and has seen what does not, leaves him notes in the margins of the ledgers he is allowed to read. She has not told him about the lantern, because she does not know; she has told him about the 600 gold under the third line, and that the pay-box went east to Fort Mercy on the House's own cart. If the party makes an ally of Pennamund, Perrin is how he gets them the House's copy of the loan-chit.

**Session 7.** On 5 Leafrot the Grandmother comes to the Keep gate in her chair, a sedan carried by eight porters. Sal walks behind the chair with the note in a leather case; Odessa stands at its right hand and speaks; Perrin stays at the mill, because somebody has to, and because she has arranged to be where Pennamund can find her. Fennel brings up the rear with the lawyers' papers and a bottle of oil for the walk.

**Fennel's price.** A Keel tin of lamp-oil buys a look at the credit-line file: Marrow's note with the third line, Fennick's countersignature, and the entry for the 600-gold advance, dated 20 Reaping and marked "to travel from Fallowden under the Regency's seal, by arrangement". Fennel does not understand what is wrong with it and will not ask.

**The prayer-ledger.** Every quarter [[Brother Audit]] lodges his ledger of prayers with the House, sealed, for carriage to the Bench at Sevenhaven. Odessa opens it and reads every page aloud to the Grandmother before Fennel reseals it with the House's own seal. Tollamund knows what Cinderford prays for, Sallow's two dated prayers included.
:::

== GM notes ==

**Voice:** the three finish each other's sentences and never contradict each other in front of a customer. Fennel talks too fast and apologises for it.

Give each a tell so the players can learn to tell them apart: Perrin's eyebrow, Odessa's small smile, Sal's habit of standing where she can see the door. The joke is that they are interchangeable; the reward for paying attention is that they are not.
