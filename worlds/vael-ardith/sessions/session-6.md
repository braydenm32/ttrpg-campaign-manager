## Session 6: Into the Rent
*Level 4. 29 Ashfall to 1 Leafrot 3011 AW: two hard days' ride east; 30 Ashfall the Unveiling on the road; 1 Leafrot (Ironday) is [[Feasts, Fasts and Holy Days|Doorsnight]], doors propped open in Cinderford till dawn.*

- *Sky*: Pallas dark the night before (the Unveiling); the Ember a thin waxing crescent, eight nights from full, low and red in the west after sunset and down within a few hours.
- *Weather*: roll twice on [[Greywind Weather]], once for each day's ride.
- *Touch these*: Marrow stays behind to hold the town; Vosk wanted to come and she would not let both of them leave. Vexa's people are at the lip, with rope if the party dealt fairly and with knives if not (scene 1). Aelith wants to come, and if she does she will try to copy the Stone and reach Uthrem first. Back home, Sallow works on the company and Mote sends a report to Caer Ardith that Kettle can copy. At the Stone, the shadow behind Borrin stands up on its own. Ysse hears both voices in the Rent, close as breath: on the rope, the other one, the one that wants to be kept ("There you are"), and at the Stone the Ninth's own tired "You came".
- *In between* (26 to 28 Ashfall): Marrow argues the expedition with Vosk and loses nothing but sleep. Pell files his Claim of Arrears and asks the party to carry a copy "should anything happen to the office". A few dozen of the Sleepless start walking east a day behind the party, to wait on the lip for the red moon; the Widow, Ambry Coyle and the rest stay in the Smokes (they are on Lantern Hill in session 7, and come east in session 8). If the Treaty Stone wasn't visited in session 5, it is on the road out. Cass's square at [[Ambercote]] is a long day's ride if her slate is still unanswered; this is the last free window before the red moon.
- *Level-up*: none. The party stays level 4 through session 7.
- *If the party…* won't go down: Uthrem comes as far as he can, which is his voice coming up the wall at dusk. Give them the truth in pieces, shouted up four hundred feet between grinding breaths; they get no rubbing, and Ysse dreams the Stone every night until she goes down alone.

**Where it is.** The Ninth Stone stands about sixty miles east of Cinderford, on the Rent's northern lip at its north-eastern end: two hard days' ride, three on foot or with a cart. Its root is on the Warden's Shelf, four hundred feet below the lip; its top hundred feet lean out over the chasm and can be seen for miles.

**Goal.** Reach the Ninth Stone, hear the truth from the one who keeps it, and come back.

### Running this session
**Last time** (read aloud). *"Old Pike sat up at his own funeral and told you the truth: the lantern was lit from a cook-fire burning on a lump of grey stone. The Rook-Court's ledger finished the sentence the dead have been whispering for nine years: the lamp is the lock. Under the cage, in the lamp's own bowl, you found a splinter of grey stone with a red crack down it. A drop of sap made the flame burn white, and for a moment Ysse saw someone in it, very large and very tired, looking at her. Marrow has decided. Someone has to go to the Ninth Stone."*

**Where the PCs are.** At the Keep gate at dawn on 29 Ashfall, with horses from the company's string and the Dunmore twins if Vosk lends them again. [[Noume Flarewick]] comes too if Ysse or Marrow asks for a signaller (see scene 3). A party taking the Cold Seam starts the same way, but turns off at the Burned Mill for Fort Mercy.

**The party should have.** [[A Glasser's Chart]] (Vexa's or Pike's copy), their wick-lamps, five days' food and water (two days out, a day at the Stone, two back; the only water on the way is at the Burned Mill and Tally's Cut), rope, and [[Hathe's Door-Key]] if Corvane let Quorl keep it. Perhaps Pell's copy of the Claim of Arrears, [[A Lithic Road-Marker]] from Ambercote, and Orla Quist (see "If the party wanders").

**If they missed something.**
- *No chart?* They can still go down: no advantage on the descent, and no hazard skipped. Vexa's people at the lip will sell them the way down if the party dealt fairly with Vexa in session 2.
- *Never found the shard?* Uthrem tells them: "There is a piece of me in your lamp. Under the flame. Look when you go home." Tallow, confronted on their return, admits it as in session 5.
- *Skipped the Treaty Stone?* It is on the road out, three miles east of town.

**The session at a glance.** The strong start is a cold open: it drops the party on the rope three hundred feet down, on the morning of 1 Leafrot. The voice on the rope is the second voice, the one that wants to be kept, wearing the Ninth's warmth; the Ninth's own "You came" comes later, at the Stone. If anyone asks Uthrem about it: "Not me. Not her. Do not answer it next time." Read the strong start, then go back two days and play the ride and the lip (scene 1) briskly, one encounter roll a day, until they are back on that rope. Short on time? Skip the ride, say what it cost them, and start at the lip. Scenes 2 to 6 are one day: the descent in the morning, Uthrem at midday, the climb out at dusk. Scenes 3 to 6 all happen at the foot of the Stone and can run in any order; keep Borrin's shadow (scene 6) for last, as they turn to go.

### Scene 1: The ride and the lip
Two hard days east along the Ash Road and then across open ash, with the Dunmore twins if Vosk lends them again. Roll on [[Ashenreach Encounters]] once each day, and on [[Travellers on the Ash Road]] once while still on the road. Water at [[The Burned Mill]] at six miles, where Nance counts them out, and at [[Tally's Cut]] at fifteen, by noon; the first night's camp is out on the open ash, thirty miles from the bridge. A party going under the glass instead (see the Cold Seam, below) sleeps the first night at Fort Mercy, and [[A Lithic Road-Marker]], laid on the floor of the black-glass chamber, points down the Seam; Ottoline Crane will show them where to lay it. On the second night Pallas is dark, the Unveiling: no white moon at all, only the Ember, a thin red sickle that follows the sun down and is gone a few hours after dusk. Quorl knows that tomorrow is Doorsnight, when the doors of Cinderford stand open till dawn so the lingering dead can leave.

> You smell it before you see it: cold stone and pears and something under both like a struck match. Then the plain simply stops. A lip of fused glass, and beyond it air, and the far side a quarter-mile off, and between, going down past where the light goes, a wall of layered rock and glass and darkness that breathes. Slowly. Out, warm, red-tinged, for the length of a long breath. Then in, cold. Along the lip in both directions, as far as you can see, figures standing, facing the gap. They do not turn round. They were not there a moment ago.

**The standing dead.** They line the lip for miles: the dead of the war, and more every red moon. They don't move while anyone watches them, and they are nearer every time you look away. The first time a PC looks back and finds one at arm's length, Wisdom save DC 13 or frightened for a minute. They don't attack. They wait. Tomorrow is Doorsnight, and when the party is back on the lip that evening, Quorl can prop a door for them: two spears and a rope across them make a doorway, and his rhyme makes it a door. Some of them walk through it, and are gone, and the rest turn their heads, very slightly, toward him.

And there is [[The Ninth Stone]], leaning out over the chasm like a drunk over a well, its crack glowing faintly from crown to root.

**Development.** The party reaches the lip at dusk on the 30th, the night of the Unveiling, and camps there: nobody climbs down a chasm in the dark. If Vexa's people are at the lip, they share a fire, or they don't. If they came with knives (the party did not deal fairly with Vexa in session 2), they are four Glasser toughs (session 2's bandits: AC 12, 11 hp, scimitar +3, 1d6+1) who want a silver a head for the use of "their" rope steps, and the chart back if the party has Vexa's. They will not start a fight with five soldiers; they make a standoff of it, and if they are refused they are still there at dusk when the party climbs out, and the price has gone up. Nobody sleeps well with the standing dead behind them. At first light on Doorsnight they go over the edge: go to scene 2, and the strong start, on the rope.

**Treasure.** Vexa's people sell good rope (a silver for fifty feet) to anyone they don't hate.

### Scene 2: The descent
From the lip: **300 feet of old company rope steps** down the north wall to the first ledge (one Athletics DC 13 check per climber for the whole climb; a failure is a slide and a burn, 2d6 bludgeoning, and a snagged rope). Then **the Glass Stair**, a long ramp of fused glass, red and clear in streaks: walk the red, never the clear (one Acrobatics DC 13 check per walker for the stair; a failure is a fall to the next turn, 2d6 bludgeoning, and lands the walker on the clear glass on a d6 roll of 1 to 3; anyone who steps or falls onto the clear wakes **the glass-stalker**). The stair comes down to **the Halfway Ledge**, where a party can camp and the Choir can be heard. **The Choir** opens in a side cleft off the stair: the leak speaks in the voices of the party's dead. Everyone passing makes a Wisdom save (DC 14); a failure costs 2d6 psychic, and the PC hears a dead face they know call them by name. Only a PC who then speaks back calls up **The Choir**: one voice for each PC who answers, up to three. A player can always grit their teeth and say nothing, and no voice follows that PC. From the Halfway Ledge a short traverse along the wall leads onto **the Warden's Shelf**.

With [[A Glasser's Chart]] (Vexa's, or Pike's own from Hennet), the party has advantage on the descent checks and can skip one hazard. Skelt's Gutter Market copies are false: they leave out the Choir and the Halfway Ledge.

**Spells down here.** Spells work, at a cost: each one frosts the walls, and the players should see it. If you are speeding up the Sleeper's clock, roll a d6 per spell and tick it on a 1 (1 or 2 for a fast clock). The same holds anywhere in the Rent or within a mile of the Ninth Stone. Ysse's player keeps her tally either way: every spell she casts in the Rent or within a mile of the Ninth Stone, cantrips included, counted from zero this session (the old notebook tally does not count). Session 8 reads it.

**The other road: the Cold Seam.** If the party went down the Cold Seam under Fort Mercy in session 2, they can come this way instead: a Lithic covered road running east under the glasslands, forty miles in the dark instead of the open ash. They sleep at Fort Mercy on the 29th and leave the horses there; Vexa's people (or the Dunmore twins, if they came) bring them round across the open ash to the lip by dusk on the 1st. Vexa lets them into the cellar for 20 gp, or for nothing if they stopped Brakk's voice. If they did not deal fairly with her in session 2, [[Ottoline Crane]] lets them in before dawn without telling her, and nobody brings the horses round: the party walks home, three days, and reaches Cinderford on the evening of the 4th.
- *The way down.* At dawn on the 30th: the black-glass chamber, ninety feet of rope, then the ladders (the extreme cold is here, and lasts well under an hour; see [[The Cold Seam]]), and at their foot the landing, where a Lithic door stands shut. [[Hathe's Door-Key]] turns it, if Corvane let Quorl keep it. Otherwise it is Athletics DC 18 to force, one try an hour, and each hour the thing that watches them try comes a little nearer Borrin.
- *The road.* Past the door the road is dry, still and only cellar-cold, with no saves, wide enough for two biers. [[A Lithic Road-Marker]] turns toward the Stone at every fork; without it, each of the three forks is a Survival DC 13 check or an hour lost. The party camps the night of the 30th at a waystation about twenty-four miles in. The thing that took Brakk walks with them in the dark, a little behind Borrin, asking questions in his voice; if the party destroyed it in session 2, Borrin's own shadow does the asking.
- *Coming out.* On the 1st they walk the last sixteen miles from first light, come out through the chasm wall onto the Lithic road somewhere below the Shelf, and climb it to the Warden's Shelf a little after midday. They climb out at dusk by the rope steps, like everyone else. For this party, read the strong start at the landing on the morning of the 30th, with the rope turned into the ladders and the knot into the last rung.

**Development.** Whichever way they came, they reach the Warden's Shelf about midday (a little after, by the Cold Seam), with the crack's light on their faces. If they are badly hurt, they can take a short rest on the Halfway Ledge first, with the Choir murmuring in the cleft above them, and every PC makes the Wisdom save once more before they go on (and may again say nothing). Go to scene 3.

### Scene 3: Warden Uthrem
> The Stone is a tower that was never built: a single finger of grey Lithic rock standing on a pillar of its own in the dark of the chasm, and down its whole length a crack a hand wide that glows the colour of the Ember. At its foot a ring of nine lesser stones, each the rough shape of a standing figure, and one of them is missing. On the step of the ninth, where the figure should be, something that was a person sits very still with its legs already grey and its hands folded in its lap, and raises its head, and the sound it makes is of two stones being drawn apart.

At the foot of the Stone, half-turned to stone himself, sits [[Warden Uthrem]]. He has stood at the Ninth Stone forty years and waited nine since the crack. He speaks on the out-breath, a few sentences at a time, and between them the stone in him grinds as he draws the next breath; he speaks more when the flame is near, and the party has five little flames at their belts. He tells the truth of the Godslaying: the Nine killed a god, and he says its name, *Ysmereth*, which only the Lithic keep, and they couldn't finish it; they became the lid and the lock; the youngest of the Nine is still awake inside this stone, and the crack is bleeding her dry. The flame is hers. It must come home, and the ward needs a heart: "a heart that chooses". Early on, Uthrem looks past Borrin once, at the ground behind him, and says nothing yet (scene 6).

**What else he knows, if asked.**
- **The digs.** "The Emperor's diggers cut at my stone's foot. In 2979. With picks, and with songs I did not like. I could not move to stop them." And: "He dug at three of us. The wet hill. The dwarves' stair. Mine. Count the wards that are failing, soldier. You will count to three."
- **Who struck first at Cinders.** "Not an army. One of the Emperor's watchers. They were still here, watching my stone, because no one had told them to stop. When the parley crowded the ford, one of them was afraid. I felt the first spell. It was very small."
- **The Cinders-born.** "A ward is a lid on a sleeper's dreams. Where it cracks, the dreams come out, and the first thing a dream touches is what has no walls yet. The newborn. Born on the night it cracked, they grow toward the crack. Fast." He looks at Tick for a long time.
- **The three flashes.** Three lamps are cut into the rock of the Shelf beside the ring, with a Lithic line under them; Noume, or whoever stands where Noume would, knows the shape at once. Uthrem reads the line aloud: "*Holding. Send relief.* It is the Wardens' lamp-code. I saw the flame fail on your hill, and I signalled. Someone there saw it. I think you are the relief."
- **What a heart means.** "The lamp holds the lock; the heart holds the lamp." The shard goes back into its socket, ten feet up inside the crack (he can point; they can see the hollow, and a thread of red light running west from it under the glass), and a living heart goes in with it, willingly. His own, if someone will carry him into the crack: it kills him and holds the ward twenty years. One of theirs: it holds for a lifetime, and whoever goes in stands in the stone, awake, for all of it. Ysse's, whom the flame has already chosen: it holds an age. He says all this plainly, and will not say which he hopes for.

**Development.** Uthrem tires as the afternoon goes on; each answer costs him a breath he may not have. Let the players ask what they want, and when they run out of questions, he turns to Ysse: go to scene 4. If Ysse isn't with them, he asks after "the one who keeps the flame", and the scene is a message for her.

### Scene 4: He waited for Ysse
Uthrem says Ysse's name before she gives it. He has heard her in the flame. He asks her to stand with him. He does not insist. He is very kind about it, which is worse.

If she asks about the voices: Uthrem confirms that the first voice is the Ninth's, the one that says *stand*. "A second borrows it. It wears her voice like a coat. It will not ask you to stand. It will ask you to keep."

**Development.** Ysse doesn't have to answer him today, and Uthrem says so. He asks only that they take the Stone's words home with them: go to scene 5.

---

### Scene 5: The rubbing
Ysse (or anyone) takes [[A Rubbing from the Ninth Stone]]. With Uthrem's help, they read it: carry the flame to the stone; give it a heart that chooses; "do not listen when it asks to be kept".

**Development.** The light in the crack dims as the sun leaves the chasm's top. It is time to climb. As the party turns to go, go to scene 6.

**Treasure.** [[A Rubbing from the Ninth Stone]]. If Aelith came with them, she wants a copy, and will pay for one or take one.

---

### Scene 6: Borrin's shadow
At the foot of the Stone, Borrin's shadow stands up from the ground on its own, looks at the Stone, and bows to it. Then it looks at Borrin and says, in his voice: "Not yet." Uthrem names it, and is afraid of it: something from the Eighth Ward, a door-warden that came up with him. It wants to see what the Ninth does.

**Development.** The climb out is the descent in reverse, and easier: the ropes are fixed and the party knows where the clear glass is (advantage on the checks, and the glass-stalker, if it lives, keeps to the clear glass and lets them pass on the red: it only hunts what steps wrong). They reach the lip at dusk. Go to "How it ends".

### How it ends
The climb out at dusk. At the top, a rider from Cinderford is waiting, exhausted, his horse blown: "Captain says come home now. They're all moving at once." He is one of Gerda's wicks, and he left at dawn on the 30th with a note in Marrow's hand: the Concord's lawyers have served notice for the Reeve's court on the 5th; Sallow's men meet in the Hock's back room every night, confined or not; the Assessor's clerk has sent a sealed packet downriver; and red surcoats have been seen on the Ash Road by the House of Unburnt Light. "She says the 4th is the last quiet day, and she wants you home for it."

Nobody rides the ash by night. The party camps on the lip for Doorsnight, with the rider and the standing dead, and Quorl's door (scene 1), if he makes one, is the last scene of the session.

**Development.** Two days' hard ride home, the 2nd and the 3rd, and nothing on the road but ash and a few Sleepless pilgrims walking east, who do not look up. The party rides into Cinderford on the evening of the 3rd and finds the company half-turned (session 7, "In between"). Session 7 opens on the morning of the 5th, so the party has the 4th: a day to rest, talk to Marrow and get ready.

### If the party wanders
- **Orla's cairn.** If [[Orla Quist]] came along (3 gp and a copy of the sheet), she paces the near cairn at the Rent's western end, thirty-eight miles out by the Ash Road, and gets thirty-three. She says nothing for a mile, then: "I owe a Glasser an apology." The 3011 line on her slate is no longer blank.
- **Doorsnight at home.** Tonight every door in the Smokes and the Keep's hall door stand propped till dawn, and the Ravelers sit in the Doorkeeper's Chapel's black doorway in a row. When the party gets home on the 3rd, the doors are shut again and the town is quieter than it was.
- **Hollowhall, by letter.** If a dwarf PC or Gerda asks what the Standing costs, a letter to [[Hold-Mother Greta Holm]] brings an answer. Sent now it is too late to bring her to the Stone for the 9th: she needs a fortnight's warning.

== Strong start ==

The rope is old company rope, tarred and knotted, and three hundred feet down it runs out. Ysse's hand is on the knot when the voice comes up out of the dark, warm and slow and patient: "There you are. I have waited such a long time." Everyone heard it; it was meant for one.
