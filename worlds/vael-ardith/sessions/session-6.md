## Session 6: Into the Rent
*Level 4. 29 Ashfall to 1 Leafrot 3011 AW: two hard days' ride east; 30 Ashfall the Unveiling on the road; 1 Leafrot (Ironday) is [[Feasts, Fasts and Holy Days|Doorsnight]], doors propped open in Cinderford till dawn.*

- *Sky*: Pallas dark the night before (the Unveiling); the Ember a waxing half, eight nights from full, red over the Rent.
- *Weather*: roll twice on [[Greywind Weather]], once for each day's ride.
- *Touch these*: Marrow stays behind to hold the town; Vosk wanted to come and she would not let both of them leave. Vexa's people are at the lip, with rope if the party dealt fairly and with knives if not. Aelith wants to come, and if she does she will try to copy the Stone and reach Uthrem first. Back home, Sallow works on the company and Mote sends a report to Caer Ardith that Kettle can copy. At the Stone, the shadow behind Borrin stands up on its own. Ysse hears both voices here, close as breath: "You came," in the warm one, and in the other, "There you are."
- *In between* (26 to 28 Ashfall): Marrow argues the expedition with Vosk and loses nothing but sleep. Pell files his Claim of Arrears and asks the party to carry a copy "should anything happen to the office". The Sleepless start walking east a day behind the party. If the Treaty Stone wasn't visited in session 5, it is on the road out. Cass's square at [[Ambercote]] is a long day's ride if her slate is still unanswered; this is the last free window before the red moon.
- *Level-up*: none. The party stays level 4 through session 7.
- *If the party…* won't go down: Uthrem comes as far as he can, which is his voice coming up the wall at dusk. Give them the truth in pieces, shouted up three hundred feet between grinding breaths; they get no rubbing, and Ysse dreams the Stone every night until she goes down alone.

**Where it is.** The Ninth Stone stands about sixty miles east of Cinderford, on the Rent's northern lip at its north-eastern end: two hard days' ride, three on foot or with a cart. Its root is on the Warden's Shelf, four hundred feet below the lip; its top hundred feet lean out over the chasm and can be seen for miles.

**Goal.** Reach the Ninth Stone, hear the truth from the one who keeps it, and come back.

---

### Scene 1: The ride and the lip
Two hard days east along the Ash Road and then across open ash, with the Dunmore twins if Vosk lends them again. Roll on [[Ashenreach Encounters]] once each day, and on [[Travellers on the Ash Road]] once while still on the road. Water at [[The Burned Mill]] at six miles, where Nance counts them out, and at [[Tally's Cut]] at fifteen, by noon; the first night's camp is out on the open ash, thirty miles from the bridge. A party going under the glass instead (see the Cold Seam, below) sleeps the first night at Fort Mercy, and [[A Lithic Road-Marker]], laid on the floor of the black-glass chamber, points down the Seam; Ottoline Crane will show them where to lay it. On the second night Pallas is dark, the Unveiling: no white moon at all, only the Ember. Quorl knows that tomorrow is Doorsnight, when the doors of Cinderford stand open till dawn so the lingering dead can leave.

> You smell it before you see it: cold stone and pears and something under both like a struck match. Then the plain simply stops. A lip of fused glass, and beyond it air, and the far side a quarter-mile off, and between, going down past where the light goes, a wall of layered rock and glass and darkness that breathes. Slowly. Out, warm, red-tinged, for the length of a long breath. Then in, cold. Along the lip in both directions, as far as you can see, figures standing, facing the gap. They do not turn round. They were not there a moment ago.

**The standing dead.** They line the lip for miles: the dead of the war, and more every red moon. They don't move while anyone watches them, and they are nearer every time you look away. The first time a PC looks back and finds one at arm's length, Wisdom save DC 13 or frightened for a minute. They don't attack. They wait. On Doorsnight, Quorl can prop a door for them: two spears and a rope across them make a doorway, and his rhyme makes it a door. Some of them walk through it, and are gone, and the rest turn their heads, very slightly, toward him.

And there is [[The Ninth Stone]], leaning out over the chasm like a drunk over a well, its crack glowing faintly from crown to root.

---

### Scene 2: The descent
From the lip: **300 feet of old company rope steps** down the north wall to the first ledge (Athletics DC 13 each; a failure is a slide and a burn, 2d6 bludgeoning, and a snagged rope). Then **the Glass Stair**, a long ramp of fused glass, red and clear in streaks: walk the red, never the clear (Acrobatics DC 13 or a fall to the next turn of the stair, 2d6; stepping on the clear wakes **the glass-stalker**). The stair comes down to **the Halfway Ledge**, where a party can camp and the Choir can be heard. **The Choir** opens in a side cleft off the stair: the leak speaks in the voices of the party's dead (Wisdom save DC 14 or answer them and take 2d6 psychic; anyone who answers calls up **The Choir**). From the Halfway Ledge a short traverse along the wall leads onto **the Warden's Shelf**.

With [[A Glasser's Chart]] (Vexa's, or Pike's own from Hennet), the party has advantage on the descent checks and can skip one hazard. Skelt's Gutter Market copies are false: they leave out the Choir and the Halfway Ledge.

**Spells down here.** Spells work, at a cost: each one frosts the walls; roll a d6, and on a 1 the Sleeper's clock ticks. (The same holds anywhere in the Rent or within a mile of the Ninth Stone. Ysse's player should be keeping her tally.)

**The other road: the Cold Seam.** If the party went down the Cold Seam under Fort Mercy in session 2, they can come this way instead: a Lithic covered road running east under the glasslands, forty miles in the dark instead of the open ash, through a Lithic door on the landing that Hathe's Door-Key turns (if Corvane let Quorl keep it after the funeral; otherwise it takes an hour and Athletics DC 18 to force, while something watches them try). The thing that took Brakk walks with them in the dark, a little behind Borrin, asking questions in his voice. The road comes up the chasm floor and climbs to the Warden's Shelf from below.

---

### Scene 3: Warden Uthrem
> The Stone is a tower that was never built: a single finger of grey Lithic rock standing on a pillar of its own in the dark of the chasm, and down its whole length a crack a hand wide that glows the colour of the Ember. At its foot a ring of nine lesser stones, each the rough shape of a standing figure, and one of them is missing. On the step of the ninth, where the figure should be, something that was a person sits very still with its legs already grey and its hands folded in its lap, and raises its head, and the sound it makes is of two stones being drawn apart.

At the foot of the Stone, half-turned to stone himself, sits [[Warden Uthrem]]. He has stood at the Ninth Stone forty years and waited nine since the crack. He speaks on the out-breath, a few sentences at a time, and between them the stone in him grinds as he draws the next breath; he speaks more when the flame is near, and the party has five little flames at their belts. He tells the truth of the Godslaying: the Nine killed a god and couldn't finish it; they became the lid and the lock; the youngest of the Nine is still awake inside this stone, and the crack is bleeding her dry. The flame is hers. It must come home, and the ward needs a heart: "a heart that chooses".

**What else he knows, if asked.**
- **The digs.** "The Emperor's diggers cut at my stone's foot. In 2979. With picks, and with songs I did not like. I could not move to stop them." And: "He dug at three of us. The wet hill. The dwarves' stair. Mine. Count the wards that are failing, soldier. You will count to three."
- **Who struck first at Cinders.** "Not an army. One of the Emperor's watchers. They were still here, watching my stone, because no one had told them to stop. When the parley crowded the ford, one of them was afraid. I felt the first spell. It was very small."
- **The Cinders-born.** "A ward is a lid on a sleeper's dreams. Where it cracks, the dreams come out, and the first thing a dream touches is what has no walls yet. The newborn. Born on the night it cracked, they grow toward the crack. Fast." He looks at Tick for a long time.
- **The three flashes.** "I signalled. Three. *Come.* It is the Wardens' lamp-code. Someone on your hill answered, though they did not know what they said."

---

### Scene 4: He waited for Ysse
Uthrem says Ysse's name before she gives it. He has heard her in the flame. He asks her to stand with him. He does not insist. He is very kind about it, which is worse.

If she asks about the voices: Uthrem confirms that the first voice is the Ninth's, the one that says *stand*. "A second borrows it. It wears her voice like a coat. It will not ask you to stand. It will ask you to keep."

---

### Scene 5: The rubbing
Ysse (or anyone) takes [[A Rubbing from the Ninth Stone]]. With Uthrem's help, they read it: carry the flame to the stone; give it a heart that chooses; "do not listen when it asks to be kept".

---

### Scene 6: Borrin's shadow
At the foot of the Stone, Borrin's shadow stands up from the ground on its own, looks at the Stone, and bows to it. Then it looks at Borrin and says, in his voice: "Not yet." Uthrem names it, and is afraid of it: something from the Eighth Ward, a door-warden that came up with him. It wants to see what the Ninth does.

---

### How it ends
The climb out at dusk. At the top, a rider from Cinderford is waiting, exhausted, his horse blown: "Captain says come home now. They're all moving at once."

### If the party wanders
- **Orla's cairn.** If [[Orla Quist]] came along (3 gp and a copy of the sheet), she paces the near cairn at the Rent's western end, thirty-eight miles out by the Ash Road, and gets thirty-three. She says nothing for a mile, then: "I owe a Glasser an apology." The 3011 line on her slate is no longer blank.
- **Doorsnight at home.** Tonight every door in the Smokes and the Keep's hall door stand propped till dawn, and the Ravelers sit in the Doorkeeper's Chapel's black doorway in a row. When the party gets home on the 3rd, the doors are shut again and the town is quieter than it was.
- **Hollowhall, by letter.** If a dwarf PC or Gerda asks what the Standing costs, a letter to [[Hold-Mother Greta Holm]] brings an answer. Sent now it is too late to bring her to the Stone for the 9th: she needs a fortnight's warning.

== Strong start ==

The rope is old company rope, tarred and knotted, and three hundred feet down it runs out. Ysse's hand is on the knot when the voice comes up out of the dark, warm and slow and patient: "There you are. I have waited such a long time." Everyone heard it; it was meant for one.
