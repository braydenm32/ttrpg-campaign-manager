## Session 4: The House of Unburnt Light
*Level 3. Coinday, 20 Ashfall 3011 AW.*

- *Sky*: Pallas past full and waning; the Ember waning too, rising later each night, its glow gone to copper.
- *Weather*: clear and cold, the first clean sky in a month. You can see the House's white wall from the Keep.
- *Touch these*: Tallow wakes nearly blind, names Ysse keeper in front of witnesses, and warns her off Seraphine without saying why. Fennick comes back grey-faced from a visit by Tollamund's clerk. If Sallow isn't confined, Old Pike watches him buy a round and says, to nobody: "Seventh had a lieutenant like that. Generous man. We buried him first." Corvane sends to ask Quorl, casually, whether anyone has said anything "odd" lately. At the House gate a dog barks at the empty air behind Borrin until a Lamp-Warden carries it off. After the House, Ysse dreams in the other voice, the sweeter one: *Somewhere warm. Keep me somewhere warm.*
- *In between* (17 to 19 Ashfall): Tallow sleeps a day and wakes nearly blind. Sallow's people go quiet and careful. If anyone pries into the mutiny, Bray is told to "have a word" with them, and brings friends. Kettle's Cargo can run any of these nights. Nib, the Assessor's errand boy, paid in biscuits, knows Mote keeps a second ledger bound in black and locks it in the desk; for a bag of biscuits he'll leave the window unlatched, any time between now and session 5. Today is Coinday, when the post-bag leaves for Caer Ardith: Kettle can copy a page of Mote's for the party, for silver or for friendship ("Report on the lamp. Progress satisfactory."). Stillwater (Gammer Teal) and Ambercote (Cass's slate) are each a long day's ride; see session 3's detours.
- *Level-up*: none. The party stays level 3 through session 5.
- *If the party…* ignores the letter, Seraphine comes to them: to the Keep gate at noon with the little girl, a cart of hot bread for the whole company, and an invitation to Marrow that she makes in front of a hundred hungry soldiers. Sallow loses his audience for a day. Run the private moments at the gate while the bread is handed out, one PC at a time. The garden door scene can still happen; somebody in the party will want to see the House after that.

**Goal.** Learn what the Saint's church wants with the flame, and survive its kindness.

### Running this session
**Last time** (read aloud). *"The red moon came up full and the whole town dreamed one dream. The Sleepless sat round Lantern Hill all night, singing under their breath. At midnight the lantern guttered again, Brother Tallow fell on the ladder, and Ysse held the flame with the first key in her hand. By dawn somebody had talked about who was at the oil store. That was three days ago. Tallow woke, nearly blind. The company is quiet, and careful, and watching Lieutenant Sallow."*

**Where the PCs are.** At the Keep on the morning of 20 Ashfall, a Coinday, after three quieter days (see "In between"). Ysse has been keeper for three nights and has hardly slept. The letter arrives at the gate mid-morning.

**The party should have.** The first cage key (Ysse), proof or a witness against Sallow (Spoons's word, Bray's confession, or the letter in the small neat hand), whatever they decided about the 600 gold, and Cass's slate ([[The Standing Village]]) for Tick. Maybe the jar of Stillwater ash from the oil store.

**If they missed something.**
- *Nothing against Sallow yet?* Scene 5 waits. Bray is still the weak link, and he has booked two horses at the Bridle & Yoke in his own name (session 7, "Two horses"), which a party asking at the stables can find now. If the party still has nothing by session 7, [[Brother Audit]] can read Sallow's prayers aloud in the yard.
- *The flame went fully out in session 3?* Nothing here changes. Tallow simply says less, and Seraphine says more.
- *The strongbox argument never happened?* Fennick brings it up at breakfast with the ledger open: the House wants its 480 gold, and someone has to decide.
- *Vosk fetched the box instead of the party?* Open with Fennick's sums and the argument from session 3, scene 2, then the letter.
- *Marrow already confined Sallow on the 17th?* Scene 5 is done: he is behind his door, and Bray is the one who is frightened.

**The session at a glance.** Six scenes over one day and night, and the middle of it is the party's to arrange. The usual order: the letter in the morning (scene 1); the House in the afternoon and over supper (scene 2); the Velvet Ledger late that night, where Tesh starts calling after midnight (scenes 3 and 4); the garden door on the walk home, if they go back (scene 6). If the party goes to the Ledger first, at noon when it is quiet, Sorrel comes to the House supper as their guest and Tesh's calling happens as they walk back past the Ledger that night. Scene 5, Sallow, happens whenever they bring their proof to Marrow: at the Keep before they leave, or the next morning. Nothing here needs a fight unless the party picks one.

### Scene 1: The letter
A child in a clean white smock brings [[A Perfumed Letter]] to the Keep gate and waits for an answer. Everyone at the Keep has an opinion. Vosk: "Go. Eat everything. Sign nothing." Tallow, from his bed, blind: "Don't let her hold the lamp. Don't let her hold *you*." He will not say why.

**Development.** The girl waits for an answer and will walk the party back to the House herself, chattering about the pears. If they accept, go to scene 2 in the early afternoon. If they want Sorrel's view first ("Sorrel knows Seraphine" is the kind of thing Marrow lets slip, or Hennet), go to scene 3 at noon and come back to scene 2. If they refuse, see "If the party…" above: Seraphine comes to them at noon. Ysse can't leave the lantern for long; someone else holds the first key while she's gone (Marrow takes it, unhappily), or Ysse stays, and Seraphine walks up the hill herself at dusk with a basket of the House's bread for the keeper: run Ysse's private moment at the foot of the ladder, gold eyes and all, with Marrow watching from the wall.

### Scene 2: The House
> A mile out on the Ash Road a wall rises on the right, whitewashed, the only white thing between here and the sky, and over it the tops of trees. Green trees. Through the gate a courtyard swept clean of ash, a fountain running, a long low house with a lamp burning in every window in daylight, and the smell of bread.

[[The House of Unburnt Light]] is warm, beautiful and a little too comfortable: hot bread from Brother Caddis's ovens, an infirmary of thirty clean beds where the company's worst cases are sitting up and laughing, the pear garden, a bath-house fed from the fountain, Halcyran wine, and a lutenist at supper. [[Seraphine Ashe]] receives them in white and saffron. She is gracious, curious, and very good at listening. She offers to "save" the flame: carry it to Halcyra, where the Saint will keep it safe forever. If the party says yes, Seraphine is delighted and in no hurry: the lantern is the Captain's to give and Marrow won't give it this week, so her wardens will come for it in Leafrot. Tick the Saint's clock. The yes stands until someone takes it back in public, which rolls the clock back one.

Twice while they are at the House (once in the afternoon, once at supper), each PC makes a Wisdom save (DC 13). On a failure they are charmed by Seraphine until they leave the House's wall: play it as wanting to agree. They won't argue with her or speak against her (they can stay silent), and their Insight checks against anyone in white have disadvantage. A PC who fails both wants to stay the night, and says so. Nothing here makes anyone hand over the flame; it only makes saying no feel unkind. She courts Marrow openly, through the party: the best guest room, a standing invitation, an offer of the Saint's coin for the company's arrears.

**The private moments.** Each PC gets a few minutes alone with Seraphine, in the garden or the bath-house or the Lamp Hall, and in each one, once, something else looks out: her amber eyes burn gold and her voice drops, warm and patient and much older. Keep it allusive. She offers each the thing they want most:
- **Ysse**: the library at Halcyra, every folio on every lamp, and the answer at the end of them.
- **Quorl**: Hathe's voice. "He isn't gone. She keeps him. Would you like to hear him?"
- **Tick**: Marla at rest. "Your mother stands in rivers. She's so tired, Tibbet."
- **Borrin**: his shadow back, or the three and a half years he lost underground.
- **Varra**: her name restored by the Mothers, said aloud at the council fire.

**Sorrel at supper.** [[Sorrel Vey]] comes to the House supper whether the party asked her or not, in black. If they have been to the Ledger (scene 3), she is their guest. If they haven't, she arrives late, uninvited by anyone, because she heard the Keep's people were dining with Seraphine and would not miss it. At some point she says it to Seraphine's face, once, on purpose, across the table: that she is Ilse Marrow's lover. Seraphine smiles and says she's glad the captain has someone. Nobody else at the table will ever forget it.

**Development.** Supper runs late, and the House offers beds. A party that stays the night wakes rested and a little too happy (each PC who stays makes the Wisdom save once more at breakfast). A party that leaves after supper walks a mile back along the Ash Road in the dark, and on Temple Row the Velvet Ledger's lamps are still lit: go to scene 3. A party that has already been to the Ledger goes home, and may want to come back to the House uninvited: scene 6. If anyone was healed at the House, or accepted the Saint's coin for the arrears, tick the Saint's clock.

**Treasure.** The House gives freely: a loaf, a bottle of Halcyran wine, a clean shirt for anyone who needs one. Seraphine leaves [[The Saint's Candle-Stub]] with whoever seemed most in pain, "for the bad nights". Her offer of the Saint's coin for the company's arrears stands until session 7.

### Scene 3: The Velvet Ledger
[[Mourn]], the Raveler doorkeeper, lets them in, and laughs at the wrong things. [[Sorrel Vey]] trades secrets for secrets. She knows the Saint's methods ("my goddess's arts, done by someone in a hurry"), and for the right secret tells the party what the church really wants: the Ninth Ward's crack to open, so the Saint can drink what leaks. For a price, and to spite her rival, she'll help them against Seraphine.

It is Coinday, so unless Marrow has confined him, [[Garrick Sallow]] is in the parlour, charming, buying the party a drink and asking after their families. He leaves before Tesh starts calling.

[[Aelith Sorn]] is on the Ledger's balcony, visiting Sorrel. She has been courting her for a month and makes, tonight, a formal offer for "a transcription". Sorrel refuses, pleasantly.

**Tesh.** Just after midnight the house goes quiet, and then someone upstairs starts calling a name, over and over: the name of one of the PCs, in a voice that isn't hers, slow, warm, patient, in the cadence of the Sleeper's voice from the dream. It is [[Tesh]], the courtesan who does not sleep. Sorrel goes up to her and comes down pale. "She does that. Lately she does it every night." Insight DC 15 on Sorrel: she knows something about Tesh she is not saying. (She is the Regency's lost line: imperial blood through a by-blow of Aurel IX's uncle, and the Regent's own claimant in the capital has stopped speaking. Mote knows. See session 7.)

**Development.** Sorrel's help depends on the trade. A secret the party gives her that she can use (Sallow's arrangement, the loan-chit's clause, what the Barrow said) buys her answer about the church (and, if supper is still to come, her company at it as their guest). A secret that costs the party something (a PC's own) buys her trust, and with it scene 4. If the party leaves with nothing to trade, Sorrel gives them one thing free, because she wants Seraphine to lose: "Ask her what she does with the ones she saves."

**Treasure.** A [[Velvet Ledger Token]], from Sorrel, for whoever she liked best.

### Scene 4: The ink
If the party wins her trust, Sorrel shows them the oldest of her tattoos, under the gown, down her spine. Saelish script, faded and re-inked by later hands; a list of names; a ward-sign. Nobody in the March can read it. (It's the record of the Saelish Trespass that Sael chose to forget; [[Aelith Sorn]] can read one line in five, and would do almost anything to read the rest.)

**Development.** The ink is a seed for session 7, when Aelith makes her move on Sorrel. The party doesn't need to understand it yet; it is enough that they know Aelith wants it badly. From here, they go back out to the House uninvited (scene 6), or straight up the hill to Marrow (scene 5).

### Scene 5: Sallow exposed, or not
With Spoons's word (or Bray's, or the letter in the small neat hand) and the coin, the party can bring [[Garrick Sallow]] to Marrow. He denies everything, then smiles: "You'd hang an officer on the word of a bookie?" Marrow confines him to quarters. The mutiny goes quiet and underground.

If the party tries to take him without Marrow, Sallow stays behind his door and Bray comes out of the barracks with two friends: **Sallow's loyalists** (see Encounters).

**Development.** Confined, Sallow is quiet and patient, and his people go underground: the Mutiny clock doesn't move: confined isn't exposed. The −1 for exposing him comes when the proof is said in front of the company in the yard (session 7). Not confined (no proof, or Marrow won't act), he buys a round at the Candle and Old Pike says his line about the Seventh's lieutenant. Either way, Bray is frightened now, and a frightened man is the party's best witness in session 7.

### Scene 6: The garden door (night)
> The garden wall is lower at the bottom, where the pears are, and the door in it is open, as it always is. No lock; there has never been a lock. Inside, the pear trees stand very still, and between them, just at the edge of the lamplight, there are more people than there should be: standing, facing the house, not talking. In the Lamp Hall a single voice is praying, and then, under it, answering it, a second.

If the party comes back to the House at night, uninvited: Seraphine at prayer in the Lamp Hall, speaking in two voices, her own and something warmer. On her desk in the next room, a letter to Halcyra, the ink still wet: "The flame's new keeper is a Vesh girl. Bring her to me." The dead among the pears don't move, speak or stop anyone; they stand and watch the house. Two Lamp-Wardens walk the house at night. Reaching the desk and getting out again unseen is a group Dexterity (Stealth) check, DC 13. On a failure a Warden finds them in the corridor and asks them, kindly, to come back by daylight. If they refuse to leave politely, run **The Lamp-Wardens** (one, called off at first blood).

**Development.** Seraphine never mentions the night visit. She knows. From now on her letters to the Keep are warmer, and addressed to Ysse. Go to "How it ends".

**Treasure.** The letter to Halcyra, if they take it, with the ink still wet.

### How it ends
However the night went, the party is home by morning, and whoever ate at the House feels better than they should. On the hill, Ysse tends the lantern, and the flame leans toward the east, toward Halcyra, like a plant toward a window. If Ysse accepted the House's invitation, tick the Saint's clock (on top of anything scene 2 ticked); if the party refused the House in public instead, roll it back one.

### If the party wanders
- **Market day at the Candle.** Pennamund's clerks come for Mother Hennet's slate-book, with an offer to clear every tab on the slate. They are polite and will wait a day. Hennet asks the party what it is worth: [[The Price Was an Arm]]. [[Brother Audit]] can certify it.
- **Bellwether.** Marn gives up the jar, its dates and Ambry Coyle's name for a forgiven season of goats (forty beasts, about forty silver in toll). The next salt-tow window is Tideday 22 Ashfall, two days off: [[The Unmarked Barrel]].
- **Bray.** He was a boy of eleven in the line of children past the Tree at [[Dunmeer]] in 2996. The story told the Dunmeer way, or a strand of the Tree's rope put in his hand, turns him before it comes to scimitars.
- **The Salt Lake.** The Soran sisters ban priests and forget nothing; on Hearthdays the Reeve and three Tollamund clerks talk charter through the steam in the alcoves. Ysolde's brother went into a Mercy House at Halcyra in 3007 and has not come out, which is worth knowing before the party walks into the House. See [[The Salt Lake]].
- **The Turncoat.** Bask's order book: six lengths of white-and-gold, ordered by Brother Iosune on 12 Ashfall, "by Leafrot, for six". That is two days before the lantern first guttered. See [[The Turncoat]].
- **Orla Quist.** [[Orla Quist]] has her father's Ardmere ledger, and Marrow is in its margin: she told him the truth about the stores, early. A quiet scene for whoever is Marrow's friend, before session 7.
- **A note** by Kettle's cousins, in a Counting-House hand: "The lamp is on a list. I thought you should know. F.W." It is from [[Fallon Wrass]], the Sevenhaven clerk who told Kettle the Table had bought the ash.
