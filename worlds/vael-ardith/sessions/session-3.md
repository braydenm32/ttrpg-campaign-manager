## Session 3: Red Moon Rising
*Level 2. Tideday, 16 Ashfall 3011 AW: from the small hours at Fort Mercy to dawn on the 17th. The full Ember. Tick the Sleeper's clock at the start.*

- *Sky*: the Ember full, the Saint's Lamp. It rises at dusk low and huge over the Ashenreach and rides the whole night; Pallas, a night past full, rises white beside it.
- *Weather*: still all day, heavy and warm; after dark, lightning without thunder far out over the glass.
- *Touch these*: Vosk holds the gate against the sleepwalkers without killing one, which is harder. Old Pike isn't by the Candle fire tonight; he is at the foot of the Keep wall under the cage, laying a ring of salt. Marrow stands on the wall all night. Sallow, after the guttering, says what everyone is thinking: "It's going out on her watch." Spoons closes the lantern pool "for maintenance", and soldiers notice. A Sleepless child, Sabe Hallows, waves at the shadow behind Borrin, and it waves back. Pell hides under his desk with a candle filling in an Incident Report; Mote, alone in the office, is the only person in town not afraid. Ysse, tending the flame, hears "Stand with me" and, under it, "Keep me."
- *In between*: none. This session opens a few hours after session 2 ends, in the Glassers' yard at Fort Mercy.
- *Level-up*: End of session: level 3.
- *If the party…* breaks the vigil by force: the Sleepless don't fight back unless attacked; every one hurt sends twenty more out of the Smokes by midnight, ticks the Sleepless clock, and turns half the town against the company. Vosk shuts the gate on anyone who has drawn blood on townsfolk.

**The Sleeper's clock.** It starts the campaign at 3 of 12 (one segment each for the Fourth Ward weak, the Eighth breached, the Ninth cracked). It ticks +1 at each full Ember (tonight, and session 8), so left alone it reaches 5 by session 8. If you want it faster, turn on accelerators from "Speeding it up" on [[The Sleeper Below]]: the obvious one tonight is +1 if the Lantern goes fully out for more than a breath (if Ysse fails the tending). Spells in the Barrow and the Cold Seam frost the walls but never tick it. At 6 the dream reaches the whole March and the dead of Cinders stand on the Rent's lip; 9 and 12 belong to the aftermath of the darker endings.

**Goal.** Keep the lantern lit through the worst night of the year, and meet the Sleepless face to face.

### Running this session
**Last time** (read aloud). *"You found the pay: six hundred gold in an iron box in a Glassers' cellar, in coin that isn't the Regency's, with a paper saying the company owes it back with interest. You bedded down in Fort Mercy's yard under two moons, one white and one red. And in the small hours, everyone in the fort woke at once."*

**Where the PCs are.** In the Glassers' yard at Fort Mercy, in their blankets by the windmill, with the strongbox at their feet. The strong start wakes them.

**The party should have.** The strongbox and [[The Concord Loan-Chit]], [[The Cut Oil Tin]], [[Dannic's Note]], and [[A Glasser's Chart]] if they traded for it. Perhaps [[Old Tally's Coin]] from Ferrow Gant, and Skelt's confession.

**If they missed something.**
- *Never went east, or came home without the box?* Vosk has gone for it instead. The party wakes in the Keep: play scene 1 there (the dream reached Cinderford too), and when Vosk rides in with the box on the morning of the 17th, play Fennick's sums and the argument over the money as the opening of session 4.
- *No chart?* Hennet hands over Pike's own copy in session 5, and Vexa will still trade if they go back.
- *Never matched Mote's hand to the poster?* It doesn't matter yet. The unsigned letter in Sallow's room (scene 7) is in the same small neat hand, for whoever compares them.

**The session at a glance.** Scene 1 is the small hours at Fort Mercy and scene 2 the ride home and the day at the Keep. Scenes 3 to 7 are one long night on Lantern Hill, and they are not a strict sequence. Scene 3 at dusk sets the stage. Scene 4 comes when the Widow sends for Tick, any time before midnight. Scene 5 is midnight. Scene 6 happens during scene 5, while every eye is on the cage, and scene 7 is whenever the party corners Spoons, usually towards dawn. **This is the session to split the party**: one or two on the ladder with Ysse, one at the oil store, one in the crowd with Tick. Cut between them every few minutes, and leave the cage for last.

### Scene 1: The dream (the small hours, Fort Mercy)
Everyone in the fort wakes at the same moment from the same dream: a vast warm dark, a voice saying "nearly", a door with no handle (roll on [[Sleeper Dreams]] for each PC's version). Tick, being Hollowborn, wakes weeping and can't say why. Glassers are standing in their tent-mouths in their shirts looking at each other. Mardle Ock is humming. On the tower, Vexa says to nobody, "Well, that's new." A PC wearing [[A Dreamless Charm]] sleeps through it and has nothing to compare in the morning; a stone pear in anyone's pocket is warm ([[Stone Pears]]).

It reached twenty-two miles. When the party rides home they will find it reached all of Cinderford too.

**Development.** Nobody goes back to sleep. At first light Vexa sees them off from the tower ("Tell the factor I was robbed, and tell him nicely"), and the ride home is twenty-two miles in the clear, still morning after the greywind. If the party has unfinished business at the fort (the Cold Seam, Skelt, the crown), it can wait or they can stay a few hours, but Tallow's warning in scene 2 needs them home before dusk. Go to scene 2.

**Treasure.** A stone pear warm in someone's pocket, if anyone bought one ([[Stone Pears]]).

---

### Scene 2: The ride home (morning)
Cinderford has had the dream. A third of the town is still standing about in the street at breakfast. On the doors of the Smokes, overnight, someone has chalked an open eye behind a closed lid. [[Odile Fairweather]] meets the party at the bridge, frightened and hiding it under a green hat: "Half my town dreamed the same dream. You're soldiers. What does it mean?"

At the Keep, [[Fennick Bottlebrook]] counts the strongbox twice and goes white. "With the interest on the chit, we owe Tollamund more than we did yesterday. Four thousand eight hundred gold. And four hundred and eighty of it was due on the fourteenth." Then [[Ilse Marrow]], quietly, in her own room with the door shut: she has been paying the company out of her own savings for a year, and she is out. Her locked box holds one gold piece and a letter she doesn't open in front of them.

**What to do with 600 gold that isn't theirs.** Let the party argue it with Marrow:
- Pay the company with it: Mutiny clock −1, the debt grows.
- Pay the 480 interest: the debt clock steps back, the Mutiny clock ticks, and Tollamund still comes, calling the box stolen House property.
- Give it back to Vexa, or bury it: the Glassers remember.

[[Brother Tallow]], at the foot of the ladder, his antennae turned east: "Tonight. Stay close to the lantern tonight."

**Development.** The afternoon is the party's to prepare in, and a party that prepares should be rewarded. Good uses of it: [[A Dreamless Charm]] from Harrow's Simples (a silver each, and Nell is nearly sold out); a guard posted on the oil store; a word with Spoons about the pool; a nap, for whoever will be on the ladder at midnight. Marrow puts the party on the hill for the night, whatever else they do. At dusk the Ember rises huge and red over the Ashenreach, and the Smokes start to walk. Go to scene 3.

**Treasure.** The 600 gold, for as long as they hold it. Whatever they decide about it, Fennick writes it down.

---

### Scene 3: The Smokes march (dusk)
At dusk [[The Dreaming Widow]] leads the Sleepless out of [[The Smokes]] in a long quiet procession with unlit candles, walking toward Lantern Hill: tanners, widows, families of the Hollowborn, children holding their parents' belts. Half the town sleepwalks behind them. The Widow is veiled, seventeen, serene. They don't attack. They sit down around the hill and wait for the dark.

**Round the hill tonight.**
- [[Mirrel Vosk]] holds the gate with twenty soldiers and orders that nobody is to be hurt. Sleepwalkers keep walking into the shields.
- [[Seraphine Ashe]] walks the Smokes alone, unafraid, and the dreamers part for her like grass. Out on the Ash Road the House of Unburnt Light keeps the Saint's Lamp: every window lit, the gate open, and the garden full of the standing dead, leaning toward her as she passes.
- [[Mother Hennet]] sends soup up the hill in two cauldrons, one for the soldiers and one for the Sleepless, "because they're somebody's".
- [[Mother Corvane]] counts the walking dead from the top of Corvel Rise and sends word for Quorl to tally the Smokes. If the dead walk in town, [[Dunny Pike]] strikes two at the Ford: a death.
- Tick feels the Rent pulling. Sometime before midnight he starts walking east and doesn't notice he is doing it (Wisdom save DC 12, or a friend's hand on his shoulder, to stop).
- Odile is seen slipping into Tollamund & Daughters during the march.

**Development.** The Sleepless sit, and wait, and sing very softly. Nothing happens for hours, which is the worst of it. Use the time for the bullets above, one at a time. Some time before midnight a Sleepless child comes up to the gate and asks for "Tibbet": go to scene 4. If Tick isn't in the party, the Widow asks for the soldiers instead and makes the same plea to all of them. Close to midnight, Tallow climbs the ladder to the cage and doesn't come down: go to scene 5.

---

### Scene 4: Tick's aunt
The Widow asks for Tick by name. [[Marla Marlowe]] stands between them, furious, invisible to everyone but Tick. The Widow is Marla's sister and says so: "Your mother dreamed this too, Tibbet. She'd want you with us." Marla scratches in the ash: NO.

If Quorl is there, he knows Marla's last words: he wrote them down himself at Marlowe's cellar door at dawn the morning after Cinders, a fledgling come for the body, from her mother's mouth. *"Wenna, hold his hand. Don't look up."* If he says them aloud, the Widow looks up, and for a moment she is eight years old and very frightened. Then the veil comes down again.

**Development.** The Widow goes back to her people's circle, and does not ask again tonight. If Tick goes with her, he is sitting among the Sleepless, singing, when midnight comes: Marla pulls at his sleeve, and he can come back to the hill (Wisdom save DC 12, or a friend who comes for him) in time to help on the ladder. Either way, the hill goes quiet as midnight comes on. Go to scene 5.

---

### Scene 5: The second guttering (midnight)
At midnight, under the full Ember, the lantern gutters again, worse than before. Every wick-lamp on the hill shrinks to a blue bead. [[Brother Tallow]] collapses on the ladder below the cage. [[Ilse Marrow]] takes the first cage key from round his neck and puts it in Ysse's hand: three keys, three keepers, and one of them down.

**Tending the flame.** Ysse climbs to the cage and has to hold the flame up with whatever she has: Arcana or Religion DC 13, each round for three rounds; two successes of three hold it. Another PC on the ladder can take the Help action (Quorl praying, Tick singing the Hollowborn song he doesn't remember learning). On each failure the flame shrinks and something in the crowd starts to sing. On a second failure the flame goes out for a long breath before it comes back (tick the Sleeper's clock), and two figures step out of the song: run **The dream given shape**.

Up on the signal tower, Noume Flarewick flashes the far patrols and gets an answer from nobody: three flashes, from inside the Rent. (They are the Last Wardens' lamp-code. Uthrem explains in session 6.)

**Development.** While every eye on the hill is on the cage, glass breaks at the foot of the tower. Whoever is not on the ladder hears it: go to scene 6. If the whole party is on the ladder, the tanner gets in and out unseen, the next dawn measure is cut again, and the flame gutters a little at dawn as well (no clock tick, but the oil store needs a guard from now on, and the tanner is gone).

---

### Scene 6: The cut oil in the store
While the hill is busy, someone breaks into the oil store at the tower's foot. If the party catches him: one of the Widow's people, a tanner, with a jar of Stillwater ash. He was to meet "the lieutenant's man" at the yard door, who would let him in: [[Dunstan Bray]], who thinks he's doing Sallow a favour and asks no questions.

**If Dannic was not laid to rest in session 1,** he rises tonight with the full Ember, walks up from the Chapel slab to the oil store where he did the swaps, and stands at its door holding an empty tin, trying to put it back. The tanner runs screaming straight into the party's arms. See **Dannic at the oil store**.

**Development.** The tanner talks, if anyone asks him kindly: Bray let him in, and he doesn't know why. Bray, collared, blusters and then looks for Sallow, who is very visibly on the wall with Marrow. This is the moment the players can see who is lying. If they go after Sallow now, he laughs at them, and Marrow tells them to bring proof, not a tanner. That proof is Spoons: go to scene 7.

**Treasure.** The tanner's jar of Stillwater ash, which smells of lake water, like the cut tin.

---

### Scene 7: Spoons cracks
If confronted with the poster, the coin and the timing, [[Spoons Aldane]] gives up Sallow: "I saw him and Dannic at the oil store. Twice. After midnight. I didn't say. I moved my copper to the red moon instead. It was the biggest book I ever ran." He looks sick.

If they never corner Spoons, session 4 still needs evidence. Two other ways in: Bray, pressed after the oil-store business, folds and weeps and tells everything he knows (enough to hang Sallow, nothing about Mote); or a search of Sallow's room turns up a letter, unsigned, in a small neat hand, about "the arrangement" and the red moon.

**Development.** Whoever confesses, the night is nearly over. Hold any confession until Marrow can hear it at dawn: she will want it said again, in front of witnesses, and she will want it in session 4 when the party brings Sallow to her (scene 5 there). Go to "How it ends". Spoons, sick with himself, asks the party a favour before they go: [[The Wager on Never]].

**Treasure.** Spoons's marker, if they take his job and finish it.

---

### How it ends
Dawn comes. The Sleepless rise and walk home, smiling as if they'd won something. If the flame held, it is thin and steady; Tallow lives but doesn't wake for a day. Ysse holds the first key and is, whether anyone has said it yet, the keeper of the lantern. Tallow, half-conscious, as they carry him in: "It isn't oil it wants. It never was." Tick the Sleepless clock. As the Sleepless go, Kettle's cousins come up from the Low Quay to the Keep gate with a slate wrapped in goat-hair and sealed with cheese-wax, for "the Hollowborn soldier": [[The Standing Village]]. Give it to Tick's player.

**End of session: level 3.**

### If the party wanders
- **Midnight in town.** Bell, drum and tower disagree when the lantern gutters: Dunny strikes two, a death; Thistle beats Stand to; Noume gets three flashes from inside the Rent. See [[Signals of Cinderford]].
- **Hesk's Chandlery.** If the march passes it, [[Mirrit]] is inside with her ankle corded to the counter, and asks them to check the knot.
- **South Street.** [[Harrow's Simples]] has had its window smashed, as it is every red moon. [[Nell Harrow]] is sweeping glass in the dark with a bandaged hand and says it was the wind. She gives the party a free dream-knot and a look.
- **The Ninepenny.** [[Auntie Brace]] sits by the stove counting her sleepers in and out (name, pennies and dream) and takes it personally when one walks out onto the plain. Her book is the plainest record in town of what the Hollowborn dream tonight.
- **Under the Widow's yard.** [[The Salt Lake]]'s warm culvert runs from the kilns under the Smokes and passes beneath the Widow's yard: a way in, tonight, while her people sit round the hill.
- **Bellwether's goat-shed** (a mile above the ford). The goats will not cross the pale ash line, which moves twenty yards a fortnight, and the jars in the shed came from Stillwater. See [[Goodwife Marn Tolliver]] and [[Tolliver's Dairy]].
- **A third cold at the weir.** At the full Ember the Cinder runs cold at [[Sedgemere]] again, and Keld sends a crow up to Corvel Rise.
- **After the night (17th to 19th).** [[Gammer Teal]] at Stillwater, fifteen miles out, is a long ride and worth it: ninety, late-eyed, and the only person on the lake the Widow did not convert. "You want it pretty, go to the Widow. You want it true, sit down and shut up."

== Strong start ==

Every PC wakes at the same moment, gasping, from the same dream: a warm dark, a door with no handle, a voice saying "nearly". It is the middle of the night in the Glassers' yard at Fort Mercy. All around you, in the light of two moons, diggers are standing in their tent-mouths in their shirts, looking at each other. Nobody needs to ask.

== Scenes ==

- The shared dream in the small hours at Fort Mercy; the whole yard wakes at once.
- The ride home: all Cinderford had the dream. Fennick counts the box: 4,800 gold owed. Marrow is out of money. Tallow: "Tonight."
- The Sleepless march out of the Smokes and sit in vigil around Lantern Hill. Vosk holds the gate; Seraphine walks the Smokes; Hennet sends soup.
- The Dreaming Widow asks for Tick; Marla stands between them; Quorl has Marla's last words.
- Midnight under the full Ember: the lantern gutters, Tallow collapses, Marrow gives Ysse his key, Ysse tends the flame.
- Someone breaks into the oil store with Stillwater ash, to meet Bray. Dannic may be standing at the door.
- Spoons gives up Sallow, or Bray does, or Sallow's room does.

== Clues ==

- The dream reached Fort Mercy, twenty-two miles out, and all of Cinderford.
- The note now stands at 4,800 gold, and the 480 interest fell due on the 14th.
- Captain Marrow has paid the company from her own savings for a year, and is broke.
- The Dreaming Widow gave the ash freely; the man carrying it was to meet "the lieutenant's man", Dunstan Bray.
- Spoons saw Sallow and Dannic at the oil store, twice.
- Reeve Fairweather slips into Tollamund & Daughters during the vigil.
- Three flashes answered Noume's signal from inside the Rent.
- Tallow: "It isn't oil it wants. It never was."
- The flame responds to Ysse's touch better than to Tallow's.
- The Widow is Marla Marlowe's sister, Tick's aunt.
