## Session 8: The Lantern Gutters
*Level 5. 7 to 9 Leafrot 3011 AW: three days east at a cart's pace; the full red moon on the 9th.*

- *Sky*: on the 7th the Ember is nearly full, low and red; on the 9th, at the Stone, it is full, low and red, the Saint's Lamp. Pallas is a little past its first quarter, waxing, white and small beside it.
- *Weather*: ash hanging in the air, not falling, for three days. Sound carries strangely.
- *Touch these*: the Dreaming Widow brings her followers to the lip; she can be stopped, saved (if Marla can reach her through Tick), or she can step off the edge (scene 3). Vexa is there with shovels, waiting to dig whatever is left. Tollamund, if she still has a claim, has her chair carried out behind the column as far as the Burned Mill, and no further. If Mote escaped in session 7, the Quiet Office sends [[Tesh]] east after the lamp, as the cipher's fifth line asked: Kettle sees her cross the bridge on a post-cart on the 7th, and she stands at the back of the crowd on the lip (she matters only in [[Ending: Use It]]). Pell walks with his writing-desk on the mule and records everything: "Someone has to." The shadow behind Borrin wants very much to be the heart. Ysse doesn't need a dream tonight: the flame speaks aloud.
- *In between*: none. The cart leaves before dawn on the 7th.
- *Level-up*: none. The campaign ends at level 5 (level 6 if you go on to a second arc).
- *If the party…* won't take the lantern east: it gutters on the hill night after night, and at midnight on the 9th the flame on Lantern Hill goes out. The company stands down by its own contract, the Stone falls into the Rent sixty miles away, and Let It Crack happens to them at home.

**Goal.** Decide the flame's fate. See [[Ending: Reseal the Ward]], [[Ending: Let It Crack]] and [[Ending: Use It]].

### Running this session
**Last time** (read aloud). *"For two days every knife in the March came out for the lantern: the Concord at the gate with its lawyers, the clerk in the night with his, the Saint's wardens on the hill road, Mother Corvane at the foot of the hill, and Sallow's mutiny in the yard. What's left of the company stood round the cage in the frost, and Marrow said it plainly: whatever we're doing with that thing, we're doing it at the Rent, under the moon, together. Before dawn, they unbolted the cage."*

**Where the PCs are.** At the Keep on the morning of 7 Leafrot, before dawn, with the cage lashed to a handcart and the company forming up in the yard.

**The party should have.** The lantern (Ysse with the first key; Marrow and Vosk with theirs), [[A Rubbing from the Ninth Stone]], and whatever allies they earned: [[Hold-Mother Greta Holm]] if they sent for her in time (she comes by her own road and is waiting at the lip when the column arrives), [[Keld Owes-Nothing]] if they found her, the Rust Choir if Varra wrote to the Mothers (see scene 2), Orla Quist with her chain.

**Read the clocks.**
- *Mutiny*: who marches, from the table in session 7 (about 120, 100 or 60).
- *The Saint ascends*: if Seraphine is still the Saint's, at 5 or under she asks Ysse once at the top of the rope steps and stands aside when refused; at 6 or 7 she takes, and you run **The Saint's champion**.
- *The Sleepless rise*: they come to the lip whatever it says; at 6 they are there first, between the party and the rope steps (scene 3).
- *The Quiet Office* and *Debt called in*: these did their work in session 7. What is left of them is Mote's knives on the road if he is loose, Tesh if he sent for her, and Tollamund's chair at the Burned Mill.
- *The Sleeper stirs*: 5 by default, and the dead of Cinders stay down; at 6 they stand on the lip (scene 4).

**If they missed something.**
- *No rubbing?* Uthrem tells them what it says at the Stone, in fewer words and with more grinding.
- *Never sent for Greta Holm?* Nobody says the Standing rite. If Uthrem is the heart, keep the epilogue's d6 in [[Ending: Reseal the Ward]]; a PC or Ysse holds without it.
- *Sided with Sallow in session 7?* Skip the "Last time" box and scene 1. Sallow marches the lantern west at dawn on the 7th, on the Fallowden road toward Ardmere and the Quiet Office's escort, with about sixty soldiers and the flame down to a bead. Marrow and Vosk get out of the oil-room that night. At first light on the 8th they ride into the column's camp, about fifteen miles out, and the party turns it: run **Mote's knives, or Sallow's diehards** from the gauntlet as that fight, with Sallow in the lead. Win it, and the column turns east on a forced march, back through Cinderford and out along the Ash Road with no second camp: about seventy-five miles by dusk on the 9th, so every marcher makes the forced-march Constitution saves (DC 10, +1 for each hour past eight in a day; a failure is a level of exhaustion). Lose it, and the lantern goes on west and goes out at midnight on the 9th, as in "If the party…" above. The Ember is full on the 9th either way.
- *Never found Keld?* Corvane at the lip is harder to talk round: Quorl's eight sentences still work, but nothing else does.
- *Ysse went with Seraphine?* The company carries the lantern without its keeper, and the flame fails faster (pay one extra gauntlet cost on the road). Ysse is waiting at the lip in white, at Seraphine's side, and the fight with the Saint's champion is for her.

**The session at a glance.** The last session runs long; plan on five hours, or two evenings. Scene 1 is the dawn the column leaves. Scene 2 is three days on the road: run one or two gauntlet fights, never more than one a day, and let the camps at night be quiet scenes for the PCs to talk. Scene 3 is dusk on the 9th at the lip. Scene 4 comes as the Ember climbs. Scene 5 is the descent and the choice, by night. Scene 6 is dawn on the 10th, and the homecoming after it. If time runs short, cut the gauntlet to one fight and the priests to Corvane and Seraphine.

**Who's still standing?** Before you start, check what session 7 left alive and free, and strike out the rest below: Mote (fled, dead or in irons), Sallow (in irons, on the road with his diehards, dead, or redeemed), Seraphine (here as the Saint's champion unless she was talked back to herself), Pell (with the column, or dead in the fire), Corvane (an ally, or the friend at the lip), Bray (dead if he talked).

### Scene 1: The tenth bell (the 7th, before dawn)
The cage is unbolted from its niche on the Keep's east wall and lifted down: lantern and cage together weigh about thirty pounds, and it takes Ysse and Vosk to steady it onto the handcart, the one hired from [[The Bridle & Yoke]] at 1 cp a day; Hob Garrity has chalked it on the company's slate, and will not be paid. The flame inside is the size of a fingernail and blue at the root. The niche is empty for the first time in nine years; there's a ring of ash where the cage stood.

As the cart rolls down the hill road, [[Dunny Pike]] climbs the bell-house stair past the knot, hauls the Treaty Bell's rope down, and pulls it: the tenth ring in nine years. He makes it four. Four is the end of the world. He has never done four; he has practised it in his head. The whole town comes out in the dark with lamps to watch the company go. Thistle beats the Muster all the way down to the bridge, because nobody told her not to ([[Signals of Cinderford]]).

**The soup.** Before the column moves, Mother Hennet takes [[Mother Hennet's Soup-Pot]] back and cooks in it for the first time in nine years: the night-of-Cinders soup, barley, salt mutton, onions and the last dried pears. Every soldier who marches gets a bowl: 1d4 temporary hit points, and advantage on the first save against being frightened or charmed before the next dawn. She does not march.

**Development.** The column crosses the bridge as the sun comes up behind the Rent, and the town watches it go. Go to scene 2.

**Treasure.** Hennet's soup, as above. Hob's handcart, which will never be paid for.

### Scene 2: The road (the 7th to the 9th)
The company, or what's left of it (see the Mutiny table in session 7), carries the lantern east across the ash under a swelling red moon. The flame is failing fast now; Ysse can barely keep it. It lasts to the Stone whatever the road costs: the gauntlet's prices below are what the company pays to get it there, and it goes out on the way only if the party turns back (see "If the party…"). Everyone who wants it follows.

**On the road.** The column passes [[The Burned Mill]] on the morning of the 7th and the pear on the tree is red; [[Nance Oddy]] counts every soldier and the handcart, and cuts the company's corner of the bench for the first time in four years. If Tollamund still has a claim, her chair comes up the road behind the column an hour later and stops here; she has it set down facing east, and waits, and watches the sky. The veterans sing The Eight Lanterns, which is sung on this road and no other ([[Songs of the March]]). If the route bends south, [[Ambercote]]'s standing turn as one to watch the flame go by.

**The gauntlet** (optional: run one or two, not all). Whoever the party hasn't dealt with comes for the lantern on the road. Pick from:
- **The Lamp-Wardens** (session 4's stats; two of them), if Seraphine was refused and not shamed.
- **Glassers** (session 2's stats: six toughs, with Vexa at their head), if Vexa was robbed or cheated. She still calls it off at half her hit points.
- **Mote's knives, or Sallow's diehards** (session 7's stats: Mote and two hired knives, or Sallow and six mutineers), if either man is loose.
- **The Sleepless** (session 3's zealot and eight pilgrims, sent ahead of the rest), who want only to watch it go out and will sit down in the road to make it happen. They are an obstacle, not a fight, unless the party makes them one.

Built for five level-5 PCs: each fight was built for an earlier level and is easy now (1,500 to 2,125 adjusted XP against an easy threshold of 1,250 and a medium one of 2,500). The price below is the point, not the fight.

Each one costs something, whatever the dice say. Choose one: a wick-lamp gutters out for good (someone's flame is gone; the lantern is a little weaker); a named NPC is wounded and has to be left with the carts (a Dunmore twin, Noume, Gerda); or a clock ticks (Sleeper, Saint, or Concord).

**The Mothers' answer.** If Varra wrote to the Mothers, the Rust Choir, her mother's company, forty orc pikes, comes up with the column at dusk on the 8th. Its price is the one the letter said the exile would know: Varra's oath to the Iron again, or something harder, and Varra's player says which. At the lip it holds the west end while the party is below (see "Holding the lip", scene 4). If Varra did not write, only the Fist who carried the letter marches with the column, and speaks for the Mothers at the lip. Either way, if you want [[Grashka]] in the finale, she is standing at the back of the crowd on the lip at dusk on the 9th, come to see what they have kept. She says nothing, and Vosk is the only one who looks away.

**Development.** Two nights' camp out on the ash, under a red moon that gets bigger every night. Give each camp one quiet scene: a PC and an NPC by the fire (Vosk and Varra, Marrow and whoever she trusts, Tallow and Ysse if Tallow came). The column reaches the lip at dusk on the 9th: go to scene 3.

### Scene 3: The priests at the lip (the 9th, dusk)
At the northern lip, above the Stone, the pantheon's priests are waiting, every one with a different reason to let it crack, all talking at once. (Optional: if the night is running long, keep only Corvane and Seraphine.)
- **Sister Brine**, for Grum: "A crack is a road. The gods walked roads before your Nine ever cut one."
- **[[Brother Audit]]**, Corvex's lay brother from Temple Row: "Every ledger closes. Let this one." He can be argued down with a contract: show him the company keeping its oath (the lantern carried, not abandoned) and he puts the stylus behind his ear and steps back.
- **Odile Fairweather**, the Reeve, with the key to the locked Aurelan shrine on a cord round her neck: Aurelan's people want the old order back, and the old order never had a lamp in it. She is here for her town, and not sure she should be.
- **[[Mourn]]**, the Velvet Ledger's doorkeeper, for Isaurana: "Some things want to be uncovered. You know that, soldier. Everyone knows that."
- **[[Mother Brisk]]**, for Maelis, with a basket of Hearthday bread: "Whatever comes up that stair will be hungry. Somebody should be ready to feed it."
- **Seraphine Ashe**, for the Saint, her eyes ember-red. If she is still the Saint's, she tries to stop them going down (see "Read the clocks"), and may become **The Saint's champion**.
- **So is a friend: Mother Corvane.** She believes putting the lamp out lets the dead rest. She has come with six Raveler undertakers and Hathe's Door-Key, and she will try to snuff it "to let them go home". Quorl has the words to answer her, the seventh in his notebook: *"Don't let them put it out."* If she was talked round in session 7, she is already on the party's side and needs no roll. If not, and Quorl reads all eight aloud, she listens. If the party fetched [[Keld Owes-Nothing]] from Sedgemere, she stands beside her sister and says one sentence to her that neither of them ever repeats. (If Quorl still has [[Hathe's Door-Key]] from session 5, she asks him for it back here, kindly, in front of everyone, and what he does is part of whether she listens.)

**At the edge.** [[The Dreaming Widow]] stands at the very edge of the lip near the top of the rope steps, barefoot in her grey dress, with her sixty behind her singing. When the Ember tops the sky she means to step off. Tick can reach her, with Marla close beside him: Persuasion DC 15, with advantage if he speaks of her mother, [[Ottilie Marlowe|Ottilie]]. On a success she sits down on the edge and weeps, and the Sleepless sit down with her (Sleepless clock −1). A PC beside her can simply catch her by the wrist, which stops her tonight and saves nothing. Otherwise she steps off the lip into the dark as the Ember tops the sky; if you are speeding up the Sleeper's clock, that is a finished Sleepless rite (+1), and her people press toward the rope steps (see "Holding the lip", scene 4). [[Ambry Coyle]] is the first of them to look away.

**The others on the lip.** Vexa's crew is at the east end with shovels and rope. If the party dealt fairly with her, they help hold the lip while the party is below; if not, they wait to dig whatever is left, and the company has to watch them too. Tollamund is not here: she stopped at the Burned Mill, and is watching the sky.

**Pennies.** Waiting on the lip, every soldier rubs his [[An Emperor's Penny|penny]], the way soldiers do, and frost blooms on the glass under their boots. A hundred at once spend the ward like a spell. If you are speeding up the Sleeper's clock, roll a d6 and tick it on a 1. A Vesh PC sees why.

**Development.** The priests talk until the Ember is well up, and nobody can be argued with for ever. Those the party has talked down step back from the edge; the rest follow the party to the top of the rope steps. When the moon reaches the top of the sky, the flame speaks: go to scene 4.

### Scene 4: The voice in the flame
As the Ember tops the sky, the flame speaks aloud for the first time, in the Ninth's warm voice, to whoever holds it: "Keep me." Everyone hears it. (It isn't the Ninth. The Ninth asks you to stand.) Tick the Sleeper's clock for the full Ember if you haven't. Left alone, the clock stands at 5 now and the dead stay in the ash: the finale is the march, the Stone and the choice. If it reaches 6 (because you sped it up), the dead of Cinders stand up all along the lip, turn, and start to walk toward the Stone (**The dead of Cinders**).

**Development.** Now someone has to go down. The party goes, with the lantern; the company holds the lip behind them against whatever is left (the Sleepless, the priests, the dead). Getting the lantern down: Vosk's rope team lowers the cage on a line beside the rope steps, or Ysse, Marrow and Vosk turn all three keys and Ysse carries the lamp itself, small enough for two hands, with the shard in its bowl. If Seraphine is still the Saint's, she tries to stop them at the top of the steps (see "Read the clocks"): **The Saint's champion**.

**The way down.** It runs as in session 6, by Ember-light, and takes the small hours: about three hours with the ropes fixed. The rope steps: Athletics DC 13 per climber, with advantage, because the ropes the party fixed in session 6 are still there; a failure is a slide and a burn, 2d6 bludgeoning. The Glass Stair is worse by Ember-light, when the red and the clear look the same: Acrobatics DC 13 per walker, with disadvantage unless someone lights the way with a wick-lamp held low. A failure is a fall, 2d6 bludgeoning, and lands the walker on the clear glass on a d6 roll of 1 to 3, where **the glass-stalker** (session 6) is waiting if it still lives. Passing the Choir's cleft, everyone makes the Wisdom save again (DC 14, 2d6 psychic on a failure), and anyone who speaks back calls up **The Choir** (session 6's stats) on the Halfway Ledge; if you are speeding up the Sleeper's clock, that is a tick too. Spells still frost the walls and still count on Ysse's tally. Then the short traverse onto the Warden's Shelf.

**Holding the lip.** Marrow goes down with the party, because the contract has to be read at the Stone; Vosk commands the lip and works the cage line. Keep a Lip clock of 6 while the party is below. It starts with one tick for each of these that is true: Corvane did not listen; the Sleepless clock is full, or the Widow went over the edge; the dead of Cinders are standing; Mote's knives or Sallow's diehards are still loose; Vexa was cheated. Tick it once when the party reaches the Halfway Ledge, once when they reach the Stone, and once for each fight on the way down. The Rust Choir buys back two ticks, Vexa's crew one if she was dealt with fairly, and any PC who stays on the lip one. If it fills, the lip gives way: whoever is still set on the lamp comes down the rope steps behind the party and reaches the Warden's Shelf while they are at the Stone, before the choice is made. Run whichever of the road's fights is still loose there; if none is, it is Corvane (page stats) and her six undertakers (scouts with shovels), who want the lamp out, not anyone dead: medium for five level-5 PCs (2,625 adjusted XP). If Corvane listened, it is a crowd of the Sleepless instead, singing on the Shelf, who have to be kept back from the crack while the choice is made. Go to scene 5.

### Scene 5: The choice at the Ninth Stone
The party goes down to the Warden's Shelf, as in session 6, with the lantern. At the foot of the Stone, [[Warden Uthrem]] is waiting. Three ways forward:

**Reseal it with a willing heart.** The shard goes back into the heart-socket, ten feet up inside the crack, with a living heart beside it. The heart-giver climbs into the crack with the shard; nobody can be pushed (a heart that is taken rots, and the crack only widens).
- **Uthrem offers first.** He is half-stone and cannot climb, so someone has to lift him the ten feet into the crack. It kills him. With Greta Holm's Standing rite it buys about twenty years; without it, roll the d6 at the end of the epilogue in [[Ending: Reseal the Ward]].
- **A willing PC** can go. That PC is gone, and the seal holds without a roll. Give them their last moment, and let the player narrate it.
- **The flame chooses Ysse** if no one else is willing. It wants her. That is not the same as her having to say yes.
- **Ysse's tally bites here.** Every spell she has cast near the Rent drained the ward. If her tally is long (more than ten, say), the shard will not seat for anyone else's hand: whoever gives the heart, Ysse has to climb in and hold the shard in the socket until it takes, and she comes out at dawn grey to the wrists, with stone in her fingers that never quite warms.
- **The Standing rite.** If the party fetched [[Hold-Mother Greta Holm]] from Hollowhall, she says the rite over whoever goes into the heart-socket and sits with them until they stop. The seal holds: skip the end-of-epilogue d6 in [[Ending: Reseal the Ward]].
- The crack closes over a night. The flame in your hands goes out. Marrow reads the contract aloud at the Stone, and the company stands down. The Ember dims to copper over a season. Sleeper's clock −3.

**Let it crack.** The shard is kept from the Stone, destroyed, or the Saint's people pour the leak into their lamp. The flame in the cart goes out. The dead of Cinders stand up all along the lip (or, if they were already standing, turn round) and start to walk toward the town. The Stone groans as the party climbs, and falls into the Rent before dawn, once they are back on the lip, and the youngest of the Nine with it. Ilyra ascends within the year. The Sleeper's clock fills.

**Use it.** Someone takes the shard and keeps it, and learns to speak to what is under the stair. The flame burns on; the company never stands down. The holder starts to dream, and over years becomes the Sleeper's voice, pushed by Vell, Tollamund, Sallow, Aelith, and the voice itself. If Mote sent for Tesh, she is on the lip, and hers is the first voice the holder hears answering in the dark: the puppet Vell meant for his throne, who already hears the voice under the stair.

Whatever they choose, give each PC one last moment: Tick and Marla; Borrin and the shadow that wants his place; Quorl's last words, his own or someone's; Varra and her grandmother's letter; Ysse's answer.

**Development.** The climb out is slow and nobody hurries it. If they resealed the ward, the crack closes over the night behind them, and they reach the lip as the sky goes grey. If they let it crack, they climb with the Stone groaning below and the dead of Cinders walking away west along the lip above them. If they kept it, the flame comes up with them, burning. Go to scene 6, at dawn.

### Scene 6: Dawn, and after
An epilogue in the ash. Who's left, what Cinderford becomes, and what happens to the company now the contract has or hasn't ended. Some of these lines happen on the lip at dawn, and some when the company gets home, three days later. Pick the lines that fit:
- Spoons pays out at the Last Candle, publicly, to the copper, because he said he would and Hennet makes him ([[The Wager on Never]]). (Reseal) Whoever had 9 Leafrot collects (Spoons rules that a night runs from dusk to dawn, so a flame that went out after midnight went out on the 9th); Keld's stake stays in the strongbox, because "never" neither won nor lost: the lamp was given back, not put out, and Spoons writes a new rule for it. (Use it) "Never" wins, and if the party found Keld she refuses the money in front of the room ("I bet to be right, not to be paid"), and Spoons stands there with the whole pot and nobody to give it to. (Let it crack) The pool pays on the night it went out, nobody is in a state to collect, and Keld's stake is the only thing in the strongbox still worth anything.
- Mother Hennet chalks a new line on the slate and rubs nothing out.
- Vosk says that if the company is finished, he will finally open that bakery.
- Tallow sits with the empty lantern until morning, and then says he would like some tea.
- Fennick insists on closing the books properly. All three sets.
- When she gets home, Odile has Dunny strike the dawn stroke at the Ford, as if it were any morning, and starts organising the clean-up.
- Kettle is on the quay selling hot chestnuts and the story.
- Pell finishes his report and signs it.
- (Reseal) The cage on Lantern Hill is dark when they get home, and the ninth pole on Corvel Rise gets its lantern.
- On the morning of the 10th, Nance Oddy picks up what fell from the pear tree at the Burned Mill. (Reseal) A pear, sweet, with one hard black seed, which she plants in the wheel-pit. (Let it crack) A stone, and the mill's dead have climbed out of the pit and gone east. (Use it) Nothing fell; the pear hangs red, and never ripens, and never rots.
- Ambercote's square: (Reseal) every figure sags an inch, and the woman at the well has set her bucket down. (Let it crack) all two hundred and twelve walk east, and the living walk after them. (Use it) they stand, and turn a little further each red moon toward whoever holds the flame.

**Development.** The campaign ends here, at level 5. A second arc starts at level 6 with whichever threads the table cares about. Leave two threads open on purpose for a second arc: Borrin's shadow, and whoever the Sleeper whispers to next. For more, see [[Who is on the Crownfast pardon list?]], [[Why does the Returned smith forge the same key?]] and, on 10 Leafrot, [[What is the sealed cargo for Frostcall?]].

== Strong start ==

The lantern is lashed to a handcart, and the flame inside the cage is the size of a fingernail and blue at the root. Ysse walks beside it with one hand on the bars. Behind you, what is left of the company. Ahead, three days of ash and then the Rent. Down in the west the Ember is setting, nearly full, low and red, so close it seems you could hear it breathing. Back at the Ford, the Treaty Bell is still ringing: four, the end of the world. Everyone who has ever wanted this flame is on the road behind you.

== Scenes ==

- Before dawn on the 7th: the cage lifted down from the Keep's wall; Dunny pulls the tenth ring, and makes it four.
- The road east with the failing lantern under a swelling red moon.
- The gauntlet: one or two factions come for the lantern on the way, and each costs something.
- The priests at the lip, each with a reason to let it crack; Mother Corvane is the friend.
- The flame speaks aloud: "Keep me."
- The choice at the Ninth Stone with Warden Uthrem: a willing heart, the crack, or the keeping.
- Dawn and the epilogue.

== Clues ==

- The voice that says "Keep me" is not the Ninth's. The Ninth asks you to stand.
- A heart that is taken rots: nobody can be pushed into the stone.
- If the lantern goes out, by contract the company stands down.
- The gods' priests each want the crack open for their own reason, and they want it before dawn.
- Quorl's seventh whisperer said: "Don't let them put it out."
