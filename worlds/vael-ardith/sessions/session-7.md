## Session 7: Three Knives
*Level 4. 5 to 6 Leafrot 3011 AW (Wardday, the Reeve's court day, and Restday). Four nights before the red moon is full again.*

- *Sky*: the Ember four nights from full and already the colour of a fresh wound; Pallas a waxing crescent.
- *Weather*: first frost. The ash crunches underfoot and the Keep's well has a skin of ice at dawn.
- *Touch these*: Sallow tries to have Vosk killed before he calls the yard (**A knife for Vosk**, under "Side threads on the 6th"). On the 6th, while every eye is on the yard, the Sleepless slip up the hill to snuff the lantern; Ambry Coyle is the one who hesitates with his hand on the ladder (**The Sleepless at the ladder**, same place). Ysse, the night of the 5th: "Not yet," in the warm voice, and "Keep me," closer than it has ever been.
- *In between* (2 to 4 Leafrot): the ride home. The party finds the company half-turned: Sallow worked on it while they were gone. Grace on the Tollamund note ends at sunset on the 4th.
- *Level-up*: End of session: level 5.
- *If the party…* sides with Sallow: he wins the yard, and Marrow and Vosk are locked in the oil-room. At dawn on the 7th Sallow pours the last cut tin into the oil-port himself (it needs no key), and the lantern gutters to a bead and stays there. Then he has the cage unbolted and marches it west on the Ardmere road with about sixty soldiers, to hand it to the Regency's escort. Mote means to kill Sallow on the road once the lantern is out of the Keep. Marrow and Vosk get out of the oil-room on the night of the 7th, and session 8 runs the chase from there (its "If they missed something").

**Goal.** Survive the two days every faction moves on the lantern, and break (or win) the mutiny.

### Running this session
**Last time** (read aloud). *"At the foot of the Ninth Stone, a Warden half turned to stone told you the truth: the flame belongs to the youngest of the Nine, it must go home, and the ward needs a heart that chooses. Something that wears Borrin's shadow stood up and bowed to the Stone. At the top of the climb a rider was waiting: 'Captain says come home now. They're all moving at once.' You rode in on the evening of the 3rd and found the company half-turned. Yesterday at sunset, the grace on the company's debt ran out."*

**Where the PCs are.** At the Keep, on the morning of 5 Leafrot, a Wardday and the Reeve's court day. Tollamund's chair is coming up the hill road. The party had the 4th to rest and get ready (see "In between").

**The party should have.** [[A Rubbing from the Ninth Stone]]; page seven of [[The Original Treaty]], and the pay clause, from the Treaty Stone's glass or the Treaty itself; proof against Sallow; the first key (Ysse). Any of these help: Hennet's slate-book, certified ([[The Price Was an Arm]]); [[Old Tally's Coin]]; [[The Lost Pay Order]]; [[The Charter of the Open River]]; Pell's Claim of Arrears; [[The Assessor's Lost Form]].

**If they missed something.**
- *Nobody has read the Treaty?* The original is kept at the Treaty Stone behind a grille with two locks, Marrow's and the Reeve's. Pell, with an Assessor's right of inspection (which opens the grille without the Reeve's key), can fetch it on the 4th, a three-mile ride, and reads page seven aloud at the gate.
- *No proof against Sallow?* [[Brother Audit]] reads Sallow's prayers in the yard (scene 5), and the two horses at the Bridle & Yoke are proof of intent.
- *Never found Form 9-K?* Mote takes it with him on his night. If he is killed, caught, or flees at half hit points, it is in the satchel he drops.
- *Ysse refused the first key, or isn't in the party?* Mote comes for whoever holds it.

**The session at a glance.** Two days and six knives, and the party can't be everywhere. On the 5th: the Concord at the gate in the morning (1), a free afternoon (see "If the party wanders"), and Mote at night (2). On the 6th: the Saint's wardens at first light (3), Corvane at the foot of the hill in the morning (4), Sallow's mutiny at midday (5), and Grashka's letter as the dust settles (6). The numbered beats run in order. The choices are in the two side threads on the 6th: a knife for Vosk at first light, and the Sleepless at the cage ladder while everyone is in the yard (see "Side threads on the 6th"). Let the players choose where to stand. Whatever they don't stop happens as written there, and its cost arrives in session 8.

**Rest.** After Mote's night, the hour before the singing is a short rest at best. The night of the 6th, after the company gathers at the cage, is a long rest under the Keep's roof (Maud Severin sees to wounds), so the unbolting before dawn on the 7th is the party getting up, not staying up.

### The 5th (Wardday)

**1. The Concord's knife (the Keep gate, morning).** [[Grandmother Tollamund]] sits in her chair in the ash at the gate; she has left the house for the second time in four years, carried. Eight porters set the chair down. Sal and two lawyers walk behind it, with four Concord veterans in livery. She calls in the debt very slowly, pausing for sips of water from a cup Sal holds: the note stands at 4,800 gold; the quarter's interest, 480, is unpaid past grace (sunset yesterday); and the collateral is the company's "possessions, contracts and standing, including the lantern", and she reads the last three words twice. If the party paid the 480 in session 3, she is not here for the default. She calls the strongbox House property taken from her factor's keeping, says the company has spent stolen coin, and claims the collateral for that instead. Page seven answers her the same way. It is court day, so [[Odile Fairweather]] the Reeve stands beside her, with the town's charter, signed but not sealed, and a pen. Whether Odile seals it or tears it up is the scene's outcome.

Talk, stall, or find the hole in the contract, with [[The Original Treaty]] or what was scratched in the Treaty Stone's glass. The pay clause, "by the signatories, or by whosoever shall hold their debt", makes the Concord the company's paymaster and creditor, so the company itself can be pledged. But page seven, the keeping of the Ninth Ward, signed by the Mothers and the Last Wardens alone, makes the lamp a fixture of the Treaty, "kept lit by those who do not know why". It was never the company's to pledge. "Including the lantern" is void; the company is collateral, the lamp is not. Tollamund, if asked straight what she wants it for: "It is a key. To a door my people closed."

**Who wins at the gate.** Tick the Mutiny clock by 1 for the Concord at the gate, whatever happens. Read Debt called in before the argument ([[The Clocks]]): at 7 or 8 Tollamund's lawyers have an answer ready for everything but page seven, and the roll below is DC 18 instead of 15. Play the argument out first. If a player reads page seven aloud and makes the case well, or the table plainly has her beaten, call it a success with no roll; if the party has nothing, it is a failure. Roll only when the argument does not settle itself: one roll decides it, History or Investigation DC 15 for whoever argues the clause, and anyone else at the gate may Help. Without page seven in hand (it is only in the Original Treaty itself; the glass carries only the pay clause, which is Tollamund's half), the roll is at disadvantage.
- **Success.** Page seven is read aloud at the gate (Debt called in −1). Odile tears the charter up in front of the porters (Debt called in −1 more), and Tollamund leaves with a stay.
- **Failure, or nobody at the gate.** Marrow loses the argument and Odile seals the charter. The House now holds the company's wages, stores and gatehouse as its paper: the four Concord veterans take the gatehouse, and Fennick hands over the stores key. The House never holds the lamp. The company is standing round the cage, page seven stands whether anyone read it or not, and Tollamund is a banker, not a general.

**A second hole**, if the party kept Mother Hennet's slate-book and had it certified: it proves the House has been the garrison's paymaster since it bought the wages in 3009, and puts the account in dispute before Corvex. It wins a stay on its own, even if the roll fails: the veterans do not take the gatehouse ([[The Price Was an Arm]]; Debt called in −1).

**Development.** Tollamund leaves the way she came, slowly, with a stay or with the gatehouse, and never with the lamp. She means to see where the lantern goes. The afternoon is the party's: Ford Street on court day, the two horses, Pardew's box, the Charter (see "If the party wanders"). Anyone watching the Assessor's Office sees the shutters close early. Night comes on: go to beat 2.

**2. The Quiet Office's knife (the night of the 5th).** [[Ansel Mote]] stops pretending. At the Assessor's Office he burns the files while [[Pell Quillon]] sleeps at his desk, then comes up to the Keep with two hired knives for the lantern and for Ysse's key. He has a copy of the third key, Sallow a copy of the second, and neither fits the first lock. The cage itself needs no key to take: it is bolted into its niche, and his knives can unbolt it and carry it off whole and locked. But nobody reaches the lamp inside without all three keys, and a lamp the Office cannot open is no use to it, so he needs the keeper's key as well, which is why he comes for Ysse. Turning any key makes the cage chime through the whole Keep. If the Quiet Office clock is full ([[The Clocks]]), a third hired knife comes over the wall with them. If [[Dunstan Bray]] talked, Mote finds him first, in the Candle's back lane, and Bray never talks again. Run **Mote's night**. If the party befriended [[Wendel Hoop]], the cooper under the Assessor's Office, he comes up the hill on the night of the 4th to say his stair smells of lamp-oil, and Mote's night starts with the party already in the street.

**Where and when.** The Office burns from about midnight. In the third watch, while the town's eyes are on the glow over the Ford, Mote and his two knives come over the Keep's east wall-walk. If Bray is still Sallow's, he has sent the sentry off for a drink; if not, the sentry is on the boards, retching from Mote's needle. They drop into the yard and go down to the oil-room under the cage, where Ysse sleeps in the corner Tallow gave her, and scatter Widow's Kisses on the stair behind them. Mote wants this quiet, and a dead keeper wakes the whole Keep, so his first move on Ysse is the needle and the flat of the blade: anyone he drops to 0 is knocked out, not killed, unless the party has already drawn steel. He takes the first key from her neck while the knives go up the trap-ladder to unbolt the cage (four rounds' work). A PC on watch in the yard or on the walls hears them come over (Perception DC 14) and is there before the first blow; that is the medium fight. Otherwise Tallow, on his cot, wakes at the first blow and shouts the Keep awake: the party reaches the oil-room stair two rounds later, onto the Kisses, and the garrison comes two rounds after that.

If the party returned Pell's form, Pell wakes in the smoke, sees, and is braver than anyone expects: he runs to the Keep in his nightshirt with the Claim of Arrears under one arm, shouting for the watch. If he didn't wake, Widow Pruitt pulls him out by the collar. Nib, the errand boy, sleeps under the office stairs; somebody has to remember him.

**Development.** Mote's night ends with Mote fled, caught or dead, and the Assessor's Office burning or saved. If he flees, he goes east, ahead of the column, and his knives are on the road in session 8; and the Quiet Office sends [[Tesh]] east after the lamp: she leaves on the Office's post-cart before dawn on the 7th, and [[Kettle Brindlewake]] sees it go. She is the puppet the holder hears first if the party chooses Use It. If the party has the form, the cipher can be read before dawn (Pell, or an hour's work): go to "The cipher" below. Nobody sleeps much: the hour before the singing is a short rest at best. At first light, singing comes up the hill road: go to beat 3.

**Treasure.** Mote's satchel, if he dropped it: Form 9-K with the cipher on its back, a purse of new Regency silver (30 sp), and a copy of the third cage key. A handful of [[Widow's Kisses]] on the oil-room stair, which look like sweets.

**The cipher.** On the back of [[The Assessor's Lost Form]] (Form 9-K), if anyone has it, is Mote's cipher ([[The Clerk's Cipher]]): column widths that are letters. Pell, or Investigation DC 15 and an hour, reads it:
- *S. paid.*
- *Oil done by the red moon.*
- *Lamp to be taken under Levy default; escort at Ardmere.*
- *Assessor knows nothing.*

**Tesh.** A fifth line, squeezed in under the others: *The girl at the Ledger is the line. Bring her with the lamp.* [[Tesh]], the courtesan at the Velvet Ledger who does not sleep, has imperial blood through a by-blow of Aurel IX's uncle. [[Marius Vell]]'s claimant cousin in the capital has stopped speaking and is failing; Tesh is the line Vell needs, and a puppet who already hears the voice under the Stone is exactly what the Regent-Chancellor wants on his throne. Mote's black second ledger lives locked in his desk at the Assessor's Office (Nib has seen it). If Nib got it out earlier, the party already has it. If not, it is in the desk tonight, and someone has to reach it before the fire does. Vell's name is in it.

**Treasure.** The black ledger, which is proof of everything the Quiet Office has done in the March since Reaping, if anyone ever gets it in front of a court. Shown to the yard in beat 5, it is proof against Sallow.

### The 6th (Restday)

**3. The Saint's knife (first light).** [[Seraphine Ashe]] comes for Ysse, not the lantern, with a crowd of the faithful singing behind her up the hill road. Gently. With an offer: Halcyra, a keeper's chair beside the Saint, the flame safe forever. If Ysse refuses, the Lamp-Wardens, in red surcoats for the road, try to take her to the Halcyra road (**The Lamp-Wardens on the Halcyra road**). Six Wardens come. Four hold the crowd back in a ring and will not strike unless struck; two do the carrying. If the party goes for the ring, Seraphine ends it at first blood with one word: six veterans is not a fight a level-4 party wins.

If anyone draws on Seraphine herself, she does not fight back. At 0 hit points she does not die: her eyes go gold, Ilyra looks out of them and says "Not yet", and the PC who struck her is frightened of her until dawn. The Wardens carry her down the hill and the crowd goes with them. Tick the Saint ascends.

**Development.** Refused in front of the crowd, Seraphine goes back to the House with her faithful, and she will be at the lip in session 8; the party refusing the House in public rolls the Saint ascends back 1. Shamed is more than refused: someone turns the crowd against her by naming what the Saint is hungry for, with a Persuasion or Intimidation DC 15 in front of the faithful (with advantage if they show the page Sorrel copied from the Ledger's book in session 4). That is the Saint's hunger exposed in front of witnesses, and rolls the clock back 1 more. Session 8 reads that clock to decide whether she asks or takes at the lip ([[The Clocks]]). If Ysse goes with her, the party has until the column leaves at dawn on the 7th to bring her back from the House, and Seraphine will not stop her if Ysse asks to go. As the singers go down the hill, someone else is coming up it, on a Raveler's arm: go to beat 4.

**4. Corvane comes down.** For the first time in a year [[Mother Corvane]] comes down from Corvel Rise, on Skrell's arm, and stands at the foot of Lantern Hill. She says it to Ysse, kindly: "The lamp is the lock, child. Put it out. Open the doors. Let them all go home." She believes it. She is wrong. Whether the party can talk her round decides who she is at the lip in session 8. It takes something she has not heard: Quorl reading her the seventh sentence from his notebook ("Don't let them put it out"), Keld's reason ("It can only be given back"), or what the rubbing from the Ninth Stone says. Persuasion DC 14 with one of these, DC 18 without.

**Development.** Corvane doesn't stay to argue. She has said it, and she goes back up to the Rise to gather her undertakers. Talked round, she comes east as a friend: she and her six undertakers carry the dead off the lip, and she stands with the party there without another roll. Not talked round, she comes east anyway, with her undertakers and the Door-Key, to put the lamp out, and she is the friend at the lip in session 8. Behind the party, in the yard, someone starts shouting the files to order: go to beat 5.

**5. Sallow's mutiny (the drill yard).** With the company split by the debt, [[Garrick Sallow]] breaks out of confinement and calls the files to the yard: "They've sold us. Let's sell them first." Half the company is listening. Turning it:
- **Expose the coin.** Dannic's purse, Hennet's cup, the pay-chit, the letter in the small neat hand, Mote's cipher: Persuasion DC 13 with proof in hand, DC 18 without. Sallow exposed with proof: Mutiny clock −1 (not again if the party already did this at the session 4 muster).
- **Read the prayers.** Bring [[Brother Audit]] up from Temple Row and show him the mutiny: he opens his ledger and reads Sallow's two dated prayers aloud in the yard, in his Coinday voice. That is Sallow exposed with proof (Mutiny clock −1; it does not stack with exposing the coin).
- **Tell them about Marrow's purse.** She fed them out of her own savings for a year until it ran out. Marrow would rather die than have it said; said anyway, it turns the yard (Mutiny clock −2).
- **Let Vosk speak.** If a PC asks him to, in front of the yard, [[Mirrel Vosk]] tells them what thirty-four years of soldiering taught him about men who sell their own side, and that he has never once sold this one. He does not say where he was in 2981; that is for Varra, later, alone. If Vosk speaks first, two mutineers switch sides before swords come out. If the knife found him at dawn and nobody was with him, he is in Maud Severin's care and cannot speak.
- **Blades.** Run **The mutiny in the yard**. Every mutineer knocked out instead of killed is one the company gets back.

**The mutiny clock and the march.** Whatever the clock stands at when the dust settles decides how many soldiers walk east with the lantern in session 8:

| Mutiny clock | Who marches |
|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | about 120 |
| 3 to 4 | about 100 |
| 5 to 6 | about 60, and Sallow's diehards are on the road behind them |

Skarra Burnbrow has two thunderjars left and offers them for the road.

**Development.** The mutiny ends with Sallow in irons, dead, gone east with his diehards, or (if the party sided with him) in charge, which is "If the party…" above. While the yard settles, a letter under the Mothers' seal reaches the gate: go to beat 6.

**Treasure.** Two [[Thunderjars]] from Skarra. Sallow's rapier and his copy of the second key, if they take him.

**6. Grashka's answer.** A reply from the Mothers arrives on 6 Leafrot, in the middle of everything, regardless of whether Varra wrote: [[Skarra]] has written home to her mother, [[Mother Hesha]] of the council, every month since Varra came, and this is the answer ([[Grashka's Letter]]), under Hesha's seal and [[Grashka]]'s. A Fist of the Iron carries it, and stays. If Varra wrote to the Mothers, it reads: "Hold the lamp. I am sending what I can." The Rust Choir then catches the column at dusk on the 8th, in session 8. If she didn't: "Hold the lamp. I am coming to see what you have kept." Then only the Fist marches east with the company. Under it, in a second ink, in knot-script only Varra was taught to read, is a line for Varra's eyes only: "I did it. Not for the throne. For this. Ask Vosk." Vosk, if Varra asks him privately: "The Guard did it. I held the door." He does not name Grashka, then or ever. If he already spoke in the yard, this is the part he left out there. Whether the company learns whose granddaughter Varra is belongs to Varra's player. The Quiet Office would very much like to know.

**Development.** The letter is the last thing to arrive. After it, the day goes quiet, and the company gathers at the cage as the light goes. Go to "How it ends".

### Side threads on the 6th
**A knife for Vosk (first light).** Sallow wants Vosk dead before he calls the yard. As the singing comes up the hill road and every eye goes to the gate, a hired knife from the Hock's back room (a thug: AC 11, 32 hp, mace +4, 1d6+2) comes for Vosk in the stable, where he looks over the horses every dawn. A PC who is with him, or who goes looking for him when he doesn't come to the gate (he never misses a muster, so anyone who knows him notices), makes it a short fight; taken alive, the knife says who paid him, in new Regency silver, which is proof against Sallow in beat 5. If nobody goes, Vosk lives, at half his hit points, and spends the day in [[Maud Severin]]'s care: **Let Vosk speak** is gone from the yard. He still marches with his key.

**The Sleepless at the ladder (midday).** While the yard shouts in beat 5, a dozen Sleepless with [[Ambry Coyle]] at their head come up the hill to the foot of the cage's locked outside ladder, and Coyle starts to climb. Anyone who looks away from the yard spots them (Perception DC 14). Coyle has a cold iron nail in his fist and does not want to use it: Persuasion DC 12 talks him down, because he wants an excuse, and he leads his people back down the hill. They are tanners and widows (commoners) and will not fight back. If nobody goes, Coyle touches the nail to the flame: it drops to a bead for the night but does not go out, because he cannot make himself hold it there, and the Sleepless clock ticks 1.

### How it ends
The mutiny is broken or it isn't. Either way the company, what's left of it, stands round the lantern in the frost. The Ember is three nights from full and the colour of a fresh wound. Marrow: "Whatever we're doing with that thing, we're doing it at the Rent. Under the moon. Together, or not at all." The night is a long rest under the Keep's roof. Before dawn, the cage is unbolted from its niche.

**End of session: level 5.**

**Clocks after session 7:** Mutiny 4 after the Concord at the gate, less whatever turned the yard (3 by default, with Sallow exposed: about 100 march). Quiet Office and Debt called in have done their work, read at Mote's night and at the gate. The Saint ascends as session 4 left it, minus 1 if Ysse refused Seraphine in front of the crowd, minus 1 more if Seraphine was shamed, plus 1 if a PC struck her. Last words decoded 4, the Sleepless rise 4 (5 if Coyle's nail touched the flame), the Sleeper stirs 4.

== Strong start ==

The Keep gate. A Shellback banker three hundred and forty years old sits in a carried chair in the ash, with eight porters, two lawyers and the Reeve of Cinderford beside her, and she has been saying the same sentence for nearly a minute, stopping only for sips of water. She is not finished. The sentence is "We are here to collect."

== Clues ==

- Mote's cipher: S. paid; oil done by the red moon; the lamp to be taken under Levy default, escort at Ardmere; the Assessor knows nothing.
- Its fifth line: Tesh at the Velvet Ledger is the imperial line Vell needs.
- Marius Vell commissioned the Quiet Office's work in Cinderford.
- Tollamund wants the lantern as a key "to a door my people closed".
- The pay clause makes the company pledgeable; page seven makes the lamp a Treaty fixture, so "including the lantern" is void.
- The Reeve signed Cinderford's charter to the Concord, and has not yet sealed it.
- Grashka struck the blow that killed the Emperor, to stop him opening a ward (for Varra's eyes only).

== Lectern ==

The Keep gate. A Shellback banker three hundred and forty years old sits in a carried chair in the ash, with eight porters, two lawyers and the Reeve of Cinderford beside her, and she has been saying the same sentence for nearly a minute, stopping only for sips of water. She is not finished. The sentence is "We are here to collect."
