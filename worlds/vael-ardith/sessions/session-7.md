## Session 7: Three Knives
*Level 4. 5 to 6 Leafrot 3011 AW (Wardday, the Reeve's court day, and Restday). Four nights before the red moon is full again.*

- *Sky*: the Ember four nights from full and already the colour of a fresh wound; Pallas a waxing crescent.
- *Weather*: first frost. The ash crunches underfoot and the Keep's well has a skin of ice at dawn.
- *Touch these*: the Concord's move goes through Fennick's debts: he can be made to sign the stores over, or to hand Tollamund's people the Keep's keys, and what he does depends on whether the party stood by him. Sallow tries to have Vosk killed before he calls the yard. Sorrel risks the Ledger for Marrow. In the chaos Aelith makes her move on Sorrel: a sleeping draught, a hired barge, a cutting-table. On the 6th, while every eye is on the yard, the Sleepless slip up the hill to snuff the lantern; Ambry Coyle is the one who hesitates with his hand on the ladder. Mirrit, at Hesk's counter, sees the shadow behind Borrin take a step the wrong way. Ysse, the night of the 5th: "Not yet," in the warm voice, and "Keep me," closer than it has ever been.
- *In between* (2 to 4 Leafrot): the ride home. The party finds the company half-turned: Sallow worked on it while they were gone. Grace on the Tollamund note ends at sunset on the 4th.
- *Level-up*: End of session: level 5.
- *If the party…* sides with Sallow: he wins the yard, Marrow is locked in the oil-room, and at dawn on the 7th Sallow pours the last cut tin into the oil-port himself (it needs no key). The lantern gutters to a bead and stays there. The company that marches is about sixty, and Mote means to kill Sallow on the road once the lantern is out of the Keep.

**Goal.** Survive the two days every faction moves on the lantern, and break (or win) the mutiny.

### Running this session
**Last time** (read aloud). *"At the foot of the Ninth Stone, a Warden half turned to stone told you the truth: the flame belongs to the youngest of the Nine, it must go home, and the ward needs a heart that chooses. Something that wears Borrin's shadow stood up and bowed to the Stone. At the top of the climb a rider was waiting: 'Captain says come home now. They're all moving at once.' You rode in on the evening of the 3rd and found the company half-turned. Yesterday at sunset, the grace on the company's debt ran out."*

**Where the PCs are.** At the Keep, on the morning of 5 Leafrot, a Wardday and the Reeve's court day. Tollamund's chair is coming up the hill road. The party had the 4th to rest and get ready (see "In between").

**The party should have.** [[A Rubbing from the Ninth Stone]]; the pay clause, from the Treaty Stone's glass or [[The Original Treaty]] itself; proof against Sallow; the first key (Ysse). Any of these help: Hennet's slate-book, certified ([[The Price Was an Arm]]); [[Old Tally's Coin]]; [[The Lost Pay Order]]; [[The Charter of the Open River]]; Pell's Claim of Arrears; [[The Assessor's Lost Form]].

**If they missed something.**
- *Nobody has read the Treaty?* The original is kept at the Treaty Stone behind a grille with two locks, Marrow's and the Reeve's. Pell, with an Assessor's right of inspection, can fetch it on the 4th, a three-mile ride, and reads page seven aloud at the gate.
- *No proof against Sallow?* [[Brother Audit]] reads Sallow's prayers in the yard (scene 5), and the two horses at the Bridle & Yoke are proof of intent.
- *Never found Form 9-K?* Mote takes it with him on his night. If he is killed, caught, or flees at half hit points, it is in the satchel he drops.
- *Ysse refused the first key, or isn't in the party?* Mote comes for whoever holds it.

**The session at a glance.** Two days and six knives, and the party can't be everywhere. On the 5th: the Concord at the gate in the morning (1), a free afternoon (see "If the party wanders"), and Mote at night (2). On the 6th: the Saint's wardens at first light (3), Corvane at the foot of the hill in the morning (4), Sallow's mutiny at midday (5), and Grashka's letter as the dust settles (6). While everyone is in the yard, the Sleepless slip up to the cage (see "Touch these"). Let the players choose where to stand. Whatever they don't stop happens as written, and its cost arrives in session 8.

### The 5th (Wardday)

**1. The Concord's knife (the Keep gate, morning).** [[Grandmother Tollamund]] sits in her chair in the ash at the gate; she has left the house for the second time in four years, carried. Eight porters set the chair down. Sal and two lawyers walk behind it, with four Concord veterans in livery. She calls in the debt very slowly, pausing for sips of water from a cup Sal holds: the note stands at 4,800 gold; the quarter's interest, 480, is unpaid past grace (sunset yesterday); and the collateral is "the property of the company, including the lamp". It is court day, so [[Odile Fairweather]] the Reeve stands beside her, with the town's charter, signed but not sealed, and a pen. Whether Odile seals it or tears it up is the scene's outcome.

Talk, stall, or find the hole in the contract: History or Investigation DC 15, with [[The Original Treaty]] or what was scratched in the Treaty Stone's glass. The pay clause, "by the signatories, or by whosoever shall hold their debt", makes the Concord the company's paymaster and creditor, so the company itself can be pledged. But page seven, the keeping of the Ninth Ward, signed by the Mothers and the Last Wardens alone, makes the lamp a fixture of the Treaty, "kept lit by those who do not know why". It was never the company's to pledge. "Including the lantern" is void; the company is collateral, the lamp is not. Tollamund, if asked straight what she wants it for: "It is a key. To a door my people closed." Tick the Concord's clock.

**A second hole**, if the party kept Mother Hennet's slate-book and had it certified: it proves the House has been the garrison's paymaster since it bought the wages in 3009, and puts the account in dispute before Corvex. Tollamund accepts a stay ([[The Price Was an Arm]]; Concord clock −1).

**Development.** Tollamund leaves the way she came, slowly, with a stay, a claim, or a lantern she can't collect. If the party wasn't at the gate, Marrow faces her alone and loses the argument, and Odile seals the charter. The afternoon is the party's: Ford Street on court day, the two horses, Pardew's box, the Charter (see "If the party wanders"). Anyone watching the Assessor's Office sees the shutters close early. Night comes on: go to beat 2.

**2. The Quiet Office's knife (the night of the 5th).** [[Ansel Mote]] stops pretending. At the Assessor's Office he burns the files while [[Pell Quillon]] sleeps at his desk, then comes up to the Keep with two hired knives for the lantern and for Ysse's key. He has a copy of the third key, Sallow a copy of the second; neither fits the first lock, which is why he needs Ysse. Turning any key makes the cage chime through the whole Keep. If [[Dunstan Bray]] talked, Mote finds him first, in the Candle's back lane, and Bray never talks again. Run **Mote's night**. If the party befriended [[Wendel Hoop]], the cooper under the Assessor's Office, he comes up the hill on the night of the 4th to say his stair smells of lamp-oil, and Mote's night starts with the party already in the street.

If the party returned Pell's form, Pell wakes in the smoke, sees, and is braver than anyone expects: he runs to the Keep in his nightshirt with the Claim of Arrears under one arm, shouting for the watch. If he didn't wake, Widow Pruitt pulls him out by the collar. Nib, the errand boy, sleeps under the office stairs; somebody has to remember him.

**Development.** Mote's night ends with Mote fled, caught or dead, and the Assessor's Office burning or saved. If he flees, he goes east, ahead of the column, and his knives are on the road in session 8. If the party has the form, the cipher can be read before dawn (Pell, or an hour's work): go to "The cipher" below. Nobody sleeps. At first light, singing comes up the hill road: go to beat 3.

**Treasure.** Mote's satchel, if he dropped it: Form 9-K with the cipher on its back, a purse of new Regency silver (30 sp), and a copy of the third cage key. A handful of [[Widow's Kisses]] on the oil-room stair, which look like sweets.

**The cipher.** On the back of [[The Assessor's Lost Form]] (Form 9-K), if anyone has it, is Mote's cipher ([[The Clerk's Cipher]]): column widths that are letters. Pell, or Investigation DC 15 and an hour, reads it:
- *S. paid.*
- *Oil done by the red moon.*
- *Lamp to be taken under Levy default; escort at Ardmere.*
- *Assessor knows nothing.*

**Tesh.** A fifth line, squeezed in under the others: *The girl at the Ledger is the line. Bring her with the lamp.* [[Tesh]], the courtesan at the Velvet Ledger who does not sleep, has imperial blood through a by-blow of Aurel IX's uncle. [[Marius Vell]]'s claimant cousin in the capital has stopped speaking and is failing; Tesh is the line Vell needs, and a puppet who already hears the voice under the Stone is exactly what the Regent-Chancellor wants on his throne. If the party searches Mote's lodging over Gannet's on Temple Row, they find his black second ledger, and Vell's name in it.

**Treasure.** The black ledger, which is proof of everything the Quiet Office has done in the March since Reaping, if anyone ever gets it in front of a court. Shown to the yard in beat 5, it is proof against Sallow.

### The 6th (Restday)

**3. The Saint's knife (first light).** [[Seraphine Ashe]] comes for Ysse, not the lantern, with a crowd of the faithful singing behind her up the hill road. Gently. With an offer: Halcyra, a keeper's chair beside the Saint, the flame safe forever. If Ysse refuses, the Lamp-Wardens, in red surcoats for the road, try to take her to the Halcyra road (**The Lamp-Wardens on the Halcyra road**).

**Development.** Refused in front of the crowd, Seraphine goes back to the House with her faithful, and she will be at the lip in session 8. Shamed, she goes with less certainty, and fights less in the finale. If Ysse goes with her, the party has until the column leaves at dawn on the 7th to bring her back from the House, and Seraphine will not stop her if Ysse asks to go. As the singers go down the hill, someone else is coming up it, on a Raveler's arm: go to beat 4.

**4. Corvane comes down.** For the first time in a year [[Mother Corvane]] comes down from Corvel Rise, on Skrell's arm, and stands at the foot of Lantern Hill. She says it to Ysse, kindly: "The lamp is the lock, child. Put it out. Open the doors. Let them all go home." She believes it. She is wrong. Whether the party can talk her round decides who she is at the lip in session 8.

**Development.** Corvane doesn't stay to argue. She has said it, and she goes back up to the Rise to gather her undertakers. Talked round, she comes east as a friend. Not talked round, she comes east anyway, with her undertakers and the Door-Key, to do what she thinks is right. Behind the party, in the yard, someone starts shouting the files to order: go to beat 5.

**5. Sallow's mutiny (the drill yard).** With the company split by the debt, [[Garrick Sallow]] breaks out of confinement and calls the files to the yard: "They've sold us. Let's sell them first." Half the company is listening. Turning it:
- **Expose the coin.** Dannic's purse, Hennet's cup, the pay-chit, the letter in the small neat hand, Mote's cipher: Persuasion DC 13 with proof in hand, DC 18 without. Sallow exposed with proof: Mutiny clock −1.
- **Read the prayers.** Bring [[Brother Audit]] up from Temple Row and show him the mutiny: he opens his ledger and reads Sallow's two dated prayers aloud in the yard, in his Coinday voice. That is Sallow exposed with proof (Mutiny clock −1; it does not stack with exposing the coin).
- **Tell them about Marrow's purse.** She fed them out of her own savings for a year until it ran out. Marrow would rather die than have it said; said anyway, it turns the yard (Mutiny clock −2).
- **Let Vosk speak.** [[Mirrel Vosk]] tells them what the Iron Guard did to the last emperor who dug at a ward, and why: "The Guard did it. I held the door." He does not name who struck. If Vosk speaks first, two mutineers switch sides before swords come out.
- **Blades.** Run **The mutiny in the yard**. Every mutineer knocked out instead of killed is one the company gets back.

**The mutiny clock and the march.** Whatever the clock stands at when the dust settles decides how many soldiers walk east with the lantern in session 8:

| Mutiny clock | Who marches |
|---|---|
| 0 to 2 | about 120 |
| 3 to 4 | about 100 |
| 5 to 6 | about 60, and Sallow's diehards are on the road behind them |

Skarra Burnbrow has two thunderjars left and offers them for the road.

**Development.** The mutiny ends with Sallow in irons, dead, gone east with his diehards, or (if the party sided with him) in charge, which is "If the party…" above. While the yard settles, a letter under the Mothers' seal reaches the gate: go to beat 6.

**Treasure.** Two [[Thunderjars]] from Skarra. Sallow's rapier and his copy of the second key, if they take him.

**6. Grashka's answer.** A reply from the Mothers arrives on 6 Leafrot, in the middle of everything, regardless of whether Varra wrote: [[Skarra]] has written home to her mother, [[Mother Hesha]] of the council, every month since Varra came, and this is the answer ([[Grashka's Letter]]), under Hesha's seal and [[Grashka]]'s. If Varra wrote to the Mothers, it reads: "Hold the lamp. I am sending what I can." If she didn't: "Hold the lamp. I am coming to see what you have kept." Under it, in a second ink, in knot-script only Varra was taught to read, is a line for Varra's eyes only: "I did it. Not for the throne. For this. Ask Vosk." Vosk, if Varra asks him privately: "The Guard did it. I held the door." He does not name Grashka, then or ever. Whether the company learns whose granddaughter Varra is belongs to Varra's player. The Quiet Office would very much like to know.

**Development.** The letter is the last thing to arrive. After it, the day goes quiet, and the company gathers at the cage as the light goes. Go to "How it ends".

### How it ends
The mutiny is broken or it isn't. Either way the company, what's left of it, stands round the lantern in the frost. The Ember is three nights from full and the colour of a fresh wound. Marrow: "Whatever we're doing with that thing, we're doing it at the Rent. Under the moon. Together, or not at all." Before dawn, the cage is unbolted from its niche.

**End of session: level 5.**

### If the party wanders
- **Ford Street on court day.** The street is empty and every window open. Odile leaves [[The Reeve's House]] at first light in a magistrate's hat, the locked case under her arm, for the Keep gate. The six white-and-gold lengths at [[The Turncoat]] are cut and collected, for the six Lamp-Wardens who come on the 6th. [[The Hock]]'s back room is the one door open: Bray (if he is still Sallow's) and three others wait there for the word.
- **Two horses.** Booked at [[The Bridle & Yoke]] and waiting in the West Gate yard: two horses, saddled and rested, booked on 11 Ashfall by Bray in his own name, half paid in new Caer Ardith silver, "for a night in Leafrot, the fifth or the sixth". It is Sallow's bolt-hole, and proof of intent for the yard. Hob will not let them go without the rest of the money, in silver.
- **The Charter at the Reeve's court.** [[The Charter of the Open River]], waved at a Concord lawyer: "It's not a bridge, it's a barge." [[Barge-Mother Posy Reedmarsh]] brings the 3009 pay order up the hill on her own legs if the party earned it ([[The Lost Pay Order]]).
- **Pardew's cedar box.** If Pell wrote to [[Examiner Lusk Pardew]] at [[Whitepost]] in his own hand by the 1st, a letter and a box of 141 sealed orders arrive today. The newest is the Office of Charters' standing instruction to pass Tollamund's crates unexamined and unrecorded.
- **The water plan.** Tollamund has written to [[Barge-Mother Thimble Fairlock]] asking her to carry "a lamp, in Leafrot, to Gilt" once she owns it. Thimble has not answered. A party that asked the Mother the right question has known for a fortnight.
- **Old Tally's seal.** Laid on the table in front of the Grandmother ([[Old Tally's Coin]]), the 11th Foot's pensions seal buys a long stillness, "Where ... did you get ... that," a lawyer sent off with a letter, and a day. Trade it for something concrete: Fennick's paper torn up, or Odile's charter back in her hands unsealed.
