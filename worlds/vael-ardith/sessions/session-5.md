## Session 5: Last Words
*Level 3. Ironday, 25 Ashfall 3011 AW.*

- *Sky*: the Ember waning, a lopsided red half that rises a little after midnight; Pallas a thin crescent before dawn.
- *Weather*: a warm wind off the Rent all day, smelling of pears and struck matches.
- *Touch these*: Mother Hennet sees the shadow behind Borrin, says nothing, and pours a second drink for it. Ambry Coyle comes to "invite" Tick to the Sleepless vaults under the Smokes. Fennick, if anyone has found out about his four casks, confesses everything in one long breathless paragraph. Aelith helps read the ledger in exchange for copies. Pell, pale, has found the Ninth's wages in his Treasury copies, signed out every quarter "to Account Deferred, by order of the Regent-Chancellor", and wants someone to know. After the sap, Ysse dreams in the warm voice: *Home.*
- *In between* (21 to 24 Ashfall): Sallow is in his quarters; his people keep their heads down. If the party refused her, Seraphine grows colder, and Iskavethi pilgrims start arriving on the Ash Road in numbers. Tollamund presses Odile, who has now signed the charter and not sealed it. Old Halloran Pike dies in his cot in the followers' lines the night of the 24th. The company still wears the black armbands from Cinders.
- *Level-up*: End of session: level 4.
- *If the party…* won't go to the funeral: Pike won't lie down. By noon Skrell runs up to the Keep with his handcart: "He's asking for the new ones. By name. He's very polite about it." Corvane will not lend the key until they come.

**Goal.** Find out what the ninth lantern was lit from, and what "the lamp is the lock" means.

---

### Scene 1: The funeral that would not end (Corvel Rise)
Old [[Halloran Pike]] sits up at his own funeral on [[Corvel Rise]] (see [[The Funeral That Would Not End]]). Before the door can open, he has a confession to make, sitting up on his bier with the coin he spat out in his lap:
- He was the deserter Pike of the company's story. He did carry the ninth lantern up to the Keep the night of Cinders. He never walked into the Rent: he went as far as its lip, lost his nerve, and came back two years later under his own name, and nobody looked twice at another Pike. ("There are a great many Pikes.")
- On the night of Cinders, at the cooks' camp in the Orchard, [[Mother Hennet]] lit the ninth lantern from a cookfire that was burning on a lump of grey stone, with no wood under it. "It was cold. In the fire, and it was cold. It talked. I thought I was dying." He carried the lantern. Hennet carried the stone after him in a soup-pot. The boy with the burnt hand, [[Brannoc Saltash]], followed them up the hill: he says he sang; Pike says he howled.
- Years later he drew a chart for Vexa of what he'd seen at the lip and what her crews told him, and signed it "-P".

Then [[Mother Corvane]], who has come down from her roost for this, lends [[Hathe's Door-Key]] (kept in the reliquary at Corvel Rise and lent for funerals) to finish the rite. When the door opens, Pike's last words are the same as the seven in Quorl's notebook: "The lamp is the lock." He is the eighth.

If Dannic was never laid to rest, he is on the Rise too, under a fresh mound that won't stay flat. Quorl can open the door for him while the key is in his hand.

---

### Scene 2: The ledger (the bone-library)
Corvane takes the party into the bone-library at the heart of the roosts and opens the Rook-Court's Ledger of Last Words: forty narrow books, every dying sentence a Raveler has heard in the March since 3002. Read a handful aloud; let the players hear the voices:
- *"Tell my brother the dog was his."* (a sapper, the Field of Lanterns, 3003)
- *"The lamp. Mind the lamp."* (a sentry on the east wall, 3004)
- *"Wenna, hold his hand. Don't look up."* (Marla Marlowe, a cellar in the Smokes, the night of Cinders; told, not heard: given at dawn by her mother to the fledgling who came for the body)
- *"Is it Restday?"* (a cook, the Keep, 3005)
- *"…is the… is the…"* (a Glasser, Fort Mercy, the night it burned, 3005)
- *"Lock the door, Mam."* (a boy, Temple Row, the Night of Open Eyes, 3006)
- *"The same five words, Mother. The tide-dead all say the same five words."* (copied from the Hask priestess's letter, 3008)
- *"Tell the Captain it was me."* (a soldier of the Ninth, the ford, 3009)
- *"It's the lamp. Isn't it. It's the lamp."* (a barge-woman, the Low Quay, 3010)
- *"…lock…"* (a widow, the Smokes, 3010)
- *"Is the bread come?"* (a follower's child, the Keep's lines, 3011)
- *"It's cold. It's cold under the glass."* (Dannic Rook, the Ford, 14 Ashfall 3011)

Quorl reading with her: Religion or Investigation DC 14 to see the pattern before Corvane says it. Corvane has had four of the words for years and guessed the fifth. Now Pike has said it, and she says it aloud: "Lock." Tick the Rook-Court's clock to full. Asked why the drowned man said it was cold under the glass: "The dead are cold because they lie near the leak."

What a full clock means: Corvane is certain now, and wrong. "The lamp is the lock. Locks are for opening, child. Put it out, and Hathe's doors open, and all of them can finally go home." From here she argues for snuffing it (session 7), and she is at the lip in session 8.

If anyone asks after her family, Corvane mentions her sister Keld, who put a silver on "never" in Spoons's pool the year it opened and then simply went away. "She always did like long odds."

### Scene 3: The Weeping Orchard
For [[Sap from the Weeping Orchard]], or following the confession, the party walks two miles north-east up the orchard lane to [[The Weeping Orchard]], under a waning Ember. The trees weep only at the full; nine days on, every bough carries beads of set sap, the oldest tree is trickling, and the Sleepless are scraping them. [[The Dreaming Widow]] is here if the party hasn't dealt with her, with [[Ambry Coyle]] at her elbow.

Complication: a cult fanatic and four pilgrims who won't let outsiders take the sap (**Orchard pilgrims**). If the party stays past midnight, the dead in the grave rows climb out at Ember-rise (**The stone-touched dead**). A line of sap on the ground stops them.

---

### Scene 4: The fire-pit
At the orchard's heart is the cooks' camp, where the company should have mustered in silence on the 14th: a ring of stone-fused cookpots, and the fire-pit, just as Pike said. Glass in the ashes. Burnt into the stone beside it, a ring the exact size of a soup-pot's foot. In the grave rows along the north wall, one grave holds a lantern-pole and no body: somebody meant to bury a lantern here, and the lantern went up the hill instead.

---

### Scene 5: The shard's fate
**At the Candle.** The Last Candle is shut, for the first time since it was built. Mother Hennet lets them in by the kitchen door. The soup-pot hangs over the kitchen fire, black and dented. Pike's name is on the slate in fresh chalk, with nothing owing. She looks at them for a long time. "Took you long enough." If the party missed Vexa's chart, Hennet takes Pike's own copy out of the book under the bar and gives it to them: "He'd want it used. He never could."

**At the Keep.** [[Brother Tallow]], confronted, admits it: he has kept [[The Ward Shard]] under the lantern for nine years and has always been afraid to say what it was. "It's under the cage." It is reached from the oil-room in the tower's base, by the trap-ladder, no key needed: look up through the oil-port at the foot of the cage, and there in the lamp's bowl under the flame is a finger-long splinter of grey stone, faintly warm, with a hairline crack of red down its length.

[[Aelith Sorn]], who has followed the party all day, wants one more thing: the stone hand in the false floor of Kettle's barge. (If the party ran Kettle's Cargo, they know exactly what she means. If not, she tells them where it is, and that she paid for it.)

### Optional: the Treaty Stone
If there's time, or if Pell asks for an escort, ride three miles east along the staked road to [[The Treaty Stone]]: pilgrims, widows leaving pebbles, the grille over the Original Treaty, and, scratched into the glass at the Stone's foot by a Raveler witness in 3002, the pay clause as it was really signed: *"by the signatories, or by whosoever shall hold their debt."* Pell reads it twice and says, "Oh dear." It is half of the argument the party needs in session 7. (This can also go on session 6's road east.)

---

### How it ends
At the Keep, a drop of sap tipped into the oil-port makes the lantern burn tall and white, and every wick-lamp on the hill burns white with it, for a whole day. For a moment Ysse sees a standing figure in the flame, enormous and very tired, looking at her. Tallow: "It wants to go home." Marrow decides: someone has to go to the Ninth Stone.

**End of session: level 4.**

== Strong start ==

The funeral is going beautifully. The Ravelers are singing, the salt is on the step, the coin is on old Pike's tongue. Then Pike spits out the coin, sits up on the bier, and says, with great dignity, "I'm terribly sorry. The door's locked." Two mourners faint. Mother Corvane closes her eyes and says, "Not again."

== Scenes ==

- Pike sits up at his own funeral and confesses: the deserter, the cookfire with a stone in it, the soup-pot. Hathe's Door-Key opens the door; his last words: "The lamp is the lock."
- Reading the Rook-Court's ledger of last words with Mother Corvane. She says the fifth word aloud.
- The Weeping Orchard under a waning red moon: beads of set sap, the Sleepless scraping them, the dead in the grave rows.
- The fire-pit at the cooks' camp: glass, a soup-pot ring, an empty lantern-pole grave.
- The Candle shut for the first time: Hennet, "Took you long enough."
- Brother Tallow: the shard is under the cage, seen through the oil-port.
- Optional: the Treaty Stone, and the clause scratched in its glass.

== Clues ==

- Old Pike was the deserter of the legend; he carried the ninth lantern up the hill, and drew the Glasser's chart.
- The ninth lantern was lit from a cookfire burning on a grey stone with no wood, at the cooks' camp in the Weeping Orchard. Hennet carried the stone in a soup-pot.
- The stone was a shard blown out of the Ninth Stone's crack.
- A thousand last words read together say: "The lamp is the lock." Pike is the eighth to say it to Quorl.
- Mother Corvane believes putting the lamp out will open Hathe's doors and let the dead rest.
- Brother Tallow has kept the Ward Shard in the lamp's bowl for nine years; it can be seen through the oil-port.
- Orchard sap makes the flame and every wick-lamp burn white; Ysse sees a standing figure in it.
- The Regency's pay for the Ninth was signed out every quarter "to Account Deferred, by order of the Regent-Chancellor".
- The dead lie cold because they lie near the leak (Corvane).
