## Session 5: Last Words
*Level 3. Ironday, 25 Ashfall 3011 AW.*

- *Sky*: the Ember waning, a lopsided red half that rises a little after midnight; Pallas a thin crescent before dawn.
- *Weather*: a warm wind off the Rent all day, smelling of pears and struck matches.
- *Touch these*: Mother Hennet sees the shadow behind Borrin, says nothing, and pours a second drink for it. Ambry Coyle comes to "invite" Tick to the Sleepless vaults under the Smokes. Fennick, if anyone has found out about his four casks, confesses everything in one long breathless paragraph. Aelith helps read the ledger in exchange for copies. Pell, pale, has found the Ninth's wages in his Treasury copies, signed out every quarter "to Account Deferred, by order of the Regent-Chancellor", and wants someone to know. After the sap, Ysse dreams in the warm voice: *Home.*
- *In between* (21 to 24 Ashfall): Sallow is in his quarters; his people keep their heads down. If the party refused her, Seraphine grows colder, and Iskavethi pilgrims start arriving on the Ash Road in numbers. Tollamund presses Odile, who has now signed the charter and not sealed it. Old Halloran Pike dies in his cot in the followers' lines the night of the 24th. The company still wears the black armbands from Cinders.
- *Level-up*: End of session: level 4.
- *If the party…* won't go to the funeral: Pike won't lie down. By noon Skrell runs up to the Keep with his handcart: "He's asking for the new ones. By name. He's very polite about it." Corvane will not lend the key until they come.

**Goal.** Find out what the ninth lantern was lit from, and what "the lamp is the lock" means.

### Running this session
**Last time** (read aloud). *"You walked out to the House of Unburnt Light and ate the Saint's bread, and for an afternoon you wanted to stay. Sorrel Vey told you what the church really wants with the flame. Lieutenant Sallow is in his quarters, or he isn't, and either way the company is watching him. That was five days ago. Last night, old Halloran Pike died in his cot in the followers' lines, and this morning the Ravelers are burying him."*

**Where the PCs are.** At the Keep, on the morning of 25 Ashfall, an Ironday. [[Skrell Already-Gone]] comes for Quorl at first light: Pike's funeral is this morning on [[Corvel Rise]], and Pike asked, before he died, for "the new ones" to be there. The company still wears its black armbands from Cinders. Everyone who knew Pike walks up.

**The party should have.** The first key (Ysse), whatever Sorrel gave them, Seraphine's letter if they took it, and [[A Glasser's Chart]] if they traded for it. Perhaps Tallow's request for [[Sap from the Weeping Orchard]] and Spoons's for [[The Wager on Never]].

**If they missed something.**
- *Tallow never asked for the sap?* He asks this morning as they leave for the Rise: "If you go anywhere near the Orchard, bring me sap. The red, not the clear."
- *No chart?* Hennet hands over Pike's own copy in scene 5.
- *Never took the Wager?* Corvane's "That was my sister" still lands, and Spoons, hearing it, asks them that evening.
- *Sallow was never confined?* He stays away from the funeral, and nothing he does today matters. Session 7 is where he moves.

**The session at a glance.** One long day. The funeral in the morning (scene 1) and the ledger at midday (scene 2) run in order. Then the party has the afternoon and evening, and the best way round is: the Last Candle first, since it is on the way from the Rise to the orchard lane (scene 5, the soup-pot); out to the Orchard with the pot in the afternoon (scenes 3 and 4), where it drops into the ring in front of them; and up to the Keep by evening for Tallow and the shard (scene 5 again). A party that goes to the Orchard first sees the ring, fetches the pot from the Candle at dusk, and takes Hennet's word that it fits; that works too. The session ends at the Keep, with the sap in the oil-port. The Treaty Stone is optional, for a party with time in hand.

### Scene 1: The funeral that would not end (Corvel Rise)
Old [[Halloran Pike]] sits up at his own funeral on [[Corvel Rise]] (see [[The Funeral That Would Not End]]). Before the door can open, he has a confession to make, sitting up on his bier with the coin he spat out in his lap:
- He was the deserter Pike of the company's story. He did carry the ninth lantern up to the Keep the night of Cinders. He never walked into the Rent: he went as far as its lip, lost his nerve, and came back two years later under his own name, and nobody looked twice at another Pike. ("There are a great many Pikes.")
- On the night of Cinders, at the cooks' camp in the Orchard, [[Mother Hennet]] lit the ninth lantern from a cookfire that was burning on a lump of grey stone, with no wood under it. "It was cold. In the fire, and it was cold. It talked. I thought I was dying." He carried the lantern. Hennet carried the stone after him in a soup-pot, and at the Keep she tipped it out into Brother Tallow's hands. "He never said a word about it after, and neither did I." The boy with the burnt hand, [[Brannoc Saltash]], followed them up the hill: he says he sang; Pike says he howled.
- Years later he drew a chart for Vexa of what he'd seen at the lip and what her crews told him, and signed it "-P".

Then [[Mother Corvane]], who has come down from her roost for this, lends [[Hathe's Door-Key]] (kept in the reliquary at Corvel Rise and lent for funerals) to finish the rite. When the door opens, Pike's last words are the same as the seven in Quorl's notebook: "The lamp is the lock." He is the eighth.

While the door stands open the room sings the long verse, *and neither was he* ([[Songs of the March]]). If anyone mentions Spoons's pool, Corvane, rattling her shawls as if it were a joke: "That was my sister." It is not a joke ([[The Wager on Never]]).

If Dannic was never laid to rest, he is on the Rise too, under a fresh mound that won't stay flat. Quorl can open the door for him while the key is in his hand.

**Development.** Pike goes through the door, and this time he stays gone. The mourners go home talking. Corvane does not: she looks at Quorl for a long moment, and then asks the party into the bone-library "before the light goes off the shelves". Go to scene 2. If Quorl isn't in the party, she asks whoever said the rhyme, or whoever looked as if Pike's confession meant something to them.

**Treasure.** [[Hathe's Door-Key]], lent for the rite. Corvane wants it back at the end of the day. If Quorl asks to keep it a while longer and gives her a reason, she lets him, which matters if the party takes the Cold Seam in session 6. Pike's spat-out coin, which nobody will touch.

### Scene 2: The ledger (the bone-library)
Corvane takes the party into the bone-library at the heart of the roosts and opens the Rook-Court's Ledger of Last Words: forty narrow books, every dying sentence a Raveler has heard in the March since 3002. Read a handful aloud; let the players hear the voices. (This year's book is kept down at the Doorkeeper's Chapel until Longnight, so Skrell has run it up the hill under his arm for the funeral; the two 3011 lines come from it, and Corvane reads them last.)
- *"Tell my brother the dog was his."* (a sapper, the Field of Lanterns, 3003)
- *"The lamp. Mind the lamp."* (a sentry on the east wall, 3004)
- *"Wenna, hold his hand. Don't look up."* (Marla Marlowe, a cellar in the Smokes, the night of Cinders; told, not heard: given at dawn by her mother to the fledgling who came for the body)
- *"Is it Restday?"* (a cook, the Keep, 3005)
- *"…is the… is the…"* (a Glasser, Fort Mercy, the night it burned, 3005)
- *"Lock the door, Mam."* (a boy, Temple Row, the Night of Open Eyes, 3006)
- *"The same five words, Mother. The tide-dead all say the same five words."* (copied from the Hask priestess's letter, 3008)
- *"Tell the Captain it was me."* (a soldier of the Ninth, the ford, 3009)
- *"It's the lamp. Isn't it. It's the lamp."* (a barge-woman, the Low Quay, 3010)
- *"…lock…"* (a widow, the Smokes, 3010)
- *"Is the bread come?"* (a follower's child, the Keep's lines, 3011)
- *"It's cold. It's cold under the glass."* (Dannic Rook, the Ford, 14 Ashfall 3011)

For more voices, roll on [[Last Words]].

Quorl reading with her: Religion or Investigation DC 14 to see the pattern before Corvane says it. Corvane has had four of the words for years and guessed the fifth. Now Pike has said it, and she says it aloud: "Lock." Tick the Rook-Court's clock to full. Asked why the drowned man said it was cold under the glass: "The dead are cold because they lie near the leak."

What a full clock means: Corvane is certain now, and wrong. "The lamp is the lock. Locks are for opening, child. Put it out, and Hathe's doors open, and all of them can finally go home." From here she argues for snuffing it (session 7), and she is at the lip in session 8.

If anyone asks after her family, Corvane mentions her sister Keld, who put a silver on "never" in Spoons's pool the year it opened and then simply went away. "She always did like long odds."

**Development.** Pike's confession and the ledger point two ways at once: out to the cooks' camp in the Orchard, where the lantern was lit, and down to the Last Candle, where Hennet has kept the secret for nine years. It is early afternoon. A party that goes to the Orchard first (scene 3) is back by dusk; a party that goes to the Candle first finds it shut, and Hennet lets them in by the kitchen door (scene 5). Either way, Corvane's wrong conclusion goes with them: from today, she wants the lamp put out.

**Treasure.** A page of the Rook-Court's ledger, copied out in Corvane's hand, if the party helped the funeral end ([[The Funeral That Would Not End]]).

### Scene 3: The Weeping Orchard
For [[Sap from the Weeping Orchard]], or following the confession, the party walks two miles north-east up the orchard lane to [[The Weeping Orchard]], under a waning Ember. The trees weep only at the full; nine days on, every bough carries beads of set sap, the oldest tree is trickling, and the Sleepless are scraping them. [[The Dreaming Widow]] is here if the party hasn't dealt with her, with [[Ambry Coyle]] at her elbow. She wants nothing from them today but Tick: "Come and sit with us at the next full, Tibbet. Just sit." If Tick is with the party she tells the pilgrims to let them scrape, and they do; if not, she watches the fight and does not join it.

Gathering a vial from the boughs takes an hour: three successes before two failures on Survival, Nature or Medicine DC 12 (see [[Sap from the Weeping Orchard]]).

Complication: a cult fanatic and four pilgrims who won't let outsiders take the sap (**Orchard pilgrims**). If the party stays past midnight, the dead in the grave rows climb out at Ember-rise (**The stone-touched dead**). A line of sap on the ground stops them: crushed beads smeared across a path, or one vial poured out, make a line about ten feet long that they will not cross for an hour. A party that gathered sap for Tallow has to decide whether to spend it here.

**Development.** The cooks' camp is at the orchard's heart, a few minutes' walk through the stone trees: go to scene 4. If the pilgrims have run them off without sap, the oldest tree in the cooks' camp is still trickling, and nobody is guarding it.

**Treasure.** A vial or two of set sap scraped from the boughs, which is what Tallow asked for. A handful of [[Stone Pears]] from the ground. Beads of [[Orchard Amber]] that the pilgrims dropped (two silver each at Gutter Market).

### Scene 4: The fire-pit
At the orchard's heart is the cooks' camp, where the company should have mustered in silence on the 14th: a ring of stone-fused cookpots, and the fire-pit, just as Pike said. Glass in the ashes. Burnt into the stone beside it, a ring the exact size of a soup-pot's foot. In the grave rows along the north wall, one grave holds a lantern-pole and no body: somebody meant to bury a lantern here, and the lantern went up the hill instead.

**Development.** The ring in the stone wants a pot, and Pike said whose pot: go to scene 5, at the Last Candle. If the party already has the pot, setting it in the ring is the proof, and they can take it straight up to the Keep. Warn them, if the light is going, that the Ember rises a little after midnight tonight and the grave rows are not quiet then.

### Scene 5: The shard's fate
**At the Candle.** The Last Candle is shut, for the first time since it was built. Mother Hennet lets them in by the kitchen door. The soup-pot hangs on its hook in the cold corner of the kitchen, black, dented and scrubbed dry. Pike's name is on the slate in fresh chalk, with nothing owing. She looks at them for a long time. "Took you long enough." She takes [[Mother Hennet's Soup-Pot]] off its hook and puts it in a PC's arms. Carried to the fire-pit and set in the ring, it fits to a hair. Asked what was in it: "A stone. I gave it to Tallow. Ask him where he put it." And, as she lets them out: "A bargee came for my book last week with new silver, the same mint as the coin in the cup under the bar. I told him no. He said he'd be back." That is the party's warning: someone should watch the Candle tonight (see "If the party wanders"). If the party missed Vexa's chart, Hennet takes Pike's own copy out of the book under the bar and gives it to them: "He'd want it used. He never could."

**At the Keep.** [[Brother Tallow]], confronted, admits it: he has kept [[The Ward Shard]] under the lantern for nine years and has always been afraid to say what it was. "It's under the cage." It is reached from the oil-room in the tower's base, by the trap-ladder, no key needed: look up through the oil-port at the foot of the cage, and there in the lamp's bowl under the flame is a finger-long splinter of grey stone, cold enough that the flame above it throws no heat on that side, with a hairline crack of red down its length.

[[Aelith Sorn]], who has followed the party all day, wants one more thing: the stone hand in the false floor of Kettle's barge. (If the party ran Kettle's Cargo, they know exactly what she means. If not, she tells them where it is, and that she paid for it.)

**Development.** With the shard found, the party has the whole of it: the lantern burns on a piece of the Ninth Stone. Tallow will tell them what he fears if they bring him the sap: go to "How it ends", at the oil-port. If they have no sap, the flame stays thin, and Marrow's decision is the same, only grimmer. Aelith's stone hand can wait for another day; it will still be pointing east.

**Treasure.** [[Mother Hennet's Soup-Pot]]. Pike's own copy of the Rent chart, if they lacked one. [[The Ward Shard]] stays where it is, under the flame. Tallow will not let anyone lift it out of the bowl, and he is right.

### Optional: the Treaty Stone
If there's time, or if Pell asks for an escort, ride three miles east along the staked road to [[The Treaty Stone]]: pilgrims, widows leaving pebbles, the grille over the Original Treaty, and, scratched into the glass at the Stone's foot by a Raveler witness in 3002, the pay clause as it was really signed: *"by the signatories, or by whosoever shall hold their debt."* Pell reads it twice and says, "Oh dear." It is half of the argument the party needs in session 7. (This can also go on session 6's road east.)

**Development.** A three-mile ride each way: an afternoon, back by dusk. Fit it in after the ledger, instead of the Orchard, only if the party can come back for the Orchard another day before the 29th.

### How it ends
Tallow will not tip the sap in without the Captain there, and sends a wick running for her. [[Ilse Marrow]] stands in the oil-room door with her arms folded and hears all of it: Pike, the pot, the stone under the flame.

At the Keep, a drop of sap tipped into the oil-port makes the lantern burn tall and white, and every wick-lamp on the hill burns white with it, for a whole day. For a moment Ysse sees a standing figure in the flame, enormous and very tired, looking at her. Tallow: "It wants to go home." Marrow watches every lamp in the yard go white, and that is what decides her: "If that's what's keeping it lit, I want to know who's holding the other end." Someone has to go to the Ninth Stone.

**End of session: level 4.**

### If the party wanders
- **The night the Candle is shut.** Mote's hired knife, a stranger off the Low Quay, comes with lamp-oil for the loft and the slate-book under the bar. A party watching the Candle catches him; he knows only that "a clerk" paid him. Nobody watching: the loft burns, the slate is scorched black and the book is gone ([[The Price Was an Arm]]).
- **The second witness.** [[Aurelio Bask]], the tailor at the Turncoat, ran from the wall at Cinders and spent the night in the cooks' camp, where he saw Hennet tip something glowing into the soup-pot. He has kept it seven years. He tells it if asked in front of a priest, or by Hennet, or by Marrow; a PC who has been kind to him hears it first.
- **Sedgemere and Keld.** "That was my sister" sends the party nine miles down the towpath (a day) or half a day by barge. [[Keld Owes-Nothing]] answers a question asked directly and respectfully, from her doorway, and gives the fifth word to anyone who tells her the truth about the lamp. She comes to the Stone only for someone who says, in person, that the lamp is to be given back.
- **Posy's pay order.** If the party has earned it, [[Barge-Mother Posy Reedmarsh]] hands over the 3009 order on the quay: [[The Lost Pay Order]].
- **Ambercote** (a long day, 26 to 28 Ashfall). The standing are plain by daylight, and the Sleepless crews come at the next red moon, 9 Leafrot: a fortnight. See [[Ambercote]] and [[The Standing Village]]. [[Orchard Amber]], cut at dawn on the 17th, is at Gutter Market for two silver.
- **Hollowhall, by letter.** To have [[Hold-Mother Greta Holm]] at the Stone on 9 Leafrot, someone must send for her now: she needs a fortnight's warning and a barge or a horse.
- **If Sorrel is threatened**, a note reaches the Velvet Ledger by barge in an old square hand: "I could read it. I am sorry. Come home, or do not; but do not let her cut it. C." Sorrel does not understand it for a day. See [[Caelo once-Mar]].

== Strong start ==

The funeral is going beautifully. The Ravelers are singing, the salt is on the step, the coin is on old Pike's tongue. Then Pike spits out the coin, sits up on the bier, and says, with great dignity, "I'm terribly sorry. The door's locked." Two mourners faint. Mother Corvane closes her eyes and says, "Not again."

- The stone was a shard blown out of the Ninth Stone's crack.
