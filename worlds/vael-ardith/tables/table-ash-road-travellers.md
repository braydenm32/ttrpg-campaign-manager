Roll once a day on the Ash Road, or whenever the party passes the Burned Mill, Tally's Cut or the glassland track and you want a face. Each entry is someone going somewhere, with something they want and a line they say; give them the line and let the party decide whether to stop. Use **Ashenreach Encounters** for the open plain and for anything that wants a fight.

== GM notes ==

Nobody here attacks the party. Most of them want to be walked somewhere, told something, or left alone. Re-roll anything the party has met already in the same session; a traveller met twice should remember them. The last three entries are for the days before a full Ember.

== Entries ==

- 2x Three widows from Fallowden on foot, roped together, with a bell, walking to the Treaty Stone to leave a thing. They have counted the stakes and got a different number from yesterday. "Which of you is a soldier? Tell us it's nothing."
- 2x A Glasser cart going west with a straw-packed crate that hums, and beside it a Concord packer who walks with one hand on the lid and does not haggle, bargain or answer. "The crate is paid for. So am I."
- 2x A company stake-party, one stake short, arguing about whose count was wrong. The sergeant wants to know if the party saw a stake with a blue rag on it. "If you did, don't tell me where. Tell me who."
- Gerda Hollin of the gatehouse on her Restday walk to the Stone, a pebble in one hand and a loaf under her arm. She will walk with the party as far as the Stone and no further, unless they are going to the Burned Mill, in which case the loaf is for the widow there. "The Lithic stood so we could walk. Walk, then."
- Kettle Brindlewake's cousins, Dob and Marigold, with a hand-cart of salt and iron bars, going south at the Burned Mill fork. They say almost nothing. Marigold says: "Cheese on the way back, if you're coming." Nobody has asked where.
- A Returned dwarf walking east, alone, without a shadow, saying nothing, at a pace that does not change. He does not stop at the Stone. Asked where he is going, he points at the ground. (One or two a season, says Hollowhall; this is one.)
- A Remembrancer's luggage on a handcart, five leather cases with Sael locks, pushed by a Cinderford carter who was paid in silver to take it to the House of Unburnt Light and does not know where the Remembrancer is. "She said she'd walk. Who walks?"
- A company patrol of four turning back at the Treaty Stone, as the patrol does, with the corporal touching the ninth mark on the glass. He wants to know if the party is going further and, if so, whether they would carry a letter to the Burned Mill. "Tell the widow the stake-party's coming in spring. It isn't. Tell her anyway."
- Skelt, drunk, walking the wrong way: east from Cinderford toward Fort Mercy with no water, or west from the fort toward town with no coin, at an hour when he should be asleep. He wants a drink, and then he wants to tell you about a chart, and then he wants very badly to change the subject. "I held the rope. That's all. Anyone can hold a rope."
- Nance Oddy's empty bench at the Burned Mill: she is not under the tree, the butt-lid is off, and the bell is ringing from the direction of the Treaty Stone, three miles west, at the wrong hour. She is on the road with her yoke and two buckets, and has seen something at the fork she wants counted by a witness.
- A pilgrim of the Unburnt Saint on foot, grey to the knees, who has been told at the mission that the Treaty Stone is "closed" and is walking there anyway. She wants to know if that is true. It is not. "They said the wardens would turn me back. Would they? Are you them?"
- A Sleepless walker in grey, alone, at dusk, carrying an unlit candle in both hands as if it were lit, walking east. He wants the party to come with him. He does not say where. "You look so tired. He'll let you rest. Walk with me." He will walk until the Rent if nobody turns him round.
- A courier for the Assessor's Office on a lame mule, with a satchel of forms and a streaming cold, who has been told the Office is at Fort Mercy and is beginning to suspect it is not. "Is this the March of Cinders? All of it? Where does it stop?"
- A stone-touched dead man standing beside the road with his arms at his sides, half turned to grey, in no colours anyone can name. He does not move toward the party. He says, as they pass, "Not yet," and then, if anyone stops, "Which lantern is lit?" (See Ambercote; see the Cold Captain.)
- A Regency surveyor's mule, saddled, with a chain and a tripod strapped on, and no surveyor. It is walking west, slowly, and will follow anyone who takes its bridle. There is a notebook in the saddle-bag with the plain measured to the Burned Mill and the last entry, in a different hand: *Told him not to. He went to look.*
- A goat-herd from the south with a she-goat on a rope and a wedge of white cheese in a cloth, going to the Treaty Stone, which he has never seen. He is twelve, has one grey eye, and wants to know if it is true that soldiers get paid. "Cass says you don't. Cass says a lot."
- Two Fort Mercy Glassers carrying a door between them, west, with something on it under a blanket that is the wrong shape for a man. They want the party to look the other way, and will pay a copper each for it. "It's not what you think. It's worse. Look at the Stone."
- A pedlar with a hand-cart of stakes, each with a fresh rag on it, selling them to travellers at two coppers a stake "for the walk back". The stakes are the company's: pulled from the road behind him. He is cheerful about it. "Fifty paces and a rag, friend. I'm just moving the rag."
- *(The day before a full Ember.)* A Lamp-Warden of the Saint in a red surcoat walking a dying man to the House of Unburnt Light, the man's arm over his shoulders, going slowly. The man wants to stop. The warden wants him not to. "Another mile. She'll see you. She sees everyone, the night before."
- *(The day before a full Ember.)* Six Hollowborn children from the Smokes, hand in hand, walking east along the orchard lane's turning with a tanner's wife behind them, not stopping them, not quite following. They are going to the Weeping Orchard to sit under the trees. The smallest wants the party to come. "It only hurts the first time."
- *(The day before a full Ember, while he lives.)* Halloran Pike, seventy, with his limp and his beard, three miles out on the road with a pipe and a bag of salt, walking east, a good deal further from the Candle fire than anyone has ever seen him. He wants to be asked nothing. "Thought I'd look at it. The road. It's still there, then." He turns back at the Stone, after a long time, and does not touch the ninth mark.
