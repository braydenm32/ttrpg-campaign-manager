Roll when a PC asks "what else have you got?" in any shop on Ford Street, Temple Row or the Smokes, or when a keeper needs a thing on the counter to talk about. These are not the Glassers' salvage ([[Gutter Market Salvage]] has that); they are what nine years of a town on the edge of the ash has left on its own shelves. Prices are the asking price; a story knocks a quarter off. For the real prices on Ford Street, see [[What Things Cost in the Ashenreach]].

== GM notes ==

Half of these are texture and half are leads. The badge, the Keel tin, the dream-book page, the survey and the warm chip all point at something; the child's shoe and the ring are there so the players stop and the keeper has to say where they came from. If a PC buys the blank 44-A, Pell will be delighted and Mote will not.

== Entries ==

- An unredeemed badge of the Seventh, the Ruddy Hundred, brass, the hundred-mark worn smooth. 5 silver, "and Mother Hennet will want to know who sold it".
- A blank Form 44-A, Declaration of Non-Receipt of Imperial Funds, slightly foxed. 3 copper. Nobody has filled one in and the shopkeeper does not know what it is for.
- A jar of Stillwater blind-fish, dried, heads off. 2 copper. The keeper will not say who took the heads off, or where they went.
- A stone pear with a bite out of it. Not for sale. Everybody says nobody has ever bitten one, and the keeper does not know whose teeth.
- A dark-lantern rusted open, the shutter seized. 4 silver. It was found with the shutter open and has stayed that way.
- A coat re-dyed from Greywake's Spears' colours, badly: the grey shows through at the seams. 2 silver. From the Turncoat, three doors down, originally.
- A framed Ardmere Receipt, No. 0088, thirty-one crowns. 1 copper, frame included. "Face value's on the paper."
- A hank of glass-spider silk from the Barrow, sharp as razors, wound on a bobbin. 1 gold. Do not pull it tight.
- A door-bell with no tongue, from a house in the Smokes. 3 copper. It was rung twice for safety on Eye-Night 3006, and then the tongue was taken out, and the keeper does not know why.
- An empty Keel tin, bell-stamped, resealed with grey wax and a moth, nothing in it. 2 silver. "Sealed means sealed. I don't ask what was in it."
- A Halcyra pilgrim-lamp, clay, with the Saint's flame on it and no wick. 2 copper. Pilgrims leave them; the keeper collects them.
- A chip of grey Lithic stone the size of a thumbnail, warm. Price on enquiry, and the enquiry is who you are.
- A loose page of the Ninepenny's dream-book, in forty hands. 1 copper. The same words forty times: a stone door, a lamp, a hand on the latch from inside.
- A child's shoe. Don't.
- A wedding ring on a nail behind the counter. Not for sale; the keeper is waiting for someone to ask for it by name.
- A horseshoe nail pulled from the Corporal's Gibbet, bent. 1 copper, and the keeper will not hand it to you; you take it.
- A bottle of Mercy, the real thing, with one measure left in it. 1 silver. Hennet says it was never hers and it was.
- A 3009 survey of the Rent on waxed cloth, thirty-one miles, with one line added since in red ink, running north-east past the end. 2 silver. The red line is not on the company's copy.
- A pair of winter boots, company issue, 3006, never worn. 2 silver. The Turncoat had thirty pairs once and these are the last.
- A single new Regency silver, 3011, a chair on the back, sitting in a saucer by the till. Not for sale. The keeper took it in change on the 8th and has not spent it because nobody will take it.
