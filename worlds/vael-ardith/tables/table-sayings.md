Roll when an NPC needs a mouth, or when a player asks what somebody meant. Each entry is the saying, who says it, and what it means. The oaths at the end are by people; nobody swears by another people's god and means it, except the soldiers, who swear by everyone's.

Some of these come from a story everyone knows ([[The Salt Feud]], [[The Hanging at Dunmeer]], [[The Ardmere Receipts]], [[The First Standing]], [[The Shellbacks Leave the Ness]]); most came out of nine years at [[The Last Candle]] and nobody can say who said them first, though Spoons will sell you a name.

== GM notes ==

Give each NPC one and let them wear it out. Vosk gets the soldiers' ones, Hennet the Candle's, Kettle the river's. A PC who uses "Dask's rule" or "Corporal, I'm not doing it" at the right moment is praying, whether they meant to or not; see [[The Hanged Corporal]]. "A never" is a quiet pointer at [[The Wager on Never]].

== Entries ==

- "Thirteen barrels." Anyone who has drunk with a Grimmark dwarf: you are being unreasonable about a small thing. From the Salt Feud, which was fought over a barrel that was not there.
- "It's not a bridge, it's a barge." Cinderford, of the toll bridge and anything else that looks fixed and charges you every time you use it. The Reeve's son has heard it.
- "Ketterin's luck." Soldiers: a small kindness that costs the giver everything. From the first Lithic who walked to the Ninth Stone and stood. The gatehouse dwarves say it reverently; everyone else says it to someone who has just bought a round they cannot afford.
- "I'll take it in Ardmere Receipts." Any camp on the continent: I do not believe a word of it. Fennick says it to the Regency's pay notices, every quarter, aloud.
- "Dask's rule." The Ninth, of a refusal: I am not doing that, and we both know why. Dask is the company's name for the Hanged Corporal. Said with a grin, to see if the sergeant objects. He has not, yet.
- "At least they stand up for their taxes." Cinderford, since the drowned started walking: said of anyone who finally gets to their feet, and of the Assessor, and of the dead, who have been seen in the ford on the days the Regency's post-rider comes.
- "Corporal, I'm not doing it." Soldiers, everywhere. The prayer. Also the chorus of the song. Also, lately, an answer.
- "I heard you the first time." The Corporal's second line, from the story. Means: ask again and see what happens.
- "The interest was too high." A banking joke, told by everyone except Shellbacks, about why the Shellbacks left the Ness. Said of anyone who has left somewhere in a hurry and will not say why.
- "Nobody has a cold for three weeks." The Candle, this Ashfall, of a man whose excuse has outlasted belief. From the Assessor's clerk, who arrived sniffling in Reaping and has not stopped.
- "He was there." Every tavern in the March: of the old man by the fire who was at Cinders, said half as a warning not to argue with him and half as a toast. In the Candle it is old Pike. At the Candle you raise a cup when you say it.
- "We go when it goes." The Ninth's own gloss on its contract. Said shrugging. Said less often since the night it guttered.
- "Pay-day." The Ninth: never. "I'll settle it pay-day." Also, grimly, the 14th of Seedwake, Highsun, Ashfall and Longnight.
- "A Fennick." The Candle: a debt you will die owing, entered honestly in a book nobody will ever open. Spoons invented it. Fennick knows and has stopped minding.
- "Swear on the cage." The Ninth: the oath that counts. A soldier who swears on the cage and breaks it is expected to climb up and tell the flame so. Two have.
- "By the goat." The March's oath, because goat is the one thing in the Ashenreach worth swearing on and there is rarely any. "By the goat, when there is goat" is the long form.
- "Count the stakes." Anyone who has walked the Ash Road: make sure of your way home before you go further. Also: check your change. Also, of a liar: his stakes don't count the same twice.
- "Evening, four-and-six." Mother Hennet's greeting, by tab total, now a general greeting in Cinderford meaning: I know exactly what you owe and I am being civil about it.
- "What hums, don't." Gutter Market's rule, and everyone's: leave the dangerous thing alone. Tam Sixfingers says it to children, soldiers and Shellbacks in the same voice.
- "The soup did it." The Candle: an unlikely cause offered with a straight face. From Hennet's account of how the ninth lantern was lit, which nobody believes and which is true.
- "Nine of something." Cinderford: a thing has gone uncanny. Ninth company, ninth ward, ninth stone, ninth year. If somebody counts nine of anything in the Smokes, the room goes quiet.
- "Red-moon reasons." Excuses. "Why were you late?" "Red-moon reasons." Accepted three nights either side of a full Ember and not otherwise.
- "A never." Spoons's book: a bet that cannot be paid out whatever happens. From a wager placed in 3003 that is still in his strongbox. Said of any promise with no date on it: the Regency's arrears are "a never".
- "Arrears." The Ninth: nothing. "What's for dinner?" "Arrears."
- "May your lantern be counted." A soldier's curse: may you end as a name on a pole at Corvel Rise, with the Treaty clerks the only ones who remember you existed.
- "Goldfall take you." Old Regency, old people: may the past have you. Ashfall was Goldfall before the battle, and nobody in the March says the old name except to curse with it.
- "Go and stand in the field." Cinderford: die. From the Field of Lanterns at Corvel Rise, and from the plain itself, which the pear-country folk still call the field. Said mildly, the way other towns say "get out".
- "May the Reeve wear your hat." Cinderford: may you lose in her court. The Reeve wears a different hat every day, and the soldiers say each one belonged to a man she ruled against.
- "Mother hear it." Orc oath, the heaviest: I swear this where the Mothers can hear, and they can. Never said lightly, and an orc who hears a human say it will correct them. "Knuckle it" is the orc's "give up": from the Mothers' rings of knuckle-bones, which are made of what their soldiers lost.
- "By the stone that listens." Dwarven oath, sworn to the nearest Lithic, who is holy and may be awake. The Returned do not swear it.
- "Last word's mine." Raveler: I win. Also the end of any Raveler argument, because it is literally true of their trade.
- "Sunk without it." Halfling, of neutrality, of a favour owed, of a cargo: the thing we cannot do without. "I'd be sunk without it" is a halfling's thank you, and the nearest they come to admitting a debt.
- "Lean back." Vesh: calm down; also, step away from the thing that is burning or enchanted, which to a Vesh is the same instruction. Tallow says it to Ysse. Ysse has started saying it to the lantern.
- "Have you ever been to the sea?" Shellback: do you understand anything at all about what you are talking about. Said very slowly, after a pause, and the only answer that does not make it worse is "no".
