> Up a stair that smells of barrel-tar, a door with a brass plate screwed on crooked that morning: OFFICE OF THE ASSESSOR OF THE MARCH OF CINDERS, BY WARRANT OF THE REGENCY COUNCIL. Inside, one room, three desks, and paper. Paper in stacks, paper in crates, paper on the sill, a form pinned to the back of the door that says FORM 11-C: REQUEST FOR ADDITIONAL FORMS. A thin, stooped man in a black coat gone green at the seams is sharpening a pen with enormous care. Behind him a thin clerk with a cold looks up, and down, and you forget his face before you have finished looking.

The Regency has not paid the Ninth Lantern since 3009, and this month it sent, by the same road the pay used to take, an assessor to collect the March's imperial tithe, arrears included. The office has been one rented room above the cooper's since 3003. Widow Pruitt kept it alone for two years; a new clerk, Mr Mote, arrived in Reaping with nine crates of forms; and [[Pell Quillon]], Fourth Assistant Under-Collector in the Office of Revenue for the March of Cinders, arrives at dawn on 15 Ashfall with a warrant and no escort. He sleeps on the premises, because it is the only room in town with a lockable door and a view of the bridge.

He means to assess the company at once. The company is trying to work out whether to laugh. The Captain has agreed to "receive the Regency's representative" on Hearthday and has not said what she will do with him when she has.

### What is here
- **Forms.** Imperial tithe schedules, garrison returns, livestock enumerations, a census form for the dead, and the one Pell is proudest of, Form 44-A, *Declaration of Non-Receipt of Imperial Funds*, which he intends to have the Captain sign so he can file it against the arrears and, he explains, "set the matter moving". Against his own assessments the company's only defence is Form 9-K, *Declaration of Wages Owed But Not Payable, Countersigned*, which he will explain with relish.
- **The strongbox**, empty but locked, as regulations require.
- **The window**, which looks straight down on the bridge and the gatehouse, and from which a patient man could count every coin that crosses.

### Who is here
Pell, who means every word. Widow Pruitt, who does the sums. Nib, who runs errands and is paid in biscuits. And [[Ansel Mote]], a thin clerk with a cold, who copies, files and fetches, lodges above Gannet's Tallow & Wicks on Temple Row, and is somehow never in the room when anyone looks for him.

### Rumours
- The Assessor is here to seize the lantern for the Regency.
- He is Quiet Office.
- He has been sent to die, so the Regency has a reason to send soldiers.
- The forms are the first Regency paper with money in it in two years, and are therefore worth something.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False. He has a form for it, but he is here for the tithe. 2. False. He would be horrified. (His clerk is another matter.) 3. Possibly true, and not his idea. 4. True; Fennick has already asked for the blanks.
:::

### Things to do
Be assessed. Fill in Form 7 (Persons Under Arms, Mercenary, Foreign) and discover it has no box for "unpaid". Watch Pell defend the Regency to a room of soldiers with such obvious decency that nobody hits him. Notice that Mote's cough stops when he thinks nobody is listening. Count the crates: nine arrived, ten are in the room.

:::gm
Mote is the Quiet Office's assassin, in town to start a mutiny through [[Garrick Sallow]] and, if the lantern can be made to fail "naturally", to see that it does. On his first evening he took tea at the Velvet Ledger's parlour to look at Tesh; on his second he stood a round at the Last Candle. He asked nothing in either, and tipped well. Since Reaping he has been buying sealed Keel oil tins at the chandlery through "a clerk with a cold", paid in advance, in the Office's name. Pell knows nothing; the tenth crate is Mote's, and holds a crossbow, three vials and a Regency seal Pell does not have. The Quiet Office clock ticks here. Pell's Form 44-A, if the Captain signs it, is a real lever: see session 2, [[The Missing Pay]].
:::

== GM notes ==

Pell is the comic heart of the campaign and must be played straight: he believes in the Regency the way Mother Brisk believes in soup. Let the players grow to protect him. Mote should be in the background of scenes before he is in the foreground of any; describe him once, blandly, and never again until it matters.
