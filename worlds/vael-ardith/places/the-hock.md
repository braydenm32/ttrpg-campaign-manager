> Three brass balls hang from an iron bracket over a door that opens inward with a bell, and a Concord licence in the window is framed, pinned and fly-spotted, with the Chain stamped in its corner. Inside, it is crowded and clean: shelves to the ceiling, every object ticketed in a neat round hand, a spyglass beside a fiddle beside a christening gown beside a helm. Behind a counter built a hand too high for anyone ordinary, on a chair that has been raised to match, sits a halfling in a good green waistcoat, writing in a ledger with a very sharp pen. There is a bucket on the floor by his chair. It is empty, but with the air of a bucket that expects work.

Bushel Tarrowdown was born on a Tarrowdown barge on the Ardis, as his people are, and learned at the age of nine that he gets sick on anything that moves. He was seasick on the river, on the quay, in bed, and once, memorably, on the Reeve's rushes. The clan, who find it the funniest thing since the Gilt Run, kept him aboard for six years because they could not agree whether to put him ashore. He left in 3005 with a spare shirt, a ledger he had kept as a boy-hand for his aunt, and a Concord licence to pledge-lend that he got by showing the licensing clerk the ledger.

He opened the Hock in 3006, two doors from the square, because that was where the soldiers crossed the street on a Coinday with something in their hand. He is polite, exact, and unfailingly kind to the poor; he lends at a rate no one calls kind, but it is a rate, it is posted, and he says it aloud before the pen touches the page: a copper in ten, every week. It sounds like less than [[Tollamund & Daughters]]' tenth a quarter. It is more, by about fifteen times, and nobody has ever got Bushel to say the figure after the loan.

He keeps the shop spotless and the back window shuttered, because the river runs behind the street and the sound of it turns his stomach. He has never in his life looked at the water from the Hock's back room, and if anyone opens the shutter he goes grey.

### Loans and pledges
- A copper in ten a week on any loan, due with the pledge on redemption. A pledge unredeemed after thirty days goes on the shelf and is sold.
- Against a company brass token, 3 cp: worth a copper in trade, priceless to a soldier, and an offence to sell. [[Ninth Lantern Brass Token|A token]] pawned is not a token sold, he says, and tells the company's sergeant nothing.
- Against a badge of one of the dark companies, 5 cp. Against the Seventh's, 2 sp 5 cp; "half what Gutter Market asks, because of Mother Hennet, and the other half is risk".
- Against a good cloak, 1 sp. Boots, 6 cp. A fiddle, 3 cp. A sword, 1 gp: "a plain one costs eight at the Forge, I don't lend the difference."
- Against a ring, by weight, and by how the owner holds her hand when she hands it over.
- Ardmere Receipts: refused, with a smile. "I'll take it in Ardmere Receipts" is the saying for *no*, and he says it, to a soldier's face, each time. Tollamund would pay forty in the hundred. He does not tell them that.

### For sale
Unredeemed pledges only, ticketed, with the day and the loan on the back of the ticket. Boots, 8 cp. A fiddle with three strings, 6 cp. A sergeant's whistle, 2 cp. A Salt Guard tabard, scorched, 5 sp. A dark-lantern, rusted open, 4 sp. A wedding ring on a nail, 2 sp: "a sad one". A dented helm with a hole in it, 1 sp, "I didn't ask whose". A spyglass with a cracked lens, 1 gp. A child's wooden horse, not for sale; it is on the top shelf and has been there four years.

### Who is here
Bushel, writing. A soldier of the Ninth at the counter at noon on a Coinday, holding something small and not meeting his eye. A Smokes widow redeeming a pot. A few soldiers of the Ninth in the back room on Tideday and Wardday nights, drinking his sour small beer. A Tollamund clerk on Coinday mornings, who buys nothing and looks at the shelves.

### Rumours
- Bushel sends the company every token that comes in so the sergeants can flog the sellers.
- He is a spy for the Concord, and every pledge goes to a Shellback in the old mill.
- The child's wooden horse on the top shelf belongs to someone who is coming back for it.
- Dannic Rook pawned his token before he ran.
- Bushel has never been seen on the water, and the barge-clans lay odds on whether he can cross a puddle.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False. He tells no one in the company and has never given a name to Vosk; he is, in his way, honourable. 2. True: see below. 3. False. The child it belonged to is one of the forty in the Glass Chapel. His mother pledged it, and then she died, and he has never sold it. 4. True: see below. 5. The clans hold a real book. He has never lost.
:::

### Things to do
Pawn something. Redeem something. Read the shelf-tickets and find a name you know. Ask Bushel about Dannic Rook's token, and see him go quiet, and then ask for the ticket number. Buy a dark-lantern for the Barrow at half what Hesk wants. Watch a soldier pledge a ring in a room full of other people's.

:::gm
**Bushel is Tollamund's canary.** The Concord licence is hers, and the price of it is a weekly ledger: every pledge, every redemption, every soldier who comes in twice. Each Coinday morning a clerk takes it to the old mill, where Perrin reads it and the old woman waits. Tollamund learns from it exactly which soldiers are desperate, which are leaving, and what they would sell. The garrison does not know that every pawned token is also a note in the debt-book of the one creditor the company cannot afford.

**Dannic's token.** On the 6th of Ashfall, a Restday, Dannic Rook pawned his own token himself, for 3 cp, with his laugh. "I'll be back for it Hearthday week," he said. He never meant to. On the 9th, two days after he was drowned, a man with a cold, a scarf and good spectacles redeemed the ticket, paid in new Caer Ardith silver, and counted the change in coppers, which clerks do not do. Bushel remembers the silver, the sniffle and the counting, and put all three in the Coinday ledger of the 14th. [[Ansel Mote]] took the ticket off the body and the token off the shelf; he meant to leave it on the Low Quay on the 12th to give his poster a sighting, and lost his nerve in the crowd round Kettle's barges. It lies in the box under his bed over Gannet's: a brass disc numbered 91, the number of the man the town is looking for.

**The back room.** Sallow's men drink here (he arranged it through Bray in Reaping) because it is a place where a drunk soldier talking about the Lieutenant is writing his own entry in Tollamund's ledger. Sallow does not know. In session 7, on Wardday, when the Reeve's court empties the street, the back room is the one door open: Bray and three others wait here for the word.
:::

== GM notes ==

Bushel's voice: a clerk's courtesy over a weak stomach. He says everything slowly, calmly, as if explaining it to a child, a trick learned from being sick in front of customers. He calls every soldier "friend" and writes down the answer. A PC who treats him kindly gets one useful thing a session, because it costs Tollamund nothing. A PC who threatens him is sent a Concord tallyman by Hearthday. The bucket is a clue that he is more frightened than he looks.
