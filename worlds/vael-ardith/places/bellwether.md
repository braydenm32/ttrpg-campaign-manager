> A low rise on the east bank, a mile above the ford, where the grass has not quite given up. Three stone steadings with turf on the roofs and smoke from one chimney in three. Forty goats on the rise, tethered or not, in no hurry. And from every door, every gate, every shed and the pump-handle, a bell: cow-bells, sheep-bells, a ship's bell, a Regency handbell with a dent in it, all hanging at the height of a hand, all of them moving a little in a wind that is not strong enough to move anything else. It sounds like a very small, very busy church.

### The lie of the land
Bellwether is what is left of the northern pear country's farms, nine years on: three holdings on a south-facing bank above the river with a drystone wall round the lot and the old orchard lane running up from its gate to the Weeping Orchard, two miles off, between its dry-stone walls that did not fall. The grass is thin but the goats like it. Past the wall to the east the ash is deep, and gets deeper.

The custom is the March's custom, and Bellwether keeps it harder than most: **a bell on every door**. The dead, say the steadings, do not ring bells. They walk in, or stand outside, or wait to be asked; they do not lift a hand to a rope. A visitor rings. The household knows by the note which door the visitor is at and what sort of visitor (the front door's cracked cow-bell is for guests, the shed's flat sheep-bell is for deliveries, the pump's is for the hour of the milk), and nobody opens to a door whose bell has not rung. In eight years nobody has opened to a door whose bell has not rung. In eight years a great many doors have been stood outside.

### What is here
- **Tolliver's**, the north holding and the biggest: stone house, two byres, the goat-shed and [[Tolliver's Dairy]], where the milk and cheese are sold. [[Goodwife Marn Tolliver]] counts the goats from the upper window every dawn.
- **Crannock's**, the middle holding: Old Dace Crannock, seventy, a cousin of the Smokes Crannocks, and his grandson Tobin. They sell wool and do not speak to the Tollivers across the gate, and have not since a dispute about a ram in the year of the first Renewal.
- **Pyle's**, the south holding: one man, Hobart Pyle, fifty, and nine dogs. He rings his own door-bell every night at dusk to see if he is dead.
- **The lane gate**, where the road from the ford meets the orchard lane, and where the best view in the March sits in Marn's upper window.

### Who is here
Marn Tolliver and her household. Old Dace on his bench, nodding at things. Hobart Pyle and the dogs. The Ninth Lantern's forage-corporal every few days with a tally-stick and a hope. And at dusk on Coinday a boy from [[The Assessor's Office]] with a handcart, who is paid in biscuits and does not know what he is carrying.

### Why visit
Goat's milk, the only milk in the town; goat cheese, hard and soft; wool; a quiet place where the company's patrol will stop and eat. The Tollivers see whatever walks the orchard lane after dark, and they keep a **tally** (a cord of knots on the upper window's frame, one for each night something went up the lane) which they will show to anyone who brings salt. One knot for each red moon, and in it the number of lanterns that went up: a dozen, then twenty, then forty. Nobody has ever counted who came down.

The company is paid in goats from here. The Ninth's steadings pay their tithe to the Keep in beasts: forty goats a quarter, driven over the bridge by a Tolliver boy and ruled, by [[Mistress Hebbe]] at the toll, to be Tolliver goats until they reach the Keep gate, and therefore tolled at 2 cp a beast. Marn says they are the company's from the moment they are owed. This argument has lasted two years and cost Marn eleven silver.

### Rumours
- The goats will not cross where the ash gets deep, and the line has moved twenty yards toward the houses since Ashfall began.
- Every bell on every door at Bellwether rings once at the full Ember, by itself, at midnight.
- Marn Tolliver can count a goat from a mile off, and has never once been wrong.
- The orchard lane is longer at night.
- Hobart Pyle's dogs do not bark at the dead, and that is how he knows.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True, and the line is the pale ash: see below. 2. False, but a bell does ring on its own about once a month, and the household takes it as the visit of a polite neighbour. 3. True. 4. True at the full Ember: the lane runs a hundred yards further than it does by day, and then, at dawn, a hundred yards shorter. 5. True: the dogs do not bark at the dead and do bark at Hollowborn, which the Tollivers find insulting on the dogs' behalf.
:::

### Hook
The goats will not cross the line where the ash gets deep, and the line has moved twenty yards toward the houses since Ashfall began. Marn thinks it is the greywinds. The goats know better. Anyone who walks the line (Survival DC 12) finds the ash along it is paler than the plain's, near-white, and smells, very faintly, of lake water.

### Things to do
Ring the bell and wait. Buy cheese. Let Marn talk about goats and tolls for an hour, and listen to what she says between. Look at the tally cord in the upper window. Walk the orchard lane to the Orchard at dusk, or not. Watch the boy with the handcart come at dusk on Coinday and see where he goes. Count the goats, and count them again, and then be told why you were wrong.

:::gm
**The jars.** Since Reaping, pale ash from Stillwater has come through Bellwether in clay jars, sealed with wax, in carts of Smokes families going home from the lake, on the Widow's word. The jars are left in the Tollivers' goat-shed for a friend of the Widow's and a copper a jar. At dusk on Coinday (2, 8, 14, 20, 26) **Nib**, the Assessor's errand boy, comes with the Office's handcart (a Regency sun on the tailboard) and takes them away, for "blotting-sand". He thinks it is blotting-sand. The carts spill ash on the way, and the spilled lake-ash is the pale line the goats will not cross: the goats can smell lake water, and a goat is a creature that knows what it knows.

Marn did not know what was in the jars until the lantern guttered on the 14th. She has not said anything, because she was paid, and because she is now frightened, and because the man who paid is Ambry Coyle, who has not slept in eleven days and is a gentle man whom nobody wants to cross. She will tell the PCs about the jars, and the cart, if the company forgives a season's goats. See [[Goodwife Marn Tolliver]].

This is the middle clue on the oil: it points at the Assessor's Office, not at Pell. Nib does not know what the jars are for. [[Ansel Mote]] does. Do not give it up before S3; after the guttering it is fair.
:::

== GM notes ==

Tone: warm, wary, small. Everything here is measured in goats. The bells make a continuous soft noise and every conversation is interrupted by one. A character who rings a bell and says her name is greeted; one who does not is stood outside until she does, and if she is a PC with a Hollowborn or a shadow-wrong companion, the bell does not ring at all, and the household look at each other.

Bellwether is also the best place to be on the 14th at dusk if the party wants a vantage over the company's silent muster, which Marrow cancelled: from Marn's window you can see the empty lane and the first lanterns of the Orchard not being lit.
