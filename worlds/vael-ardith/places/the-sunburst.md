> A long low house of lime-washed brick, with a board over the door painted with a sunburst gone brown in the weather and a line of smoke that smells, from forty yards, of rising dough. The common room is one long hall with trestles down it and a baker's oven in the end wall like a second hearth. Brass excise dies hang in rows along the beams, each stamped with a small seal, the way other inns hang horse-brasses. Two sifters at the end of the table are dicing for the right to be examined first. A woman in a floury apron is shouting something at the oven, with great affection.

The Sunburst was the imperial post-house, built to feed and change the Emperor's couriers on the road east, and it took its sign from the sun-disc of Aurelan on the post across the way. For a century it fed couriers; from 3002 to 3009, the garrison's pay wagons and their escorts; then, in 3009, the pay stopped and the guests with it, and everyone said the Sunburst would close. It did not close because Jenet Gant baked.

She took the house over from her mother twelve years ago and learned the oven from Bethan, who learned it from the last courier-cook, who had learned it from a soldier, and the loaf that comes from it is called the **Sunburst**: round, dense, sour, scored with six cuts like a child's drawing of the sun. It keeps nine days in a pack. Regency couriers carry it. Half of Fallowden's carters will take a detour for it, and the Ninth Lantern's own forage-wagon has been known to turn in a day early on the strength of a rumour that there was rye.

The brass dies on the beams are the inn's other fame: old excise stamps, melted down and re-struck each time the Regency changed a duty, left behind when the Examiner's office needed a fresh die. Jenet says each is a promise made and broken in the Regency's name, and she thinks it a very good wall.

### Menu
- The Sunburst loaf, 1 cp. Free if you sleep.
- Mutton and barley pottage, 4 cp. Egg and cress, 1 cp each. Pease and bacon, 2 cp.
- Small beer, 1 cp. Fallowden red with the excise stamp on the jug, 4 cp the cup (the duty is why it is dearer than the Last Candle's).
- Honey-mead, 3 cp a cup; the honey from the Examiner's hives, which the Examiner sells to the inn at a price he calls "a courtesy to the Crown".
- Honey on bread, 1 cp a spoon, with the Examiner's compliments in a stamped label.
- A bed in the long room, 8 cp (the beds are old courier-cots; the springs are a lesson). A room with a door, 2 sp. Stabling, 2 cp. A stall for a wagon in the yard, 1 cp a night, under the Examiner's seal.
- A Writ of Safe Conduct for the dead, 1 sp, written at the Excise House. The inn sells nothing else against the dead.

### Who is here
Jenet at the oven, shouting. Her mother **Bethan Gant**, seventy-one, in the back room with a cup and a stool, who is not often seen and likes it so. A cook-boy called Pip who runs the yard. The sifters at the end of the table. A carter or two. Whoever is waiting for the Examiner, drinking mead with the forced cheerfulness of someone who has been told to wait. Once a month, a Regency post-rider on the courier's horse, who eats standing.

### Rumours
- The Sunburst's bread is baked with a pinch of the Emperor's ashes. Nobody knows which Emperor.
- The inn has a ghost in room four: a courier who died of a bad oyster, and still delivers a letter nobody can read.
- The register of guests goes back to the Cinders and has never been altered.
- Bethan Gant has a husband and a son in the ash, and will tell you about them if you buy her the second cup.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False, and Jenet has been asked a dozen times by officials from the Office of Rites. 2. True of the oyster; the letter is an invoice for six dozen oysters, and the ghost is cross. 3. True. Every guest since 3001, signed, with the crate count; Jenet sleeps on it. 4. True, and see below.
:::

### Things to do
Eat the bread and be told how it is made, in a voice that suggests the answer is a secret. Dice with the sifters. Pay an Examiner's writ for the dead and take it for luck. Read the brass dies. Ask Bethan about the 11th Foot and be told a great deal, none of which you needed. Sit in the long room at supper and watch every cart that passed the post come in to eat.

:::gm
**The register.** The inn's guest register, kept in a locked bread-bin behind the oven, lists every traveller, party and wagon east or west since 3001. Under **14 Reaping 3011**: *Mote, A., clerk, Office of Revenue, with nine crates and a tenth he would not let the porters touch; had it up three flights and down again before dawn himself; paid in new Regency silver; asked the way east and the price of a cart.* It is the only record of the Assessor's clerk's arrival before he came to Cinderford, and Jenet remembers him: "A very careful little man. He sneezed on the bread."

**The widow of the 11th.** Bethan Gant is the widow of **Quartermaster-Sergeant Harl Gant** of the 11th Foot, Old Tally, whose chest the Glassers found at [[Tally's Cut]] on 3 Ashfall 3008 (see [[Old Tally's Coin]]), and the mother of [[Ferrow Gant]], the Cut's crew-boss, whom she believes died with the regiment; he has let her believe it for nine years. She has never been told the chest was found. She knows where he was posted, and what the 11th was told to guard, and has not spoken of it in nine years because the one person who asked was a Regency officer. She will tell it, slowly, to the person who buys her the second cup and says his name as a friend would. Jenet does not know. Neither of them knows Ferrow is alive, fifteen miles east of the Treaty Stone, with one lung.

The yard-book across the road ([[Whitepost]]) and the Sunburst's register together make the paper trail of 4 Ashfall: the escort's reckoning for the night they were dismissed, paid by "the House's driver" in Concord silver (14 sp, with the House's mark), and the two House drivers' names in Jenet's hand. She did not think anything of it. She does not like the Concord's drivers: they paid with a single coin, and tipped.
:::

== GM notes ==

Jenet is brisk, warm, bossy and busy, and talks to the oven as if it were her mother, which it partly is. She never lets a guest leave hungry. She cannot abide waste, lies or Regency couriers who eat standing.

The register is the best clue the Sunburst has, and it asks to be burgled gently or asked for politely; Jenet will show it to any PC who has eaten two loaves and is not rude to the oven.
