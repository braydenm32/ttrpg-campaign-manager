> Nine miles below the bridge the Cinder takes a corner too fast and leaves a piece of itself behind, a long arm of brown water as still as a pond. A fence of black stakes, slanted like bad teeth, runs across the arm's mouth, and the river combs through it slowly and drops what it was carrying. Eels, silver-black, in hundreds, drying on rails. A child's boot. A barrel hoop. A heron with an opinion. Thirty yards out in the still water a little stone roof stands on four pillars with nothing under it but the river. It is a bell-cote. The smoke from forty chimneys goes straight up, which is rare in the Ashenreach and makes the place look guilty about something.

### The lie of the land
Sedgemere is what the first spring after the battle left behind. In the thaw-flood of 3003 the Cinder jumped its bank at this bend and took a new road, and the old channel became a dead arm. It drowned the village's lower street on the way, including the chapel of Maelis, whose roof went under but whose bell-cote is still there, with a heron on it. The families who stayed rebuilt on the ash terrace above the waterline in ash-brick (grey, square, warm in winter) and, because a flood that takes a street should be made to pay something back, drove the black stakes across the mouth of the dead arm. The Weir is the village. It catches eels first, then driftwood, then everything else the Cinder has been carrying from the ford and the Quay and the places above them. Nobody thinks this is strange. They think it is the river's way of sending the post.

There is no Reeve. Odile Fairweather's writ runs nine miles on paper and has never been served here, and the Reeve, who is not stupid, has never tried. The Weir is kept by the households in turn (the Wattles hold it this year). The Barge-Mother decides everything else. The Raveler at the weir's end buries whatever nobody claims. Sedgemere's three laws are all unwritten, and the first is that you do not argue with a weir.

It is also the last place on the water where a tow-string coming up from the Ardis is counted before it reaches the Quay, and the first place anyone coming down from Cinderford can step ashore without being asked a question. [[The Barge Clans]] winter twenty boats here from Frostcall to Thawmere, tied stern to stern in the dead arm, and in Ashfall a dozen are still moored: the Reedmarsh Water, under [[Barge-Mother Posy Reedmarsh]], who has not moved her flagship since Reaping.

### What is here
- **The Weir.** Two hundred black stakes, a walkway along the top, a tally-board where Tansy Wattle chalks the day's eels and anything else. Sluice-keys on her belt; the village's whole income on her tally.
- **The bell-cote.** Thirty yards out, four pillars and a roof. At low water in a dry autumn the wall-tops of the chapel show as a ring of dark stones. The children dare each other to swim out and touch it, and are told, by everyone, that the bell does not ring. It does not ring.
- [[The Thirteenth Barrel]], a tavern in a beached barge-hull at the head of the village, and the only place for miles where "river business after the bell, not before" is posted over the door.
- **The raveler's hut**, on stilts at the weir's far end, a tarred box with a slate on its wall and a crow on its roof. [[Keld Owes-Nothing]] takes the drowned off the stakes there.
- **The tar-yard**, where the Clans re-pitch hulls for a fair price, and where a bargeling can hear more in an hour than a Quiet Office clerk in a month.
- **Smoked eel**, a string of six for 4 cp, and reed eel-traps for 2 cp. The only thing Sedgemere sells that anyone upriver pays for.

### Who is here
Posy Reedmarsh, aboard the *Long Patience*, receiving visitors one at a time. Keld, who says the door-words over the drowned and does not say her own name. Cobb Latchwater behind his own bar. Tansy Wattle on the walkway, sixty and gruff, who dislikes being thanked and will tell you so for some time. Barge-folk coming and going. In the dead arm at night, a lantern at every stern, so that the water looks like a second town.

### Rumours
- The Weir catches more than eels, and the Raveler at its end reads all of it.
- The Reedmarsh flotilla has not stopped at Cinderford in nine years. The bridge wants a penny.
- The drowned bell rings under the water at red moon.
- When the river runs cold at the weir, something has gone wrong at the ford.
- Concord silver comes up the Cinder in salt-barrels, and Sedgemere counts them.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True, and the slate is how she does it. 2. True, and the penny is the whole of the quarrel (see [[The Charter of the Open River]]). 3. True: it rang on the night of 2 Ashfall, with the Keel bells, in time with the Treaty Bell's single stroke; the Sedgemere bell is the old Cinderford chapel's twin, struck by the same founder at Fallowden, flat-toned the same way. 4. True, twice this Ashfall: see below. 5. True, and the count is wrong on purpose: twelve barrels carry the Concord's salt-mark, and a thirteenth, unmarked, is not salt.
:::

### Things to do
Eat eel pie at the Barrel. Read the slate at the weir's end, the day's catch and the years behind it. Watch the evening barrels come under the stakes and count them, and count them again. Take tea with Posy and be asked a true thing about the river. Hire a boat to the Ardis. Send a letter by clan boat for 1 sp and see it travel faster than the post. Sit on the weir-walk at dusk with a Weir-man and learn what a stake sounds like when something hits it.

:::gm
**The cold water.** Twice this Ashfall the river has run cold at the Weir for most of a day: grit of black glass in it, the smell of a drowned man, and no drowned man. The first time was **8 Ashfall**; the second was **13 Ashfall**. It is the Glass Barrow breathing out through the culvert at the third pier. On the 8th Dannic Rook's body went into the culvert and down; on the 13th it came back up the stair to stand in the shallows. Each time the Cold Captain turned over, and the cold ran out into the Cinder and down nine miles to the stakes. [[Keld Owes-Nothing]]'s slate has both dates in her own hand. Together they are the best proof in the March that Dannic was in the water four days before the poster says he was buying passage on the Quay: it takes the "last seen on the 12th" claim apart without a Medicine roll. Anyone who learns it can take it to [[Quorl Many-Words]], [[Hester Pruitt|Widow Pruitt]] (who paid the printer on the 13th) or the thread [[Why is Dannic's wanted poster dated after his death?]]. It will come again whenever the body moves; a third cold spell at the full Ember (S3) is a fair cue.

**The barrels.** A tow of three Concord barges comes up the Cinder from the Ardis about every sixth day, carrying salt in barrels: twelve with the Concord salt-mark burnt in the head, and a thirteenth, plain, which is not counted. The thirteenth holds new Sevenhaven silver, the House's working coin, for Tollamund's buying of the town (the Reeve's charter, [[Fennick Bottlebrook]]'s credit, [[Jaspin Sweet]]'s purchases). The Barge-Mother lets the tow through the Weir unopened and takes a tithe: **one coin in thirteen**, which she calls "the barrel's share". It goes into the *Long Patience*'s ballast. See [[The Unmarked Barrel]] for the quest. Sedgemere's silence about the thirteenth barrel is old, and fond, and it is not the Clans' neutrality, it is their cut.

**The bell.** A diver (or a Hollowborn who looks hard, or anyone at red moon from the cote's roof) sees the chapel's bell hanging in its frame under four fathoms of brown water, green with weed, rope still on the wheel. At the full Ember it swings one stroke late, as the Stillwater bell does, and nobody on the bank hears it but the Raveler.
:::

== GM notes ==

Tone: a place that has made its peace with receiving other people's leavings. Nobody here is surprised by a body and nobody here is cruel about it. They are practical, dry, and fond of the river in the way you are fond of a relation who lends money. When the party first arrives, have someone at the Weir ask "What did you lose?" It is the greeting.

Pace: this is a one-visit place for sessions 2 to 5 (S2 and S5 are best). A day on foot there, a day back; half a day by barge each way, and Kettle can fix the barge. Do not let the party stay long: the dead on the stakes are a morning's work, not an adventure.
