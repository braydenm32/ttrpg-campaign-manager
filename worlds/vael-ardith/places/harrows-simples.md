> A narrow shop with two doors and a smell that argues with itself: honey, camphor, pear peel on a stove, burnt sugar, and under it something green and cold, like the inside of a stone wall. Strings of dried herbs hang from every beam. Along the back wall, where an apothecary would keep a row of jars, there are a hundred and fifty faded ribbons tied in knots and pinned in rows to a board, red once and grey now, each with a twist of grey salt tied into the fold. A woman in a pear-coloured apron is weighing a powder on a scale the size of a thimble, and says, without looking up, "Front or back?"

Nell Harrow's shop sits at the corner where South Street turns off Ford Street toward the Smokes, and has two doors for that reason. The front door gives onto Ford Street and the town's ordinary ailments: coughs, burns, cuts, and the sorts of hangovers that need a name. The back door gives onto South Street and, in practice, the Smokes. Through it come the women with children who dream, soldiers who do not want to, and kiln-hands whose dust-cough has gone from cough to something else.

She came to Cinderford in 3003 from the pear country, a widow at Cinders with a boy of nine, and was apprenticed in the Smokes to [[Ottilie Marlowe]], the midwife, for four years. She opened the shop in 3007. She sells honey-and-pear syrup for the dust-cough, burn salve for sappers, and knots of ribbon and salt that keep off the dreams. [[Maud Severin]], the company surgeon, buys her lint and her willow-bark and tells everyone the prices are theft; Nell sells her the lint at cost and the willow at a loss, and the two women have not agreed on a single thing in four years and would die for each other's patients.

She and Mam Ottilie have not spoken since 3007, when Nell started selling dream-knots to Hollowborn parents. Ottilie said it was selling their children's inheritance back to them as a sedative. Nell said it was a bed and a night's rest. They were both right, and neither says so.

### Stock
- Dust-cough syrup, honey and pear and something else, 3 cp the bottle.
- Burn salve, 5 cp the jar. [[Skarra]] buys one every Restday for [[Tick]], without a note.
- Bandage and lint, 1 cp the roll. Willow-bark twist, 1 cp.
- Hangover powder, 2 cp. "For the Candle's morning after. Take it before you need it."
- Longnight drops, 2 sp the vial: sleep, deep and grey, six hours. Not for the Hollowborn. Not for children.
- A dream-knot, 1 sp (3 after a red moon, when she runs short): a faded ribbon knotted round a twist of grey salt. Tie it to the wrist or the bedpost. See [[A Dreamless Charm]].
- Rest and leave: free, for glass-lung. A bowl of broth and an hour in the back room with the shutters open. "I'll not take money off a man for dying."

### Who is here
Nell herself. A kiln-hand in the back room, breathing carefully. A soldier of the Ninth at the counter on Restday morning, ashamed of the bruising under his eyes. A Smokes mother with a child of four who stares at the ribbon board. [[Maud Severin]] on a Tideday, buying lint and telling Nell she is overcharged. No Sleepless, ever; a hand-lettered card above the counter says so, and under it, in a different hand, *or they'll be asked to leave.*

### Rumours
- Half the Keep goes there on Restday, and that is why the sentries sleep through the watch.
- Her charms work: tie one on and you do not dream at all.
- Every red moon the Sleepless smash her window, and on the Ironday after it is glazed again by a glazier who does not charge her.
- She poisoned a Hollowborn child and the Smokes will not have her name said.
- The ribbons are cut from the dead tree on Temple Row, and that is why they hold.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True, and Sallow knows which sentries to watch. 2. True. 3. True; the glazier mends it on the Ironday after, every time, for nothing, and never says why. 4. False, but it has a root. Nell dosed her own Hollowborn son with dream-knots for five years, and the Smokes knows that, and calls it poison. 5. True: the dead tree is a cutting of the Weeping Orchard and is not entirely dead. The salt is from the shore at Stillwater.
:::

### Things to do
Buy a salve for a burn. Sit in the back room while a kiln-hand breathes. Ask for a dream-knot and be asked, quietly, what you dream. Watch Nell stitch a sapper's hand with the fishing line [[Maud Severin]] gave her and complain about it. Replace a window pane. Hear what the Smokes thinks of the Keep, and what the Keep thinks of the Smokes, in the same quarter-hour.

:::gm
The knots are ribbons cut at dusk from the dead tree on Temple Row (a cutting of the Weeping Orchard, and not entirely dead), tied round a twist of Stillwater salt, and blessed under its branches before the tree can take them; by morning they have gone grey and hold. A Hollowborn who wears one stops dreaming the true dream. The Dreaming Widow calls it **blinding** and the Sleepless smash the window to stop it, not to threaten Nell. A PC who wears one stops dreaming within two nights, and sleeps through the shared dream of session 3 (and misses its clue; the others can tell them). The ribbon crumbles after a full Ember (see [[A Dreamless Charm]]).

Nell's son Jem, seventeen, born in the foothills the winter the Eighth Ward broke, is in a Waker cell under [[Ambry Coyle]]'s tannery. He ran at fourteen, when the knots stopped working. She leaves his supper on a shelf in the back room every night. She has told nobody but will tell a PC once, if they ask why the window is smashed and she keeps selling. Maud has guessed.
:::

== GM notes ==

Nell is brisk, kind and rude; she calls everyone "my duck" and means it as a threat or a blessing depending on the second half of the sentence. She is the best short-scene resource in the south end of town: a place to drop a hook about the Sleepless, or Tick, or the cost of silence. Her window is smashed at dusk on 16 Ashfall; a party member standing guard sees which Sleepless do it, and that one of them is hers.
