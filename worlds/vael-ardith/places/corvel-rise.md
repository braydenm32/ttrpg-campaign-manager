> South of the Smokes the ground rises to a long low hill, and the hill is a graveyard, slate after slate in rows that follow the contour, nine years of a town and a company and a battle. At its crest, black against the sky, a tangle of timber: platforms and roosts and walkways built into three dead oaks and out from them on stilts, grey-robed figures moving along the walkways with the bob and tilt of birds, and over all of it, wheeling, crows. Hundreds. They go quiet when you start up the path, and then, when they have had a look at you, start again.

[[Ravelers]] follow armies and bury what is left, and when the armies stopped at Cinders the Ravelers stopped too. Corvel Rise is the biggest rookery in the south: the Rook-Court's seat in the March, where the dead of the battle were laid in the first winter and the dead of everything since have followed. The Ravelers built upward because they do not like to sleep on the ground they have filled.

### What is here
- **The Field of Lanterns**, the battle-dead's ground, where eight iron copies of the companies' lanterns stand on poles over the eight mass graves, dark, and a ninth pole with nothing on it, by the company's request.
- **The town rows** and **the company rows**, slate by slate; the company's are the ones with a number instead of a name, because that is how the Ravelers heard them called.
- **The roosts**: the Ravelers' homes, sixty feet up, reached by ladders that are pulled up at night.
- **The bone-library**, a long hall at the heart of the roosts, walled and shelved with bone, where the Rook-Court keeps the Ledger of Last Words: every dying sentence a Raveler has heard in the March since 3002, bound in forty narrow books, in order, with the current year's book kept down in the Doorkeeper's Chapel, read aloud on the Rise on 14 Ashfall and shelved here at Longnight.
- **The reliquary**, a cage of black iron in the oldest oak, holding the Court's holy things: a feather from before the count, a jar of ash from the battle, the eight companies' burial-rolls, and a plain iron key on a nail that the Ravelers call [[Hathe's Door-Key]] and touch only to lend it for funerals.
- **Mother Corvane's roost**, the highest, which she has not come down from in a year.

### Who is here
[[Mother Corvane]]. [[Quorl Many-Words]], who was fledged here and comes up with every burial cart. Sixty Ravelers of all ages with a dead god's humour. The crows, who are consulted.

### Rumours
- The crows carry the last words to Corvane before the Ravelers do.
- The ninth lantern-pole will get its lantern the day the company's goes out.
- The bone-library is built from the battle's dead.
- Hathe's key opens the Doorway on Temple Row.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. Possibly. 2. The Ravelers do not deny it, and it is the truest thing said on the Rise: the Reseal ending hangs the ninth lantern there. 3. True, with their leave; the Ravelers asked each one. 4. It opens a door. The Ravelers are not sure which, and are afraid it is all of them.
:::

### Things to do
Bury someone properly. Climb to the roosts and be looked at by sixty crows and sixty Ravelers and not know the difference. Ask to see the ledger and be refused; ask Quorl, and be shown a page. Stand at the ninth pole. Be told by Mother Corvane, if she judges it time, four of the five words.

:::gm
The Rook-Court's clock, Last words decoded, lives here. Corvane holds four of the five words of *the lamp is the lock*, built from the Court's ledgers and the Hask priestess's letter, and has guessed the fifth; she believes snuffing the lamp frees Hathe's doors and lets the dead rest, and she is wrong. The ledger's last words, read together, spell it out across nine years, and session 5 is where the party reads enough of them to see it. The Door-Key in the reliquary is the item [[Hathe's Door-Key]]; the Lithic doors on that road were shut by the standing dead, so the key of the dead turns them: the Glass Barrow's, the Cold Seam landing's and, in the end, the root of the Ninth Stone. The Court lends it for funerals. Corvane is "the friend" at the lip in session 8. Corvane will give it to Quorl when he asks the right question, which is not "can I have it" but "what is it for".
:::

== GM notes ==

Ravelers in numbers are a chorus: deadpan, affectionate, morbid, finishing each other's jokes. Corvane is the still point; let her say almost nothing until session 5. The crows should react to the PCs' secrets before the Ravelers do.
