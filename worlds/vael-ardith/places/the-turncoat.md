> A shopfront hung to the doorframe with coats, a hundred of them, every one the same dull grey-brown and every one, if you look, a different shape under the grey: the stripe of a collar that was once red, the ghost of a badge on the breast, the pale oblong where a company's lantern was sewn and unpicked. In the yard behind, a dye-vat the size of a boiler is steaming in the cold. Inside, a man in a tabard sits cross-legged on a table among scraps, stitching, and the tabard is on inside out: the pale lining shows, grey and plain, and the badge is on the back where only the wall can read it. He looks up, and has the face of a man who has been looked at before, and says, "Coats, or boots, or something that can't be mended?"

Two winters after Cinders the old tabards were still coming off the dead in the Glassers' barrows by the sackful, and no one would wear them: the colours of eight companies, every one a sentence, every one worn by someone who had not come home. Aurelio Bask bought them by the lot from a rag-picker in 3004, the winter he came back, and dyed them in a vat in the yard behind a shopfront he rented from the Reeve, and sold them as coats at three silver. They are cheap, warm and anonymous. They are the only coats in the March you cannot tell by the colour whose husband wore them. The town calls them Bask's greys, and calls the shop the Turncoat, and Bask calls it that too, because he is the one who knows.

The dye works like this. Every tabard goes into the vat a different colour, and comes out the same grey, "eight colours, one grey", and for a few hours in the vat the water runs through all eight like a rumour, red and blue and a lemon-gold that may have been the Ruddy Hundred's. He does not let anyone watch. The cloth is thick. Some of the old stains do not come out.

He wears his own old tabard, the blue-and-white of Greywake's Spears, the Second Company, inside out. It is the only one in the shop that has not been dyed. He has worn it so since 3004, and nobody knows whether it is penance, pride, or a joke he has forgotten the end of.

### Prices
- A coat, re-dyed from a tabard, 3 sp. Lined, 4. A cloak, 2 sp.
- Boots, resoled, 2 sp. They are the Ninth's winter boots, the ones the old quartermaster sold off in 3006. Bask bought the whole lot from a peddler and sells them back a pair at a time. [[Fennick Bottlebrook]] has been quietly buying them back for four winters, at 2 sp a pair, and has got to the eighty-first.
- A shirt, 8 cp. Breeches, 1 sp.
- Mend a tabard, 2 cp. Re-dye a coat to a different grey, 1 sp.
- A tabard turned inside out for penance, 5 sp: for a soldier who has something to carry, or a mercenary who was not at Cinders and would like to look as if she had. A **Salt Guard tabard** turned inside out is the one he sells most, to the Concord-paid men who are called the new Salt Guard in Fallowden.
- Cloth by the yard: grey wool, 3 cp; black crape, 2 cp; Halcyran silk-and-wool in white and gold, 4 sp, and he has never stocked it before.

### Who is here
Aurelio Bask, stitching. A rag-and-bone woman called Nettle on Hearthday mornings with a sack. Soldiers' widows going through the coats, looking for one that fits and one that does not remind them. A Raveler undertaker who buys the dead's last coats and sells them back as shrouds. [[Spoons Aldane]], who has never once bought anything and who comes to look at his own coat, three sizes too big and won at cards, and to refuse the offer of alteration, which Bask makes each time and Spoons refuses each time, as a bet. Now and then a Brother of the House of Unburnt Light, buying pins.

### Rumours
- The coats are made from the dead's own tabards, and some of them are still warm.
- Bask deserted at Cinders and the company would not have him back, so he makes coats for everyone else.
- A Salt Guard tabard worn inside out keeps the wearer from being hanged.
- The boots Fennick wears are the ones the old quartermaster sold, bought back one pair at a time.
- The House of Unburnt Light has ordered a bolt of white-and-gold from Bask, which he does not have.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False. The vat is hot, not haunted, and the coats are only warm because the cloth is thick. 2. True, though the "would not have him back" was Vosk's decision, and the company let it stand. 3. False, as far as anyone knows. 4. True, to the pair. 5. True: he has had to order it from a Halcyran factor at Fallowden.
:::

### Things to do
Buy a coat in the colour of nobody. Have a tabard mended. Learn the thread-count of every company's cloth from a man who has handled them all. Watch Bask refuse to talk about the tabard and talk about the thread instead. Ask him for the colour of the Ruddy Hundred's, and see him go to the vat without a word.

:::gm
**The orders.** On the 12th of Ashfall [[Brother Iosune]] of the House of Unburnt Light came in and ordered **six lengths of white-and-gold**, Halcyran silk-and-wool, "by Leafrot, for six". Bask has sent to Fallowden for it. It is the colour the House's Lamp-Wardens wear indoors (they wear red surcoats on the road). The six are for the six Wardens who come to the Keep gate on 6 Leafrot (session 7): new tabards, to be worn over the red as a mark of the Saint's honour, for an escort. The order was placed two days before session 1, before the lantern had guttered even once. Seraphine ordered it on instructions she does not entirely understand; the Saint sees further than the House does.

**Later.** On the 25th of Ashfall, an hour before the funeral, [[Mother Hennet]] buys three yards of black crape at 2 cp, and does not say what for.

**Bask's secret.** On the night of Cinders he hid in the Weeping Orchard with the cooks and the walking wounded, and in the small hours he saw [[Mother Hennet]] lift a soup-pot out of the fire with her one hand. *There was something in the pot that glowed, he saw it through the steam, and she did not look at it.* A man he did not know ran past with a lantern that had not been lit a minute before, a one-armed woman's voice shouting after him. Bask knew the face again in 3004, the week [[Halloran Pike]] signed on at the Keep as an old man with a beard and a limp, and the same week the Ninth turned Bask away at the same gate. He has kept the deserters' silence ever since, which is the only oath he has not broken. He will say it in session 5 if the old man dies, and not before.
:::

== GM notes ==

Bask's voice: soft, wry, apologetic, faintly amused at himself. "I'm a turncoat. Only the one side, mind." He is easy to like and easier to underestimate, and the table should find out what he saw at the right time. He is the only witness to the soup-pot who can be asked in front of a priest, and he knows it. The shop is a great place for a quiet scene in session 2 or 3: the vat, the greys, the sentence a man has been turned inside out to avoid saying.
