> Three steps down behind the lamp-stall's awning, a door of old plateau cedar with no handle on the outside and a row of clay lamps along the sill, left burning, forty or fifty of them, like shoes outside a temple. Knock, and it opens a hand's width on a dark so complete after the square that you cannot see the man who opened it; you can smell him, sweat and cedar and the sour note of the Mercy Houses' lye soap, and hear him say "Lamp?" and, when you say no, "Then come in." Inside is a long low vault of cut white stone that someone has blacked with soot to the ceiling, and pallets along both walls, and breathing, a great deal of breathing, slow, not asleep. A voice in the dark says, quite calmly, "Is it still lit?" and nobody answers, because the rule is that in here you do not have to.

Halcyra does not go dark. Every window, step and rooftop holds a lamp and the lamps are never allowed to go out, and for most of the city that is the Saint's mercy made visible; for some of it, it is a thing you cannot sleep through. The Mercy Houses heal, really heal, by the laying on of hands and the lighting of a lamp, and the healed go home walking, and a good many of them find that they no longer sleep: they lie in the light with their eyes open, warm and wanted and awake, and after a month of it they come down to the foot of the Steps and knock. The cellar under the Ilms' stall has been the place for them since the Saint's first years. It was a pottery store. A Vesh lamp-keeper who had been dismissed from the Steps blacked the walls in 3003 and took in the first sleepless, and kept it till she died, and others have kept it since, and in 3009 a lay-brother of the Lower Mercy House called Anzo Ferrin walked out of the House in the middle of a night shift and down the hill and knocked, and the keeper of the day opened the door, and Anzo has not left since. In Halcyra, leaving a Mercy House is called desertion. The Synod has sent wardens to the door three times. The door has no handle on the outside.

### Menu
- A pallet for the night, 2 cp. Straw, a blanket, the dark.
- Water, 1 cp the cup, from the Steps' cistern, cold. There is nothing else to drink and nobody wants anything else.
- Bread in the morning, 1 cp, from the stall above, passed down the steps by Dovan Ilm, who does not come in.
- The dark, free. Anzo says this at the door to everyone, and means it as the price list.
- A thing said aloud that cannot be said in the light, free, and not repeated. "Nobody sees your face. That's the whole trade."

### Who is here
The healed who do not sleep: plateau farmers, pilgrims from the Regency, a Lamp-Warden once who took off his white-and-gold at the door and wept. Hollowborn pilgrims from the Smokes and from Stillwater, brought to Halcyra for the Saint's mercy and waiting on the Mercy Houses' lists, who are the only ones in the room who can see in it, and who lie very still and watch the wall. Vesh dismissed from the Steps, who come to be somewhere a flame is not. Anzo, at the door, all night. And, on the far wall at the room's end, where the soot is thinnest, a drawing in chalk gone grey: a door, standing open a hand's width, and above it a small lamp in a cage, done by a child's hand in 3006, and nobody has washed it off because nobody has seen it except in the Hollowborn's way of seeing, and they will not touch it.

### Rumours
- The Synod will burn the room out the night the Saint ascends.
- Anzo Ferrin left the Lower Mercy House because he saw what happens to the ones who are "seen again".
- The chalk drawing on the end wall is the same one the Hollowborn children draw on the walls of the Smokes at Cinderford, six hundred miles away.
- If you say the Saint's name in the Unlit Room, one of the lamps on the sill outside goes out.
- The girl who drew it is dead on the far side of the Glass Sea.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. Probably. The wardens have the oil. 2. True, and he will not say what, because the house rule cuts both ways. What he saw: the Hollowborn taken in are warmed, fed, lamp-lit, and *listened to* at every full Ember by Mothers with wax tablets, and the ones whose dreams are clearest are moved to the Upper House, and from the Upper House to the Lamp Court, and the takings-roll writes "seen again" and means it as a kindness. 3. True. It is the same picture [[Sabe Hollins]] draws in the Smokes: a door open a hand's width and a lamp in a cage above it, [[Hathe]]'s sign under the Ninth's flame; the girl drew it five years before Sabe was old enough to hold chalk, and neither has ever seen the other's. 4. The sleepless say so, and the Vesh will not test it. 5. False. See [[Mother Casimira]]: her name was painted over on the Qerath wall last month because she is alive, and wrote.
:::

### Things to do
Lie in the first real dark you have been in since the Spine and find out whether you sleep. Hear the room talk, which it does, low, in the small hours: what the Mercy Houses do with the ones whose dreams are clear, what the Lamp Court smells like from the lattice, what the Saint's voice sounds like in a lit room when it is not Seraphine's. Find the drawing on the end wall by touch, and if there is a Hollowborn in the party, by sight, and realise where you have seen it. Ask Anzo about the girl, and be told the one thing he will say about his old House: her name, and the day she was taken up the hill, and that she was twelve and from the Ardis country by her accent, with one grey eye, and that she drew on every wall she was given.

:::gm
The girl is Ilka Dunne, grey-named Moth by the children of the Smokes: Hollowborn, born in [[Ardmere]] in the siege winter of 2994 to a family that came east to Cinderford in the rebuilding and settled on Tanners' Lane in [[The Smokes]]. Iskavethi preachers took her to Halcyra "for mercy" in Reaping 3006, after the Night of Open Eyes, when she was twelve; she slept here one night before the Lower Mercy House's takings-girls came for her, and drew the door and the lamp in the dark with a stub of chalk from her pocket. [[Mother Casimira]] took her in, kept her four years, found her dreams the clearest on the rolls, and sent her to [[Qerath]] in Frostcall 3010 to sail for [[Ostrakai]] on the Seedwake ship as the forty-first of the Saint's missionaries. She sailed. She is alive. She wrote back. Her mother, Agnet Dunne, still keeps a tanner's yard on Tanners' Lane and does not know any of it.

The Unlit Room is the Church's shadow and the campaign's clearest look at what "the Saint's mercy" costs the Hollowborn: not death, but attention. The healed do not sleep because the healing draws on dead Hathe's stolen power and leaves the door in them ajar ([[The House of Unburnt Light]]); the Hollowborn in the room can see the dead standing among the sleepless, waiting to be comforted, and the dead face the end wall. Anzo knows none of this and all of it. He deserted because he was told to carry a Hollowborn boy from Stillwater up to the Upper House at a full Ember and the boy asked him, asleep, not to let them put it out.

Post-session 8: if the Ninth is resealed and the Saint's ascent stalls, the Unlit Room is where the Synod's anger lands first, and Anzo and forty sleepless are in it, and a party in Halcyra can get them out through the potteries. If the Saint ascends, the room is the first thing in Halcyra to burn, and the lamps on the sill are what light it.
:::

== GM notes ==

Run the room in the dark: describe sound and smell and the keeper's voice, and let the players have no faces either. Anzo is kind, frightened, and bound by his own rule, so he cannot tell them what he saw unless they say it first; let a Hollowborn PC see the drawing and say "that's Still's", and watch him break. The drawing is the hinge: a six-year-old chalk picture of the campaign's whole argument, the door and the lamp, in a cellar under the holy city, drawn by a child who is now the only living person who has been to Ostrakai and come back to say what she found.
