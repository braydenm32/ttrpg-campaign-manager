> A pillar twelve feet high stands beside the road, whitewashed so thick it has no edges, and on top of it a gilded sun the size of a cartwheel, green at one rim. Beside it a lime-washed house with a pair of brass scales hung over the door, a long row of straw hives along its south wall, and a wooden notice-board the length of a coffin. A walled yard with a gate wide enough for two wagons. An inn with a painted sunburst on its sign and a thin line of smoke. From here the road runs on east, straight and white, and about a mile along it begins, quite gently, to turn grey.

### The lie of the land
The post was raised in the Imperial age, when the road to the east was the Emperor's and Whitepost marked its last mile-stone before the Marches. Its sun has been regilded by every Regent since the Kingslaying in the way a widow keeps her husband's chair. At the post's foot, cut in the stone, two lines: CINDERFORD, XLVI MILES. FALLOWDEN, XLVI MILES. That is two days by cart, either way; the old imperial road is the best road in the Ashenreach and this is the last piece of it that has a maintenance budget.

Here the Regency's excise stops collecting and starts counting. Everything coming out of the ash from the March (glass, amber, wool, goats, the odd corpse in a barrel) passes the **Excise House** of the Marches, where the Examiner opens the cart, looks, says one sentence, and decides the duty. Everything going the other way passes under the same eye. Beyond the post the road is the Treaty's; the Regency does not like to think about this and has put the post exactly where it need not.

For ninety years carters and the Emperor's couriers overnighted in the **wagon-yard**, and from 3002 to 3009 the garrison's pay wagons did, a walled pen for forty wagons with a smith, a farrier, a well, and a gate that is barred at dusk by a yard-master who has not missed a night in twenty years. Since 3009 the yard has been nearly empty. The population has gone from two hundred to about fifty, who stayed, and who live by examining what comes out of the ash: examiners, scale-masters, sifters of cartloads, a smith who has forgotten how to shoe a horse and become a very good assayer, and the people who feed them.

### What is here
- **The white post**, the sun on top, the stone mile-marks at the foot. A bench under it where people wait. It is considered bad luck to wait with your back to the post.
- **The Excise House.** Two storeys; the Examiner lives upstairs, the office downstairs. The **Schedule** is nailed to the notice-board outside, in a clerk's hand, and revised every year at Greening.
- **The wagon-yard**, barred at dusk by Hollis Tarne, yard-master, who has seen every wagon that has come through since 3001 and wrote it down.
- [[The Sunburst]], the inn, under the Regency's own sign, with the only bed between Fallowden and the ash that has not been slept in by a regiment.
- **The apiary**, forty straw skeps along the Excise House wall. The Examiner's. Do not touch.
- A small chapel of Aurelan, locked, with the Regency's sun on the door and a priest who comes up from Fallowden on Restday and does not stay.

**The Schedule** (the notice-board's current revision, 3010):

> **Glass, black, cold, per hundredweight:** 3 sp.
> **Glass, humming, per hundredweight:** 12 sp. *(Revised 3010. See note.)*
> **Amber, red ("Orchard"), per ounce:** 1 sp.
> **Pears, stone, per bushel:** forbidden. Apply to the Office of Charters.
> **Lamp-oil, Keel, per cask:** 9 sp.
> **Salt, per barrel, Concord-marked:** 2 sp. *Unmarked: at the Examiner's discretion.*
> **Goats, per head:** 1 cp.
> **Dead, walking, per head:** no duty. A Writ of Safe Conduct, 1 sp, is recommended.
> *Note to the revision of 3010: "humming" is any glass that can be heard from the far side of a cart at a distance of three paces. The Examiner's ear is final.*

### Who is here
[[Lusk Pardew]], Examiner of Excise, in the Excise House or among his bees. Hollis Tarne, the yard-master, sixty, dour. Jenet Gant at the Sunburst. The scale-masters (Orrin, Orrin's brother, and a woman they all call "the other Orrin"). A rota of sifters. Carters bound west with glass under sacking and a very straight face, and carters bound east with their carts empty and no desire to be asked.

### Rumours
- The Examiner has never been bribed, because a bribe is taxable.
- Whitepost taxes humming glass, so somebody in Caer Ardith knows exactly what comes out of the Rent.
- The pay wagons used to sleep in the yard, and the yard-master wrote down every one.
- Nothing that goes east past the post comes back with the same driver.
- The Sunburst has the best bread between here and Fallowden, and the worst beds.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True. He logs the offer instead, and it goes into the ledger under "Other Receipts". 2. True, and the schedule is a census: the Regency counts what the Glassers dig and who carries it west. 3. True; see below. 4. False, mostly; a surprising number of carters come back with a different cart. 5. The bread is true. The beds are an honest 8 cp.
:::

### Hook
Since 3010 the schedule has carried a line for "glass, humming, per hundredweight". Whoever drew it up knows precisely what a Glasser's cart carries. The Examiner is the only man in the Regency who has ever actually listened to it.

### Things to do
Have your cart examined and learn what the Examiner's one sentence is. Read the Schedule. Buy a Writ of Safe Conduct for the dead, and keep it. Eat the bread. Sleep at the Sunburst. Count the empty stalls in the wagon-yard. Ask Hollis Tarne about the pay wagons and see him reach for his book.

:::gm
**The yard-book.** Hollis Tarne's yard-book, one volume a year in a locked chest in the gate-house, is the best record in the Regency of who has slept at Whitepost since 3001. Under **4 Ashfall 3011** it reads:
> *Escort (Concord colours, Regency tabards), Sgt. Dobbin Kerr and nineteen. Paid, turned back, 8 sp a man and a bonus. One pay wagon, sealed, left in the yard. Two drivers signed in, under the House mark (T. & D.), took the wagon on east at dusk.*
This is the only written proof in the Regency that the escort was dismissed and the wagon handed to Tollamund's men on the road. Hollis will show it to anyone the Examiner has told him to, and to nobody else. A burglary (Thieves' tools DC 14) or the Examiner's word will do. The sergeant is still at the Three Cups.

**Pardew's order.** A standing instruction from the **Office of Charters**, dated the week after the Gilt Run in 3010, over the clerk-seal of the Seat of Charters: *"All crates, casks and carts bearing the House mark of Tollamund & Daughters, Sevenhaven, are to pass the Excise at the Eastern Marches unexamined and unrecorded."* Pardew has obeyed it, and kept a copy. The wagon of 4 Ashfall was not unexamined; it was *sealed*, and that is why he wrote it in the yard-book and not the ledger.

See [[Lusk Pardew]] for what he will do with this, and [[Pell Quillon]] for who he would give it to. The Sunburst's keeper is the daughter of Old Tally's widow, and the sister of [[Ferrow Gant]], who has not come home (see [[Old Tally's Coin]]).
:::

== GM notes ==

Whitepost is a pause in a bureaucracy, a place that proves the Regency is a real state and not a shape in a ledger. Keep it orderly and slightly bored. Everyone here is very good at looking. The comedy is that the Regency's last post on the road is the single most competent thing the Regency has left. The danger is that competence is also records.

Use in S1 as where the PCs have come from (a bench under the post, a Writ of Safe Conduct in a backpack). In S4 to S7 it is a letter away: a post-rider will carry a request to Pardew for 5 sp. After S8 it is the first place the road leads to going home.
