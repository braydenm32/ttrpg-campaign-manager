> West of the river there is still grass, thin and grey-green, and the ash lies only in the furrows. East of it the grass gives up. The road runs straight out across the plain toward a smear on the horizon that might be weather and might be the Rent, and the only things standing between here and there are a leaning stone, a line of petrified trees, and the smoke of somebody's cookfire that you would be wise not to walk toward.

The March is what the Treaty of Ashes calls the land ten miles either side of the river Cinder, from the old pear country in the north down to the glasslands. It is the only part of the Ashenreach anyone has rebuilt, because the river is the only water that does not taste of ash. [[Cinderford]] holds the ford; a dozen steadings cling to the western bank ([[Sedgemere]] the biggest); and east of the river a few hard families graze goats on the stubble and keep a bell on the door.

### What is here
- [[Cinderford]], the town, at the ford.
- [[The Treaty Stone]], three miles east on the Ash Road, where the war was signed away.
- [[The Weeping Orchard]], north-east along the old lane, what is left of the pear country.
- [[Corvel Rise]], the Raveler rookery on the hill south of town, above the burial ground.
- [[The House of Unburnt Light]], Iskaveth's mission, a mile out on the eastern road.
- [[The Ash Road]], which runs east to Fort Mercy and the Rent and is lost in every greywind.
- [[Sedgemere]], nine miles downriver, the biggest steading: an eel-weir, a drowned chapel's bell-cote and the Barge Clans' winter mooring.
- [[Bellwether]], three goat steadings with a bell on every door, a mile north of the bridge on the east bank.

### Who is here
[[The Ninth Lantern]] holds the ford under the treaty and patrols as far east as the Treaty Stone, further when paid, which is to say not lately. Reeve [[Odile Fairweather]] speaks for the townsfolk. The steading families answer to nobody and pay the company in goats. [[The Glassers]] come in from Fort Mercy to sell at Gutter Market and are tolerated as long as they leave their picks at the bridge.

### Rumours
- The Treaty Stone bleeds at red moon.
- The Regency is sending a new garrison to replace the Ninth.
- There is a Lithic city under the whole March and the ford is its roof.
- The pear trees will fruit again when the lantern goes out.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False. That is the Orchard. 2. False, so far. 3. Truer than anyone likes. 4. Who knows.
:::

### Things to do
Escort a steading's goats across the ford. Walk the eastern patrol to the Treaty Stone and back before the wind turns. Help a family dig out after a greywind. Take a Glasser's coin to guide a dig. Carry a letter to the mission and see what the Sister offers for the trouble.

:::gm
The March is the campaign's home ground; everything in sessions 1 to 5 happens within two days' walk of the ford. Keep it small and familiar until the Rent expedition in session 6 makes the horizon suddenly real.
:::
