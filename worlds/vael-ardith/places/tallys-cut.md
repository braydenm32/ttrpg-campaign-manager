> The road has been glass for two miles, black and rippled underfoot with shapes in it you have agreed not to look at, and then it is ash again, and off to the right a cart-track goes south across the glass toward a smear of smoke. Half a mile on, the glass opens: a pit the size of a barn, sheer-sided, with a windlass over it and a rope going down, and round its lip a ring of tents and lean-tos the colour of the plain, a cookfire, a goat on a string, and a long low tent with a barrel-head nailed over the door-flap and RIPE ONE burnt into it with a hot iron. Somebody is singing down the pit, badly, and the glass is singing back. On the lip, apart from everything, a cairn of fused stones with a sword-hilt driven into the top, and on the hilt, catching the light, one silver coin.

Before the war this was Lowbarrow, a farmstead in the pear country with a cider-house and a well. On the night of Cinders it went to glass with everything in it, and in the morning of 3 Ashfall 3008 a crew of Glassers under [[Vexa Glassjaw]] put a pick through what they thought was a cellar roof and opened a bubble in the glass the size of a wagon. Inside, dry and whole, sat the pay-chest of the Regency's 11th Foot, nine thousand crowns in imperial silver, and the quartermaster-sergeant who had been counting it when the sky came down ([[The Glassers' First Big Find]]). The crew called him Old Tally, buried him on the lip where he came up, and have dug here ever since. The pit is forty feet deep now and still giving.

The Cut is the Glassers' second camp after [[Fort Mercy]], seven miles short of it, and the only roof between the Burned Mill and the fort. Vexa comes once a month to count the take. Between times it belongs to [[Ferrow Gant]], who was holding the lamp when the bubble opened and has kept the grave since.

### What is here
- **The pit**, a shaft cut down through the fused farmstead by windlass and rope, with ledges of glass at every ten feet where a crew stopped and a new one started. The lowest ledge is cold. The glass down there hums, louder than anywhere in the Ashenreach but the Rent's edge, and the cutters work it in short turns and come up with their teeth aching.
- **Old Tally's grave**, a cairn of fused stone on the pit's eastern lip, a Regency sword-hilt for a marker. There is always one coin on it, imperial silver, and every coin anyone has ever looked at was struck before 3002. Diggers take the coins off to read the dates and put them back, and some of them keep a list.
- **The Ripe One**, the camp's tavern, canvas and barrel-heads and a floor of planks over the glass: see [[The Ripe One]].
- **The windlass**, the cookfire, the sorting-blankets, the straw-barn where finds are packed for the barges, and a water-cart that goes to Vexa's well at the fort every second day and comes back selling.
- **The crews' tents**, twenty of them in a ring, each with its crew-mark chalked on the pole. Two of the poles have a different mark: an open eye behind a closed lid.

### Who is here
Ferrow Gant. Four crews of cutters and breakers, loud, filthy, cheerful, bargaining about splits. Runners with sledges for Gutter Market. A Concord packer, now and then, with straw and nails and a cart, who does not drink. Kettle Brindlewake's cousins, who buy slag here for the kilns and sell salt. [[Skelt]], when he is sober enough to walk from the fort and drunk enough to want to.

### Rumours
- Old Tally's coins are a message. Lay the dates in order and they make a map to the rest of the 11th's silver.
- The pit goes down into a Lithic road and the hum is a bell ringing under the glass.
- Two of the crews don't sleep.
- Vexa pays the Cut in Concord coin, and it comes out of the pay wagon.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False, and expensive: three crews have gone bust digging where the dates told them to. The coins are Ferrow's doing (see his page), and the dates are the dates of a dead man's wages. 2. True. The pit broke into a culvert of one of the Nine Roads of [[Ul-Tarran]] at its lowest ledge last spring; the hum is the ward's leak running under the glass toward the Ninth Stone, the same hum as the Red Seam in the Barrow and the Cold Seam under the fort. The Glassers roofed the hole with planks and dig beside it, not into it. Spells cast in the pit frost its walls (a minor ward-node, as the Barrow: no tick of the Sleeper clock). 3. True. Two crews are Sleepless, the Widow's: Wakers who came east to be near the hum. They dig well and sing at night, and the other crews have noticed that nothing they bring up ever goes to the factor. 4. Half true, and the half that is true is not the half anyone means. Vexa pays the Cut's splits in Concord silver, through the Ripe One's lottery, because the Concord factor pays her in it for the humming crates and she would rather nobody asked why Glassers were suddenly flush with Sevenhaven coin. None of it is from the strongbox, which sits unopened in Fort Mercy's cellar. The escort's travelling purse, which Glasser runners sold to Jaspin Sweet, is the only pay-wagon Concord coin loose in the March.
:::

### Things to do
Buy water at double the fort's price and be told it is a bargain. Hire a cutter at five silver a day and understand, suddenly, why soldiers at five copper desert. Buy a dig-chart, "accuracy not warranted". Go down the pit on the rope to the cold ledge and put your hand on the glass and feel the hum in your teeth; Ysse feels the lantern on the hill from here, fifteen miles, as a warmth on the back of her neck. Read the coin on the grave and be told, by anyone passing, not to pocket it. Ask who leaves the coins and watch Ferrow not answer. Stay for Tally's Day, if it is 3 Ashfall, and be made to drink to a man you never met ([[Feasts, Fasts and Holy Days]]).

:::gm
### The truth
The Cut matters for three things. **The seals.** The chest held, besides the silver, a roll of the 11th Foot's company seals, the quartermaster's own: claims, vaults, widows' pensions, the paper of a dead regiment. Vexa has them in the fort's records vault beside Ilbert Crane's half-burned notes, and has never seen a use for them. Ferrow has. See [[Old Tally's Coin]]. **The crates.** This is where the Concord factor's humming crates are packed: ward-glass cut at the Rent's edge comes here by sledge, is bedded in straw in the barn, nailed up under Cousin Pennamund's eye and goes down the track to the Low Quay and so by barge to Cowl and Gilt, one a month, into [[Tollamund & Daughters]]' vault ([[Gilt]]). The packer is a Concord man and will not say what the crates are for; he does not know. **The Sleepless.** Two crews of the Cut are the Widow's, and they are the crew that means to cut the two Salt Guard men out of Ambercote's square (see [[The Standing Village]]). Ferrow does not know what they are; he knows they do not sleep, that they have never once been short-handed on a shift, and that it is not his business until it is.

**Tally's Day** is 3 Ashfall, the day of the find: the Glassers' one feast and the one day they do not dig. It begins at dawn at the grave, every Glasser from the Cut and the fort standing round the cairn while Ferrow changes the coin and says the one sentence he says every year ("Counted, Tally. All here."), and the Ripe One stands the first thimble; then the crews walk the fifteen miles to Gutter Market to be drunk by noon and in the Smokes by dark, and Ferrow stays with the grave. A digger who was not at the cairn at dawn has to say why. This year one was not: Skelt, who was at Gutter Market before noon like everyone else and then, that evening, holding a rope in the culvert under the third pier for [[Dannic Rook]], and who came in to the fort on the 5th with rope-burns. Ferrow noticed the dawn and the rope-burns. He has not said so to Vexa, because he does not know yet what it means. He will say so to a soldier who buys him a drink and asks about Skelt by name. It is the easiest road to Skelt in session 2.

### If trouble starts
Ferrow first, slowly, with a pick; then whichever crew is up the rope, 2d6 Glasser toughs (bandits with glass-shard cudgels, AC 12, HP 11). The Cut has no love for the company but it needs the road open, and a fight that costs it a day's digging will be ended by Ferrow on whichever side is losing.
:::

== GM notes ==

Play the Cut as a mining camp with a sense of humour: the dead are a crop here, and everyone knows it, and the kindness is real anyway. It is the party's first look at the Glassers on their own ground before Vexa's tower (session 2), a place to sleep on the way to the Rent (session 6), and where Old Tally's Coin starts. The hum is the thing players remember; let a Hollowborn or Ysse feel it before anyone mentions it.

The 11th Foot was a Regency regiment of the Regency's own army at Cinders, not one of the eight companies of the Treaty's schedule. Its chest was Regency pay for Regency soldiers, and the Regency wants it back; see the Find's page.
