> The Thousand Steps go up the hill in a white stair so long that the top is a smear of light, and every tenth step is a landing with a lamp on it, and the lamps are lit, all of them, in daylight. At the foot, where the steps meet the square and the pilgrims come off the road and go down on their knees, a trestle stall under an awning of oiled cloth, and on it rows of small clay lamps, red and buff and white, hundreds, every one with a wick, and a lit one at each end of the trestle so the air over the stall shivers. Behind it a lean brown man of forty with a burn-scar across the back of one hand and a voice already going, telling a woman from the plain the story of a healer who went up the Steps a woman and came down a saint, and the woman is weeping, and she has not yet paid for the lamp.

Nobody climbs the Thousand Steps without a lamp. The Synod does not require it; the plateau does. A pilgrim carries a lit clay lamp up on their knees, sets it on the landing nearest the top that they can reach, and is healed, often, and comes down empty-handed and lighter. The lamps are made in the potteries below the hill and sold at the foot, and the stall at the very foot, where the first step meets the square, has belonged to the Ilms for three generations: grandfather, father, and now [[Dovan Ilm]], who took it over in 3007 when his brother Tamas went up the Steps and into the Lamp Court and did not come down.

That is the story. Dovan tells it with every lamp. The air over the stall smells of hot clay and orange-blossom water and the oil he sells by the penny fill, and the Steps go up behind him into the light, and he does not look at them while he tells it.

### Stock
- A pilgrim's lamp, red clay, 2 cp. The plain one. Most pilgrims buy this.
- A white lamp, 4 cp, for the dying or for a vow. Dovan asks which, and does not charge for the asking.
- Oil, 1 cp the fill, from a tin jug with the Keel bell stamped on it: Keel whale-oil, which the whole continent burns, brought over the Spine by mule. A fill climbs the Steps and burns an hour on the landing.
- Orange-blossom water, 3 cp the flask, to wash the face before the climb. The plateau's one luxury, and Halcyra smells of it.
- A Saint's candle-stub, 5 cp: white wax from the Lamp Court's own candles, cut down and sold by the Synod's leave. These are not the relic ([[The Saint's Candle-Stub]]) and Dovan says so, flatly, to every buyer: "That one's the envoys'. This one's wax."
- "An unburnt wick", 1 sp: a cotton wick that has, Dovan says, been passed through the Saint's flame and not caught. He sells perhaps one a day and looks at the ground while he does.
- The story, free with any lamp. Twice, with two.
- Beneath the stall, down three steps behind the awning, the one dark room in Halcyra: [[The Unlit Room]], a bed 2 cp, which Dovan lets to the keeper and does not go into.

### Who is here
Pilgrims, ten thousand on any night, buying lamps by the hundred. Potters' boys with barrows of new clay. Lamp-Wardens of the Synod in white and gold, who buy nothing and watch the square. Vesh lamp-keepers of the Steps, who trim the thousand landing-lamps in relays and come to Dovan for oil and gossip. [[Brother Iosune]] bought his lamp here at fourteen, before the coals; [[Seraphine Ashe]] bought a white one here in 3008, the morning she walked out of the city on foot under her vow, and paid for it, and Dovan told her the story and she listened to all of it and said "I know", and he has never forgotten that she already knew. Mother Casimira of the Lower Mercy House sends her takings-girls here for the Mercy Houses' lamps by the gross, and gets a price.

### Rumours
- The lamps on the landings never go out because the Saint's breath is in the oil.
- Dovan's brother is alive in the Lamp Court and has written once.
- The "unburnt wick" is a cheat, and Dovan knows it, and the Synod lets him sell it because it needs the pilgrims to believe in small miracles on the way up.
- Seraphine Ashe bought the lamp she carried west at this stall, and it is the one in the House of Unburnt Light's chapel, on the altar.
- There is a room under the stall where lamps are allowed to go out, and the Synod has tried three times to close it.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False: the Vesh keep them, in relays, and a Vesh on the Steps who lets one gutter is dismissed the same night, which is why Halcyra's Vesh are the most frightened lamp-keepers on the continent. 2. True; see [[Dovan Ilm]]. 3. True, and it is worse than a cheat: see Dovan's page. 4. True of the stall and false of the altar; the House's altar lamp is Halcyran bronze and came by cart. Her clay one she carried to the Spine and set on a cairn at the top of the pass, where the mission's mule-drivers say it is still lit, and nobody fills it. 5. True on both counts.
:::

### Things to do
Buy a lamp and hear the story, and watch a man tell it for the thousandth time as if it were the first and as if it cost him. Buy a second and hear what he leaves out the second time. Ask for an unburnt wick and watch his face. Ask about the Lamp Court and be told what everyone is told: the Saint's household does not write, the Saint does not need letters, and the Steps are that way. Fill your lamp from a Keel jug six hundred miles from Keel and think about who else has been buying Keel oil this year. Go down the three steps behind the awning into the one dark room in the holy city and find out what the healed do at night.

:::gm
The stall is the Church's front door seen from the pavement, and Dovan is the one person at it who is not selling the Saint. The lamp Seraphine set on the Spine's cairn in 3008 is a small true thing the GM can use: it is still lit, three years on, without oil, and the mission's mule-drivers will not pass it after dark; it is the first miracle on the road west and the Church has never claimed it, because Seraphine never mentioned it, because she did not notice. The Keel oil is canon texture: every lamp in Halcyra burns Keel whale-oil, the same oil [[Ansel Mote]] bought at Hesk's to cut, and a Keelmund factor in Qerath has the plateau's whole supply, and the Church has lately asked for more of it than the Steps need.

The story Dovan tells is the public version ([[The Saint Declared]]): the healer, the fire, the five years' prayer, the balcony. What he leaves out the second time is his brother, because the second telling is for the ones who came back for it, and he has learned that the ones who come back are either grieving or Synod. See his page for the letter.
:::

== GM notes ==

Dovan's stall should be the first thing a party sees in Halcyra and the last: the Steps behind him, the ten thousand lamps, and one tired man selling clay. Play the story straight, and let him be good at it. The orange-blossom water and the Keel jug are the two smells of the holy city; use them. The Unlit Room is three steps down and a page of its own.
