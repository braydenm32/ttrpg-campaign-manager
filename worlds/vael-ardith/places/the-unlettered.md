> Below the dry halls of the Archive, where the valley wall goes soft with moss and the paths stop being paths, a beech stands that is hollow for twenty feet of its height and alive above that, and the hollow is the hall. The floor is root. The light is green and comes from nowhere in particular. Thirty or forty elves sit on roots and ledges with cups of wine the colour of the light, and every one of them has sleeves rolled to the elbow, and on every forearm there is almost nothing: a line at the wrist, a name, a date. In the centre of the hall a branch as thick as a thigh comes down through the roof and up again, and along it, fresh and white, are rows of small cuts. Behind it a tall barefoot elf with a face like a weathered post is cutting another one with a thumbnail, and not looking at it.

The Unlettered is named for the lowest rank of [[The Remembrancers]]: the young elves with a few lines on them who carry the satchels and ask the questions, before they are Readers, before they are inked. Every elf in [[Ilmareth]] passes through the rank and most pass through the hall, and the hall's joke, which is also its creed, is that you come in Unlettered and leave the same. Nothing is written here. The tally is cut into the living branch and the branch grows over it by dawn; a debt here lasts one night, which is what the Unlettered think a debt should last. The Readers upstairs, who spend their lives bent over other people's skin, come down at the Archive's lamp-lighting to drink where nobody will read them, and are served, and are not spoken to, by agreement.

The hall is older than the Archive, which was cut into the valley wall above it, and the elves do not remember who first hollowed the beech or whether anyone did. Liriel Tarn has kept it for a hundred and twenty years. She is a warden of [[Orvanne]], the Green Wild, with his antler inked on the sole of her foot so that every step is a prayer, and almost no other ink on her at all, which at four hundred is a scandal the Circle has stopped mentioning. She says the wood has been thirsty since before she was born and she does not need it written down to feel it.

### Menu
- Saelish green wine, 4 cp the cup: pale, cold, tasting of the valley. It is the only drink. Ask for another and Liriel will tell you where the gate is.
- Water from the beech's own root-spring, free, in a cup you bring back.
- Bread and soft cheese and whatever the wardens brought in, 2 cp. There is meat at the Culling and not otherwise.
- A ledge to sleep on, 1 cp, and the branch will not remember you did.
- A song, free, if someone is singing. There is one the hall has been singing since Greenreach that nobody can say where they learned.

### Who is here
The Unlettered, by the score, sleeves up, loud by elven standards. Readers at the lamp-lighting, sleeves down, silent. Wardens of the Wild in from the forest with mud to the knee and the antler on the sole, who drink standing. Humans, almost never: the Saelwood has been closed since 3005 ([[The Closing of the Saelwood]]), and an outsider in the hall has come by one of the three gates with a bond lodged, or by a route of [[Kettle Brindlewake]]'s, and either way Liriel wants to know which. [[Merane Tarn]], when she was home, in the corner, with her three exam tattoos and her sleeves rolled down over them because her mother had looked.

### Rumours
- The branch heals because the beech is older than the elves and remembers for them.
- The Readers come down to the Unlettered to drink because the Archive's ink burns them when the red moon is full.
- The song nobody remembers learning is about a fire at the Fourth Ward, and the Circle has sent someone to listen to it.
- Liriel Tarn has only one tattoo because she burned the rest.
- The elves on the Ness of Thurm never came to Ilmareth and still remember everything.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. The elves say so. The GM may let it be so. 2. True; see [[The Remembrancers]]. The oldest ink on the oldest elves burns at every full Ember and the Readers do not know why, and the beech's hall is the one room in Ilmareth with no ink in it. 3. True on both counts. The listener is a Reader called Ilvane, who sits at the lamp-lighting with a wax tablet under her sleeve, which is against the house rule, and Liriel knows. 4. False. She has the antler, Merane's name and birth-date on her left wrist, and her own name at the collarbone, and has refused every needle since 9 Greenreach 3005, the day the forest closed. 5. Nobody knows.
:::

### Things to do
Drink green wine on a root and be, for one evening, in a room where nobody is reading anyone. Watch the branch heal. Hear the song: a drinking-song in the plain Saelish of the Unlettered with a chorus of four words in the old script that nobody in the hall can parse, and watch a Reader at the back put down her cup. Ask Liriel about her daughter and get a long look and a short answer. Ask her about Aelith Sorn and get a longer look and no answer at all.

:::gm
The song came up through the Unlettered because a song is not skin. When Sael burned the memory of [[The Saelish Trespass]] in 1102 it burned ink, records, and the tattooed; it could not burn a tune the young sang in a hollow tree, and the tune has gone round the hall for nineteen centuries, changing its words every generation and keeping its chorus, which nobody has been able to read since the generation that could read it chose not to. The four words are *We came because called*, in the oldest script, the same four that open the oldest ink on the oldest elves ([[The Coming of the Elves]]) and the first line on [[Sorrel Vey]]'s back. The verses, as currently sung, are about a fire in a clearing with no trees in it, eleven cups set out and nobody to drink them, and a tree that was planted the next morning to stop a hole. The Unlettered think it is about a wedding.

[[Aelith Sorn]] learned the song here as an Unlettered nearly three centuries ago and does not remember learning it, but it is tattooed on the inside of her left arm in her own first hand, and it is a quarter of why she can read one line in five of Sorrel's ink. The Reader Ilvane was sent by the Circle in Greenreach to take the verses down, which is the Circle finally noticing what the hall has known forever: the thing Sael forgot was left lying in the one place the Remembrancers never look, which is a room with no ink in it.

Post-session 8: if the party comes to Ilmareth with Sorrel, or with a copy of her back, this is where it can be sung against the ink and read whole, and Liriel will let it happen in her hall, once, with the Readers sent upstairs, because Orvanne's wardens have wanted to know what the forest was before the Trespass for nineteen centuries, and because the branch will not remember it in the morning. See [[Liriel Tarn]] for what she wants in return.
:::

== GM notes ==

Liriel's hall is the one restful room in Sael, and it should feel it: green light, root under the boots, elves being young. The dread is the song, and the Reader at the back with the tablet. Let the players hear the chorus before anyone translates it; let an elf PC with the right tattoo almost read it. Liriel herself is the warden's silence with a bar in front of it: she says little, pours, cuts the branch, and watches the door for her daughter.
