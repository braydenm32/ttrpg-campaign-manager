> You smell it before the lane turns: tan-bark and piss and something sweeter, the hot-sugar stink of the glass-dust kilns. The houses here are lower, built of whatever came off the plain, ash-brick and glass-slag and old shield-boards. Children in the lane have one pale eye each, and look at the space beside you a moment longer than they look at you.

The Smokes is the south end of town, downwind of everything, and it is where Cinderford puts its tanneries, its kilns, its poor, and the families that were born here the night of the battle and after. Nearly every Hollowborn in the March lives in these six lanes. So does the glass-dust trade: Glassers bring in slag from the plain, the kilns grind and sift it, and the dust goes down the river for glaziers and alchemists who do not ask where it came from.

### What is here
- **Tanners' Lane**, the river end, the smell, the vats.
- **The kilns**, three of them, running day and night, with a dust-haze over the lane that gives everyone the cough by thirty.
- [[Hesk's Chandlery]], where the town buys its candles and the Keep, until this year, bought its oil.
- [[The Ninepenny]], the doss-house, where beds are nine pennies and dreams are free.
- [[Harrow's Simples]], at the top of South Street where it meets Ford Street: the herbalist the Smokes trusts, with a back door the Hollowborn use.
- [[The Salt Lake]], the Iskavethi bathhouse on Ford Street, whose steam can be seen from the hill; the kiln-heat of the Smokes runs under the street to it.
- **The Widow's yard**, a walled court at the lane's end where nobody goes after dark except those who do.
- **Marlowe's**, the house [[Tibbet Marlowe]] was born in, still standing, still his.
- **Eye-Night**, 17 Reaping, when the lamps go out, the doors open and the Hollowborn children walk the lanes in a line, and the company's patrol stands at the Chapel's end of Temple Row all night and does not come in ([[Feasts, Fasts and Holy Days]]).

### Who is here
The Hollowborn families: the Marlowes, the Teals, the Crannocks, forty households that keep their own counsel and bury their own. The children are nine or younger and born to it, by the tally of the birth-rolls; the grown ones with a grey eye took it the night of the battle or the night the lake rose, and are called late-eyed, politely, to their faces. Hesk the chandler. [[Auntie Brace]] at the Ninepenny. [[Tam Hollowell]], a Returned dwarf with no shadow, who sets a place at his table for himself. Agnet Dunne, a tanner's widow on Tanners' Lane, whose Hollowborn daughter Ilka, called Moth, was taken east by Iskavethi preachers "for mercy" in Reaping 3006 and has not been heard of since. And somewhere, not in any house you can point to, [[The Dreaming Widow]], who speaks in the Widow's yard on the nights the Ember is near full, and whom the company has never managed to arrest because nobody will say which door she went in.

### Rumours
- The Hollowborn all dream the same dream.
- The Widow is the Reeve's sister.
- The kilns' dust is what gives the children the eye.
- The dead walk the Smokes at red moon and the Hollowborn go out to talk to them.
- The Sleepless are harmless: sad people who want to sleep.

### Things to do
Buy candles. Be looked at by children. Help a kiln-hand who has collapsed in the dust. Hear the Widow preach, from a doorway, with your hood up. Follow someone from the yard and lose them in a lane that was not there yesterday. Be recognised, if you are Hollowborn, by people who knew your mother. Take the Reeve's job about the cellar on Tanners' Lane flooded with water so cold it wears a skin of ice in Ashfall, and look for where it drains.

:::gm

At the full red moon in session 3 the whole quarter dreams together and walks out onto the plain in its sleep; the company's attempt to stop it is where the town's goodwill breaks or holds.
