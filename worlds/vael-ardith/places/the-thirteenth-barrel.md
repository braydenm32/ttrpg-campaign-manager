> A grain-barge the size of a chapel lies on its side in the shallows, hauled up the bank and left there by a flood, with doors cut in its flank and a stovepipe through the deck. Inside, the floor slopes ten degrees toward the river and everything has been built to suit: the benches are barrels, the bar is a hatch-cover on two more, and every cup sits in a ring cut into the wood so it does not walk. Over the bar a cord forty feet long hangs from the beam in loops, thick with knots of a dozen colours. A one-eared halfling is running it through his fingers like a man checking his own pulse. Tea is on. Somebody has just lost an argument about eels.

The hull is the *Long Wait*, a Fallowden grain-barge that grounded on the Sedgemere bar in the thaw-flood of 3003 and was never refloated. Cobb Latchwater took it off its owner's widow for an unpaid ferry fare, cut doors in it, and has been pouring since. He kept the name over the door. A barge that stays is a tavern, he says; a barge that goes is a rumour.

The tavern's own name is the Salt Feud joke. In the winter of 2996 the Harl tallyman counted thirteen barrels where the Dunmarrow reeve had counted twelve and started eight years of war, and Cobb, who was a deckhand on a Hask salt-fish barge that winter, swears he watched the tallyman's finger move. He has been telling the story ever since to people who assume it is a bar story. In the Clans, a thirteenth barrel is the one that was never counted, never taxed and never asked about, and the phrase has become the polite way to refer to anything in a hold that is none of your business. Cobb thinks this is hilarious. Posy Reedmarsh drinks here every Wardday and has never once laughed.

What Cobb keeps is **the cord**. The Clans write nothing down: a ledger is a thing a magistrate can seize. A grudge, a debt, a promise, a lie, a death, a wedding, a barrel that was short: each is a knot, tied by colour, by cord-weave and by position, and Cobb is the one man on the Cinder who can run the whole length through his fingers and tell you what the river owes and who owes the river. The cord is hung over the bar because a thing nobody can read is a thing nobody will steal. In the Barrel it is considered rude to ask what a knot is. It is considered normal to buy him a drink and stand where he can see you.

### Menu
- Thirteenth-barrel ale, 1 cp a mug. Brewed in the staves of Grimmark salt-fish barrels, so there is brine in it and it tastes of a very honest ocean. Either it grows on you or you grow on it.
- Eel pie, 2 cp, hot, with the skin on. Jellied eel, cold, 1 cp, "for those who need to prove something".
- Smoked eel, a string of six, 4 cp.
- Fish stew with river-cress, 3 cp a bowl, bread free if you ask nicely.
- Fallowden red, 3 cp a cup, 1 sp 5 cp the jug. Cheaper than anywhere else in the March, because there is no toll on a cup that comes upriver by barge.
- The Returned (Grimmark whey-spirit), 5 cp a thimble.
- Barge tea, free, with news. If you bring none the cup is smaller.
- A bunk in the bilge, 3 cp, dry above the third rib. Do not sleep below it.

### Who is here
Cobb at the bar, always. Bargelings carrying messages on a rota: four of them, sitting on the stairs, who will sell you the room's gossip for a copper and sell the room yours for two. River pilots in their good coats. Eel-men in their bad ones. On Wardday evenings [[Barge-Mother Posy Reedmarsh]] in the corner chair she has carried over from her boat, which nobody else may sit in. [[Keld Owes-Nothing]], rarely, for the soup, which she takes standing at the end of the bar and pays for in a coin she puts on the wood and does not push across. [[Kettle Brindlewake]], when she is up from the Quay, in whichever seat looks like the best seat for hearing the rest of the room.

On the beam above the door is Cobb's **barrel-count**: a sweepstake, a copper a guess, chalked up in a bargeling's hand. Every time a tow comes up the river, the house runs a book on how many barrels it carries. The winning guess is always twelve. Nobody has ever been disappointed.

### Rumours
- Cobb's cord remembers everything, including things that have not happened yet.
- Posy Reedmarsh takes a tithe on every tow and the Clans pretend not to know.
- The *Long Wait* sits on the bar because there is something under it that the bar is holding down.
- The Ninth Lantern's quartermaster sold lamp-oil to the Clans for bread, and it is in a hold on the Quay.
- Somebody paid Cobb to cut a knot out of the cord, and he refused, and that is why he has one ear.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. He remembers everything. As to the other, he says no, and has been right so far. 2. True; see below. 3. False, but the hull's double bottom does hold something. 4. True: four casks, aboard the *Grey Dumpling*; Cobb has a knot for it, a red one, with a small knot of Fennick's family on it, and will not discuss his cousins' cargoes. 5. False and irresistible: he lost the ear to a boathook on the Ardis in 2991. The story is better with a knot in it and he has never corrected it.
:::

### Things to do
Eat the pie and look at the cord. Drink with the pilots and learn which of them is lying about the river's depth. Put a copper on the barrel-count and lose, and be told why. Play the Sedgemere game, "river rules": everyone at the bar says a true thing about the river, and the one who is caught lying buys the round. Hire a bargeling to carry a message nobody can read upstairs. Sit in Posy's chair when she is not looking and learn what the room does.

:::gm
**The double bottom.** Under the sloping floor, beneath a trapdoor in the third bench, the *Long Wait*'s true bottom lies two feet below the floor you can see. It is a salt-bin; it is also a drop. On a tow-night two salt-barrels of the Concord's cargo come up from the dead arm by a rope through the stern, are opened, and one coin in thirteen goes into a canvas bag in the bilge, which Posy's bargelings carry to the *Long Patience* before dawn. Search the barrel-count slate against the tow (Investigation DC 13): the sweepstake guesses are the barrels the house was *told* to guess; the barrels that actually passed are in Cobb's cord (a thirteenth knot, ash-grey, every sixth day). Cobb will not read it. He can be made to say which knot is which if you hold up something he values: a true thing about Posy that he does not know, or the names of the people who paid for the cord's last forty feet.

**Cobb's loyalty** is to the water, then Posy, then the bar; he will lie to a magistrate, a Quiet Office man or a PC he likes, but he will not lie to Kettle, which makes Kettle's price for his silence the cheapest on the river.

**If the tithe is exposed** Posy's neutrality shatters in front of the Clans, and the Barge-Mother of the Water can be deposed by a Quay-Moot. Cobb knows this and drinks more than he did in Reaping.
:::

== GM notes ==

Cobb's voice: slow, dry, wet, with a river-man's habit of answering a question by looking out of the window first. He calls everyone "love" or "sir", never "friend". He talks to the cord when he thinks nobody is listening, quietly, in a list. Make that audible once.

Use the tavern as the Sedgemere hub: Posy and Keld both come here, the tow-strings are counted from its window, and the sweepstake is a perfectly reasonable thing for a PC to be curious about. Do not explain the barrels; let the number twelve repeat until someone asks why it never changes.
