> A narrow shop with one very long table under the window and a surveyor's chain hung over the door like a dead snake, each link bright with use. Every wall is a map: the town in six sheets, the plain in four, the river as a long ribbon with its shoals inked in red, and in the corner by the stove, pinned flat with brass tacks and a little frayed, a long hand-drawn strip of a chasm with the word RENT across it in capitals. A woman is standing at the end of the table with a pair of dividers in one hand, looking at you. She is not looking at your face. She is, you realise, judging how far it is from your boots to the door.

Orla Quist came to Cinderford in the spring of 3003 with the treaty commissioners, to survey the ground the Treaty of Ashes had divided and the Rent that nobody had named, and stayed when the commissioners went home. She measured the Rent in 3003, 3005, 3007 and 3009, in the company's pay and under its escort, with a chain, a plumb-line and two hired chain-hands, and cut the figure into the slate on the cairn at its western end each time: twenty-nine miles, then thirty, thirty, thirty-one. It is the only honest number anyone has. The company's pay stopped, the survey stopped, and the slate for 3011 is blank.

She opened the shop in 3005, on Ford Street, because a town that is being rebuilt needs boundaries. It is the only place in the March where you can have a plot of ground drawn, a dead man's claim recorded, or the line between two neighbours settled before the Reeve on Wardday with a ruler instead of a knife. The sign over the door says QUIST & DAUGHTER. There is no father now; he died in the winter of 3009, in Cinderford, and is buried on Corvel Rise under a stone she cut herself. She did not repaint the sign. "He was the first half of every figure I own," she says. "It would be bad arithmetic."

### Charts and services
- A copy of the company's Rent survey (the 3009 sheet), 1 sp. It is the best map of the Rent there is, and she will say so.
- The Ash Road stake-chart, 5 cp, redrawn every Seedwake; she walks the stakes herself and corrects the ones the Glassers move.
- The town plan, 2 sp, in six sheets.
- A Glasser's dig-chart, copied, 3 sp: "accuracy not warranted by the house". She will copy any chart a Glasser brings her; she will not stand behind it.
- A chart of the Glass Barrow: refused. "A chart of a place nobody has seen whole is a lie with a border round it."
- A map of the continent, 2 gp, hand-coloured, with Caer Ardith in the wrong place on purpose, and a note on the back saying so.
- Measure a plot for Wardday court, 1 sp. Witness a boundary, 2 sp. Draw a dead man's claim and swear to the lines, 5 sp.
- A day of her time with the chain and two hands, 1 gp; to the Rent and back, three, and an escort.

### Who is here
**Orla Quist**, thirty-eight, at the table. Two chain-hands, Smokes cousins named Tib and Tansy Redd, who hold things and are not allowed to speak while she is calculating. A child from the Ford with a boundary dispute about a hen. A Glasser, now and then, with a chart and a lie. Odile Fairweather, once a month, to check the line of the Ford against the river, which moves.

### Rumours
- She measured the Rent four times and got the same number every time.
- Her father is buried on Corvel Rise with a sum on the stone.
- The Regency means to tax the plain by the acre, and her last survey is why.
- She has a chart of the Barrow, and the Glassers have paid her for it.
- She can tell a man is lying by how far apart his feet are.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False, and this is the point: 29, 30, 30, 31. The Rent is growing; she knows it and has not said so. 2. True. The stone reads *H. QUIST, 2940 to 3009. He kept the count.* 3. Half true: the Regency's Assessor has her sheet. Pell does not know why. 4. False. She has the Barrow's lines from forty Glasser charts and has never drawn them. 5. True, within a pace.
:::

### Things to do
Buy a map of the Rent before you go east. Have a boundary drawn. Argue with her about a figure, and lose. Hire her for the chain and the cairn. Watch her measure a man by eye and say, quietly, how many paces from honest he is. Buy a chart from a Glasser and have her say how many of its lines are real.

:::gm
**What she holds.** Under the table, in a flat chest of map-drawers, lies **Helmar Quist's ledger**, forty narrow pages of a dead commander's hand. He kept a ledger of every promise made to Ardmere during and after the siege, and noted whether it was kept. Almost none were. His daughter inherited it and has read it twice. She has told no one what is in it.

The entries that matter in play: the Regency's promise to the **Long Spoons** (the orc company that took the water-gate), of land on the Ardmere bend, "forty acres a head", never surveyed, which Quist wrote *"surveyed 2995, by my hand, and it is good land"*; and on the page for day ninety of the siege, in Quist's own margin, beside the name of the supply clerk of the Salt Guard who had counted the garrison's stores: *"I. Marrow. She counted every sack and told me the number. I did not like the number."* Marrow was inside Ardmere for all 211 days; she is the one who told the commander the truth, early, and was not believed. She has never spoken of it. The Long Spoons' pot-boy who came through the water-gate with the first pot is [[Spoons Aldane]].

Orla's secret is that she has not shown the ledger to Marrow because she does not know whether Marrow would want it. If Marrow asks, she gives it. If Spoons asks, she reads him the line about the land, and he does not laugh for a day.

**The 3011 survey.** If the party escorts her to the cairn (session 6 route), the slate will read **33**, the Glassers' figure, not the company's: two miles in two years where it took six for the first two. She cuts the figure and says nothing for a mile. The Rent is accelerating, and the slate is the first sign the party can show anyone.
:::

== GM notes ==

Orla's voice: precise, unhurried, entirely unsentimental. She counts steps, distances and years aloud. She measures people in paces and says the number: "You are about eleven paces from honest, Corporal. Four if you sit down." She is not unkind, but she cannot lie, because she has never been taught how, and it comes out sounding like a joke. A good source of a plain fact, a bad source of comfort, and a useful ally if the party is standing at a cliff. Introduce her at the cairn in session 6, or in session 4 if the party needs a survey.
