> The causeway runs out from the quay to Ledger Island on a hundred stone arches, and under the fourth arch from the land, where the tide slaps and the causeway's lamps hang low, a door opens on noise. A long room with a floor of ship's planking and a ceiling of ship's beams, salt-white, a bar made of a barge's keel, and at every table a Counting-House clerk in grey with the ink still wet on his cuffs and a barge-captain in oiled wool pretending not to listen. At the room's end a round table with seven chairs. Six have clerks in them. The seventh has a cup of black punch in front of it, full, and nobody is looking at it, and everybody knows exactly where it is.

The Seventh Chair stands where the Long Counting-House's clerks must pass twice a day, going out to Ledger Island at the morning bell and coming back at the closing, and it has stood there since the Concord was signed. The tale, as the house tells it, is that [[The First Concord]] was signed at a table with seven legs and seven chairs, one for each port's clerk, and that the seventh clerk, [[Cowl]]'s, was found at the last moment drunk in this very room, under this very arch, and a Shellback bell-ringer sat in his chair and counted the seven copies signed, and took the fee for the hour. The house has kept a seventh chair empty ever since, with a drink before it, "for the one who was late". The Counting-House calls this story a slander. The Counting-House's clerks drink here every night.

Aud Pellamy has kept the house eighteen years, since her husband's barge went down in the Bay with him on it, and runs it on two principles: a clerk who has drunk is a clerk who talks, and a captain who has listened is a captain who pays. She sells the first to the second and both to the Clans, and nobody has ever accused her of being unfair about it. She tends the seventh chair's cup herself, every night, and pours the old one into the harbour at dawn with a word to the Toll-Keeper. On the morning of 3 Ashfall this year she came down and the cup was empty, and dry, and standing where she had left it, and the pot-boy who sleeps under the bar had seen nothing, and she has not been right since.

### Menu
- Bay ale, 2 cp a pot. Brewed on Ledger Island with harbour water, and tastes of the fact.
- Marrowport black-resin punch, 4 cp a cup, 2 sp the bowl: rum, resin, lime, and something that makes the clerks say what they think of the Table. The drink on the seventh chair is this.
- Oysters, 1 cp each, opened in front of you by a boy with a knife and no fingernails to speak of. A dozen for 1 sp and a copper for the shells, which go to the wall.
- Fish stew, Sevenhaven style, with saffron that is not saffron, 3 cp.
- Bread and eel, 2 cp. "Concord lunch."
- A bed, 1 sp, in the loft over the arch, where the tide talks all night; 2 sp for the one with a door, which the clerks hire by the hour to say things.
- A seat at the round table, free, if there is one. There usually is one. It is not the seventh.

### Who is here
Counting-House clerks, in grey, in grades, drinking the day's figures out of their heads. Barge-captains and Mothers of the Water, who have factors in Cowl and cousins in Cinderford and pay Aud by the pennyweight for what the clerks let fall. Tide-wardens of [[Sefra]] off the Chain, who drink for nothing because the house's licence says so. [[Fallon Wrass]], once a junior clerk and now a senior one, who has been told not to come here and comes, at the far end, alone, with a bowl. A Shellback, sometimes, who takes the whole evening to drink one cup and is never the one in the seventh chair. [[Kettle Brindlewake]] was here in Emberwane, on a crate, with her pipe; it was at this table that a clerk told her the Table had bought the ash down to the last candle. Aud remembers, because Kettle tipped in information.

### Rumours
- The seventh chair's drink is for the Shellback who never came to the signing, and the house has owed him a drink for fourteen hundred years.
- Whoever drinks it dies within the year.
- The Table has bought the whole Ashenreach, the garrison, the town, the Regency's share, and keeps it in one ledger with one line at the bottom.
- The drink was found drunk on the morning of 3 Ashfall, and the same night the drowned bells of Keel rang on their own.
- There is a Shellback on the Table who wants something in the ash that is not money.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. Half: the house's own story says it is for the late clerk of Cowl; the older story, which only the tide-wardens tell, says the bell-ringer who sat in the chair was not paid by the hour but by the century, and is still owed. 2. False, so far. 3. True; the ledger is the Table's Ashenreach account and [[Fallon Wrass]] has read it. 4. True on both counts, and the Treaty Bell at Cinderford sounded once with no hand on it the same night. 5. True: [[Grandmother Tollamund]]. The Table suspects; [[Cousin Pennamund]] was sent east to find out.
:::

### Things to do
Buy a clerk the bowl and learn what the Table paid for the Ninth Lantern's paper, to the crown. Sit at the round table and feel six clerks watch you not sit in the seventh chair. Ask Aud what happened on 3 Ashfall and be told, flatly, that the cup was empty, and then be asked what *you* know about the night of the second, because she has heard the ash country's bells rang too. Hire the room with a door for an hour and find a tide-warden already in it, waiting, with a drowned-list that has a Ninth Lantern name on it that was never on a boat. Pay a barge-captain to carry a letter up the Ardis and the Cinder to Cinderford, twelve days against the current, 1 sp, no questions, which is the best deal in the Concord.

:::gm
**What drank the drink.** Under the Chain, on the tidal islet the Fifth Ward stands on ([[The Nine Great Wards]], the Drowned Stone), a Lithic family keeps a warden on the Bench of [[Corvex]]'s stipend: the only ward on the continent with a bought heart. On the night of 2 Ashfall, when the Treaty Bell at Cinderford sounded alone and the bells of [[Keel]] rang in the deep, the warden on the islet, a Lithic woman called Telune of the Fifth who has not left the stone in forty years and is stone to the hip, walked off it at the dead low water, up the causeway stair, in under the fourth arch, drank the cup on the seventh chair, and walked back before the tide turned. The pot-boy saw her and has told nobody, because he is nine and she looked at him. The tide-wardens know, because the islet's watch-lamp went out while she was gone and they have been paid by the Bench not to say. Why she came is the GM's: the Ninth's crack was felt by every ward that night, and the Fifth's warden, who has sat under the Concord's money for forty years listening to the Sleeper through the Chain, got up to see whether the bargain the Shellbacks are making in the ash is one she will be asked to pay for. She drank the drink because it was there and she had been thirsty since 2971.

The house is where a party in Sevenhaven after session 8 finds the Table's clerks, Fallon Wrass's account, [[Barge-Mother Thimble Fairlock]]'s letters and the tide-wardens' drowned-list in one room, and where anyone who asks about the lantern aloud is reported to the Counting-House by the closing bell. The Concord's Debt clock does not tick here; it is counted here.
:::

== GM notes ==

Aud's voice is the Concord's: friendly, exact, and already working out what you are worth. Shellbacks are rare in the room and change it when they come; play one entering as a tide coming in, the clerks straightening, the captains going quiet. The seventh chair is the house's one superstition in a city that has none, and the clerks who laugh at it never sit in it. If the party sits in it, make them pay for the room. Do not explain Telune unless they dig: the empty cup is the clue, the ward is the answer, and the pot-boy will draw her for a copper, stone from the hip down.
