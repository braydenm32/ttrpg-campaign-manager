> A long stone byre with a counter cut into one wall and a bell on the rope above it, cracked, with a note like a cough. Behind the counter, tin pails with cloths over them, hanging cheeses in muslin like pale fruit, a trough of whey, and a smell of warm straw, milk and wet slate. A child of seven with one grey eye and one brown sits on the counter with her feet swinging, eating a heel of bread and watching the door. A woman with a plait round her head is counting something out loud at the far end and does not stop when you ring.

Bellwether's dairy was a pear-press before the war; the press-stone is still the counter's weight. Marn Tolliver bought the byre cheap from a family that left and has run it since. By the time the Ninth Lantern had settled at the Keep she was already the March's only milk-seller, because nobody else was mad enough to keep goats a mile from the ash. By now the Keep takes a third of her output, the Reeve's table another third, and the town the rest, carried across the bridge on a cart at dawn at 5 cp (the toll on a cart) by a nephew who has learned to look through the toll-clerk.

The cheeses are named, because Marn is a woman with a goat for every mood and a cheese for every goat. **Hard Rain** is the ten-month, grey rind, crumbly, sharp, a soldiers' cheese. **Wet Wednesday** is the soft white, fresh each morning, which goes off by Tideday. **Second Thoughts** is the middle cheese, washed in the last of the Fallowden red. Nobody asks where "Wednesday" comes from, since the March has no Wednesday, and Marn's answer is "it was the husband's".

### Stock
- Goat's milk, 1 cp a mug, 3 cp a jug (bring the jug). The only milk in Cinderford; the company's sick are given it for free by order of Maud Severin.
- Wet Wednesday (soft), 2 cp the round. Second Thoughts, 5 cp the wedge. Hard Rain, 1 sp the half-wheel.
- Whey broth, 1 cp, "for the stomach, and the conscience".
- Goat's-wool blankets, 4 sp, grey, scratchy, warm, and twice as long-lived as the company's own.
- Goat-hook, 2 sp, "better than a spear for anything that bites and worse for anything that thinks".
- A live goat, 5 sp. They sell well and walk home on their own.

### Who is here
Marn at the far end. Ember on the counter, who watches the door and says one sentence per visit, usually correct. A Tolliver nephew churning, rhythmically, with a face like a man doing sums. The Ninth's forage-corporal, with a tally-stick and a hope. A goat, in the doorway, whichever is currently in disgrace.

### Rumours
- The Dairy's milk is the only thing in the March that has not tasted of ash since Cinders.
- Marn Tolliver has never lost a goat and has never sold one that was hers.
- The whey from Bellwether cures the walking sickness, if drunk at dusk.
- The pale ash at the east wall is the lake's, and somebody is carrying it.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True: the goats will not eat ash-grass and Marn's pasture is cut by hand. 2. False: she lost nine in the winter of 3005 and sold a hundred and four, and counts both. 3. False, and she is mortified every time someone asks. 4. True: see [[Bellwether]]. Marn will not volunteer it.
:::

### Things to do
Buy a cheese and be told what you should have bought instead. Get Marn onto the toll and lose an hour. Let Ember tell you what she thinks of you (she is nearly always right). Buy a goat. Ask to see the third byre and watch Marn's face. Offer to carry a cart of milk across the bridge at dawn and see what Mistress Hebbe says to a goat.

:::gm
The third byre holds a single unopened clay jar under the straw: Marn's evidence of the Stillwater ash. See [[Goodwife Marn Tolliver]] for the price.
:::

== GM notes ==

A gentle shop. Pace it like an afternoon. Everything is counted and none of it is secret. Let the party leave with cheese and one thing they did not know.
