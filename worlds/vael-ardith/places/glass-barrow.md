> Under the bridge the river is loud and the light is green. The third pier stands on a shelf of black glass, and at its foot, where the water pulls hardest, a low arch of old brick opens into the dark: the culvert that drained the village square, before the square was the riverbed. Cold air comes out of it, steady, and smells of wet ash and, faintly, pears. On the lip of the arch, in the slime, is a handprint. It is pointing in.

Before the battle, Cinderford's square stood where the ford is now. The sorcery of that night melted the square, the chapel, the bakery and forty houses into a single mass of dark glass and dropped it twenty feet; the river moved over it within the year. The Glassers call what is under the ford the Barrow, because of what is in it. They dug one shaft in 3007 and did not dig another.

The deserter [[Dannic Rook]] went in through the culvert on 3 Ashfall with a Glasser called Skelt holding the rope, and cached what he had in the Cold Room. On the night of 7 Ashfall he was drowned under the third pier; the current brought him back in through the culvert, and the cold here will not let him lie still.

:::gm
**Running it.** Seven areas, roughly in order; the party can skip 3 and 7. Everything is tilted ten to twenty degrees, and the floor is glass under a skin of silt: moving faster than a walk means a DC 10 Dexterity (Acrobatics) check or a fall, 1d4 slashing. Light sources reflect everywhere; Perception checks for ambush are at disadvantage unless the light is hooded. Casting any spell in the Barrow frosts the walls and makes the glyph in area 5 glow for a round, wherever the caster is: a ley-echo of the ward. Nothing changes on the hill: the lantern's first guttering is at the end of session 1, whatever was cast down here. The Barrow is a minor ward-node, so this does not tick the Sleeper clock. Each spell cast in the Barrow after the first gives the Captain in area 6 an extra 5 hit points (maximum +10): the ward is paying.

**1. The Culvert.** A brick tunnel, five feet high, forty long, ankle-deep in running water. DC 12 Perception: scuffs on the brick at shoulder height where someone dragged a heavy tin along the wall, and a hank of something like hair caught on a snag: glass-spider silk, razor-fine (1 slashing to a bare hand). The culvert ends in a drop of eight feet onto area 2 (DC 10 Athletics to climb down; the fall is 1d6). Low water comes at dusk and lasts about four hours; after that the culvert floods to the roof, and the party must find the Quay cut from area 2 instead.

**2. The Flooded Street.** The old High Street, canted twenty degrees, knee-deep in black water on the low side, dry on the high side. House-fronts of melted stone lean in like candle wax; a door-knocker is fused mid-knock. Shapes in the walls. Hollowborn characters see them turn their heads. The far end is a slope of rubble up to the chapel (area 4). On the left, half way, the bakery (area 3). On the right, low down and under water, a brick arch with a rope tied through it: the smugglers' cut, which runs fifty yards to the culvert under the Low Quay. Barge-clan characters, or anyone who asks Kettle, know of it. A tin cup, a boot and a soldier's belt-pouch lie on the dry side: Dannic's, empty but for a single new Regency silver.

**3. The Collapsed Bakery.** The front has fallen in; the ovens at the back are intact, three brick mouths, and glass-spiders have nested in them. Webs of transparent silk span the room at knee and neck height, and ring like small bells when touched: DC 13 Perception to see them by hooded light, DC 15 by open; walking into one means a DC 12 Strength save or restrained (5 hp, AC 10, vulnerable to bludgeoning; a web cut with a blade does 1 slashing to the cutter). Stealth DC 12 to pass the bakery without waking them. Two glass-spiders (AC 13, HP 13; bite +4 for 1d6+2 piercing plus 1d6 poison, Con DC 11 for half; web, recharge 5-6, +4 ranged, restrained by ringing glass thread, Strength DC 11 or 10 damage to break; Spider Climb) are the colour of the glass they live in and have advantage on Stealth here; add a third if the party is rested or a level above. Treasure in the ovens: a baker's hoard of 23 sp and 140 cp fused into a lump (worth its weight, 4 gp, at Gutter Market), and a stone loaf, perfectly detailed, warm. Ada Pennock of the Baker's Wife wants the wedding ring in the middle of the lump (see [[The Baker's Hoard]]): [[Ninefingers Forge]] cuts a fused lump open for 1 sp. A fair fight for level 1 with two spiders and a hard one with three: let the party retreat into the street and fight at the door, or skip the room if the night is short.

**4. The Glass Chapel.** The lower nave of the old chapel of Hathe, whose lintel and bell were thrown clear when the rest of it melted; the roof is gone and the walls have flowed and set. The pews are full. Forty villagers sit facing the altar, fused where they sat, glass-skinned, heads bowed, and the detail is terrible and exact: a child's hand in its mother's, a man half-turned toward the door. Tom Pennock, Ada's husband, is in the fourth pew from the front, on the aisle, his head a little toward the door, the shape of his cap still on him. If Quorl asks for him by name after the congregation's sentence, he has a line of his own (see [[The Baker's Hoard]]). The altar is a black stone slab with a doorway carved in it, Hathe's sign. Anyone who sits in a pew hears, very faintly, forty people breathing in. Quorl, or any priest of Hathe, can take the congregation's last words here (DC 12 Religion or a short rite): they all say the same thing, which is the sentence the dying have been whispering to Quorl. Behind the altar, where the floor dropped, a stair goes down (area 5). At the chapel door, scratched in the glass, in chalk gone hard: *D.R. 3 Ashfall. Back for the rest.*

**5. The Cold Stair.** A spiral stair of fitted stone, older than the chapel by a thousand years, going down a hundred feet, and every step colder. Frost on the walls after thirty. Over the stair's first arch, cut deep into a lintel of grey Lithic stone, a line of glyphs. Ysse (Arcana DC 13), Borrin (surface-dwarf script, DC 10 for a dwarf), or anyone with the Lithic language reads it: *WHAT WE BURIED IS NOT AT REST.* Below it, scratched newer and shallower, a second line in the same script that the readers cannot make out: a rubbing of it is worth something to Aelith Sorn, Warden Uthrem and Tollamund, in that order of honesty. The stair is the Barrow's oldest part: the village was built on a Lithic waystation, and this is its throat. Halfway down, a landing with a pile of Dannic's gear: a bedroll, a dark-lantern out of oil, a Glasser's pick, and seven empty Keel oil-tins with the seals broken, rinsed clean. Whatever he was doing, he was doing it with oil.

**6. The Cold Room.** A round chamber thirty feet across, domed, walls and floor and dome furred with frost that glitters in any light. Breath smokes. In the centre a plain stone chair, Lithic work, and in it sits a dead man in the ruin of a captain's coat, the Seventh's badge (the Ruddy Hundred) fused to the breast, a lantern-pole across his knees with no lantern on it. His eyes are open and white with frost. Around the chair, in a ring, Dannic's stash: a grey company blanket tied into a bundle, a clean Keel tin, [[The Cut Oil Tin]] (a dented Keel tin with the Keel bell on its base and Hesk's grey wax, stamped with a moth, over the seal; heavier than it should be, and the grey slurry inside smells of lake water), and a third tin, empty. In the bundle: a purse of twelve new Caer Ardith silver; a note in a poor hand, *S. says one more swap and Im square. Then the clerk wants a word. Dont like the clerk.*; and a pay-chit of the Ninth Lantern for 3009 stamped PAID IN FULL under a Regency seal that nobody in the company knows.

The Captain does not move until something is lifted from the ring. Then he stands. **The Cold Captain** (wight scaled down: AC 13, HP 30, speed 30; Str 15; lantern-pole +4 for 1d8+2 bludgeoning, or Life Drain +4 for 1d6+2 necrotic and DC 11 Constitution save or hit-point maximum reduced by the damage until a long rest; resistance to necrotic and to cold; vulnerable to fire; does not leave the room). He is the Cold Captain of the Seventh. When he first stands he says *"Held. We held. Is it morning?"* and then he is slow, sure and silent, and he fights to put things back. A character who sets down what they took and steps out of the ring is not attacked again. A character who shows a lit wick-lamp or the company's lantern token makes him stop for one round and look, and in that round he asks: *"Which lantern is lit?"* If the answer is "the Ninth", he sits down again and does not rise for this party. A flame from the Lantern (any wick-lamp) makes him recoil: he has disadvantage on attacks against its bearer. Burned, or reduced to 0 hit points, he comes apart into ash and frost; the chair stays cold. Treasure on him: the badge, which Mother Hennet will want, and, in his coat, a folded and refolded note in a woman's hand that says only *come home*.

**7. The Red Seam.** Behind the chair, a crack in the dome's wall wide enough for one at a time, running down and east. Frost gives way to warmth within twenty feet. The passage ends after sixty at a door of grey Lithic stone with no handle, and across the floor in front of it, under a skin of glass, runs a hairline of red light, the colour of the Ember, pulsing slowly, east. It hums. Hollowborn characters standing here dream awake for a moment. Ysse feels the lantern on the hill as if it were in the next room. The door does not open, this session or for several more. (The Lithic doors on this road were shut by the standing dead, so the key of the dead turns them: this one, the Cold Seam landing, and the Stone's root.) The keyhole in it matches a key the Returned smith in Hollowhall keeps forging and melting; it also matches [[Hathe's Door-Key]], which is not here. Leave it. Note what the players touch.

**Getting out.** Back up the stair and the street; the culvert if the water is low, the Quay cut if it is not (the cut is fifty yards; the last fifty feet are flooded: DC 10 Athletics to swim it, with light out; on a failure the swimmer's wick-lamp goes under and goes out). The party surfaces under the Low Quay at the floating market's stair, in front of whoever is there at that hour, with a tin of oil that someone in the Keep does not want found.

When the body moves the cold runs out through the culvert at the third pier and down the Cinder nine miles to the stakes at [[Sedgemere]]: on 8 Ashfall (the body goes in) and 13 Ashfall (it comes back up to stand in the shallows). [[Keld Owes-Nothing]]'s slate has both dates.

== GM notes ==

The Barrow should feel like a drowned church, not a cave: quiet, cold, full of people who are still there. Play the congregation for grief, the spiders for sudden violence, the Captain for dread and then, if the players are clever, pity. End the session with the lantern on the hill guttering as they come up, whether or not they have spoken to the Captain; the Cut Oil Tin is the clue, the gutter is the stakes. For the Lithic rubbing, see the handout [[A Rubbing from the Ninth Stone]] for the style of script; this one is shorter.
