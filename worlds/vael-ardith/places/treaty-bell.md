> A post of black oak in the middle of the square, iron-strapped, and on it a bell the size of a barrel, green with weather and cracked a hand's width along the lip. A rope hangs from it, looped up out of reach and knotted. A boy sits under it on a stool with a stick across his knees, and looks at you as if you might be thinking of touching it.

The bell was the old Cinderford chapel's, dug out of the ash after the battle by cooks and walking wounded who wanted something to ring. It was carried to the Treaty Stone and rung once at the signing of [[The Treaty of Ashes]], then brought back and hung here, and the Reeve's first ordinance was that its rope should be pulled thereafter only for alarm or for matters of the treaty.

It has been rung nine times. Once at the signing. Twice for greywinds that reached the walls. Three times for the dead walking in from the plain at red moon. Once when the Regency pay stopped, which the Reeve ruled a treaty matter. Once by a drunk, who was flogged. Once, nobody knows by whom, in the middle of the night of 2 Ashfall this year, a single stroke, with the rope still knotted up out of reach; the bell-ward swears he was awake.

The bell-ward's mallet strokes from the bell-house stair, for the ford at dusk and dawn, deaths, fire, and someone in the water, do not count as ringing; nobody touches the rope but on the Keep's order.

The bell is one of three alarm languages in town, with the company's drum and the signal tower's lamps; all three are set down in [[Signals of Cinderford]].

### Who is here
Dunny Pike, who holds the post because his father held it, and his father is dead. He takes it seriously enough that the gate-watch have stopped teasing him. He learned the strokes from old [[Halloran Pike]] by the Candle fire, no relation, there being a great many Pikes, and he carries the old man's pipe over to him every evening. His code, struck with the mallet: one stroke, the ford closes; two, a death; three, fire; three and three, someone in the water. Four, he says, is the end of the world, and he has never struck it. He knows everyone who crosses the square and is a better source than the toll-clerk, for the price of a pear.

### Rumours
- The crack in the lip happened the night of the battle, when the fire came.
- When the bell rings itself, somebody in the March has died who should not have.
- The tenth ring will be the last.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True. 2. True: it rang itself on the night of 2 Ashfall; the next day Dannic Rook was missing from muster. 3. Soldiers' talk, and nearer the mark than they know: the tenth pull of the rope is the night the lantern leaves the Hill (session 8).
:::

The bell is a plain bell. The stroke on 2 Ashfall was the night the Keel bells rang under the water and the pay wagon left Fallowden with a Concord-paid escort; something tugged at every bell in the south that night and the GM need never say what. Ringing the Treaty Bell for the mutiny in session 7, or the march in session 3, is a strong beat: whoever rings it is claiming the treaty.
