> A low long dairy on the east lane with a window cut into the wall so that the whole crossroads and the black oak can be seen from the counter. On the shelves, rounds of white cheese in grey cloth and wedges of yellow under glass bells, and a hanging ham of something that was once a ram. Beside the till a wicker basket full of cut rope-ends tied with a wisp of straw, a penny sign propped against it. A woman in a kerchief is wrapping a cheese for a corporal in a coat three sizes too small, and charging him exactly what she said she would.

The Lennard farm made cheese for the Fallowden fair for two hundred years. When the village burned in 2996 the farm burned with it, and the family's cheeses with it, and Mab Lennard rebuilt the dairy herself, stone by stone, on its own foundations, with four cows and an uncle's recipe-book. By 3002 she was selling to the Fallowden table again. By 3005 she had added the basket.

The cheeses are named, as everything in Dunmeer is, for the thing it remembers. **Hard No** is the long-aged one, grey-rinded, crumbly, sharp enough to make you stand up straight. **Soft No** is its fresh sister, rindless and mild. **Not Today** is a washed-rind that smells like a boot and tastes like a promise. **Yes, Sir** is the pale, rubbery, entirely inoffensive cheese Mab sells to Regency officers at twice the price of the others, and she has never been known to explain why.

The strands in the basket are rope, new, cut from the same coil as the Tree's current shrine-cords, and sold for a copper. They are not the Corporal's own rope, and Mab says so. She says they work anyway, "about one in five", and she sells forty of them on a quiet week and four hundred on Rope Day. The coppers go in the dish at the Tree, and from there to the chapel's hearth. Buttons are free, one to a pilgrim, from a tin by the till.

### Stock
- Soft No, 3 cp the round. Not Today, 6 cp the wedge. Hard No, 8 cp the wedge, 1 sp 5 cp the half-wheel.
- Yes, Sir, 1 sp the round, "for officers, and for those who have decided".
- Strand of rope, 1 cp. Button from the tin, free. A cord worn at the wrist, 2 cp: the way soldiers wear the Corporal's rope.
- Fresh milk, 1 cp the mug, in the morning only.
- A cup at the Tree on Rope Day (6 Frostcall), free to anyone who waits for it to reach the newest soldier.
- Bread and a hunk of cheese for a soldier's pack, 2 cp, "for the road, and the refusing".

### Who is here
Mab at the counter, wrapping, weighing, watching the oak through the window. A girl of fourteen called Linn who does the churning and listens at every conversation. Pilgrims, always: three or four in an afternoon, soldiers mostly, with the look of people who have come a long way to ask something. The Forty, dropping in for the day's whey. A cat on the counter, called Corporal, who does not obey.

### Rumours
- Mab Lennard sells strands of the real rope, and says it is new only to keep the Regency from asking.
- Eat the cheese called Not Today before an order you do not mean to follow and the order will go astray.
- She will give a deserter a night's bed, and has never been caught.
- Mab keeps a count of every prayer said under the Tree and what came of it.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False: the real rope was her byre-halter, and it went with the levy; she does not have it. 2. She says it is the cheese. It is the Corporal, about one time in five. 3. True: the loft over the dairy has slept eleven since 3003. 4. True: a school copybook under the till, a mark a prayer, a second mark if something broke. "One in five" is an honest count.
:::

### Things to do
Buy cheese and be given an opinion on your sword. Choose a strand and wear it. Stand at the counter window and watch the pilgrims at the Tree and learn what each of them is asking. Ask Mab why she calls it new rope and see her hands. On Rope Day, queue for the cup.

:::gm
The dairy loft is a safe house for deserters. Mab has never been asked and has never asked; the rule is one night, no names. A PC who needs one night of safety on the road in the Fallowden marches can have it at the Tree for the price of a cheese and a refusal of something small. The Regency's tax-collector arrives once a quarter and always leaves a coin on the counter, which she puts in the dish.

For what Mab knows of the Salt Guard's clerk, see [[Mab Lennard]].
:::
