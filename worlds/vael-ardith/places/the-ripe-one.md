> A long low tent, sailcloth over a frame of salvaged roof-beams, with a floor of planks laid over black glass that shows through the gaps and, when the lamps are low, shows things in it. The bar is three barrels and a door. Behind it, on a nail, hangs a Regency quartermaster's sash, faded to pink, and under it a board chalked with the day's odds: WHAT THE PIT BRINGS UP: BONE 2 to 1 / IRON 3 to 1 / COIN 9 to 1 / SOMETHING THAT HUMS 20 to 1 / A ROOF 40 to 1. A dozen diggers on upturned buckets, grey to the eyebrows, passing a thimble. The man behind the bar pours with great care and breathes as if each breath were a decision.

The Ripe One was a tarpaulin over the pit's first windlass the week after the find, when Vexa's crew had nine thousand crowns in silver and nowhere to drink to it, and it was named the way every Glasser names a lucky thing: a ripe one. It has been rebuilt four times, once after a greywind took it to Fort Mercy, and it has never once been shut on a digging day. Ferrow Gant keeps it as he keeps the grave, carefully, and the Cut trusts his measure the way it trusts nothing else in the Ashenreach: his thimble is a real thimble, brass, a quartermaster's issue, and it is the same thimble every time.

The spirit is made from stone pears. Nobody at the Cut will say how, because the how is a cutter called Pim who learned it from a Fallowden still-man and died of it in 3009, and the recipe went into a crew-book with his name on it. It is clear, cold, tastes of nothing, and then of pears, and then of the back of your own head.

### Menu
- Stone-pear spirit, 2 cp a thimble. "The Harvest." Clear. You will not taste it until you have swallowed.
- "Mercy", 5 cp a thimble: the spirit aged a season in a cask that came out of the glass with the cider still in it. Called Mercy because it is kinder than the Harvest, and because of where the cask was dug.
- Hot bone-broth, 1 cp the bowl, with a hard biscuit. The bones are goat. Mostly goat. Ferrow says goat.
- Well-water, 2 cp the bucket, carried from Vexa's well at the fort, which charges one. "You're paying for the seven miles, friend, not the water."
- Goat cheese from Ambercote, 3 cp a wedge, when Kettle's cousins have been by. Grey rind, white inside, better than it has any right to be.
- A place on the planks, 1 cp the night, your own blanket. The glass underneath is warm. Do not ask why.
- A dig-chart, 2 sp, "accuracy not warranted". Ferrow's own are good; the ones in Skelt's hand are not, and Ferrow will tell you which is which for free.
- A cutter's hire, 5 sp a day, 7 for the cold ledge. Breakers 3 sp. "That's what a man's worth who'll go down a hole. Ask your captain what she pays you to stand on a wall."
- The Ripe One lottery, 1 cp a ticket: pick what the next pit brings up, from the board. Pays at the chalked odds, in silver, on the night.

### Who is here
Ferrow, pouring. Whichever crew is off the rope, arguing splits. A Concord packer with his own cup who drinks water and watches the board. Two crews who sit together, drink the broth and never the spirit, and sing low after the lamps are trimmed. Kettle Brindlewake's cousins twice a month, buying slag and selling salt and saying, as far as anyone can tell, nothing at all. Skelt, on a bad night, telling the chart story again.

### Rumours (heard over the thimble)
- The lottery is rigged. Ferrow knows what the pit will bring up because he's been down it.
- The planks are warm because there's a fire under the glass that's been burning since Cinders.
- The quartermaster's sash on the nail is Old Tally's, and Ferrow was in the 11th Foot.
- Mercy is made with water from the Cold Seam, which is why it's cold going down.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False about the pit. True in a way nobody has guessed: the lottery is not rigged but it is laundered. Vexa pays the Cut's splits through it. A crew's split is written as "winnings", paid out in the Concord silver the factor pays Vexa for the humming crates, and nobody at Gutter Market asks why a Glasser has Sevenhaven coin, because he won it. Tam Sixfingers has started to wonder. None of this coin is the strongbox's (that box is unopened at Fort Mercy). 2. False, but the glass is warm because the Nine Road culvert under the pit carries the ward's leak, which is warm the way the Red Seam is warm; the planks are the warmest sleep in the Ashenreach and nobody sleeps well on them. Roll on Sleeper Dreams for a Hollowborn who does. 3. True, both halves. See [[Ferrow Gant]]. 4. False. It is cold going down because Pim knew his trade.
:::

### Things to do
Buy a thimble and have it measured in front of you. Buy a lottery ticket on SOMETHING THAT HUMS and watch the Concord packer write something down. Hire a cutter for the Rent and discover that the good ones ask who is paying before they ask where. Ask for Ferrow's own chart of the glass south of the Cut and be told it is not for sale, and then be shown it, if you have been kind about the grave. Sit up with the two quiet crews and be offered bitter tea.

:::gm
**Secret.** The lottery is the Cut's wage-book. Ferrow keeps it honest in the only way he can: the odds on the board are real and the pit really does pay them, but under the board, in a crew-book, each crew's split for the month is entered as "won", and Vexa's Concord silver goes out across the bar as winnings. Ferrow hates it and does it, because the alternative is Vexa paying crews in coin that stinks of the factor and the Low Quay asking questions, and the Cut cannot afford questions. A PC who reads the crew-book (Ferrow keeps it under the sash; DC 13 Sleight of Hand or a long enough conversation about the 11th) has the Glassers' whole income from the Concord in a column, and a date in Reaping when the factor's payments doubled: the month Pennamund paid Vexa to receive a wagon.

The two quiet crews are Sleepless. Their tea is the Widow's.
:::

== GM notes ==

A tavern for a mining camp: warm floor, cold spirit, every coin honest and every coin dirty. Use it to make the Glassers people before Vexa makes them an obstacle (session 2), and as the party's bed on the road east (session 6, and the column's first night in session 8 if the GM wants a last drink). The joke of the place is the board of odds, which is Spoons's book with a shovel; let a player who has met Spoons notice.
