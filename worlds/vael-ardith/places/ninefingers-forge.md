> Heat reaches you before the door does. Inside it is dark and orange, a long low shed with a roof of black beams furred with a decade of soot, and the hammer coming down in a rhythm so even you can set your breath by it. A bucket by the door is full of nails, a heap of horseshoes hangs from a nail, and on the far wall, where other shops keep a sign, there is a single iron lantern-cage with no flame in it, hung on a hook, exactly the size of the one on the hill. An orc in a scorched apron works the bellows without looking up. One eye is black and steady. The other is the colour of boiled milk, and seems to be looking at something behind you.

The first shop on Ford Street was a forge, because the first thing a town needs when it has come back to a burnt site is nails. Hurkul Ninefingers came east in 3003 with a cart of anvils that had belonged to the Iron Tithe, the company whose lantern went dark in the first hour at Cinders. He had not been on the field. He had been two days behind it with the company's forge-wagon, mending axles, and he has not forgiven himself the axles. He set up at the West Gate because it was where the last carts came in and where a man could hear, from the other end of the street, whether the hammer had stopped.

He is the company's smith by habit, not by contract: nobody has paid the Ninth for three years, so nobody has paid him, and he has kept every horse in the garrison shod and every spear pointed anyway and put it on a slate the size of a door. Fennick Bottlebrook comes by once a week to apologise to the slate. Hurkul works under a Mothers' writ, because an orc outside a company needs one, and has opinions about that too, none of them out loud.

On Ironday, at dusk, he walks across to the anvil under the tin roof on Temple Row and says Grum's words over it: "Hold. Hold. Hold, you rusty bastards." That is all of them. It is considered sufficient. [[Grum]] has never been known to object.

### Prices
- Re-point a spear, 2 cp. Sharpen a blade, 1 cp. Re-haft, 1 sp.
- Horseshoes, 4 cp a shoe, fitted. A horse shod all round in 1 sp 6 cp. A mule, the same, with a remark.
- Mend mail, 5 sp a hand's-breadth. "Not a hand's-breadth of mine. Yours."
- Glass-on: set a salvaged shard of glass along a blade's edge, 1 gp. It holds. It will not take an edge again. He charges extra to say that three times.
- A plain sword, 8 gp. 12 gp for one with a story: bring your own story if you do not like his.
- Grumbler cages, 3 cp each: the iron collar and plug-seat that hold the clay shell together, to [[Tick]]'s pattern. Fennick has asked him to stop. He has asked Fennick to stop asking.
- Nails by the handful, 1 cp. Charm-nails, 2 cp: a nail with the head struck flat as a noose-knot, for soldiers to tie their [[The Hanged Corporal's Noose-Knot Charm|charm]] round. He takes the nails back from [[Grum]]'s offering bucket on Ironday and sells them on, and calls that the economy.
- Cut a fused lump of coin free of its glass, 1 sp, with a chisel the width of a coin and a long silence.
- Buys scrap at 1 cp the pound. [[Gutter Market]] sells it at 2. He knows exactly what that makes him and says so.

### Who is here
[[Hurkul Ninefingers]]. His apprentice, a Smokes boy of fifteen called Pim Gorse, who holds things and is not allowed to hit them yet. [[Skarra]] on Restday evenings with a pipe she has been told not to light, to argue about fuses. [[Ismere once-Fenn]], who was Iron Tithe and has a seat by the water-trough with her own cup. Soldiers waiting on a horse. Now and then a Glasser with something wrapped in sacking that he has not told the smith the whole truth about.

### Rumours
- Hurkul has ten fingers and is hiding the tenth on purpose.
- The iron cage on his wall is a spare for the lantern, in case the real one is stolen.
- He will not shoe a horse that has been to the Rent.
- He melted down the Iron Tithe's lantern himself, the night he came, and cast the pieces into nails.
- He was a farrier in the old Emperor's stables and has not slept since.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False, and the first thing he will show you. 2. False. It is a pattern: the ninth iron lantern for the Field of Lanterns on Corvel Rise, where eight poles hold eight copies and a ninth pole stands empty. He forged the eight in 3003. He kept the pattern for the ninth, and has not forged it. 3. True: the shoes come off a horse that has stood at the Rent by themselves, and he does not like the way they fall. 4. False. He did not retrieve the lantern; no one did. The nails are from the Iron Tithe's forge-wagon, which is why every one in the bucket rings slightly flat. 5. Half true: he was at the Palace barracks forge in Caer Ardith in 2981, a farrier-smith to the Iron Guard, and the sleeping is a different matter.
:::

### Things to do
Get a horse shod. Haggle over a sword and lose in the same sentence. Hand the smith a glass-fused blade and watch him weigh whether it is a blade or a grave-marker. Ask what the iron cage on the wall is for. Be told to stop touching the anvil. Join the Ironday words, if you are asked, and say the third "hold" like you mean it. Take a fused lump of coin to be cut free (see [[The Baker's Hoard]]).

:::gm
Hurkul will not forge the ninth lantern for the Field until someone who knows the day comes and says so; he does not know what that means either, but the pattern has hung on the wall for eight years and he will know it when he hears it. If the Reseal ending is reached, the cage is the first thing he lifts off the wall: he forges the ninth lantern in a night and sets it on the pole himself, and does not charge. In Use or Crack, the cage stays on the wall.

His secret is Vosk. On the evening of 14 Frostcall 2981 the Iron Guard walked out of Caer Ardith, and a number of them stopped at the Palace barracks forge for their gear. One handed Hurkul a sword to be put right, a younger Vosk, and said: "No notches. Never used." Hurkul checked. There were no notches. He has known for thirty years what the Second Blade of the Ninth Lantern did not do at the Gilded Gallery door, and every Ironday, when Vosk comes to have a dagger edged that does not need it, neither of them says anything about it.

He will not serve [[Varra Nine-Knuckles]] without the Mothers' nod, because the writ he works under is the Mothers' and an exile is outside it. He will sell to her friends. He will "forget" a pair of her boot-nails on the bench for a week if she asks the right way. She cannot get the nod, and he knows exactly why.
:::

== GM notes ==

Hurkul's voice: deadpan, a smith's economy, three words where a clerk would use thirty. When angry he counts aloud, one to nine, and then says nothing; "and the tenth is for you" is the thing he does not say, and the table should be allowed to supply it. He is fond of the Ninth in the way you are fond of a roof that leaks. He does not talk about the Iron Tithe. The cage on the wall is a good slow clue for a table that has not yet noticed the Field of Lanterns.
