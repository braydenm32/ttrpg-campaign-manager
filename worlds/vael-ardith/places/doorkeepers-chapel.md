> A doorway of black stone, taller than a man, standing at the end of the lane with the chapel built round it afterwards. There is no door in it, and through it you can see the chapel's back wall, plain grey, four paces off, and yet air moves through the opening steadily, out, as if from somewhere large. Inside, benches, a cold slab, a lamp, and shelves of narrow ledgers. Three figures in grey with the heads of crows, or masks of them, or something between, are washing a body with the unhurried competence of people who have done it ten thousand times. One of them is humming.

[[Hathe]] kept the doors between living and dead, and died at Cinders, and since then the dead do not always pass. The [[Ravelers]] kept his doors in life and keep them in death: a dead god's priests, doing a job that has got harder. The Chapel is the Rook-Court's house in Cinderford, at the end of Temple Row where Tanners' Lane runs into the Smokes. Every body in the March comes here before it goes into the ground on Corvel Rise, and every one that still has breath when a Raveler reaches it has its last words taken down.

The Doorway is the old lintel of the Cinderford chapel of Hathe, thrown clear of the chapel that melted into the Barrow and the only thing that survived the battle standing. It was found upright in the ash with the ground glassed round it. The Ravelers say it is still a door. They do not say to where.

### What is here
- **The Doorway.** The draught through it is cold and smells faintly of pears.
- **The slab**, where the dead are washed, and where the drowned are laid when the ford gives them back. Lately the Rook-Court keeps someone sitting up with them.
- **This year's book** of the Ledger of Last Words, on a lectern by the slab: every dying sentence heard by a Raveler in the March since Hollowfrost, in order. The other thirty-nine volumes are in the bone-library at [[Corvel Rise]]. Not for sale. Not for reading, except by the Court.
- **The Court's room** upstairs, where [[Mother Corvane]]'s seat is kept empty, with a pot of ink at its elbow; she has not come down from the Rise in a year. The Door-Key is not kept here: the Chapel holds the lintel, the slab and the current book.

### Who is here
[[Skrell Already-Gone|Skrell]], who runs Mother Corvane's errands and her bodies. [[Quorl Many-Words]], when the company has a body, which is often. Three or four Court priests at any time, and crows, actual crows, on the roof, who are either pets, kin or colleagues depending on which Raveler you ask.

### Rumours
- The Doorway leads to where Hathe is.
- Mother Corvane has already read the message in the last words and will not say.
- Hathe's own key is kept somewhere on the Rise and opens the Doorway.
- A body laid on the slab three days will speak once more.
- The Ravelers charge the dead for burial and collect in the next life.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. The Ravelers do not deny it. 2. Nearly true: she holds four of the five words. 3. It is on the Rise. What it opens is the question. 4. True for some. 5. A joke. Theirs.
:::

### Things to do
Bring a body. Hear a last word taken down and witness the signature. Ask what the Doorway is for and be told, "Later." Stand in the draught and smell the pears. Notice, if you are Hollowborn, the queue.

:::gm
Dannic Rook drowned on the night of 7 Ashfall and was never buried: the current took him into the Glass Barrow, and Nan Crupp and the ford-watch have seen something standing in the shallows since. When the ford-watch hook him out at noon on the 14th, the Ravelers will lay him on this slab, and he will get up. The Ravelers have not tried to stop whatever is in the ford, because you do not stop a man looking for a door. See [[The Drowned Man]]. The Rook-Court's clock ticks here. Corvane has guessed "the lamp is the lock" and is waiting for the last words that will tell her which lamp.
:::

== GM notes ==

Ravelers are funny; let them be. Deadpan, literal, fond of the living in the way of people who will tidy up after them. Corvane is the exception: she is very old, very tired, and speaks only when she has something to say, which makes the PCs lean in.
