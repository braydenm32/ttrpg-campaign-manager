> A long low shed with a stove at one end and forty pallets in two rows, most of them full of sleeping kiln-hands, Glassers, pilgrims who could not afford Temple Row, and one orc with her boots on. The air is thick and warm and smells of bodies and wet ash. At the stove a stout woman with one clouded eye is writing in a book by candlelight, and without looking up says: "Nine for the bed, one for the blanket, and if you dream, you tell me what, that's the house rule."

The Ninepenny is the cheapest roof in Cinderford and the only one that asks about your dreams. [[Auntie Brace]] was born in the pear country, lost a husband and both sons at Cinders, and took the grey eye herself that night, a grown woman waking with one eye gone to milk. She has kept the doss-house since because, she says, somebody has to count the sleepers. For the prices round the town, see [[What Things Cost in the Ashenreach]]. Her book records, beside every lodger's name, what they dreamed. Most nights it is nothing, or the usual. Three nights either side of a full Ember the entries start to match.

### What is here
- Forty pallets, a stove, a bucket.
- **The room with the door**, at the back, 3 sp, for anyone who needs to be alone, which Brace decides.
- **The dream-book**, nine years of it, on the shelf above the stove.

### Who is here
Brace. Whoever is poor tonight. A Sleepless or two, always, sleeping with their eyes open, which is the sign.

### Rumours
- Brace is the Dreaming Widow.
- The dream-book is what the Widow preaches from.
- Sleep three nights at the Ninepenny at red moon and you wake up Hollowborn.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False. She has been asked. She laughed until she coughed. 2. True. Brace lends it. She does not approve, and lends it anyway. 3. False. You wake up having dreamed their dream, which is worse.
:::

### Things to do
Sleep cheap. Read the dream-book over Brace's shoulder, and see the same words in forty hands. Wake at the third bell to find half the room sitting up, facing east, eyes open, breathing together.

:::gm
[[Tibbet Marlowe]] slept here a year, at about seven by the calendar, when he had outgrown his grandmother's boxroom; his dreams are in the book. The dream-book is the best evidence in town of what [[The Hollowborn]] dream, and a quiet way to feed the players the Sleeper Dreams table. The matching entries say, in various words, a stone door, a lamp, a hand on the latch from inside. At the session 3 red moon the Ninepenny empties onto the plain at midnight, every sleeper walking.
:::
