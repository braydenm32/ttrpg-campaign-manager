> Low room, long table, a fire that is mostly ash. Forty soldiers in various states of off-duty. On the wall behind the bar hangs a lantern with no flame in it, draped in black, and under it a slate the size of a door, chalked edge to edge in columns of names and numbers. Beside it, on a shelf, a stuffed crow wearing a corporal's hat. By the bar, nailed up where the drinkers queue, a wanted bill with a bad drawing of a man's face, signed at the bottom in a neat clerk's hand: A.M. The woman behind the bar has one arm and uses it to pour, point, and stop fights, apparently at once. Somebody is singing the one about the Corporal. Somebody else is betting against the singer finishing it.

The Candle was the first building rebuilt after the battle, before the Keep had a roof, because the surviving cook of the Seventh, who ran the Ninth's kitchen in its first winters, decided her people needed somewhere to drink more than somewhere to sleep. She was right. Hesper Hennet, whom everyone calls [[Mother Hennet]], lost her arm at Cinders, gave the Keep's kitchen to Brannoc when she did, and never wanted it back. The dark lantern over the bar is the Ruddy Hundred's, the Seventh Company on the wall that night; she cooked for them before. The company's story says she lit the ninth lantern; she says it was the soup that did it.

The slate is the Candle's famous thing. Every soldier who has ever drunk here is chalked on it, the dead included, and nothing on it is ever rubbed out; when a column fills Hennet copies it into a book under the bar and starts the column again beneath the old one, smaller. The company's pay has been late so often that the slate is, in effect, the only honest account of what the Ninth Lantern is owed, and Hennet knows it. When the Concord's clerks came asking to see the book she told them the price was an arm.

The crow in the corporal's hat was alive once and belonged to the Seventh's sergeant-major; it outlived him by six years and died on the bar, and Hennet had it stuffed because, she says, it was the only one in the company who never ran a tab. It faces the door. The wanted bill beside it went up on the 13th, for [[Dannic Rook]], deserter, with a reward of ten silver nobody has claimed and the initials of a clerk nobody can place. By the door, at shoulder height, is the soldiers' private shrine: a single iron nail in the wall with a loop of rope hung from it, which every soldier who comes in touches and none will explain. It is the Corporal's nail.

### Menu
- Small beer, 1 cp a jack. The water tastes of ash; the beer tastes of ash and hops.
- Fallowden red, 4 cp a cup, 2 sp the jug, "and don't ask what year".
- Grimmark whey-spirit, 6 cp a thimble. Called "the Returned" because you come back without your shadow.
- Four-and-six stew, 3 cp a bowl, with bread: fish, mostly, and Hennet will not say what else. It is worth four shillings and sixpence, she says, and the difference is on the slate. Better than the Keep's.
- Goat, when there is goat, 8 cp.
- Pear brandy, 1 sp a thimble, from the last cask of the pear country. Thirty-one measures left; Hennet counts aloud.
- A bed in the loft, 1 sp, two to a pallet, three on a payday.

### Who is here
Old [[Halloran Pike]] drinks free by the fire, and tells the lantern's legend wrong on purpose; he is no relation to Dunny at the bell-house, whatever the boy tells you. [[Mirrel Vosk]] at the end of the long table most nights, holding court on the deaths of recruits. [[Spoons Aldane]] running the book on anything. [[Garrick Sallow]], rarely, buying rounds he cannot afford and watching who drinks them. The Captain never; her credit is in the book like everyone's. Veterans with the Cinders look. New recruits learning the songs.

### Rumours (see Last Candle Rumours)
- Mother Hennet is owed more by the company than the Concord is.
- The crow in the hat talks at red moon.
- The dark lantern over the bar lit itself the night of Cinders, once, for a breath.
- Spoons has a side-book on who dies next.
- The pear brandy is the Ember's colour because the cask was in the orchard that night.

### Things to do
Drink on tick. Hear the contract explained by a drunk. Learn the Corporal's song and its forty verses. Be challenged by Vosk to say something funny about your own death. Read the slate and find a name you did not expect: Dannic Rook's, with a chalk line through it, the only mark of its kind on the whole wall. Ask her who did that and watch her face. Read the wanted bill, and wonder who A.M. is and why a Regency clerk is posting rewards for a mercenary's deserter.

:::gm
Hennet crossed out Dannic's name herself on 5 Ashfall, when he paid his tab in full: in new Regency silver, this year's Caer Ardith mint, the first new Regency coin anyone in the Ashenreach had seen in two years. She still has it in a cup under the bar. The wanted bill is the handout [[Wanted: Dannic Rook]], posted by [[Ansel Mote]] in the Assessor's name and set by Ottun Fray on Temple Row, with Widow Pruitt paying the printer on the 13th; Pell has not arrived yet and does not know it exists. The coin is the first clue of session 2 lying in plain sight in session 1.
:::

== GM notes ==

Mother Hennet's voice: dry, Grimmark, never raises it. She calls everyone by their tab total ("Evening, four-and-six"). She was in the Seventh and knows exactly how a company dies; she is watching the Ninth for the signs and will say so to a PC she likes, once. She knew old Halloran Pike for the deserter of the legend the night he signed on, and has never said; he drinks free. When Pike dies (session 5) she shuts the Candle for the night, the first time it has ever closed.
