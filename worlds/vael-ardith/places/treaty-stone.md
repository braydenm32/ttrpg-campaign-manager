> The road's last stake has a scrap of red on it, and beyond it the plain is unmarked to the sky except for one thing: a stone the size of a hay-wain, standing alone, sheathed in dark glass that has run down its sides and set in ropes. Around its foot, trodden into the ash, a ring of offerings: bootlaces, ribbons, badges, a wedding ring on a nail, a tin plate with a name scratched on it. The glass is scratched too, everywhere a hand can reach, and the scratches are words.

The stone was a waymark on the old road, a Lithic figure worn to a lump before the empire was born. On the night of the battle the fire came over it and sheathed it in glass. Two months later, because it was the only thing standing between the armies' camps that everyone could see, the signatories' envoys met at it, put a drumhead on top, and signed [[The Treaty of Ashes]] on it in the rain. The Treaty Bell was rung. The envoys went home. The companies stayed.

The signatures are still there, on the glass under the drumhead's rust-ring: nine names scratched with a dagger-point, the Regency's, the Concord's, Iskaveth's, the Holds', Sael's, the Mothers', three claimants nobody remembers, and under them the eight garrison companies named in the schedule, with the Ninth added at the end in a smaller hand, as an afterthought, at the first renewal on 14 Ashfall 3003.

On the lee side, where the wind is least, a niche cut square into the Lithic stone is shut by an iron grille with two locks. Behind it, in a waxed-oak case, lies the Original Treaty. One key is the Captain's and one is the Reeve's; the Assessor has right of inspection, which no Assessor has yet exercised. Everything below the signatures is graffiti. PAY US, a hundred times. Names of the dead. Prayers. A very good drawing of the Regent-Chancellor. Someone has been scratching the word LIAR under the Regency's signature once a week for two years, and someone else has been polishing it out.

### Who comes here
Pilgrims: widows of the eight companies, who walk out from Fallowden and further to leave a thing and go home. Soldiers of the Ninth, on patrol, who touch the ninth mark and turn round. Iskavethi from the mission, who leave lamps. Vandals, which is to say anyone with a grievance and a knife. Ravelers, who keep an eye on the ring of offerings and take nothing. On the Treaty's anniversary, 20 Frostcall, everyone.

### Rumours
- The stone weeps at red moon.
- There is a tenth signature, under the glass, that was there before the treaty.
- The Regency's envoy signed with a false name.
- If you scratch your name on the stone you will die in the March.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False; the Orchard does. 2. True; see below. 3. True. He was Quiet Office. 4. Everyone has. Everyone will.
:::

### Things to do
Walk the patrol and touch the mark. Read the graffiti for news; it is faster than the post-stair. Find your own name, if you were at Cinders. Leave something. Look under the drumhead's rust-ring with a good light and see that the glass is thinner there, and that there is writing in the stone beneath it, older than the glass.

:::gm
Under the glass the Lithic waymark bears the same glyph-line as the Cold Stair in the Barrow and the Ninth Stone: *what we buried is not at rest*. More useful in play: a Raveler witness at the signing scratched the treaty's one honest clause into the glass under the drumhead, where the envoys could not see it, and it reads *paid by the signatories, or by whosoever shall hold their debt*. That clause is in [[The Original Treaty]] and in no public copy, and it is why the Concord's purchase of the Regency's debt makes it, in law, the garrison's new master. Aelith Sorn has read it. Tollamund drafted it, by letter from Sevenhaven, in 3002.

The tenth signature is older than the Treaty and older than the glass: a plain hand-print in the Lithic stone, the Last Wardens' mark, which also signs page seven of the Original Treaty ("the keeping of the Ninth Ward"), the page the Concord's lawyers have not yet noticed is the one that matters.
:::
