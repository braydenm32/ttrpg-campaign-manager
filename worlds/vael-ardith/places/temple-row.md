> The lane curves along the hill's foot and every third door is a god's. Most are the size of a cowshed. A hearth with a kettle always on it; an anvil under a tin roof with a bucket of nails for offerings; a stone the size of a cart, undressed; a bucket of salt water; a dead tree with ribbons. At the far end a doorway of black stone with no door in it, and a crow-headed figure in a grey robe sweeping the step. Halfway along, a bigger building with a red door and lamps lit in daylight, and the sound of a lute.

Temple Row grew because soldiers from eight companies, then one, wanted somewhere to swear at their gods, and Cinderford obliged. Nothing here is grand. Everything is used.

### The shrines
- **Maelis**: the hearth-house, a kettle, [[Mother Brisk]], soup for anyone. The most visited. The town's birth-rolls are kept here too, in a chest under the kettle-shelf; anyone who stirs the soup may read them.
- **Grum**: the anvil under the tin roof. No priest; the company's farrier says the words. Offerings are nails and curses.
- **Corvex**: a lectern and a ledger, kept by [[Brother Audit]], Corvex's lay brother (not the Concord's, whatever the Quay says), who writes down what you pray for and reads it back on Coinday.
- **Sefra**: a bucket of salt water carried from Sevenhaven in 3003 and topped up with tears, allegedly.
- **Orvanne**: a dead tree with ribbons and an antler nailed to its trunk. Nobody tends it. It is always tidy.
- **Tharos**: an undressed stone the surface-dwarf masons brought in. The Returned do not go near it.
- **Aurelan**: a proper little chapel with a sunburst door, locked since 3002. The Reeve keeps the key and opens it alone on Crownfast, with one candle.
- [[Isaurana]]: [[The Velvet Ledger]], the red-doored house with the lute, which is a temple whatever the Reeve says.
- [[Hathe]]: [[The Doorkeeper's Chapel]], the black doorway, the Ravelers.
- [[The Hanged Corporal]]: [[The Corporal's Gibbet]], a gallows post at the lane's end with flowers at its foot, which nobody put up and nobody will take down.
- [[The Unburnt Saint]]: a lamp in a niche in the hill wall, lit every night by nobody anyone has seen.

### The trades
The Row is not all gods. Two doors in it sell candles and ink.
- **Gannet's Tallow & Wicks**: Gannet is deaf in one ear, so the price depends on which side of him you stand; a clerk lodges upstairs and keeps his window shut.
- **[[Ottun Fray]], jobbing printer**: handbills, wanted bills, banns, a broadside on the price of coal. Fray set the wanted bill for Dannic Rook.

### Who is here
[[Mother Brisk]]. [[Brother Audit]]. Gannet and his lodger; [[Ottun Fray]], ink to the elbows. The Ravelers under [[Mother Corvane]]. [[Sorrel Vey]] and her house. Soldiers at all hours, drunk and sober. Pilgrims on their way to the Treaty Stone, looking for a bed.

### Rumours
- The Aurelan chapel is locked because the Emperor's ghost is in it.
- Brother Audit's ledger of prayers is read by the Quiet Office.
- The lamp in the Saint's niche is lit by Sister Seraphine herself, walking in from the mission every dusk.
- Pray at the Gibbet and your sergeant will take the punishment meant for you.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False. It is locked because the Reeve took its plate to pay for the bridge. 2. False. By Tollamund. 3. False. It is lit by a kiln-girl from the Smokes whose brother came home from Halcyra. 4. It has happened twice.
:::

### Things to do
Eat Mother Brisk's soup. Swear at Grum. Have a prayer written down and regret it. Tie a ribbon on the dead tree and come back next day to find it gone and a stone pear in its place ([[Stone Pears]]). Read the birth-rolls for 3002 and find six names dated the night of Cinders, each with a column of heights in the margin, measured every red moon, that climbs twice as fast as any other on the page and then stops. Knock on the red door. Stand in the black doorway and feel the draught that has no source.

:::gm
Every priest on the Row except Brisk and the Ravelers is nudging, gently, toward the crack being opened; they do not know why they think it would be merciful, and the GM does. The dead tree is a cutting from the Weeping Orchard and is not entirely dead. Gannet's lodger is [[Ansel Mote]]; Fray was paid for the wanted bill on the 13th by a mousy little clerk with a cold, who paid in new silver and asked for the type to be broken up afterwards (Pruitt carried the money; Mote stood in the doorway). The six names in the birth-rolls are the Cinders-born ([[Why did the Cinders-born grow up so fast?]]); Tick is the fourth. Fray tells anyone buying ink that the Assessor's Office pays in advance.
:::
