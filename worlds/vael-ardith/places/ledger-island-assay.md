> A square stone room on the island's landward edge, floored in slate, every flag scored with a weight and a date. Along the walls, scales: brass, bronze, one of black Lithic stone taller than a man, with chains instead of cords. In the centre, under a lamp a Vesh is trimming, a Shellback so old his shell has gone the grey of a wet road, carved line by line with figures worn smooth, his neck extended a full foot toward a silver coin on a pan, and not moving. On the back wall a slate the width of the room, chalked in a clerk's hand with names and sums, and at the bottom, underlined twice: THE NINTH LANTERN (COMPANY, LIVING): 4,800 CROWNS: NOT FOR SALE.

The Assay is the oldest shop on Ledger Island and the Long Counting-House was built round it, the way a town is built round a well. Veramund & Heirs set a true weight here in 1360, ten years off the boats from the Ness, and have set it every morning since; the Concord's single grain-measure, written into [[The First Concord]], was weighed on the stone scale in the corner and the clerks who signed the seven copies came here first to have their seals checked. Every honest weight on the continent is a copy of a copy of the one on the slate under Grandfather Veramund's foot. He is the last of the Grandfathers who was alive when the Concord was signed, and he does not mention it, because mentioning it would be hurrying.

The front of the house weighs. The back of the house sells debt. This is not a secret; it is on the wall, in chalk, at noon. A soldier's paper, a dead company's, a town's, a province's: Concord law says debt is property and property has a price, and the Assay sets the price the way it sets a weight, publicly and without apology. Mistress Hebbe, who keeps the toll at [[Cinderford]]'s bridge and tests every coin by its ring, learned the ring here as a girl, from a Veramund clerk who tapped coins on the Lithic scale and made her say which note was wrong.

### Stock
- A true weight, Concord standard, stamped with the Veramund tally, 1 sp for the pound, 3 sp for the set. Every honest shop in Sevenhaven has one and the dishonest ones have two.
- Coin assayed, 2 cp the piece, 1 sp the purse: rung on the stone, weighed, bitten by a clerk whose teeth are insured, and handed back with a verdict in chalk on the pan. "New Regency silver, this year's date: good, and where did you get it?" "New Sevenhaven coin, Tollamund house-mark: good, and so is the question."
- A note of hand drawn on Veramund & Heirs, good at any Concord house, 2 in the hundred.
- **Debt, on the slate at noon.** A living soldier's pay-paper, forty in the hundred, which is the Ardmere rate and has not moved since 2995 ([[The Ardmere Receipts]]). A dead soldier's, by the bundle, cheaper, "under Article Nine": the Concord's clause that passes the debts of the dead to the land they died on, which makes a dead company's paper a claim on a field. The Eleventh Foot (dead at Cinders): 1,100 crowns the bundle, on the slate since 3003, unsold, and the clerk becomes very busy if asked who holds the field. A town's rebuilding loan: Ardmere's, in three lots. The Regency's garrison share for the March of Cinders: "held", no price.
- Not on the slate: the Ninth Lantern's note, which is written at the bottom with a sum and the words *not for sale*, and has been since Thawmere 3009, and the sum was rubbed out and chalked higher in Ashfall.

### Who is here
Grandfather Veramund, at the scale, for as long as it takes. Three clerks of the house, humans, who speak for him at human speed and are corrected, slowly, when they get a figure wrong. A Vesh lamp-trimmer who keeps the light the coins are read by and nothing else. Buyers at noon: factors, tallymen, Shellback cousins with satchels, a Regency agent pretending to be a wool-merchant. Soldiers, rarely, who have come to see what their own paper is worth and leave quieter. On the first Coinday of each month, [[Cousin Pennamund]]'s letters from the March, which Veramund reads before the Table does.

### Rumours
- The slate's last line was chalked by Grandmother Tollamund's own hand, by letter, and nobody on the island dares wipe it.
- Veramund has a weight for a soul and does not use it.
- The Table bought the Ninth Lantern's Regency paper at forty in the hundred and has sold it on at ninety to someone who is not in the Concord.
- Article Nine has never been used, and the Table is waiting for the right field.
- The Grandfather can tell a coin's mint by the sound it makes falling.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True in spirit: the line is Tollamund's instruction, written to the Assay in Thawmere 3009 and renewed each quarter, and Veramund honours it because the Houses do not undercut one another in chalk. He thinks it is the strangest line on his wall. 2. False, and he will take a very long time to say so. 3. False. The Table holds it; the one buyer who has asked is the Church of the Unburnt Saint, through a Qerath factor, and was refused. 4. Half: it has been used once, in 3003, by Tollamund & Daughters, on the field at Cinders, and the slate's Eleventh Foot line is the proof: the bundle is on sale but the land the clause attaches it to is already hers. 5. True.
:::

### Things to do
Have a coin rung and learn in one breath what mint it came from and what that means. Watch a dead company's debt sold by the bundle to a man who writes the field's name in a book. Read the slate to the bottom and find your own company there, underlined, with a sum that is wrong by six hundred, and ask why, and be given a chair. Buy a true weight so that when the Concord's lawyers come to the Keep gate on 5 Leafrot someone in the room can weigh what they are owed. Ask Grandfather Veramund whether he has ever been to the sea, and be the first person in four hundred years to ask a Shellback that, and watch what it does to the room.

:::gm
The Assay is where the campaign's money becomes visible. The debt-slate makes the Concord's method plain in chalk: paper bought cheap from the desperate, Article Nine turning the dead into claims on land, and at the bottom the Ninth Lantern, the one debt in the Concord nobody may buy, because [[Grandmother Tollamund]] wants the thing it is secured on and not the money ([[The Concord Promissory Note]]). Veramund does not know what she wants. He knows she drafted the Treaty's pay clause in 3002, because the Assay witnessed her letter, and he knows that when a Shellback three hundred years old stops counting the interest and starts counting the days, something has gone wrong under the shell. He is the only Shellback alive who might say so to her face, and he is thinking about it, which at his pace means Longnight.

The Eleventh Foot's bundle is the thread to [[Old Tally's Coin]]: whoever buys it, under Article Nine, buys a claim on the dead company's share of the field at Cinders, and the Glassers' find of 3008 was that company's chest. Tollamund holds the land; the paper has sat unsold for eight years because every buyer who asks about the field is told, slowly, who owns it, and goes away.

A party that shows Veramund the loan-chit from the Fort Mercy strongbox (session 2) gets the one thing nobody else will give them: a Shellback's reading of the third line, aloud, at a Shellback's pace, ending with the words "That is lawful. ... I did not say ... it was right."
:::

== GM notes ==

Veramund is Tollamund's mirror: as old, as slow, as honest, and curious where she is hungry. Play his silences as weighing, not menace. The clerks speak for him until a figure matters, and then the room waits for him. The joke of the shop is that the most honest room in Vael Ardith sells human debt by the pound and sees no contradiction, and the players should be allowed to be angry at a slate. "Not for sale" is the line to land: the players' company, the one thing in the Concord nobody may buy, because somebody already wants it more than money.
