> A door in a grey front like any other on Ford Street, except that the stone round it is dark with damp and a thin plume of steam drifts from a chimney behind it, bending toward the hill. Inside, the warmth is the first surprise, and the smell is the second: orange-blossom water and wet slate and, faintly, lamp-oil. A narrow hall tiled in blue and white. Along it, ten curtained alcoves with names chalked above them in Iskavethi script, and at the end of it a counter with two women behind it, dark-haired, sharp-boned, one older and one a little younger, who look up together and say, "Boots, please." There is a rack of boots already. Nobody has ever got past them with theirs on.

The Salt Lake is named for the lake below Halcyra, where in the old days of Iskaveth there was a bathhouse on every terrace, and where you went to be clean and to hear what the city said when it thought you were not listening. The two sisters who run it kept one such house on the lower terrace for twenty years. They left Halcyra in 3007, the year the Synod of Lamps declared Ilyra a living saint and the lamps went up on every roof, and the Mercy Houses began to take in whoever would come. The younger sister, Ysolde, had a brother who went in to one of them, and nothing was said, and they left. They came east by way of the sea, [[Vessary]] and the Cinder, and arrived in Cinderford in the winter with a cart of tile.

They found what a bathhouse needs: heat. The Smokes' three kilns run day and night, and their waste heat was going up the chimney at no charge. The sisters paid a kiln-master a barrel of Iskavethi wine and a quarter of a share, laid a stone culvert from the Smokes under South Street and Ford Street, and had a warm cistern by the next frost. It is the warmest building in the March. The Salt Lake smells of oranges in a town that smells of ash, and the town has taken it up the way it takes up any small mercy: with suspicion, followed by a slate.

### Prices
- A hot bath in the common cistern, 2 cp. A private tub with a curtain, 1 sp.
- Steam, 3 cp, in the long room, an hour at a time, as many as can sit.
- Laundry, 1 cp a shirt, back by evening, smelling of oranges.
- A shave, 1 cp. A haircut, 2 cp. Both with a hot cloth and no conversation.
- A splash of orange-blossom water in the bath, 1 cp.
- Salt-mint tea, 1 cp.
- **The Lamp**: an hour's quiet in a small round room with forty lamps lit round the walls and a cushion in the middle, 5 cp. Nobody speaks. The sisters will not say what it is for. It is full on Cinders night.

### Who is here
**Dalia Soran**, the older, forty-five, who runs the counter, the water and the accounts, and is the one you argue with. **Ysolde Soran**, thirty-eight, who tends the steam-room, sits in it for hours with a book she does not read, and is the one who answers. A girl of the Smokes who keeps the fires. The Reeve on Hearthday afternoons; [[Maud Severin]] on a Restday, after a bad week; [[Mirrel Vosk]] on Tideday, the only orc in the Ninth who bathes, and who does it in the private tub with the curtain shut and the Ember's phase written on the door. Three clerks of [[Tollamund & Daughters]], together, on Hearthday at the second bell, in the three alcoves in a row. Kiln-hands on a Restday evening, in a line, coughing and grateful. [[Sorrel Vey]]'s two Iskavethi girls on Restday mornings, in a tight pair, who do not look at the sisters and are not looked at.

### Rumours
- The Reeve and Tollamund do their business in the steam, so that nothing is written.
- Everything said in the Salt Lake is heard, and the sisters sell it.
- The warmth comes up from the Glass Barrow, and one night you will hear singing in the pipes.
- No priest has ever got past the counter. One tried once, in a bath towel, and was removed with the towel.
- Ysolde has a brother in the Saint's Mercy House who she says is dead.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True, every Hearthday: the Reeve and the three clerks of Tollamund's house sit in adjacent alcoves with the curtains open and talk through the steam about the charter, and it is true that nothing is written. 2. False. They sell nothing. They remember everything, which is worse and cheaper. 3. False, but near: the culvert runs from the kilns, under the Smokes, and passes under the Widow's yard on the way. 4. True of the first half: the priest was Brother Audit, auditing the water, and the towel was returned. 5. Her brother **Tavan**, a lamp-maker, went into the Lower Mercy House on Halcyra's lake in 3007 "to rest". The roll says *seen again*. Nobody has seen him.
:::

### Things to do
Soak. Sweat. Be shaved by Dalia, who will tell you three things about yourself you did not know you had said. Sit in the Lamp and be quiet with forty flames. Watch the clerks of the counting-house sit in three alcoves in a row. Learn from a kiln-hand what the heat does to the chest. Ask Ysolde a question and wait for her to finish the page she is not reading.

:::gm
**What the sisters hear.** The stone culvert from the kilns passes under the Widow's yard on its way, and the steam-room's far wall is the culvert wall. When the Sleepless meet on red-moon nights, the chant comes up the pipe and the long room fills with it. The sisters have heard the Widow preach, in pieces, for three years. They know what the Sleepless want. They have told no one.

They have also heard, in the steam, the Reeve say to Perrin: *"Under seal, not until Leafrot."* They know the charter was signed and not sealed. They know [[Ansel Mote]] takes the steam on Wardday evenings and speaks to no one: he sits with his scarf on in the hottest room in the March, where nobody has ever worn a scarf, and coughs on the beat of the Treaty Bell. Ysolde thinks he is ill. Dalia thinks he is not. Ysolde keeps a slip of paper under a loose tile with the days he came, in case somebody someday asks the right question.

**Why they stay.** The Church of the Unburnt Saint has Mercy Houses in three cities. Ysolde believes her brother is alive in one, and Dalia believes he is not, and they have argued about it every day for four years while pouring each other's tea. If [[Seraphine Ashe]] or Brother Caddis tries the door, Dalia says "No priests," and Seraphine goes. If the party asks where Tavan is and means it, the Mercy House's takings-roll in Halcyra holds the answer; see [[Mother Casimira]].

**The Lamp** burns forty flames, and each was lit from the lamp in the Saint's niche on Temple Row on a different night, by Ysolde, who goes at dusk and takes nothing from the kiln-girl who tends it but a taper. It is her private rebuke, and the Saint's niche has never once gone dark in three years.
:::

== GM notes ==

Dalia is clipped and exact and treats every customer as a debtor of her time. Ysolde is quiet and warm and the better listener. The sisters never raise their voices and never lend a towel. Steam gives a clean mechanical hook: a PC who takes the steam on a Hearthday afternoon makes a DC 12 Perception check to hear a fragment of the charter talk, and a DC 15 to realise whose voice it is. In session 4 the sisters are the best source on Seraphine without being sentimental about it. In session 7 they are the Reeve's alibi, if she wants one, and will not give it.
