> Down a stair that smells of candle-snuff and river. A long low vault with brick ribs, the cellar of the oldest of the three Hall of Records buildings, and along one wall the stone goes green and damp and is older than the brick: the old river wall, with the Ardis on the other side of it. At the sixth bell a handbell rings upstairs and the clerks come down the stair like a tide going out, a hundred black coats gone green at the seams, and nobody speaks until they have a cup. Then everybody speaks. Behind the bar a broad grey woman with a Collector's seal on a chain round her neck, worn as a joke, pours Ardis white into cups without looking at them. In the corner, under a hook with a folded cloth on it, a thin old man in very clean black sits alone with a plate nobody has uncovered.

The Nine Generations is where [[Caer Ardith]]'s paper goes to die for the evening. The Hall of Records fills three streets and employs, by its own count, four thousand clerks, and at the sixth bell every one of them who can afford two coppers comes down the Cellar Stair. The tavern was a records vault once, which is why the ceiling is vaulted and the walls sweat, and a Collector's widow called Bettany Crake bought the lease in 2998 from a Council that had lost the deed and could not prove it owned the room. She has one rule and enforces it with a bucket: no paper on the tables after the sixth bell. The clerks, who spend eleven hours a day with paper, treat the rule as the holiest thing in the Regency, and a man who pulls out a note after the bell is told to drink it.

It is named for the Mallorys, the family that has kept the keys of the throne room in the Sun Palace for nine generations and changes the dust sheet on the Empty Throne every morning. The first Mallory drank here when it was still a vault and the vault-keeper owed him money. The ninth, [[Orrin Mallory]], drinks here every night because, he says, he cannot sleep in a house that is above the throne, and the Nine Generations is below it, more or less, across the water. The cloth folded on the hook over his table is yesterday's dust sheet. He brings a new one every evening. Nobody has asked him to stop.

### Menu
- Ardis white, 3 cp a cup. Thin, sharp, honest. The clerks call it "the Second Grade".
- Regency gin, 2 cp a thimble, and the thimble is a real thimble, a Treasury one, from a case that went missing in 3004.
- Small beer, 1 cp. For the boy clerks, who are paid in it.
- "The Council's dinner", 5 cp: whatever the Council did not finish at its sitting, carried down the hill by the kitchen boys in covered dishes. Good on Wardday, when the Treasury Seat dines in. Nothing on Restday.
- Eel pie from the river stalls, 3 cp. Coinday only.
- "The Examination", 1 sp: a plate of whatever is best in the house, served under a square of dust-sheet linen, which you lift yourself. A claimant's last supper. The clerks buy it for a man on the night before his hearing, his dismissal or his audit, and nobody buys it for themselves.
- A bed: none. "Go home. You have one. That is more than the garrison has."

### Who is here
Clerks, by the hundred, in grades. The Disbursing Office sits nearest the stair because it leaves first; the Records clerks nearest the fire because they are owed it; the Charters clerks by themselves. [[Edda Haverleigh]] of Martial Disbursement, Third Grade, at the end of the long table on the three nights a week she can afford it, not drinking much and listening a great deal. Orrin Mallory in his corner with his sheet. Vesh archivists, who come down at the sixth bell as the rest leave for home, and drink in the dark end. And the men who tip: quiet, well-shaved clerks in good coats who buy rounds for grades below their own and ask after nobody's health. The clerks know exactly who they are and call them "the Auditors" out of their hearing, and nobody has ever seen one write anything down.

### Rumours
- The floor hums if you put your ear to it after midnight.
- The Regency keeps the real Ninth Lantern pay in a strongroom under the Hall, and the clerks drink on it.
- The vintner's son from Fallowden, the claimant they examined in Reaping, drank here the night before they took him up to the Palace, and he said the floor was humming then.
- Mallory has every dust sheet his family ever took off the throne, nine generations of them, in a cellar under his house.
- The thin clerks who tip are Quiet Office, and they come to hear who is drunk enough to say the Regent-Chancellor's name without the title.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True, and the Vesh will not sit at that end. 2. False: there is no strongroom and the pay goes into "Account Deferred", which is a line in a ledger in the Chair's own office (see [[Edda Haverleigh]]). 3. True; Crake served him the Examination and he lifted the sheet and said "Can't you hear that?" to a room that could not. 4. True; see [[Orrin Mallory]]. 5. True. They are Examiners, the Office's listening rank, and they pay for the room's drink about one night in four. Crake knows, takes their coin, and waters nothing.

### Things to do
Drink with four thousand people who know where every form in the Regency goes and will tell you, after the second cup, that none of them goes anywhere. Buy Orrin Mallory the Examination and hear what the throne room sounds like at dawn. Ask the Disbursing Office table, carefully, what "to Account Deferred" means, and watch the table go quiet and a well-shaved man in a good coat turn round. Put your ear to the floor at midnight and hear a sound that is not the river. Be told by Bettany Crake, kindly, that your pay-chit is paper, and the rule is the rule, and she will hold it behind the bar till morning.

:::gm
The cellar's back wall is the old river wall of Caer Ardith, and across the water from it, closer than the clerks like to think, is the island and the Sun Palace, and under the Palace the First Ward, the Hearthstone (see [[The Nine Great Wards]]). The hum in the floor is the Ward: dulled by three centuries of court sorcery, "lifted" twice by [[Marius Vell]]'s workmen, and since Cinders audible to anyone who lies still and listens. The Vesh archivists hear it every night and have a rule about not looking at the wall.

Orrin Mallory drinks here because the hum is quieter on this side of the river than in the throne room, and because since the first of Ashfall the dust he has been shaking off the sheet each morning is grey, and fine, and smells of nothing he can name, and he has not told anyone except Crake, who told him to drink more. If the party comes to the capital after session 8, this is where they find both of the people who can prove what the Regency did: Edda, who has the account number, and Orrin, who has the ash.

The Examiners' standing instruction is to note anyone from the March of Cinders who asks about the garrison's pay. A party that asks loudly is followed home by a clerk with a cold. [[The Quiet Office]]'s clock may tick here, once, if they are careless.
:::

== GM notes ==

Bettany Crake is the best bar-keeper in the Regency and should sound it: broad, dry, unimpressed by rank, the one person in Caer Ardith who tells a Councillor's secretary to take his hat off. She knows the Auditors for what they are and treats them exactly like clerks, which is the only protection the room has. The joke of the place is that four thousand people who run an empire by paper have made one room where paper is forbidden, and defend it like a shrine; play it warm. The hum is the dread: let one player hear it first and the others not.
