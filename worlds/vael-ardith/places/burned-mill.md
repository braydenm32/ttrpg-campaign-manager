> The road has been nothing but stakes and rags for three miles when a shape comes up out of the grey: a stone building with no roof, its walls black to the height of a man and pale above where the fire did not reach, and beside it, impossibly, a tree. A pear tree, old and bent, in leaf, green leaf, with a bell hung on a post beside it and a stone bench under it. On the bench a woman in a man's coat is cutting a notch into the stone with a nail, and does not look up until you are close enough to count. "Four," she says. "And a mule. Water's in the butt. Sit down before the wind does it for you."

The Oddy mill ground the pear country's barley for a hundred and forty years, on a brook that came down off the eastern downs and is now a trench of ash. The night of Cinders the mill was full of people who had run east from the fighting and then found the fighting was in front of them too; the roof came down on them. The walls stood, the wheel-pit filled with ash, and the pear tree in the yard, which should have burned with everything else in the pear country, did not. It stands now in a plain where nothing else green grows for twelve miles, and it bears, every year, exactly one pear, which does not fall.

Nance Oddy came back in Seedwake 3003, when the company was still arguing about which way the road had gone, and has not left. She lives in the one corner of the mill that keeps a roof, a lean-to of wagon-boards and sailcloth over the old grain-store. Travellers call the place the Burned Mill because that is what it is. She calls it the mill.

### What is here
- **The bench**, a slab of millstone under the tree, scratched edge to edge with tallies: every traveller to pass in nine years, in groups of five, in rows, with a letter for which way they were going. The company's stake-parties have their own corner, and the last four springs of it are blank.
- **The water-butt**, a barrel sunk to its rim by the wall, roofed with a door. The company's stake-party used to fill it on Stake Day; since 3010 Nance fills it herself from the Treaty Stone's pilgrim-cistern, three miles each way with two buckets on a yoke, and will not be thanked. The water is free. Taking more than you can drink is the one thing that makes her shout.
- **The bell**, a cracked cow-bell on a post by the road, for travellers lost in a greywind to steer by. She rings it every quarter-hour in bad weather, all night if she has to. Soldiers call it the Widow's Bell and pretend not to be glad of it.
- **The fork.** A hundred paces east of the mill a track leaves the road, south of east, not staked: the glassland track to [[Fort Mercy]], which the Glassers use because it keeps them off the company's road. Where it leaves, half-buried, lie the black ribs and iron tyres of a burnt wagon.
- **The wheel-pit**, under the dead wheel, full to the brim with grey ash that never blows away. Nobody digs in it. Nance will not have it dug.
- **The pear tree**, and the pear.

### The pear
It hangs on the lowest bough on the yard side, at a man's eye-height, green and hard, a little smaller than a fist. It has hung there since the autumn of 3002. Nobody has picked it: not the Glassers, who are afraid of it, not the soldiers, who have a bet on it ([[Spoons Aldane]] has it at forty to one against "this year"), not the pilgrims, who tie ribbons on the bough above it. This Ashfall, for the first time, it has begun to redden at the stem.

### Who is here
[[Nance Oddy]], always. Glassers on the track, who stop for water and say nothing about where they are going. Company stake-parties in spring, when there are any. Kettle Brindlewake's cousins twice a month, with a hand-cart of salt and iron for somewhere south that they do not name. Pilgrims who have miscounted the miles to the Treaty Stone and are surprised to be here. Now and then a dwarf of the company, [[Gerda Hollin]], who walks the extra three miles from the Stone on a Restday with a loaf under her arm.

### Rumours
- The pear tree is alive because someone is buried under it.
- Nance Oddy counts the stakes and knows who moves them.
- If the pear ever falls, the road is finished.
- Nance has a book with every traveller in it, and she sold a page of it to the Glassers once.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True: her husband, Wat, and their two children, who were in the mill that night. She buried what she found under the tree in 3003 and does not say so, but she will not have the ground dug. 2. True. See her page. 3. Nobody knows, Nance included. She has decided it is true and acts accordingly. 4. False about the page; she has never sold a line of the Toll. True about the Glassers, in a way she has not forgiven herself for, and that is on her page too.
:::

### Things to do
Drink, and fill your skins, and be told how many you are. Read the bench and find the company's corner and count the blank springs. Buy a sentence of the count: she will tell you, for a copper each, how many stakes stand between here and the Stone today, how many stood yesterday, and which Glasser crew went down the track last and how many came back. Ask to see the Toll. Sit under the tree at dusk, which she allows, and listen to her tell the pear that it is nearly time. Ring the bell in a greywind and hear, from the direction of the Stone, somebody ring back.

:::gm
### The Toll
Nance keeps a ledger she calls the Toll, in a tin box in the grain-store: one line a party, the date, the count, the beasts, the way they went. She charges nothing for passing. She wrote it, she says, "so somebody would know who went east", and it is the only written witness to what happened at the fork in Ashfall. The lines the pay-wagon mystery needs, in her square hand:

*7 Ashfall. Dawn. A cart, House-sealed, two drivers in grey, no escort. Did not stop for water. Turned south at my fork. Heavy.*
*8 Ashfall. The same cart, back, light, two drivers walking beside it. Four Glassers met it at the fork, took the mules, stripped the iron and burnt the rest where it stood. Burned till noon. The drivers walked west. Did not stop for water.*
*9 Ashfall. One child from the Keep, the drummer, alone, no rope, no bell. Poked in the ashes. Took a buckle. Would not stay for water. Told her she'd die out here. She said everyone says that.*

She will show the Toll to anyone who is kind to the tree. The House seal she describes is the Tollamund house-mark; she did not know what it was, and does not care. Pair it with Mistress Hebbe's toll-log at the gatehouse ("House cart, sealed, east, 5 cp") and the party has the whole route of the strongbox from the bridge to the fork, and from the fork there is only one place to go. Tracking from here to [[Fort Mercy]] runs on the glassland track (Survival DC 12, three successes before a greywind buries the ruts).

### The pear
The mill stands where one of the Nine Roads of [[Ul-Tarran]], the covered Lithic way that runs under the March toward the Ninth Stone, passes under the old millstream in a culvert of fitted grey stone; the mill's cellar floor is its roof, and the Oddys, for a hundred and forty years, kept their cheese on it. The pear tree's roots have gone down through the cellar into the culvert, and the tree is alive for the same reason the Weeping Orchard is: it is drinking the ward. For nine years the ward held and the pear hung. The pear is reddening now because the ward is failing, and the tree is being fed more than it can hold.

The pear falls on the night of 9 Leafrot, the full Ember of session 8, whatever the party does, and what it is when Nance picks it up at dawn depends on what they did. **Reseal**: a pear, sweet, with one hard black seed in it; she plants the seed in the wheel-pit and the ash takes it. **Let it crack**: the pear is stone before it hits the ground, the dead of the mill climb out of the wheel-pit and walk east, and the tree is bare of leaves by noon. **Use it**: it does not fall. It hangs red, and never ripens, and never rots.

Eaten before then: by a Hollowborn, the true dream (as the Orchard's sap: see [[The Weeping Orchard]]); by anyone else, a DC 12 Wisdom save against a short, horrible one, and a day of feeling the lantern on the hill as a warmth on one side of the face, six miles off. Eating it does nothing to the ward. It does a great deal to Nance.

### The wheel-pit
Twenty-six people died under the mill's roof on the night of Cinders, Nance's three among them, and twenty-three of them did not pass Hathe's door. At the full Ember they stand in the wheel-pit to their waists in ash, facing east, and a Hollowborn in the yard sees them plainly; anyone else feels the cold come off the pit like a cellar door opening. They do not leave the pit and they do not speak, except that Nance, who is not Hollowborn and cannot see them, sits on the bench with three cups and talks, and now and then the ash in the pit moves as if something in it had shifted its weight. If the party is here at a red moon, let a Hollowborn PC see a man and two children at the pit's edge, nearest the bench, listening.

### If trouble starts
Nobody comes. The company's patrol turns back at the Stone. Nance has a boar-spear, a bell and a pear-country woman's opinion of people who start trouble under her tree, and the Glassers, who need her water, will kill anyone who hurts her, afterwards, slowly, which she knows and they know she knows.

== GM notes ==

The Burned Mill is the first shelter on every eastward journey (sessions 2, 6 and 8) and should feel like one: the first place the party stops, drinks, and is counted. Use it three ways. In session 2 it is the clue: Nance's Toll and the burnt wagon turn the pay-wagon hunt south toward Fort Mercy. In session 6 it is the last ordinary conversation before the Rent. In session 8 the column passes it on 7 Leafrot and the pear is red; let a player see it and say nothing, and then tell them in the epilogue what Nance found under the tree on the morning of the 10th.

The one fruit is the ash-road rumour made solid ("There is a pear tree still alive at the Burned Mill": true, and it has one fruit on it). Do not let anyone eat it before session 5 unless they are willing to break Nance's heart on camera.
