> The hall under Hollowhall's gate is a quarter-mile long and the forges run down the middle of it like a street of fires, forty of them, each under its own flue cut up through the mountain, and the sound is a weather: hammers, bellows, the hiss of the quench, and under it all a single low note the whole hall makes together that you feel in your back teeth. The near side is lit and loud and has the market on it, trestles of tools laid out like silver. The far side is dark, lit only by the forges themselves, and the smiths there work with their faces to the fire and their backs to the hall, and they do not speak, and when they turn to the quench the floor behind them is lit by the forge and nothing stands on it.

The Underlook is the hold that holds the gate, the Hall-Mother's own, and its forge-hall has made Hollowhall's tools since the first carvers cut the gate in 412 and needed chisels. A Hollowhall chisel keeps its edge through a mile of granite; a Hollowhall pick is passed from mother to daughter; the Regency's great builders bought here and the Glassers of the Ashenreach would sell a cousin for a case of the drills. Since the Return the hall has two sides. The lit side is the surface clans', trade and tally and shouting. The dark side belongs to the Returned, who came back up in 3004 wanting warm food, a wall at their backs and work, and were given the forges nearest the rock, because a forge is the one place a dwarf's missing shadow does not show. They work without speaking. Their work is better than the lit side's and the lit side knows it and says nothing, because nothing is what is said down here.

Forge-Warden Harrek Underlook keeps the hall. He is big, red-plaited, loud and shrewd, sets the prices with a piece of chalk on a slate the size of a shield, and carries every question for the dark side across the hall himself and brings the answer back, which is usually a nod. He trained Mardle Ock as a cutter before the war and will say so to anyone from Fort Mercy, with a face that suggests Mardle still owes him for a pick.

### Stock
- A Hollowhall chisel, 5 sp. The thing itself. Cold-set, hold-marked, and the Warden will sharpen it for life if you bring it back.
- A pick, 2 gp. A glass-pick for the Ashenreach's cuts, with the long narrow head Vexa's crews want, 3 gp.
- A shovel, 3 gp: forged, not cast, and in the Glassers' slang "a bishop", because it is the one tool they will not lend.
- A deep-oil lamp, 1 gp: a miner's lamp of black iron with a wick-guard and a reservoir that burns a day on a gill. Takes any oil. The Returned side's lamps have no shutter, because they do not want the dark.
- Rope, mountain-hemp, 1 sp the ten fathoms; the company rope on the Rent's steps came from here in 3004 and is due renewing.
- Nails, rivets, hinges, hoops, by the hundredweight, at the slate's price, which changes with the Harl iron.
- Drills, borers, wedges, feathers and a mason's full set in a leather roll, 12 gp, the price of the one the Regency's Master of Works ordered in 2979 and never paid for; the Warden has the paper, and shows it.
- **Not for sale, on the dark side:** a key, hand-long, black iron, bow shaped like a shut door, lying on Durn Mallow's anvil until he finishes it, and then not lying anywhere.

### Who is here
The Warden, at his slate, shouting. Surface smiths and their prentices on the lit side. Returned smiths on the dark, thirty or so, in silence; [[Durn Mallow]] at the last forge against the rock. Buyers: hold masons, Grimmark clansmen for pass-chains, Regency engineers with letters of credit nobody trusts, a Glasser once a year with a wagon and cash. Gerda Hollin bought her shield here and her grandmother, [[Hold-Mother Greta Holm]], comes down from the gate-shrine on Wardday to stand by the dark side's forges while they are cold and say nothing, which is the hold's idea of a visit.

### Rumours
- The Returned smiths' work is better because they are not distracted by their shadows.
- There is a smith on the dark side who has made the same key three hundred times and melted every one.
- The low note the hall makes is the mountain's heart and the forges are tuned to it.
- The Regency's Master of Works, Crane, had a key cut here in 2979 and took it to the Ashenreach, and that is what he was digging with.
- The Warden knows which door the key fits and has told the Hall-Mother and nobody else.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. A joke, and the surface smiths tell it, and the Returned let them. 2. True; see [[Durn Mallow]]. 3. The surface dwarves say so. The Returned do not tune to it; they tune to something under it. 4. False. Crane bought drills and a mason's set and left the hold owing twelve crowns; the paper is real and the Warden shows it to anyone from the ash to make the point. 5. False: nobody at the Underlook knows which door. The one who comes nearest is [[Hold-Mother Greta Holm]] at the gate-shrine, who has guessed, and has not said.
:::

### Things to do
Buy a chisel and feel the difference. Watch the dark side work and realise after a minute that the forge-light behind each smith reaches the wall with nothing in between. Ask the Warden about Mardle Ock and get Mardle's whole apprenticeship and a bill. Ask him about the key and be told, loudly, that it is not for sale and never has been and that the smith does not know why he makes it and neither does anyone, and watch the dark side not turn round. Show him the Regency's paper for twelve crowns from 2979 and have him point at it with a hammer: "Drainage Works. Western. That's what they wrote on the order. Drainage."

:::gm
The hall is the campaign's second door into the Eighth Ward. The low note is real: the forges stand above the Stair That Waits ([[The Carving of Hollowhall]]), the sealed stair down to the Deep Stone, and the Returned smiths work with their backs to the hall because they are facing it. Spells cast in the forge-hall frost the quench-troughs, the way the Glass Barrow frosts; the hall is a minor ward-node and does not tick the Sleeper clock, but the dark side will stop working, all at once, and turn, and that is a thing the players should see once and not want to see again.

Durn Mallow's key is the twin of [[Hathe's Door-Key]] in every tooth. It opens the Lithic doors on the ward-road under the Ashenreach, the Glass Barrow's Red Seam door among them; see his page for why he melts it. The Warden will make a key for a buyer who brings him the words cut over the Glass Barrow's cold stair, *what we buried is not at rest*, in the Lithic hand, because Durn has asked him, once, in writing, for "the words off the door", and the Warden does not know what door, and Durn does not either. [[A Rubbing from the Ninth Stone]], or a rubbing from the Barrow lintel, will do; the Warden carries it across the hall, Durn reads it, and the next key he makes he does not melt.

Ilbert Crane's unpaid bill of 2979 is a thread to [[The Emperor's Digs]]: the Underlook sold the tools the Emperor's diggers used at the Ninth Stone, and the order was signed *Drainage Works (Western)*. Ottoline Crane, at Fort Mercy, would like to see that paper very much.
:::

== GM notes ==

Harrek Underlook is the loud, warm, grasping surface-dwarf merchant the campaign has not had yet: play him big, and let his tenderness toward the dark side show only in how carefully he carries questions across the hall. The forge-hall is a set piece: let the players hear it before they see it, and see the dark side's lit floors before anyone says the word. The joke is the bishop, the drainage order, and the Warden's bill for Mardle's pick; the dread is a hall of smiths facing a stair.
