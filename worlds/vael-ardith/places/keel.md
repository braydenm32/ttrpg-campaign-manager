The Bay of Bells is a fjord, deep and dark and barely a mile wide, and Keel clings to its western wall in tiers of tarred timber. The name is older than the town. There are bells under the water, say the pilots, a drowned temple's worth, and on still nights with the tide turning you can hear them. Keel's pilots bring ships up the sound in fog by the sound of the bells alone.

[[Sefra]]'s face here is the Bell-Ringer, oldest and least friendly of her seven, which has no face, only the sound; her priests go down on ropes once a year to touch the bells and come up changed. Whale-oil is the trade: Keel's boats go out past the Southern Deep's edge and bring back lamp-oil the whole continent burns, including, until this year, the Lantern Keep.

### Hook
The drowned bells rang on their own on the night of 2 Ashfall, all together, for the length of a breath. The last time they did that was the night of the Battle of Cinders. The priests have written to Sevenhaven and had no reply.

:::gm
The bells rang the night the Treaty Bell at the Ford sounded once with no hand on it, and for the same reason: the Ninth Ward flinched. Keel's pilots have started to dread what the next ringing will carry, and the Concord Table has begun to ask why Keel's oil stopped being bought.
:::
