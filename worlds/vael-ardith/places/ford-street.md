> A gate in a palisade of charred posts, and past it a street of grey stone fronts shouldering each other like men in a queue. The first thing is the sound: a hammer on iron somewhere to the left, a handbell, a woman calling a price, a goat objecting to being called anything. The second is the smell, bread and hot metal and wet wool and, under it, a thin sweet warmth, as if the street had a kitchen beneath it. The paving is stone in some places and black glass in others, and where the glass is wet it shines like a river and drops a man like one. At the far end the street opens on a square, a post with a bell on it, and over the roofs a hill with a cage on it. Everybody on the street looks up at the cage once as they walk, and does not mention it.

Before the battle the main street of Cinderford ran from the ford to the chapel, and it is now twenty feet down in the dark under the river: [[The Glass Barrow]] has its flooded High Street, canted and silent. When the town came back it did not dig the old street out. It laid a new one, a hundred yards uphill, on the line of the Fallowden road, and called it Ford Street because it goes to the ford. The first building on it was a forge, because the first thing the returning people needed was nails. The second was a bakery. By the third winter it had a pawnshop, which tells you how the third winter went.

It is the town's high street and the only street in the March a stranger would call one. Six hundred paces, give or take the goat. The shops are the kind a place builds when it has decided, against its better judgement, to stay: practical, grey, with hand-painted signs, and every one of them has a slate by the door with the day's prices and the day's debts, because nobody here pays on the day. On Coinday the stalls come out and the street fills shoulder to shoulder from the gate to the square, and on Wardday the Reeve's court sits at the far end and the whole street goes quiet and looks innocent.

### The street, west to east
- **The West Gate**, a gap in the palisade where the Fallowden road comes in; two company spearmen who stop nobody and a Tollamund clerk who counts everybody. Beside it on the north side, [[The Bridle & Yoke]], stables and carters' yard.
- **[[Ninefingers Forge]]**, south side by the gate, with a lean-to against the gatehouse wall at the bridge end where the same smith shoes the company's horses and says Grum's words on Ironday.
- **[[The Turncoat]]**, south side: a tailor, rag-shop and dye-vat, run by a deserter who wears his tabard inside out.
- **[[Quist & Daughter, Surveyors]]**, north side: maps, measuring and a chain hung over the door like a snake.
- **[[The Baker's Wife]]**, south side, the first lit window of the morning.
- **[[The Salt Lake]]**, north side, with a plume of steam over its roof that can be seen from the hill.
- **The cooper's yard**, south side, opposite the bathhouse: [[Wendel Hoop]] makes casks and hears everything through his ceiling. The single room above it is [[The Assessor's Office]], where [[Pell Quillon]] sleeps among the paper and [[Hester Pruitt]] fights the stove.
- **[[The Hock]]**, north side, near the square: three brass balls on a bracket, a Concord licence in the window, and a halfling in a high chair.
- **[[Harrow's Simples]]**, south side at the corner where South Street turns off to the Smokes: front door on Ford Street, back door in another town.
- **[[The Reeve's House]]**, north side on the square: the only house with glass in the windows.
- And the square itself, with [[The Treaty Bell]] on its post, the company gatehouse with [[Mistress Hebbe]] in it, the old mill with [[Tollamund & Daughters]] in that, and River Lane running down beside the mill-race to the water.

### Who is here
Dawn: bakers, the gate-spearmen yawning, [[Dunny Pike]] crossing the square at a run with the first signal of the day. Morning: housewives with baskets, kiln-hands off shift going home the long way, soldiers on errands for [[Fennick Bottlebrook]] that involve a lot of standing in doorways. Noon: the street at its busiest and loudest; [[Mirrel Vosk]] at the gatehouse reading the bills aloud with commentary. Dusk: the ford closes (one stroke of the bell), lamps are lit, the shops put up their shutters one by one from the west, and the steam from the Salt Lake turns pink. Night: the Reeve's watch, two at a time, and whoever is drinking in the Hock's back room. [[Ansel Mote]] goes up and down at dusk with his scarf over his chin and buys nothing.

On Coinday, everyone, plus the hawkers from the Low Quay and a hedge-priest of Orvanne selling blessings by the ribbon. On Wardday, the Reeve's justice and a very orderly crowd. On Restday the street sleeps late and the Smokes comes in by the back doors.

### Rumours
- Tollamund has people on the street who count what comes through every shop door.
- The Assessor's room is full of nine crates that came in with the clerk, and he had a tenth.
- You can buy anything on Ford Street except what you need.
- The street is warm because it sits over the Barrow's kitchen, and sometimes at night you can smell bread baking in the ground.
- Every shopkeeper on the street has a slate by their door, and it is the same slate: everyone is on it.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True: the Hock's keeper sends her a ledger every Coinday, and the Salt Lake's steam is heard by two sisters who forget nothing. 2. True: nine crates for the Office and a tenth that was Mote's own; Wendel Hoop's casks hid it. 3. Glib and mostly false: the street sells nearly everything, at a price that makes you wish it did not. 4. False, but the warmth is real: it is the Smokes' kiln-heat running under the street in a stone culvert to the Salt Lake, and the smell of bread is the Baker's Wife. 5. Near enough; the slates are the town's true census and the Regency's tax assessors would kill for them.
:::

### Things to do
Walk the length of it at dusk and be looked at from every door. Get a horse shod and a sword mended in the same hour. Lose a pledge at the Hock and buy it back from the shelf for double. Go into the steam and come out knowing what the Reeve said to Tollamund on Hearthday. Watch the shops shut in order and learn their order. Buy bread on credit and watch it be written down.

:::gm
Ford Street is the most watched six hundred paces in the March, and the one thing that happened in it unwatched is the thing that matters. On the night of 6 Ashfall Tollamund's wagon came to the mill-yard by the river road outside the palisade and down River Lane, never passing a shop door. The Bridle & Yoke's gate-book, Hebbe's log and the Salt Lake's steam each hold one fragment. Nobody on the street saw it, which is how the street knows it was done on purpose.

Sallow's men drink in the Hock's back room on Tideday and Wardday nights. They talk, and Bushel Tarrowdown writes it down for Tollamund. Mote walks the street at dusk to be seen walking it, then goes home along Temple Row by the back lanes; he has been counting how many of the street's shopkeepers owe Tollamund (nine of ten), because Vell wants to know who the Concord already owns.
:::

== GM notes ==

Run the street as a stage set that listens. Every shop on it has a keeper who knows exactly one thing the party needs and wants something small first. For a quick scene, roll on [[Street Events in Cinderford]] once per half-hour of play. In session 1 (Coinday, Cinders night) the market is up from dawn and the street is thin by dusk, because the company has lit its lanterns and the town goes quiet to watch them. In session 7 (Wardday) the Reeve's court clears the street and the Hock's back room is the only open door.
