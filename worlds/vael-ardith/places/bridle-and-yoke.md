> A yard behind a double gate, deep in straw, steaming faintly in the morning cold. Eight stalls on one side and a cart-shed on the other, with a wagon-bed upended against the wall to make a bench. A hand-lettered board over the gate gives prices in chalk and a warning in red: THE ASH EATS HORSES. A heavy bald man in a carter's smock is forking straw with a deliberate, unhurried rhythm, and a grey mule is watching him do it with an expression of settled contempt. The man does not stop. "Gate-book's on the nail," he says. "Sign it. I read it back. Then we'll talk about the beast."

The Bridle & Yoke was a stage-house on the old imperial road before the battle, and the stage-house was a stable before that, and nobody remembers which came first. It was burnt to the footings with the rest, and Hob Garrity rebuilt it in 3004 with timber hauled from Fallowden on the last cart he drove for pay. He had come east in Longnight 3008 with the last pay wagon that had coin in it; the Regency never paid the carter's fee either, and Hob has the Treasury chit in his smock to prove it. He drove home empty, got as far as Cinderford and found the stable standing and a lease for sale at a fair price. He has been here since, a stranded man who has made his peace with it, and writes to a wife in Fallowden on the first of every month.

It is the last ostler on the road, and the only place in the March where a stranger can hire a mule, a horse or a carter, and be quoted a price that does not change by the time the beast is saddled. Hob's rule is that the price on the board is the price, double. This is because Fallowden's carters, who used to run the road weekly, now run it only "when someone pays double", and he is a Fallowden carter, and the rate is a point of honour. Nobody on Ford Street argues it any more.

### Rates
- Stabling a beast, 3 cp the night; 5 cp with feed. The hay comes from Fallowden and costs Hob more than he charges.
- A mule, 5 cp a day. Mules are sensible. A horse, 2 sp a day and a 5 gp deposit: "the ash eats horses", and he has had more than one come back without its hooves.
- Saddle and bridle, 2 cp a day to hire.
- A cart and carter to Fallowden, 4 gp, four days out, and the carter makes his own way back at his own cost. "Double rate. It is the only rate."
- A cart to the Treaty Stone and back, 5 sp.
- A cart to Fort Mercy and back, 1 gp, if the carter has not heard the weather. He has.
- A seat on the Fallowden wagon, 1 sp, on Restdays, when it runs. It does not always run. When it does not, he refunds the silver and writes the weather in the gate-book.
- A handcart, 1 cp a day, return optional. A good one, with a rope harness and one wheel that was a wheelwright's pride before the war.
- Shoeing: next door, at [[Ninefingers Forge]]; Hob will tell you what it costs and send you there anyway.

### Who is here
Hob. A stable-lad of eleven called Dibs, who sleeps in the hay and runs messages for a penny. The grey mule, **Provisional**, who is Hob's own and has been "only until something better comes along" for six years. From the 15th, the Assessor's mule in the next stall, boarded at the Regency's expense, by which Hob means his own, and which refuses the hill. Whoever has come in off the Fallowden road that day with a story and no money. [[Ottoline Crane]]'s dowsing mare, now and then, between digs. A company courier asleep in the straw.

### Rumours
- The pay wagon came in by the West Gate on the night of the 6th, and Hob was paid to say it did not.
- A pair of horses has been booked for a night in Leafrot by someone in the Keep's colours, and nobody has been told which night.
- The grey mule bites only officers.
- Hob is a spy for Fallowden's wool-factors, and the board's price is the one he writes to them.
- There is a horse in the back stall that has been to the Rent and has not stopped looking east.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. False, and Hob will swear it, which is true and useless: the wagon came by the river road outside the palisade and down River Lane to Tollamund's mill-yard, and never passed his gate. His gate-book for the 6th reads *quiet; one pilgrim; a Fallowden drover who wanted a wheel*. 2. True: see below. 3. Provisional has bitten a Regency courier, a Concord tallyman and, in Reaping, [[Ansel Mote]], and has bitten nobody who was not wearing a clerk's coat or an officer's sash. 4. False. He writes to his wife. 5. True, and it is a Glasser's horse, abandoned; the horse does look east.
:::

### Things to do
Hire a mule for the ash. Haggle with Hob and lose gracefully. Sign the gate-book and hear it read back. Read the gate-book. Bribe Dibs for a name. Learn what a Fallowden carter thinks of the Regency, in detail. Ask for a handcart, and be told how many wheels it is guaranteed to keep.

:::gm
**Sallow has booked two horses.** On the 11th of Ashfall a man in the Keep's colours, slow-smiling and broad, who gave his own name, Bray, because it did not occur to him not to, and paid half down in new Caer Ardith silver for two horses, saddled and rested, to be ready at the West Gate yard "for a night in Leafrot, the fifth or the sixth; I'll send word". It is **Sallow's bolt-hole**: if the mutiny fails or the Keep turns, he and Bray ride west, and at the waystation another man is waiting. Hob took the money because it was new silver and he has not seen new silver since 3008. He does not know whose horses they are. He has a name, a date and an unease, and will say so to a PC who has been kind to the mule. In session 7 the horses are the physical proof of Sallow's intent; they are in the yard on the 5th, and Hob will not give them up unless paid the rest in silver, which Sallow, confined to quarters, cannot pay.

**The handcart** for session 8: the company hires it from Hob for a penny a day, unpaid, and he writes it in the gate-book as a loan to the Ninth Lantern, "return when convenient". It is the cart the lantern goes east on. He does not ask where it is going. At dawn after the choice, if the party returns, the wheel that was a wheelwright's pride is the one that has survived.
:::

== GM notes ==

Hob's voice: slow, unhurried, a Fallowden vowel that elongates anything to do with money. He says "the beast" instead of the animal's name and then gives its name, grudgingly, as if it had been a concession. He loves being asked about the road; it is the only conversation left to him that is not about debt. His one dream: to be paid for the Longnight 3008 trip by someone, anyone, before he dies. A Regency chit from a Treasury clerk named in the S7 sealed orders (via [[Whitepost]]) is a cheap way to give a player a small win.
