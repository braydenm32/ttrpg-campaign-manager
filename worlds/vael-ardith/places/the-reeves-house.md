> A grey stone house at the square's north side like every other house, except that it has glass. Eight panes at the front, thick, bubbled, a little green, and every one of them whole. The door is open on court days, and past it a long hall with a plain table across the far end, benches, a rush floor, and a wall of pegs along one side, each with a hat on it: a felt with a fox's tail, a straw one with a wasp, a mariner's cocked hat, a widow's black. By the door, on a nail at the height of a hand, hangs a single brass key as long as a finger. Nobody is looking at it. Everybody is aware of it.

The Reeve's house was the first stone one built after the battle, and the only one with glass. Odile Fairweather had the panes cut from salvage, a glazier brought from Fallowden, and the work done in the second winter, for the plain reason that a magistrate in a house with shutters is a magistrate with something to hide. She is on the north side of the square, facing the bell, where the whole town can see in. She says the light is for the justice. The town, which has been rebuilt on the principle that nothing is wasted, says it is for the hats.

There are thirty-one hats. The Reeve wears a different one every day, and has since the first court in 3003 when a man was fined five silver for contempt, for laughing at her felt. She said the hat was a decision, not a joke. The next day it was a straw bonnet with a wasp in the band, and nobody laughed, and the custom has stuck. The soldiers keep a book on it. She knows and has never given a bookmaker a thing to work with.

The court sits on Wardday, from the second bell, with the Reeve at the long table, a clerk at her elbow, and a one-legged beadle called Old Sim who strikes the floor with his staff and calls the case. She asks three questions, rules once, and says "Next." The other days the hall is a dining-room for the useful and the back door is open to petitioners.

### What is here
- **The long hall**, court on Wardday: the table, the benches, the pegs of hats.
- **The key on the nail**, a brass key by the front door, which is the only key to the locked chapel of Aurelan on [[Temple Row]]. It has hung there for eight years in full view of the town. Nobody has ever touched it.
- **The back room**, a table for twelve, where the Reeve dines with the Captain, the Assessor, the factor, the cooper, a Glasser, a drover, whoever is useful that week.
- **The cellar**, under the Reeve's own lock.
- **The letter-case**, in the upstairs room, locked.
- **The Reeve's board** by the door: four jobs at the Reeve's rates, pinned with a horseshoe nail, paid on the day.

### The scale of fines
- Drunk and disorderly, 1 sp. Short weight, 5 sp and the weights. A quarrel on the Low Quay, 2 sp each. A beast loose in the street, 2 cp the beast, goats double.
- Toll evaded: ten times the toll, which is a fortune on a cart. Coin that rings wrong: the coin, and a talking-to.
- A boundary disputed: the loser pays for the survey (see [[Quist & Daughter, Surveyors]]), and the winner pays for the beer.
- Contempt: five silver. It has been charged once.

### Who is here
**Odile Fairweather**, in the hat of the day. **Old Sim**, the beadle, who has one leg and a great deal of dignity. The Reeve's cook, who is also the court's witness to the Reeve's temper. The Reeve's watch, six men and women in a plain brown coat, two on at a time. Her surviving son, on Hearthday evenings, who holds the toll and eats in silence. A kiln-master with a complaint. A drover with a goat. A woman who has been waiting since dawn to be heard about her husband's debt.

### Rumours
- The Reeve keeps the key to the Aurelan chapel on a nail where anyone could take it, and nobody does.
- The Reeve has glass in her windows because the Concord paid for it.
- You can tell her verdict by her hat.
- She has a locked case upstairs, and what is in it she has promised to somebody who is not the town.
- The grain in her cellar would feed the town two winters, and she has never let the company know.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. True, and it is the point: a thing hung where everyone sees it and nobody dares touch is safer than any lock. 2. False. She paid for the glass in grain, over three winters. 3. Close. The hat does not say what she will rule; it says how long it will take. 4. True: the locked case holds the Concord's draft of the charter's sale, signed in her hand and not sealed. 5. True. The company does not know because it would eat it in a month.
:::

### Things to do
Sit in court on Wardday and watch three questions and one ruling. Be fined. Be fed at the long table and hear what the Reeve wants from you by the way she serves the bread. Read the Reeve's board. Ask about the key and be told, courteously, that it is a Reeve's matter. Stand outside the glass at night and see her at the table with the letter-case open.

:::gm
**The key.** The shrine of Aurelan was locked in 3002 by the Regency's last magistrate, who left the same night. In her first spring, 3003, short of coin to rebuild the bridge, Odile took the shrine's plate (twelve pieces of old Regency gold, the lamp and the chalice) and sold it at Fallowden, and kept the door locked afterwards so nobody would count what was missing. The nail by the door is not carelessness. It is a decision. In 3006, when her elder son Edmar died of the glass-lung in the bad winter, she cut his name into the inner face of the chapel door with a sharp point, at the height of a kneeling woman's hand: *Edmar Fairweather, 2986 to 3006*. She opens it once a year, on Crownfast (15 Highsun), alone, with one candle, and locks it again. If the chapel were ever opened by a stranger, the Church would find a name to look at before it found a hole. She does not want the town to know that the bridge was paid for by a god, and the god's cost was her son's name.

**The case.** In the upstairs room: the Concord's draft of the charter sale (see [[What has the Reeve signed?]]), signed, unsealed. The fine print includes the Keep and "all fixtures and fittings". She has read it eleven times. She does not read the last clause aloud.

**On 5 Leafrot** (session 7, court day), the court sits in the morning with the street empty and every window open, and she leaves it at noon in the hat of a magistrate and walks to the Keep gate with the case under her arm. What happens next is hers to choose: see [[Odile Fairweather]].

**The grain** is the truth under the gossip: the cellar holds forty sealed barrels, two winters' worth, and her private plan if the company stands down. If the party learns of it, it is the card that answers Tollamund's offer.
:::

== GM notes ==

A courtroom with a stage-manager's eye. The hall should feel like a room that has seen everyone's worst day and is not impressed. Her hats are a joke with a spine: use them as clocks. When the party first sees her, give the hat; when she is afraid, the hat is wrong (a mourning one, on a day of no death). The nail and the key are a long clue to the Reeve's private grief; do not dwell. If a PC takes the key, she will say nothing, hold out her hand, and wait, and the PC will return it.
