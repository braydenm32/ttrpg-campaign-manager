> The lane runs between dry-stone walls that have not fallen, and at its end the orchard begins, and it is still an orchard. Row on row of pear trees, old and well-pruned, in full leaf. Then you are among them and see that every leaf is grey stone, every twig, every fruit, and that the ground under them is not grass but ash with a crust on it, and that the fruit, when you touch it, is warm.

Before the war the Cinder valley was the pear country, and the orchards ran for miles. The battle burned them all but this one, and this one did not burn. It turned to stone in a night, leaves and fruit and the birds in the branches, and nobody knows why, though everyone who was there knows what else happened in it that night: it was where the deserters, the cooks and the walking wounded of eight companies hid, and where, by dawn, a ninth lantern was found burning that nobody had counted.

At the full of the Ember, every twenty-third night, the trees weep. Red sap runs from the bark where the branches fork and from the stems of the stone fruit, and drips, and sets by morning into beads that the Glassers sell as [[Orchard Amber]]. Between fulls every bough keeps its beads of set sap, and the oldest tree in the cooks' camp trickles a vial a night. The Hollowborn of the Smokes walk out here that night and sit under the trees and listen. Nobody else stays past dark if they can help it.

### What is here
- **The rows**: four hundred and eleven trees, in twenty rows, every one bearing ([[Stone Pears]]).
- **The lane gate** at [[Bellwether]], where the orchard lane leaves the road a mile above the ford. The Tollivers keep a knotted cord in their upper window, one knot for every red moon's lanterns.
- **The cooks' camp**, at the orchard's heart, a ring of stone-fused cookpots and a fire-pit with glass in it, where the ninth lantern was first seen lit. On the anniversary of Cinders, 14 Ashfall, the company musters here at dusk in silence, and nobody speaks.
- **The ribbon tree**, by the lane, the one living thing: a sucker from a stone root, waist-high, with three green leaves and a thousand ribbons tied to it.
- **The grave rows**, along the north wall, where the dead of that night were buried by the Ravelers before Corvel Rise existed. Shallow. Not all still occupied.

### Rumours
- The pears are sweet if you can bite them.
- Brother Tallow lit the lantern here, from his pipe.
- A stone bird in the fourth row sings at red moon.
- The sap is the Sleeper's blood.

:::gm
**Which are true.** 1. Nobody has. 2. He says not. He says he found it lit. (He followed it up the hill; he did not light it.) 3. True. 4. The Hollowborn say so. They are not entirely wrong.
:::

### Things to do
Walk the rows and count the fruit, which changes. Tie a ribbon. Sit in the cooks' camp with a veteran who was there and hear what they will say, which is little. Come at red moon, with the Hollowborn, and taste the sap, and dream. Dig, if you dare, in the grave rows, and find that one of the graves holds a lantern-pole and no body.

:::gm
The lantern was lit here, not at the Keep, from [[The Ward Shard]]: a splinter of the Ninth Stone blown sixty miles west in the blast, which fell into the orchard and petrified everything it touched. The trees are, in effect, Lithic now, and the red sap is the ward's own substance leaking through them at the moon's pull. Session 5 brings the party here with the Rook-Court's ledger and a dying veteran's confession; the confession is old [[Halloran Pike]]'s, the deserter who carried the lantern up to the Keep that night, and it names the cook who lit it from a fire with the shard in it and carried the shard itself to the Keep in a soup-pot: [[Mother Hennet]], who keeps [[The Last Candle]] and has never told anyone. (She has known Pike for what he was since his first night.) In 3011 Marrow cancels the anniversary muster after the pay notice, and the ring of cookpots stays empty on the 14th. Drinking the sap gives a Hollowborn a true dream and anyone else a DC 12 Wisdom save against a short, horrible one.
:::
